# Product survey: how audio-to-chord-sheet / tab products handle strumming patterns, riff tab, two guitar parts, rests and uncertainty (as of October 2026)

Scope note: this survey covers commercial apps, discontinued products, open-source projects and the closest research papers. Reddit was out of reach. Several Chordify pages (support.chordify.net, chordify.net/pages, chordify.net/learn-songs, play.google.com listing) returned HTTP 403 or truncated to the fetcher; where a quote from those pages is given it was obtained through a search-engine snippet of the page and is marked "(via search snippet)". Everything else was read from the cited page.

Headline for the reviewer: no shipping product claims to derive strumming patterns from audio. Chordify says outright that it cannot and does not expect to; Ultimate Guitar, Chordify (song lessons), Yousician and Simply Guitar supply patterns by human curation; Songsterr, Klangio, Songscription and Moises output notes/chords/tab but no stroke-direction row. The only strum-from-audio work found is three 2025 research papers (two from Yousician and KIT), none shipped. No product found uses greyed/dashed/"approx." marks for uncertain content; the industry convention is instead (a) silence about confidence plus an edit tool, (b) difficulty tiers ("simplify chords", Easy/Medium/Advanced) that trade detail for reliability, and (c) Soundslice's "ask the user a question" flow for low-confidence detections in its (image-based) scanner.

## Key question 1: Chordify — how does its strumming-pattern feature work, and what do its blog, help centre, patents or research papers say about chord/strum extraction and showing confidence?

### Takeaway
Chordify has no audio-derived strumming-pattern feature and states it cannot detect patterns accurately; the patterns it does show live in hand-made "song lessons" (per song section) in its iOS Toolkit. Its chord pipeline (beat/downbeat + chroma via Sonic Annotator, then the HarmTrace harmony model) resolves uncertain beats by tonal-harmony rules rather than by displaying confidence, and its public accuracy figures are informal (70–95% founder estimate, 2013). Its own research shows expert annotators agree only ~73% on major/minor labels, which the company frames as a ceiling on any accuracy claim.

### Cited Findings
- Chordify support article "Does Chordify show the strumming patterns?" says (via search snippet; page returns 403 to fetcher): "We're not able to accurately detect strumming patterns/rhythms unfortunately", and that it is near-impossible to detect accurately with algorithms and the company does not expect to add it — [Chordify support](https://support.chordify.net/hc/en-us/articles/360019489618-Does-Chordify-show-the-strumming-patterns)
- A 2023 community feature request ("Guitar: Show Intro and Strumming Pattern") got the same answer and a user asked for a way to add patterns manually (via search snippet; page 403) — [Chordify community](https://support.chordify.net/hc/en-us/community/posts/8035864931101-Feature-Guitar-Show-Intro-and-Strumming-Pattern)
- Apple App Store (KR) listing text (via search snippet) says Chordify offers "chords, progressions and strumming patterns per song section in our song lessons"; the fetched KR and TN listings confirm "Song lessons" exist (v8.31.0 / v9.3.0 release notes: "Song lessons now flow in the right order") and that Premium's listed features are transpose, capo, slow-down, loop, MIDI download, PDF printouts — strumming is not among them — [App Store KR](https://apps.apple.com/KR/app/id1073624757); [App Store TN](https://apps.apple.com/tn/app/chordify-chords-songs-tuner/id1073624757)
- Chordify's "Learn songs" page (via search snippet; 403) describes the Toolkit on iOS with a heading "Learn the strumming patterns"; the Toolkit page (via search snippet; 403) describes "real-time feedback on strumming technique" as part of the Premium + Toolkit bundle — [chordify.net/learn-songs](https://chordify.net/learn-songs); [Toolkit page](https://chordify.net/pages/premium-guitar-toolkit/)
- Guitar Chalk's April 2026 review: strumming patterns are "Not shown"; the reviewer "didn't believe Chordify provides them" and a reader noted users must work out strumming by ear. A reader comment says the program "often moves a chord change out of place". A mobile user complained diagrams showed inversions; Chordify attributed this to the small display. "Simplify chords" is selected by default on the iPhone app — [Guitar Chalk](https://www.guitarchalk.com/chordify-review/)
- Chord pipeline (ISMIR 2015 late-breaking demo, J. P. Magalhães, Chordify): "Chordify uses the sonic annotator for extraction of audio features. These features consist of the downbeat positions and the tonal content of a piece of music. Next, the Haskell program HarmTrace takes these features and computes the chords... At beat positions where the audio matches a particular chord well, this chord is used in final transcription. However, in case there is uncertainty about the sounding chords at a specific position in the song, the HarmTrace harmony model will select the correct chords based on the rules of tonal harmony." The demo also introduced a chord-editing feature "which allows people to correct mistakes in the automatically recognised chords", lets users "change the measure of the song", and says "In the near future, we will use the edits to improve the algorithm itself, and to implement a system that merges edits from various users into one single corrected version." — [ISMIR 2015 LBD42 PDF](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/LBD/LBD42.pdf)
- The demo cites the underlying papers: de Haas et al., "Improving audio chord transcription by exploiting harmonic and metric knowledge", ISMIR 2012; "HarmTrace: Automatic functional harmonic analysis", Computer Music Journal 37(4), 2013; HarmTrace code on Hackage — [ISMIR 2015 LBD42 PDF](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/LBD/LBD42.pdf)
- A 2019 SMC paper by Pauwels et al. on chord-based search states "the underlying transcription algorithm [3] therefore produces a measure of confidence in addition to its chord output" and uses it for ranking; whether [3] is the Chordify algorithm could not be confirmed from the search result — [SMC 2019 D3_06](https://www.smc2019.uma.es/articles/D3/D3_06_SMC2019_paper.pdf)
- Accuracy: co-founder Dion ten Heggeler (2013) "reckons that Chordify is about 70% to 95% correct when it comes to chords. Though this does, of course, depend on the type of music you give it" — [TNW 2013](https://thenextweb.com/apps/2013/05/02/chordify-taps-soundcloud-and-youtube-to-show-you-the-chords-to-your-favorite-songs/)
- Annotator subjectivity: Koops, de Haas, Bransen, Volk, "Annotator subjectivity in harmony annotations of popular music", Journal of New Music Research 48(3), 2019: the Chordify Annotator Subjectivity Dataset (50 songs, 4 expert annotators) shows "only 73 percent overlap on average for the traditional major–minor vocabulary and 54 percent overlap for the most complex chord labels", and the paper argues current chord-estimation systems "perform beyond this subjectivity ceiling by about 10 percent" — [Southampton eprints 431806](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/431806); [UvA PDF](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/62264010/Annotator_subjectivity_in_harmony_annotations_of_popular_music.pdf); Chordify press release summarising the study (quotes 76% simple / 59% complex, figures differ from the abstract) — [chordify.pr.co](https://chordify.pr.co/180546-chordify-team-members-publish-an-article-about-subjectivity-in-chord-recognition)
- Instruments: "Animated Chord Views: Visualize how to play with dynamic chord diagrams for guitar, piano, and ukulele" — [App Store TN](https://apps.apple.com/tn/app/chordify-chords-songs-tuner/id1073624757)

### Inferences
- Chordify's "uncertainty policy" is to hide it: ambiguous beats are filled by a harmony prior so the sheet always shows a chord, and the escape hatch is the user editor plus a "simplify chords" tier. This is the opposite of the Youkulele greyed-stroke approach and worth stating explicitly in the review.
- Chordify's decision not to ship audio-derived strumming (while shipping curated per-section patterns in lessons) is the strongest industry signal that strum-from-audio is not yet product-grade — consistent with the Youkulele ear-check finding that patterns are wrong more often than right.
- The 73%/54% annotator agreement result gives a defensible ceiling argument for any chord-accuracy claim in the Youkulele record.

### Gaps
- No Chordify patent was found (a Google Patents query for "strumming pattern" detection returned only unrelated hardware/rhythm patents, see Q6). I found no Chordify blog post describing a confidence measure or how it is surfaced; the SMC 2019 reference to a confidence output could not be tied to Chordify's production algorithm.
- Whether Chordify's song-lesson strumming patterns are authored in-house or contributed could not be confirmed (pages blocked).

## Key question 2: Per-product behaviour — Moises, LALAL.AI, Klangio, Soundslice, Songsterr, Ultimate Guitar, Yousician, Simply Guitar, Riffstation, Chord ai, AnthemScore, Melody Scanner, Capo, Transcribe!

### Takeaway
Products split into four camps: (1) chord-only from audio with difficulty tiers and an editor but no confidence display (Chordify, Moises, Chord ai, Capo, Riffstation); (2) note-level tab from audio with an editor and warnings but no confidence display (Songsterr AI, Klangio, Songscription, Melody Scanner, AnthemScore, Lamucal); (3) curated human arrangements where strumming patterns exist because a person wrote them (Ultimate Guitar, Yousician, Simply Guitar, Chordify lessons); (4) manual-transcription aids that refuse to automate (Transcribe!, Soundslice). None outputs a strum-direction row from audio; multi-guitar handling is either "pick a stem" (Moises/LALAL) or acknowledged as unreliable (Songsterr, Klangio reviewers, Yousician paper).

### Inferences
- "Riff over chords": none of the chord apps attempts to separate a riff from the chord bed; the tab apps (Songsterr, Klangio) do the reverse and transcribe notes without a chord row. No product found prints both a chord/strum row and a riff tab for the same bar, nor a "riff heard, not transcribed" placeholder.
- "Two guitars": the only product-level mitigation is to let the user ask for "rhythm guitar" and "lead guitar" as separate output parts (Songsterr) or to separate a guitar stem first (LALAL.AI, Moises, Chord ai, Lamucal); independent testers (heise, MusicRadar) say mixed guitars are where these tools fail.
- "Rests/silence": Capo documents that weak harmony yields "none at all" chords (i.e. blank), Ultimate Guitar's convention is empty space in the stroke row, and the Yousician paper uses explicit empty patterns per time signature. Printing nothing for silent bars is the industry norm.

### Gaps
- No Moises help-centre or changelog text on chord accuracy, confidence or strumming was reachable; the "synced guitar tabs" claim comes only from third-party app-store mirrors.
- Simply Guitar: no source on how songs are arranged beyond "adjusted to your playing level".
- Chord ai's current Pro feature list and whether tabs were ever added could not be confirmed from a primary page.
- MusicRadar's Klangio review date/byline could only be attributed via a secondary citation; no star rating found.

## Key question 3: Open-source projects that produce strumming patterns, chord sheets or tab from audio

### Takeaway
Open-source chord tools (autochord, chord-extractor/Chordino, Omnizart, ChordMini) output beat- or time-aligned chord labels with no confidence shown; tab tools (audiotab, jammer-worker, Fingerstyle Tab MCP) chain Demucs + Basic Pitch + a string/fret assigner and do not model strumming. The only released strumming-pattern code is Yousician's companion repo (bar-line post-processing + audio excerpts, no model or dataset).

### Cited Findings
- **autochord** (ISMIR 2021 LBD, cjbayron): NNLS-Chroma VAMP features into a Bi-LSTM-CRF; 25 classes (12 maj, 12 min, N); "test accuracy of 67.33%" (dataset unnamed); outputs (start, end, chord) tuples and MIREX .lab files; JS visualiser can overlay a second .lab (e.g. ground truth) for comparison; Linux only; Apache-2.0; 166 stars — [github.com/cjbayron/autochord](https://github.com/cjbayron/autochord)
- **chord-extractor** (ohollo): Python wrapper around the Chordino Vamp plugin; outputs ChordChange(chord, timestamp) lists, labels like 'C', 'Am7b5', 'N'; multiprocessing batch mode; no accuracy claims; GPL-2.0; 249 stars — [github.com/ohollo/chord-extractor](https://github.com/ohollo/chord-extractor)
- **Omnizart** (Music and Culture Technology Lab): chord recognition via the Harmony Transformer encoder-decoder, "25 chord types (including major, minor, and absence of chord)"; audio input only; incompatible with ARM macOS; the paper says pre-trained models "closely reproduce the performances reported in the original papers" — [arXiv 2106.00497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00497v1); [JOSS paper](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.03391.pdf)
- **ChordMini / ChordMiniApp** (ptnghia-j): Next.js + Python web app; YouTube or upload; chord models Chord-CNN-LSTM, BTC-SL, BTC-PL; beats via Beat-Transformer (Spleeter-separated) or madmom; segmentation via SongFormer; Gemini for Roman numerals/enharmonic correction; guitar diagrams from chords-db with slash-chord matching; experimental melody via Sheet Sage with the caveat "timing and accuracy may be limited"; no per-chord confidence display; MIT; 408 stars; positioned by its author as an alternative to "Chordify or ChordAI" — [github.com/ptnghia-j/ChordMiniApp](https://github.com/ptnghia-j/ChordMiniApp); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/chord-mini)
- **Basic Pitch** (Spotify): outputs MIDI with pitch bends, optional note-event CSV and raw NPZ; polyphonic, instrument-agnostic, "works best on one instrument at a time"; mono downmix, 22050 Hz; Apache-2.0; 5.7k stars; README documents only min/max frequency parameters (onset/frame thresholds exist in the CLI but are not described on the README page fetched) — [github.com/spotify/basic-pitch](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch)
- A Basic Pitch-based chord pipeline ("Amadeus/Chordzart") documents stages of pitch-class clustering, smoothing and a confidence stage built on top of Basic Pitch note events — [Chordzart docs](https://amadeus-chordzart.readthedocs.io/en/latest/pipeline/chord_detection.html)
- **audiotab** (LilioYa): downloads audio, transcribes with Basic Pitch, renders tab/score via LilyPond to PDF; "simple" mode reduces to a single melodic line, "full" keeps chords; no strumming model — [audiotab mirror](https://gitblind.noratr.app/LilioYa/audiotab)
- **Fingerstyle Tab MCP Server**: Demucs separation + Basic Pitch notes + role-based string/fret assignment; targets fingerstyle, not strumming; MIT — [glama.ai listing](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/ahzgz77uqu)
- **jammer-worker** (private project): Demucs + Omnizart/Basic Pitch, writes ASCII tabs via MIDI-to-tab; not a strum detector — [git.alexako.com](https://git.alexako.com/alex/jammer-worker/commit/51e43f0ef27332e180467273221947f30fd43061)
- **robust-guitar-tabs/code**: research training code for tablature transcription on GuitarSet-style data, not an end-to-end tool — [github.com/robust-guitar-tabs/code](https://github.com/robust-guitar-tabs/code)
- **YousicianGit/rhythmic-pattern-transcription**: companion repo (14 stars, 6 commits) with `downbeat.py` (bar-line post-processing) and two 10-second excerpts per difficulty level (full mix + guitar track with clicks at predicted strum onsets); no model weights, no pattern vocabulary, no license stated — [GitHub repo](https://github.com/YousicianGit/rhythmic-pattern-transcription)
- **TART** (arXiv Oct 2025): four-stage audio-to-tab (piano-transcription model adapted to guitar, MLP technique classifier for slides/bends/percussive hits, Transformer string/fret assignment, LSTM tab generation); no code release or accuracy figures on the abstract page — [arXiv 2510.02597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02597)

### Inferences
- The open-source stack the Youkulele pipeline uses (Demucs-style separation + librosa/torch) is the same stack every hobby tab generator uses; none of them has solved strumming, and the accuracy figures that exist (autochord 67%) are below the commercial anecdotes.
- autochord's "load a second .lab for comparison" visualiser is the only open-source UI pattern found for showing disagreement between a prediction and a reference — a possible model for an ear-check overlay.

### Gaps
- No GitHub project was found that outputs a down/up/mute stroke row from audio. The Yousician repo does not release the model, and the KIT/ISMIR 2025 strum-direction paper does not state a code release.
- Omnizart's chord-model accuracy on a named chord benchmark was not found in the fetched material.

## Key question 4: Ukulele-specific tools — transcribing or arranging from audio, and mapping guitar parts to ukulele

### Takeaway
No tool was found that transcribes audio specifically for ukulele. "Ukulele support" in every chord app means a ukulele chord-diagram view on top of instrument-agnostic chord detection; tab tools export guitar tab and leave the re-entrant/octave mapping to the player. Human arrangers (Yousician) produce separate, lower-level ukulele arrangements rather than mechanically converting the guitar part.

### Cited Findings
- Chordify: instrument selector "guitar, piano, or ukulele" with animated chord views — [App Store TN](https://apps.apple.com/tn/app/chordify-chords-songs-tuner/id1073624757)
- Chord ai: chord diagrams for "guitar, piano, and ukulele" — [chordai.net](https://www.chordai.net/)
- Capo: guitar diagrams can be adapted for "bass, banjo, ukulele, mandolin"; user-drawn tabs support "Guitar, Bass, Ukulele, Banjo, and Mandolin in various tunings" — [Capo 4 press release](https://supermegaultragroovy.com/products/capo/press/pr/2020-11-17/); [TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2013/12/13/funbits-master-your-fretboard-with-capo-3-for-mac/)
- The Chord Detector (iOS): ukulele/banjo/mandolin diagrams as an in-app purchase — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id420873903)
- Ultimate Guitar has ukulele versions of tabs/chords — [UG help: Tabs](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741306-tabs)
- Songscription (vendor, 1 Jul 2026): "Songscription does not export a ukulele-specific tab"; mapping rules given: standard uke is g-C-E-A re-entrant; guitar's top four strings with a capo at fret 5 give the same pitches, so "a note's fret on the guitar maps to the ukulele with five frets subtracted" when the tab uses only the top four strings; the high g "sits an octave above the guitar equivalent, so bass notes below the melody may not land where a guitar tab expects"; mapping is "clean" for melody/picked figures in the middle and upper range; baritone (D-G-B-E) transfers top-four-string guitar tab unchanged; recommended workflow: take chords as-is, adapt specific riffs by hand, transpose to C/G/F/D/A — [Songscription ukulele](https://www.songscription.ai/blog/audio-to-ukulele-tabs)
- Yousician's ukulele arrangements are made by a separate Music Education Designer (Vellu Halkosalmi) and sit at lower levels than the guitar versions; the ukulele chords exercise covers strumming — [Yousician blog](https://yousician.com/blog/new-song-landslide-for-guitar-ukulele-and-singing)
- Commercial ukulele riff arrangements (e.g. "Iron Man Guitar Main Riff for Ukulele") are sold in GCEA tuning, "playable along to the original with linear (low G) and re-entrant (high G) tuning" — [Ukealong Gumroad](https://ukealong.gumroad.com/l/iron-man-main-riff); Redemption Song simplified intro riff — [Ukealong](https://ukealong.gumroad.com/l/redemption-song-simplified-intro)
- A ukulele writer noted some tunes "don't transfer directly to a re-entrant uke as pleasingly, but they would work well on a low-G uke... or on a baritone" — [Ukulele Hunt](https://ukulelehunt.com/blog/page/159)
- Melody Scanner and AnthemScore pages do not mention ukulele — [melodyscanner.com](https://melodyscanner.com/); [lunaverus.com](https://www.lunaverus.com/)

### Inferences
- The Youkulele pipeline's "ukulele tab when a riff repeats steadily" goes beyond any product found; the closest commercial practice is a human arranger choosing low-G vs re-entrant and simplifying the riff, which suggests the sheet should state the assumed tuning (high-G vs low-G) explicitly, as the Gumroad arrangements do.

### Gaps
- No ukulele-specific audio transcription or automatic guitar-to-ukulele arrangement tool was found in any source; no documentation of how any app handles re-entrant tuning in automatic transcription.

## Key question 5: User-facing conventions for marking uncertain or unmeasured content on a sheet (grey, dashed, "approx.", confidence bars)

### Takeaway
No music product surveyed greys out, dashes or labels low-confidence chords/notes; the shipped conventions are (1) warn in prose and hand the user an editor, (2) offer a simplified tier, (3) show blank where nothing was detected, (4) Soundslice's "turn an uncertain detection into a question" review step, and (5) autochord's two-.lab overlay. Editorial/critical-edition notation (brackets for editorial additions, dashed items for conjecture, a half-dashed slur for "incomplete in source") is the nearest established visual vocabulary.

### Cited Findings
- Soundslice scanner: uncertain stem direction or tie target becomes a question on a review screen; rendered notation shown above the source image for spot-checking — [Soundslice blog 226](https://soundslice.com/blog/226/pdf-and-photo-scanning-beta); [Soundslice blog 256](https://soundslice.com/blog/256/improvements-to-pdf-image-importer-sept-21)
- Chordify fills uncertain beats via the harmony model (no visible marker) and provides a chord editor — [ISMIR 2015 LBD42](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/LBD/LBD42.pdf); "simplify chords" default on mobile — [Guitar Chalk](https://www.guitarchalk.com/chordify-review/)
- Moises: Easy/Medium/Advanced chord tiers — [Moises blog](https://moises.ai/blog/latest/advanced-chord-detection/)
- Capo: prose warning ("isn't perfect and will make mistakes"), blanks where harmony is weak, editing of chords and bar/beat positions — [Capo user guide](https://supermegaultragroovy.com/products/capo/user-guide/latest/concepts/how-capo-detects-chords.html)
- Klangio: prose warning that if a human tabber would struggle the AI may too; Edit Mode — [klang.io/guitar2tabs](https://klang.io/guitar2tabs/); Piano2Notes: "won't detect 100% of the played notes and there will also be wrong detections" — [AppFollow listing](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/piano2notes/1589420929?country=ru)
- AnthemScore: a slider that adds/removes notes automatically (a user-controlled threshold rather than a displayed confidence) — [lunaverus.com](https://www.lunaverus.com/)
- Transcribe!: "chord guessing" explicitly labelled as a guess, "not a magic bullet" — [Seventh String](https://www.seventhstring.com/xscribe/overview.html)
- Ultimate Guitar strumming row: "empty space means no strum"; small X = muted strum; big X = palm mute; ">" = accent — [UG help](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6744662-strumming-patterns-how-to-read-play)
- Yousician paper output: slash notation with bar lines and time-signature markers; "%" for repeated pattern; "Empty patterns, one per time signature, cover measures with no strums"; the decoder's transition cost "favor[s] repeating patterns" to improve readability (pattern discontinuity 27.8% to 15.4%) — [arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756v1)
- autochord visualiser loads a second .lab file "for comparison, such as ground truth or another model's output" — [autochord](https://github.com/cjbayron/autochord)
- Speech-to-text analogue: Speak Notes "Transcribe Confidence Map" uses "darker shades indicating higher confidence levels" (not music) — [Medium](https://medium.com/@nautilustechlabs/speak-notes-your-ultimate-audio-transcript-generator-882e4e1f00bd)
- Editorial notation conventions: Dorico documents dashed slurs for "an optional slur", half-dashed slurs for slurs incomplete in the source, and editorial slurs drawn solid "with a smaller vertical line at the midpoint, to show the editor added them" — [Dorico help](https://steinberg.help/dorico/v1/en/dorico/topics/notation_reference/notation_reference_slurs_styles_r.html); medieval-polyphony editions use brackets for editorially supplied material and dashed brackets for conjectural ligation — [DIAMM symbols PDF](https://www.diamm.ac.uk/documents/692/Payne-F-Symbols_and_Abbreviations_hHJEaQg.pdf); Corpus Musicae Ottomanicae: "square brackets within the transcription indicate an editorial intervention", asterisk for a comment — [CMO wiki](https://projects.academiccloud.de/projects/corpus-musicae-ottomanicae-cmo-public-wiki/wiki/7-notes-on-transcription)

### Inferences
- Greyed strokes for uncertain patterns (Youkulele) have no commercial precedent found; the nearest precedents are editorial notation (dashed = conjectural) and Soundslice's question flow. A reviewer could reasonably ask whether a grey stroke row is read as "quiet" rather than "unsure", since UG readers are trained that blank = rest and X = mute, with no symbol for doubt.
- "Riff heard, not transcribed" resembles Capo's "no chords here" blank and Transcribe!'s "guess" labelling more than anything in the chord apps, which never admit an untranscribed part.

### Gaps
- No product documentation describing a per-chord or per-stroke confidence display was found. No published study on how guitar learners read uncertainty marks on a chord sheet was found.

## Key question 6: Published accuracy claims and independent evaluations (including research closest to strum-from-audio)

### Takeaway
Product accuracy numbers are marketing or founder estimates (Chordify 70–95% in 2013; Melody Scanner "up to 86%" piano; Chord it "95%+"); independent reviews are qualitative and consistently say single-instrument pitch is good, mixed guitars and rhythm nuance are bad. The rigorous numbers come from research: Yousician's strum detector reaches 96.9% F1 on strums and ~95% F1 on reconstructed patterns on its own curated test set, but relies on a proprietary 924-pattern vocabulary and is not shipped.

### Cited Findings
- Chordify founder estimate 70–95% (2013) — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/apps/2013/05/02/chordify-taps-soundcloud-and-youtube-to-show-you-the-chords-to-your-favorite-songs/); human-annotator agreement 73% (maj/min) / 54% (complex) — [Koops et al. 2019](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/431806)
- Melody Scanner: piano "up to 86% accurate", solo instruments only — [melodyscanner.com](https://melodyscanner.com/)
- Chord it: "95%+ accuracy" (developer marketing) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chord-it-ai-for-musicians/id6475572143)
- autochord 67.33% test accuracy — [autochord](https://github.com/cjbayron/autochord)
- Riffstation: major/minor "correctly almost all the time" on rock/pop, no 7ths/dim (TNW review, 2018) — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/fender-riffstation-desktop-free)
- Capo 3: "more subtle chords were often not detected" (TidBITS, 2013) — [TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2013/12/13/funbits-master-your-fretboard-with-capo-3-for-mac/)
- Songsterr AI (heise, 2025): fairly accurate; palm muting often ignored; multi-guitar separation unreliable; "wrong note, but not an inappropriate one" — [heise](https://www.heise.de/-10356152)
- Klangio (MusicRadar, 2026): "doesn't come anywhere near the accuracy and reliability of a skilled human transcriber"; good single-instrument pitch, decent chords, bad multi-instrument/swing/drums — [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/klang-io-promises-to-turn-audio-into-notated-transcriptions-lead-sheets-and-guitar-tabs-but-does-it-actually-work-klang-io-transcription-studio-review)
- Chordify (Guitar Chalk, 2026): "often moves a chord change out of place" (reader) — [Guitar Chalk](https://www.guitarchalk.com/chordify-review/)
- **Yousician strumming-pattern paper** (Lukoianov & Klapuri, arXiv 2510.05756, Oct 2025): pipeline = HTDemucs "other" stem (chosen because guitar-stem separation quality was poor) → MERT-v1-95M + MLP per-frame strum-onset probabilities (peak-pick above 0.5, ±40 ms) → Viterbi over an expert-curated vocabulary of 924 patterns with transition costs favouring repeats and constant time signature; bar lines from BeatThis + DP post-processing. Dataset 931 proprietary recordings of 410 songs at up to four difficulty levels. Results: librosa onset baseline 83.0 F1 vs fine-tuned MERT 96.9 ± 0.2 (Played 95.3, Synth 98.5); full mix 96.5; pattern decoding with ground-truth bars 96.8, with BeatThis bars 95.3, with post-processing 94.7; bar-line F1 88.4. Limitations: steady-tempo post-processing "usually makes things worse" for drastic tempo changes; "extra guitar parts in the backing tracks can draw the model's attention away from the target guitar"; human performances deviate from the written pattern; dataset not released — [arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756v1); companion repo — [GitHub](https://github.com/YousicianGit/rhythmic-pattern-transcription)
- **Joint strum direction + chord** (Murgul, Schimper, Heizmann; ISMIR 2025): CRNN detects strum events, up/down direction and chord from microphone audio; 90 min real recordings labelled with an ESP32 smartwatch motion sensor plus 4 h synthetic; "significant improvements over baseline onset detection algorithms"; hybrid data best; no numbers on the abstract page; solo acoustic guitar, not polyphonic mixes — [arXiv 2508.07973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07973); related KIT multimodal up/down classification (audio for events, motion for direction) — [KIT](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000184108)
- Patents: a Google Patents query for strumming-pattern detection returned only US8507781 "Rhythm recognition from an audio signal" (sets delay timing from a demonstrated rhythm on an electric guitar) and US8614389 (capacitive strum sensors on a controller instrument); neither detects strum patterns from recorded music — [US8507781](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8507781); [US8614389](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8614389)
- Survey context: Pauwels, O'Hanlon, Gómez, Sandler, "20 Years of automatic chord recognition from audio", ISMIR 2019 — [ISMIR 2019](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/2019.ismir_conference-2019.4)

### Inferences
- The Yousician result shows that strum *onsets* are now detectable at ~97% F1 from a Demucs "other" stem, but turning onsets into a readable per-bar pattern depended on (a) a curated vocabulary of plausible patterns, (b) a prior that patterns repeat, and (c) human-defined ground truth that admits "one right pattern often can't be defined objectively". The Youkulele failure mode (patterns wrong more often than right) is consistent with decoding free-form strokes without such a vocabulary/repeat prior. Note also that the paper detects onsets only; up/down direction is not in the Yousician output, and the only direction classifier found (KIT) needed a wrist motion sensor to label training data.
- Every independent reviewer's failure list (multi-guitar mixes, palm mutes, swing/irregular rhythm) matches the Youkulele "two parts in one stem" failure, so the review can treat it as an unsolved industry problem rather than a pipeline defect.

### Gaps
- No independent, quantitative benchmark of any commercial product's chord or tab accuracy on a public test set was found; all commercial numbers are self-reported or anecdotal.
- Accuracy numbers for the ISMIR 2025 strum-direction CRNN were not on the abstract page (full PDF not fetched).
