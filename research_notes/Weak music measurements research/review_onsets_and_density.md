# Review: onset detection and strike density (research against code, 2026-10-08)

Read against main at 74c11e0. Line numbers are from `src/youkelele/music/onsets.py` (onsets), `src/youkelele/music/recall.py` (recall), `src/youkelele/stages/strums.py` (strums) and `src/youkelele/evaluate.py` (evaluate). The research note is `onset_detection_and_density.md` (Q1 to Q6); the report section is Area 1.

One correction to the research before anything else: the stems are 44.1 kHz stereo 16-bit (`runs/*/01_separate/stems/guitar.wav`), and `detect_onsets` passes that rate to librosa with the default hop of 512. A frame is therefore 11.6 ms, not 23 ms, and `pre_max`, `wait` evaluate to `0.03 * 44100 // 512 = 2` frames (23 ms), `pre_avg` to 8 and `post_avg` to 9 frames (about 100 ms each). The research's "one frame" arithmetic assumed 22050 Hz; the conclusion holds (the picker still passes two peaks 35 ms apart), the numbers are just finer.

## A. Diagnosis

**Every onset on the stem is a strike.** `quantise_bar` (onsets 130-152) turns each onset into `S` or `x` and never asks whether the event was a strum: the only per-onset attributes carried are `times`, `centroid` and `zcr` (`Onsets`, onsets 31-35). This is the mechanism behind every "too many clicks" verdict where the spike found the onsets real:

- Summer of '69 31-41, clip 13: "a quarter note and then the rest of the measure is a single tone"; the harness reads 4.4 strikes per bar. `strokes.md` section 2 shows the onsets spaced at one eighth slot (209 to 232 ms against 217 ms), only 1 of 45 weak, no doubles: the detector is right that something re-articulates on each eighth; the ear does not count the single-note re-articulations as strums. The research says the strum-versus-note question is answered by polyphony at the onset (Q3 inferences: "a strum excites several strings within one combine window"; Q2: a held tone moves energy within one band, a strum re-excites all bands).
- Summer of '69 53-58, clip 14 ("clicking 16th notes when it's playing 8th notes") and 95-111's last bars, clip 16: same statistics (5.2 and 3.4 onsets per bar, no doubles, odd slots as strong as even). The song is on the eighth grid (`slots_per_bar` 8 at 138.6 bpm), so the clicks are eighths; the spike's reading is that "the ear's eighth is the grid's quarter". Nothing in the picker produces this; it is the strike definition again, or a second part.
- All Fired Up 104-110, clip 29: "a damped note, a pause, and then 8th notes": the member prints the section's `D-D-D-DU`; its own onsets include the damped note counted as a strike.

**Weak onsets fall under a threshold pinned to the loudest frame of the stem.** `detect_onsets` (onsets 47-48) calls `librosa.onset.onset_detect(y=y, sr=sr, units="time", backtrack=False)` with `normalize=True` and `delta=0.07`, so a peak must stand 7 % of the stem's single loudest flux frame above its ±100 ms mean (research Q1, inferences; `.venv/Lib/site-packages/librosa/onset.py` 157-182 confirms the defaults). The recall module's own docstring states the symptom (recall 164-167: "one global threshold on a full-band envelope, set by the sharpest attacks in the song"). Failures:

- Need You Tonight 24-31, clip 23 ("3 1 beat notes, all cut off, and then 9 fast notes") and 8-12, 1.7 clip 10 (the same figure): `strokes.md` section 3 counts 29 of the run's 36 sixteenth positions found, five below `delta` 0.07 and two with no peak. The research's up-strum result explains the pattern (spectral flux: down-strums 85.4 %, up-strums 68.6 %, arXiv 2508.07973). The recall gate cannot help: `gate_section` returns `"sixteenth grid"` before trying anything (recall 287-288), and this song votes on 16 slots.
- The Cars 72-76, clip 25 ("not enough clicks"): the printed sparse half equals the raw onsets; every missing eighth has a peak below `delta`, the found ones are weak (0.08 to 0.20 after normalisation). The gate skips the section as `"dense"` (recall 291-292): `SPARSE_SHARE` 0.4 × 8 slots = 3.2 strikes per bar and the section reads 5.2.
- The coupling is two-way: `strokes.md` section 2 found that any `delta` change "re-normalises the envelope and flips the recall gate", because `before` in `gate_section` (recall 286) is counted from today's detector. `SPARSE_SHARE`, `MIN_GAIN` and `FIT_TOLERANCE` (recall 183-188) are constants measured on the output of `delta` 0.07; they are not independent of it.

**The mute rule compares to the song, not the passage.** `mute_mask` (onsets 121-127) marks `x` when centroid < 0.85 × and zcr < 0.65 × the song medians over all onsets. All Fired Up 90-97, clip 27 ("completely, hilariously, wrong"): a sparse sustained stretch whose onsets are dull against the song's medians prints `xUDxxxx-`. Chelsea Dagger's intro, 1.7 clip 9, prints `D-xxxUx-` certain and "the clicks do not fit". A chuck is dull and short; these onsets are dull and long. The per-onset decay already exists (`stroke_decay_db`, ring.py 26; written per stroke by `_stroke_decays`, strums 168-181) and the mute rule does not read it. This matches the report's cross-cutting rule ("decide against a local reference with hysteresis").

**The grid is one decision per song from an onset share.** `choose_slots_per_bar` (onsets 103-111) picks sixteenths when more than `ODD_SIXTEENTH_SHARE` 0.25 of all onsets round to odd sixteenth positions, a statistic the research names as the per-onset approach the tatum literature replaced with whole-passage IOI histograms or autocorrelation scored by two-way mismatch (Q4). It is not the cause of the Summer of '69 complaints (eighth grid), but it is why Pour Some Sugar On Me prints 103 of 103 bars grey on 16 slots with densities 0.21 to 0.62 and why the Need You Tonight run "crossing the barline" cannot be expressed: `quantise_bar` and the vote are per bar.

## B. Suggestions, ranked by benefit to the reader

### B1. A scale-invariant detector with a local threshold

Change `detect_onsets` (onsets 38-64) to build the envelope itself and pick peaks without file normalisation:

```python
env = librosa.onset.onset_strength(y=y, sr=sr, hop_length=512, max_size=3, aggregate=np.mean)
found = librosa.onset.onset_detect(onset_envelope=env, sr=sr, hop_length=512, units="time",
                                   normalize=False, delta=DELTA_DB, wait=WAIT_FRAMES,
                                   pre_avg=PRE_AVG, post_avg=POST_AVG, backtrack=False)
```

`max_size=3` is SuperFlux (Q1: "the cheapest change targeting the vibrato/bend false positives; a one-argument change"); `normalize=False` keeps `delta` in log-flux units so the threshold is Böck's moving-mean rule rather than a share of the loudest frame (Q2: "difference log-magnitudes ... but then min-max normalises the summed envelope, re-introducing file-level dependence"; https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2012/paper/000049.pdf). If a fixed log-flux `delta` still fails the pooled sweep, the fallback is dividing the envelope by a rolling percentile over a few bars; the research marks that as inference, not citation. `wait` of 3 to 4 frames (35 to 46 ms) brings the picker to madmom's 30 ms combine and SuperFlux's 50 ms `post_max` (https://github.com/CPJKU/SuperFlux/blob/master/SuperFlux.py); `strokes.md` found few sub-60 ms pairs, so `wait` is safety, not the fix. Keep the `fmin` branch for the gate until B4 retires it.

Addresses: Need You Tonight 24-31 and 8-12 (the five run onsets under `delta`), The Cars 72-76, and the `delta`-gate coupling. Risk: every section's onsets move, so every printed pattern can move; the regression list is the 1.6 pass's accepted patterns (Summer of '69 4-19, 75-83, 95-111; Chelsea Dagger 61-71; Need You Tonight 13-24 and 79-86; All Fired Up 55-61) plus the ear-right patterns the 1.6 spec names (All Fired Up 33-49, 61-90, 128-132; The Cars 11-19, 68-72; Need You Tonight 13-24) and Wet Leg 26-32 from 1.7. Generality: `DELTA_DB`, `WAIT_FRAMES` and the averaging windows come from one pooled sweep over every ear-marked section of every run folder, chosen on the flat part of the pooled curve (Q6: MIREX's ten-value P-R sweep); re-tune the windows whenever the ODF changes, as Böck did. Install: none.

### B2. A per-onset strum class from band-grouped flux

New module `src/youkelele/music/strumness.py`. At each onset frame compute `librosa.onset.onset_strength_multi(y=y, sr=sr, hop_length=512, channels=[...])` over N log-spaced band groups (for example 6 groups between 60 Hz and 8 kHz) and record the count of groups whose rectified flux at the onset exceeds a fraction of the onset's total; store it on `Onsets` as `bands` beside `centroid` and `zcr`. An onset exciting fewer than `STRUM_MIN_BANDS` groups is a note onset, not a strum: `quantise_bar` gives it a new class `n`, which the vote treats as a rest for the pattern (or prints as a small dot once the renderer has a glyph). A cheaper first version reuses `onset_chroma` (riff.py 28) and its per-onset single-pitch-class test (riff.py 81: `sum(row >= 0.5 * peak) == 1`), which already runs over every detector onset in `_riff_features_member` (strums 255-259); the riff features use its section median, but the per-onset flag is right there.

Addresses: Summer of '69 31-41, 53-58, 95-111's end; All Fired Up 104-110. Research: Q3 inferences (polyphony at the onset is the implicit strum test of both 2025 systems; https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756v1); Q2 (band-grouped ODF, https://github.com/librosa/librosa/blob/main/librosa/onset.py); Schlüter and Böck's gains from separate harmonic and percussive detectors (https://research.jku.at/en/publications/improved-musical-onset-detection-with-convolutional-neural-networ/). Risk: riff sections the ear passed as strum patterns on single notes (Need You Tonight 13-24, The Cars 68-72, All Fired Up 33-49) would lose strikes if the threshold is high, so the constant is read from the pooled distribution with those sections as anchors; a riff member already prints its own strokes (1.6 ruling 3), so the class can feed the riff path there. Generality: one constant from the pooled band-count distribution; no song path. Install: none.

### B3. Mute only when dull and short

`mute_mask` (onsets 121-127) gains a third conjunct: the onset's `stroke_decay_db` (already computed by `_stroke_decays`, strums 526, for every onset after the gate) at or above a `MUTE_MIN_DECAY_DB` read from the pooled decay distribution of x-marked onsets. Requires computing decays before `mute_mask` (move strums 525-526 above 474) or passing decays in. Addresses All Fired Up 90-97 and Chelsea Dagger's intro. Risk: the x marks the ear accepted (Need You Tonight verses, 1.6 clips 8 and 9 "damped"; Wet Leg Verse 4 `DUDUxxDU`; All Fired Up intro `DxxUDUD-`); `strokes.md` section 1 warns that cut and ringing decays overlap on every statistic, so this may not separate either and D4 must run first. Generality: a pooled band; no song path. Install: none.

### B4. Re-measure, then retire or re-derive the recall gate

After B1 the gate's premise ("set by the sharpest attacks") is gone. Rerun `gate_section` on all eleven run folders with B1's detector and count accepted sections; if the union is never accepted or never changes a printed pattern, delete `recall.py`'s call path (strums 450-472) and the `fmin` branch; otherwise re-derive `SPARSE_SHARE`, `MIN_GAIN` and `FIT_TOLERANCE` from the new onsets and drop the `"sixteenth grid"` skip (recall 287-288), which today denies the one song whose under-firing the ear named. Addresses the coupling in A; risk is the five All Fired Up and two Chelsea Dagger boosted sections. Install: none.

### B5. Per-section grid evidence (measure before changing the schema)

Replace the odd-sixteenth share in `choose_slots_per_bar` with an autocorrelation test on the unnormalised envelope: the ratio of autocorrelation at lag beat/4 to beat/2 per section, plus a two-way-mismatch occupancy score of the sixteenth grid (empty sixteenth cells penalised), switching only when the margin clears a band (Q4; https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7273978; Yousician's transition cost, https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756v1). The song-wide slot count is wired through `Strums.slots_per_bar`, the vote floors (`UNCERTAIN_BELOW_SIXTEENTH`) and the renderer, so a per-section grid is a schema change; the step worth taking now is D3, printing the statistic per section in `evaluate` and seeing whether it separates the three sixteenth songs' sections. Install: none.

## D. Spikes to run first

1. **Threshold sweep (confirms or kills B1's fixed `delta`).** For each of the eleven run folders, run the B1 detector over `01_separate/stems/guitar.wav` with `delta` in ten log-flux steps, `wait` in {2, 3, 4} frames, `max_size` in {1, 3}; quantise with the run's `grid.json` and compare `bar_onsets` per section with the stored `strums.json`. Before truth exists the question is: is there one operating point where Need You Tonight 24-31 gains at least five of the seven missing run onsets and The Cars 72-76 fills its empty eighths while the passed list's `bar_onsets` are unchanged? None found kills the fixed `delta` and sends B1 to the rolling normaliser.
2. **Strumness separation (B2).** On the same folders compute the band count and the single-pitch-class flag per onset; plot both for the over-dense sections against the anchors. Overlap as total as the decay statistics in `strokes.md` section 1 kills B2; a gap with Need You Tonight 13-24 on the strum side confirms it.
3. **Grid statistic (B5).** Per section, autocorrelation of the unnormalised envelope at beat/2 and beat/4 lags, and sixteenth-cell occupancy. If Pour Some Sugar On Me's sections and Need You Tonight's split while Summer of '69's all sit on eighths, the per-section grid is worth a schema change; if the statistic tracks the song-wide choice, drop B5.
4. **Dull-and-short (B3).** Decay distributions of the x-marked onsets in All Fired Up 90-97 and Chelsea Dagger 7-19 against those in Need You Tonight's verses and Wet Leg Verse 4. Overlap kills B3.
5. **Gate redundancy (B4).** Count accepted sections with the winning spike-1 detector.
6. **Drum bleed share.** Drum-stem flux at each guitar-stem onset, per section. No ear-verified failure here is attributed to drum bleed (Chelsea Dagger's "doorbell" was a rests question; Cream is bass bleed), so a share near zero closes the question.

## E. Do not do

- madmom's CNN or SuperFlux: 2018 sdist, Python 2.7 to 3.7 classifiers, Cython and NumPy alias breakage, CC BY-NC-SA weights (research Q1, install table).
- essentia: no Windows wheels, AGPLv3 non-commercial.
- aubio: sdist only on PyPI; Windows binaries only through conda.
- Basic Pitch: Apache-2.0 and ONNX on Windows, but Python 3.7 to 3.11 against this project's `requires-python ==3.12.*`, and it reports note onsets with a 120 ms minimum note, not strums.
- The 2025 strum detectors (KIT CRNN, Yousician MERT head): no code or weights released.
- Raising or lowering `delta` on the normalised envelope: `strokes.md` section 2 showed it re-normalises and flips the gate; the research says the fix is the coordinate system, not the constant.
- Minimum-gap and strength-floor filters on today's onsets: tried in `strokes.md` section 2; none reached the ear's density without moving ear-passed patterns, because the over-dense onsets are not weak or doubled.
- Per-song or per-section `delta`: the owner's generality rule; the pooled sweep is the only sanctioned route to a constant.
- A pattern-library Viterbi decode of grid level: Yousician's 924 patterns are proprietary, and grid choice belongs to the vote area once the onsets are right.
