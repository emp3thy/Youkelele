# Instrument activity, silence and rest detection per bar on a separated stem (as of 2026)

Scope note for the writer: the pipeline under review (v1.7) rests a bar when guitar-stem RMS < 5 % of mix RMS (about -26 dB relative to the mix) OR the stem's energy share below 330 Hz is < 0.005. Observed: 6/7 disputed resting stretches right, one wrong (a pickup bar at ratio 0.024 with no detected onset), two bars just above the floor (0.050, 0.062) print strokes nobody hears, and bars with a carried-over tail have onsets but no new strokes. Everything below is organised so the reviewer can map each finding to those three failure modes: (A) a quiet-but-real bar under a fixed floor, (B) flicker just above the floor, (C) tail versus new attack.

## KQ1. What the literature says about frame- and segment-level instrument activity detection (features, models, accuracies)

### Takeaway
Published instrument activity detection (IAD) works at 1 s or coarser resolution, derives its *ground truth* from stem energy with a soft logistic threshold (MedleyDB's "activation confidence"), and reaches mean AUC of roughly 0.77-0.80 across 18 instruments, with guitar classes among the weakest; nobody publishes bar-level rest detection for a single separated stem, so the v1.7 rule has no direct benchmark, only analogues.

### Cited Findings
- Gururani, Summers and Lerch (ISMIR 2018) built IAD "with temporal resolution ranging from one second to the track level" for 18 instruments using MLP, CNN and CRNN models on MedleyDB + Mixing Secrets multitracks; audio is "normalized by the root mean square energy" and "chunked to 1 s long snippets" — [Gururani et al. 2018, ISMIR PDF](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf)
- Their ground truth: "Instrument activation confidence is annotated automatically according to the process described in [MedleyDB]. These annotations are computed with time-resolution of 0.0464 s ... The annotations are aggregated by picking the maximum value across the time-axis to obtain one activation value per instrument per snippet. This allows for instruments to have a large activation value in the snippet even if they were active for a small period of time, as opposed to a value close to 0 if the mean was chosen ... Finally, the activations are binarized with a fixed threshold θ = 0.5." — [Gururani et al. 2018](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf)
- Bleed handling in the same paper: "The datasets contain tracks where the stems have cross-talk or bleed ... tracks with bleed are not considered for the IAD dataset" (bleed is excluded, not detected) — [Gururani et al. 2018](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf)
- Results (mean AUC, Table 3): at 1 s resolution MLP 71.28, CNN 77.55, CRNN 77.5; at track level 71.1 / 80.92 / 80.1. A fixed 0.5 threshold "led to 0 detections for string section and electronic organ", so per-class thresholds were chosen by best validation F-measure; the chosen thresholds "range from 0.02 to 0.55 with lower thresholds for minority instruments" and correlate 0.9 with the class frequency in training data — [Gururani et al. 2018](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf)
- Guitar-specific weakness: confusion between clean and distorted electric guitar "could also explain the poor performance for clean electric guitar", and distorted guitar vs synthesizer confusion is "an interesting case since the variance in sound for both ... is very large" — [Gururani et al. 2018](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf)
- Hung and Yang (ISMIR 2018) do frame-level recognition on MusicNet with CQT input (44.1 kHz, 512-sample window, 3 s segments), 7 classical instruments (no guitar); per-instrument thresholds "from 0.01, 0.02, ... to 0.99" are picked by maximising F1 on the training set; average F1 0.887 for the CQT-only model, 0.896 with estimated pitch and 0.933 with ground-truth pitch — [Hung & Yang 2018, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1806.09587)
- Hung and Lerch (ISMIR 2020) use instrument activation to gate separation; activity labels are "at the frame level ... the same time resolution as the input spectrogram" (window 4096, hop 1024 at 44.1 kHz, i.e. ~23 ms), obtained by binarizing the datasets' activation probability "with a threshold of 0.5 as suggested"; for MUSDB-HQ they "apply the energy tracking method proposed for MedleyDB with a threshold of 0.5" — [Hung & Lerch 2020, arXiv 2008.00616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00616)
- Same paper on detection quality: "'Piano,' 'Electrical Guitar,' and 'Bass' tend to have lower detection rates ... which the model might easily confuse with the background music" — [Hung & Lerch 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00616)
- OpenMIC-2018: 20,000 10-second CC-licensed excerpts "partially labeled for the presence or absence of 20 instrument classes"; the authors note an ideal dataset would give "at each instant in any recording, full information about all active instruments", which none achieves; the baseline is "the mean and standard deviation of VGGish features over the clip's duration" fed to a random forest, giving "an overall gain in accuracy of more than 10 percent point" over the class bias; the pre-training InstrumentDNN reached macro-F1 0.514 and micro-F1 0.656 — [Humphrey, Durand, McFee 2018, ISMIR PDF](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2018/paper/000248.pdf)
- A 2025 hierarchical minority-instrument study on MedleyDB labels 1 s frames as active "if it is active at any point during that duration" (frames with < 0.1 s of activity are "less than 0.26 %" of frames), uses 80-bin MFCCs and a VGG-style CNN, and reports micro-F1 of 0.74-0.78 for instrument groups but only 0.41-0.45 for individual instruments — [arXiv 2506.21167](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21167v1)
- Kumar, Luo and Mesgarani (Interspeech 2018) note "the problem of joint source activity detection and separation remains unexplored" and use the DANet separation embedding to "dynamically estimate the number of outputs depending on the active sources" — [ISCA archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2018/kumar18c_interspeech.html)
- The Cocktail Fork paper (speech/music/SFX) trains a CRNN (DCASE 2020 Task 4 baseline) on 64-band mel (FFT 2048, hop 512); CRNN pooling reduces 864 input frames per 10 s to 108 output frames (~93 ms); "The activity predictions are obtained by applying a median filter of size 5 over time and thresholding at 0.5" — [Petermann et al. 2022, arXiv 2212.07327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07327)
- Same paper: ground-truth activity is "defined by the presence of an active excerpt at frame n as defined in the metadata, rather than its energy, so pauses in a speech utterance or a song may still be considered active". Results (Table IV): segment-based F (1 s) 0.97 music / 0.97 speech / 0.92 SFX for the mixture baseline, 0.98 / 0.98 / 0.94 with separated sources as extra input; event-based F (750 ms collar, 20 % offset) 0.77 / 0.84 / 0.50 baseline vs 0.82 / 0.88 / 0.55 early integration — [Petermann et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07327)

### Inferences
- The field's own "truth" for activity is an energy envelope through a logistic, not a hard ratio; and it is per-stem normalised against the summed stems (see KQ3), not against the mix RMS. The v1.7 5 % mix-RMS floor is therefore an uncalibrated constant relative to the literature's practice of normalising to the song.
- Published detectors deliberately use *max* pooling inside the window so a short burst keeps the window active (Gururani); a bar-mean RMS does the opposite and under-weights a short pickup — a plausible mechanism for failure mode (A).
- Even DNN detectors trained on hundreds of multitracks get guitar wrong more than other instruments; the guitar-vs-synth confusion reported by Gururani is the same symptom the low-band-share rule tries to patch.
- Segment-based F at 1 s is near-saturated (0.97) for "is music present", but event-level boundaries are far worse (0.77-0.82); bar-level rest decisions are boundary decisions, so expect the hard cases at section edges.

### Gaps
- No paper found that evaluates per-bar activity for a single separated instrument stem against listening truth; the closest is 1 s segment-based evaluation on ground-truth stems.
- Per-instrument AUC values for guitar from Gururani's Figure 3 were not extractable (figure only).

## KQ2. How separation pipelines decide a stem is empty or only bleed; published thresholds

### Takeaway
Separation toolchains do not ship an "empty stem" gate; evaluation code treats exact-zero references/estimates as undefined (NaN or error), researchers zero near-silent windows by an absolute amplitude rule (0.01), and bleed is measured after the fact with spectrogram-difference metrics (MVSEP Bleedless/Fullness) or avoided by excluding bleeding multitracks, not detected per bar.

### Cited Findings
- museval's `validate()` raises on silent inputs: "All the reference sources should be non-silent (not all-zeros)" and "having a silent estimated source will result in an underdetermined system"; inside `bss_eval` the comment reads "# if we have a silent frame set results as np.nan"; silence is detected by `np.all(np.sum(sources, axis=...) == 0, axis=1)`; default framing is a 2 s window with 1.5 s hop (88,200 / 66,150 samples) — [museval metrics.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sigsep/sigsep-mus-eval/master/museval/metrics.py)
- museval README: matching is more robust because "true sources are not silent throughout the whole recording", and v4 uses time-invariant distortion filters since time-varying ones "tend to overestimate performance" — [sigsep-mus-eval README](https://github.com/sigsep/sigsep-mus-eval)
- Wave-U-Net: "in case of silence, the SDR is undefined, which happens often for vocal tracks"; near-silent references give very low SDR when the output is quiet but not silent — [Stoller et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.03185)
- A diffusion-refinement MSS paper states SDR "becomes numerically undefined for all-zero stems, so museval package filters them by design" — [arXiv 2412.06965](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.06965)
- A score-informed classical separation paper found museval's exact-zero rule misses near-silent references and pre-processed truth: "set all of the silent one-second windows to exact zeros" where every sample is below 0.01 in absolute value (URMP and Aalto datasets) — [arXiv 2503.07352](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07352)
- A real-time DSP separation paper reports that the common "cSDR" is a plain energy ratio rather than museval's BSSEval-v4 projection, differing by up to about 1.3 dB per stem, and that silent frames had a disproportionate effect on aggregate SDR despite being perceptually negligible (per the search summary; verify in the PDF) — [arXiv 2609.12201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12201)
- MVSEP's Bleedless/Fullness metrics "use magnitude spectrogram differences between estimated and reference signals" on a mel/dB scale: "The Bleedless metric penalizes average positive differences", "the Fullness metric penalizes average negative differences", both normalised to [0, 100]; the FAQ also notes Fullness and Bleedless are usually mutually exclusive in training — [MVSEP FAQ](https://mvsep.com/en/faq)
- Demucs: the facebookresearch repo is read-only since 1 Jan 2025 and the maintained fork is adefossez/demucs v4.1.0; the 6-stem model's guitar output is described as "okay" and piano as having "a lot of bleeding and artifacts" (third-party blog, not an official claim) — [aireiter.com Demucs guide](https://aireiter.com/blog/demucs-stem-separation-models-flags-cost)
- Gururani et al. exclude multitracks with bleed from training rather than attempt detection — [Gururani et al. 2018](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf)
- A 2026 studio-timing paper detects leakage between real stems by "comparison of two different stems of a multi-track session" using "the similarity of their amplitudes or the similarity of their frequency content" — [arXiv 2609.13881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13881)
- Parry and Essa (DAFx 2005) argue the usual separation assumption is that "all sources are active at all times" and that the pattern of source activity/inactivity is itself a separation cue — [DAFx archive](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/APiD_NX49sgBq-Ql6Z9Dsg)
- Hung and Lerch gate the separated magnitude spectrogram with the binary activity: "false negatives of the predicted labels might mistakenly suppress wanted components ... Even if the gaps have only the length of a few milliseconds, it will have negative impact on the perceived quality. Second, the binary mask might cause repeated abrupt switching between silence and sound ... musical noise", hence "a median filter is applied to smooth the predicted instrument activities", which "generally increases performance across all instruments" — [Hung & Lerch 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00616)

### Inferences
- No published RMS-to-mix ratio exists for "stem is bleed only"; the nearest absolute rule is the 0.01 linear-amplitude (-40 dBFS) per-1 s-window zeroing used for evaluation truth, which is an absolute level, not a ratio to the mix.
- Bleed is characterised in the literature by spectral similarity to the *other* stems (MVSEP, leakage paper), not by a single band-energy share. A generic check closer to the literature: correlate the guitar stem's bar spectrum with the "other" stem's bar spectrum; high similarity at low absolute level is bleed, low similarity is a real but quiet part.
- Hung and Lerch's "few-millisecond gap" argument is the frame-level version of failure mode (B): a hard binary gate on a continuous instrument produces audible holes; their fix (median filter over activity) is the same medicine as hysteresis/min-duration at bar level.

### Gaps
- No Demucs/Spleeter/Open-Unmix code found that outputs a per-stem "empty" flag or threshold; the only candidates are evaluation-time rules.
- MVSEP does not publish the exact formula or any threshold for calling a stem empty.

## KQ3. Theory of energy-based activity detection: adaptive thresholds, hysteresis, minimum duration, windows, tails vs attacks

### Takeaway
Every mature energy detector normalises to the signal itself (peak or summed-stem envelope), uses two thresholds (hysteresis) or a hangover/min-duration to stop flicker at the floor, and post-filters decisions (median, double threshold); MedleyDB's reference implementation gives concrete numbers (theta 0.15, lambda 20, amplitude floor 0.01) that can be compared directly to the 5 % / 0.005 constants.

### Cited Findings
- MedleyDB `compute_activation_confidence(mtrack, win_len=4096, lpf_cutoff=0.075, theta=0.15, var_lambda=20.0, amplitude_threshold=0.01)`: each stem is loaded mono at 44.1 kHz; frames use a Hann window of 4096 with hop 2048; the per-frame energy is half-wave-rectified, square-rooted, windowed and averaged (an envelope follower, not RMS); the summed-stem envelope E0 is used to normalise, `H = n_stems * H / max(E0)`; frames where E0 < 0.01 are set to zero ("Energies below this value are set to 0.0"); H is low-passed with a 2nd-order Butterworth (normalised cutoff 0.075, zero-phase `filtfilt`); then `C = 1 - 1/(1 + exp(var_lambda * (H - theta)))` — [medleydb activation_conf.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/marl/medleydb/master/medleydb/annotate/activation_conf.py)
- librosa `effects.split(y, *, top_db=60, ref=np.max, frame_length=2048, hop_length=512, aggregate=np.max)`: frame RMS is converted with `amplitude_to_db(..., ref=ref)` and a frame is non-silent when `db > -top_db`; `top_db` is "The threshold (in decibels) below reference to consider as silence"; `ref` "By default ... compares to the peak amplitude in the signal"; `trim` states "By default, ref is set to the signal's maximum RMS value" — [librosa effects.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/effects.py)
- Essentia `StartStopSilence` uses a `threshold` in dB, range (-inf, 0], default -60, "below which average energy counts as silence"; `SilenceRate` outputs 1 when "the instant power of the input frame is below the given output's respective threshold" for each of a list of thresholds — [StartStopSilence](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_StartStopSilence.html); [SilenceRate](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_SilenceRate.html)
- pyannote's VAD pipeline exposes hysteresis and duration constraints: `onset`/`offset` are the "onset/offset activation thresholds", `min_duration_on` removes "speech regions shorter than that many seconds", `min_duration_off` fills "non-speech regions shorter than that many seconds". Tuned values: AMI onset 0.684 / offset 0.577 / min_on 0.181 s / min_off 0.037 s; DIHARD3 0.767 / 0.377 / 0.136 / 0.067; VoxConverse 0.767 / 0.713 / 0.182 / 0.501 — [pyannote/segmentation model card](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/segmentation)
- Double thresholding for sound event detection: with φ_hi = 0.75 and φ_low = 0.2 the method "marks all values being larger than φ_hi as being valid predictions" and extends them to neighbours while probability stays above φ_low; proposed as "a window-independent filtering alternative to median filtering" because a median filter (ω = 51) "raised the overall score mainly by favoring long clips at the expense of short ones" — [Dinkel & Yu 2019, ar5iv 1904.03841](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.03841); the follow-up describes double thresholding as a probability-level step and median filtering as a hard-label step — [arXiv 2101.07687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.07687)
- Median-filter trade-off: it "removes false positives or negatives that last only a few frames" but the window "must balance over-smoothing of boundaries against suppressing noise, and ... different event types may need different sizes" — [arXiv 2208.09201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.09201)
- Speech VAD hangover (telecom patents): hangover "bridges short inactive segments to ensure that quiet trailing, unvoiced sounds ... are classified as active", typically "about 40 to 300 msec of inactive speech following an active speech burst", shortened adaptively when a following period is "clearly inactive (i.e., very low energy with a spectrum similar to the measured background noise)"; a fixed threshold is replaced by comparison to a running background-noise estimate — [US 7,180,892 (Broadcom)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7180892); energy-only VAD "works reasonably well for conditions where the SNR is good but not for low SNR cases" — [US 10,607,633 (Ericsson)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10607633)
- Hung and Lerch, on binary gating: gaps of "a few milliseconds" from false negatives are audible and "repeated abrupt switching between silence and sound" creates musical noise; median filtering the activity "generally increases performance across all instruments as it eliminates spurious prediction errors" — [Hung & Lerch 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00616)
- Cocktail Fork uses a median filter of size 5 on ~93 ms activity frames before thresholding at 0.5 — [Petermann et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07327)
- `ohun` energy detector (bioacoustics): a minimum-duration parameter removes detections shorter than a set value, "applied after any hold-time merging" — [ohun energy_detector](https://docs.ropensci.org/ohun/reference/energy_detector.html)
- Onset-gated activity: madmom `OnsetPeakPickingProcessor(threshold=0.5, smooth=0, pre_avg=0, post_avg=0, pre_max=0, post_max=0, combine=0.03, delay=0, online=False, fps=100)`; `pre_avg`/`post_avg` are seconds of context "used for the moving average", `pre_max`/`post_max` for the moving maximum, `combine` reports "only one onset within this many seconds"; the docstring says to set `pre_avg` and `post_avg` to 0 for activations "that don't depend on signal level, such as neural network outputs" (i.e. a signal-level ODF such as spectral flux should use a moving-average-relative threshold) — [madmom onsets.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CPJKU/madmom/main/madmom/features/onsets.py)
- Essentia SuperFluxPeaks uses a ratio threshold "with respect to the novelty_signal/novelty_average rate" and a 20 ms time threshold for double detections — [Essentia SuperFluxPeaks](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_SuperFluxPeaks.html); aubionotes uses an onset peak-picking threshold (0.5 "suggested in case of over-detections") and a minimum inter-onset interval of 0.030 s by default — [aubionotes man page](https://www.mankier.com/1/aubionotes)

### Inferences
- Mapping MedleyDB to the v1.7 rule: its reference is the *summed-stem* peak envelope scaled by the stem count, its soft threshold theta = 0.15 of that, and its absolute floor 0.01 (-40 dBFS). The v1.7 reference (mix RMS per bar) moves with the drums and vocals in that bar, so the same quiet guitar part scores differently in a loud chorus and a sparse verse. A song-relative reference (e.g. stem RMS vs the stem's own 90th-percentile bar RMS, in dB, as librosa's `top_db` does against peak) is what the library implementations do.
- Hysteresis in bar units: pyannote-style onset > offset pairs (0.684/0.577, 0.767/0.377) and SED double thresholds (0.75/0.2) separate "start playing" from "stop playing" by a factor of 1.2-3.75. For v1.7 that means e.g. enter-rest below 0.035 but only leave-rest above 0.07; the 0.050 and 0.062 bars would then inherit the state of their neighbours instead of flickering.
- min_duration_off (fill short gaps) is the direct analogue of "a lone resting bar inside a played phrase is probably a tail or a quiet bar, not a rest"; min_duration_on (drop short blips) is the analogue of "a lone playing bar inside a rest needs onset evidence". pyannote's tuned values show min_off and min_on are *independent* knobs, so set them separately in bars.
- The low-pass in MedleyDB (0.075 of the ~21.5 Hz frame rate, i.e. ~0.8 Hz) and the max-pooling in Gururani both bias the detector toward *keeping* an instrument active across short dips, the opposite bias from a per-bar hard floor.
- An energy VAD assumes the background (bleed) is stationary; the patents' adaptive background estimate suggests estimating the stem's bleed floor from bars the onset detector marks attack-free, then expressing the threshold as dB above that floor rather than a fixed ratio to the mix.

### Gaps
- No source states a ratio-of-stem-to-mix threshold; all found thresholds are either absolute (-60 dB, -40 dBFS, 0.01) or relative to the signal's own peak/sum.
- No source quantified hysteresis ratios for music stems specifically; the values above are speech/SED.

## KQ4. How transcription tools (Moises, Chordify, Klangio, Soundslice, Basic Pitch) handle rests and "no instrument here"

### Takeaway
Only Basic Pitch documents its silence logic (frame/onset thresholds and a 127.7 ms minimum note length); chord tools represent silence as an explicit "N" (no-chord) class on the beat grid; Soundslice and Klangio document rests only as editable notation objects, and Moises publishes nothing on the matter.

### Cited Findings
- Basic Pitch `predict()`: `onset_threshold = 0.5` ("Minimum energy required for an onset to be considered present"), `frame_threshold = 0.3` ("Minimum energy requirement for a frame to be considered present"), `minimum_note_length = 127.7` ms ("The minimum allowed note length in milliseconds"), `melodia_trick = True` ("Use the melodia post-processing step"); note length in frames is `round(minimum_note_length / 1000 * (AUDIO_SAMPLE_RATE / FFT_HOP))` — [basic_pitch/inference.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/inference.py)
- Basic Pitch packaging: `pip install basic-pitch`; macOS, Windows and Ubuntu; Python 3.7-3.11; default runtime on Windows is ONNX (TensorFlow optional via `basic-pitch[tf]`); Apache 2.0 licence — [spotify/basic-pitch README](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch)
- Chordify's pipeline (ISMIR 2015 LBD): Sonic Annotator extracts "the downbeat positions and the tonal content", chords are assigned to beat positions, and the HarmTrace harmony model resolves ambiguous audio "based on rules of tonal harmony"; an editing feature lets users correct recognised chords — [Chordify LBD, ISMIR 2015](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/LBD/LBD42.pdf)
- No-chord convention: madmom's chord recognisers encode "Id 24 represents 'N', the no-chord class" alongside 24 major/minor ids — [madmom chords docs](https://madmom.readthedocs.io/en/v0.16/modules/features/chords.html); a MIREX-format example labels the first 2.6 s of a song with "the no-chord symbol N", and the same project reports accuracy "with and without no-chord labels" (67.33 vs 70.03) — [autochord on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/autochord/0.1.2)
- Soundslice documents rests as manually inserted/edited objects ("insert rests, change their durations, delete them") and its editor "doesn't enforce rhythmic stability"; the transcribing help describes a manual workflow — [Soundslice rests help](https://soundslice.com/help/en/creating/notations/318/rests); [Soundslice transcribing help](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/creating/transcribing/)
- Notation software (Finale) exposes a "Minimize Number of Rests" quantisation option so a slightly short held note does not generate a stray small rest — [Finale quantization settings](https://usermanuals.finalemusic.com/FinaleWin/Content/Finale/QUANTMORESETTINGS.htm)

### Inferences
- The products that work (chord apps) sidestep per-instrument rests entirely: they label the *mix* per beat and emit "N" only where no harmony is detectable. A strum-sheet generator is closer to Basic Pitch's problem (frame activity + onset + minimum duration), and Basic Pitch's three-part rule (onset gate, lower frame gate, minimum length) is the published pattern.
- Basic Pitch's frame threshold (0.3) being lower than its onset threshold (0.5) is hysteresis by another name: a note needs strong evidence to start and weaker evidence to continue.

### Gaps
- No documentation found for Moises or Klangio on rests, silence or minimum note length (Klangio FAQ URL returned 404; Moises appeared only in third-party listings).
- Chordify's user-facing representation of silent beats (blank vs "N") is not documented.

## KQ5. Telling a note tail from a new stroke within a bar (onset-gated energy, attack detection)

### Takeaway
The standard answer is an onset detection function evaluated relative to its own local average (spectral flux / SuperFlux / CD-ODF with madmom-style peak picking), optionally fed by the percussive HPSS component so sustaining tails are suppressed; on guitar pickup audio, spectral flux reaches F1 ≈ 79 % and a trained CRNN ≈ 98 %, and the literature recommends max-pooling activity within a window so a brief attack counts.

### Cited Findings
- Joint strumming/chord transcription (2025): on pickup audio, onset F1 (50 ms tolerance, mir_eval) is Spectral Flux 79.49 % (P 78.53 / R 81.86), SuperFlux 74.36 %, CD-ODF 79.32 % (P 68.50 / R 98.15), proposed CRNN 97.60 % (P 96.54 / R 98.73); dataset 90 min from three guitarists at 60/80/100 BPM plus ~4 h synthetic; "chord labels are only available at strumming event times" and muted strums are future work — [arXiv 2508.07973](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973)
- Onset character of plucked strings: "onsets of most instruments, including plucked and struck string instruments, are characterized by high energy across the frequency range", while time-domain energy-rise methods are limited because "not all instrument onsets show such characteristics in the time-domain" — [Chirp group delay onset paper, arXiv 2408.13734](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13734v1)
- Phase-based detection "is more robust to steady-state signals (sustained notes, drones) than spectral flux" — [yup onsets docs](https://yup.readthedocs.io/en/latest/dsp/onsets.html); Essentia notes a phase-based variant "reacts better on tonal sounds such as bowed string, but tends to over-detect percussive events" — [Essentia OnsetDetection](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_OnsetDetection.html)
- madmom's SuperFlux is "a spectral flux variant with maximum-filter vibrato suppression" (Böck & Widmer, DAFx 2013) and ComplexFlux adds "local group delay-based tremolo suppression"; CNN onset detection follows Schlüter & Böck 2013, RNN onset detection Eyben et al. 2010 — [madmom onsets.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CPJKU/madmom/main/madmom/features/onsets.py)
- Peak picking relative to local context: a frame counts as an onset when it is a local maximum within `pre_max`/`post_max` and exceeds the moving average over `pre_avg`/`post_avg` plus `threshold`; `combine` (default 0.03 s) merges doubles — [madmom onsets.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CPJKU/madmom/main/madmom/features/onsets.py)
- HPSS: librosa `decompose.hpss(S, *, kernel_size=31, power=2.0, mask=False, margin=1.0)` splits "S = H + P, where H contains the harmonic components, and P contains the percussive components" using a median filter along time (harmonic) and along frequency (percussive), after Fitzgerald (DAFx 2010) and Driedger, Müller, Disch (ISMIR 2014) — [librosa decompose.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/decompose.py)
- Window aggregation choice: Gururani's max-over-window aggregation exists precisely so an instrument "active for a small period of time" still registers, "as opposed to a value close to 0 if the mean was chosen" — [Gururani et al. 2018](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf)
- Basic Pitch separates "onset" evidence (0.5) from "frame" evidence (0.3) and enforces a 127.7 ms minimum note length — [basic_pitch/inference.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/inference.py)

### Inferences
- A bar decision built from two features — (i) per-bar *percussive* (HPSS P) energy or onset count from a flux ODF with madmom-style local-average peak picking, and (ii) per-bar harmonic energy — separates the three cases: tail-only bars have harmonic energy but no peaks above local average (print "let ring" or a tie, not strokes, not rest); a quiet pickup bar has a flux peak relative to the preceding near-silence even at ratio 0.024 (print strokes); bleed-only bars have neither.
- The pickup bar's missing onset is likely a windowing/threshold artefact: with a global ODF threshold, a stroke after silence is small in absolute terms; madmom's `pre_avg` makes the threshold relative to the immediately preceding (silent) context, which is the published remedy. Look-behind/look-ahead of about a beat across the bar line (pre_max/post_max) also catches strokes that land exactly on the bar boundary.
- Max-pooling the activity within a bar (Gururani) rather than averaging RMS over the bar directly addresses a one-beat pickup diluted by three silent beats (0.024 is consistent with one quiet beat out of four).
- Tails that trigger false onsets (reported for the carried-over bars) are the known weakness of plain spectral flux on sustained tones; SuperFlux's maximum-filter suppression or a phase/complex-domain ODF is the literature's fix, and `combine`/minimum inter-onset interval (30-150 ms) kills doubles.

### Gaps
- No paper measured onset detection on *separated* guitar stems (all strum results are on clean pickup or mic audio); separation artefacts will lower the 79 % flux baseline by an unknown amount.
- No published number for how long a strummed chord's tail stays above typical bleed levels.

## KQ6. Evaluation: per-bar precision/recall against ear truth; choosing thresholds inside a band across many songs without fitting one song

### Takeaway
The standard tools are segment-based F-score/error rate at a fixed grid (sed_eval, here one bar), AUC sweeps to pick operating points without committing to a threshold, per-class thresholds chosen on a held-out validation set, pooled (not fold-wise) scoring, and grouped splits by song; the literature warns that tuned thresholds track the class prior (r = 0.9), which is exactly the overfit-to-one-song risk.

### Cited Findings
- sed_eval segment-based metrics compare reference and system output "on a fixed time grid" with `time_resolution` default 1.0 s; P = TP/(TP+FP), R = TP/(TP+FN), F = 2PR/(P+R); error rate uses S = min(FN, FP), D = max(0, FN-FP), I = max(0, FP-FN) summed over segments; event-based metrics use `t_collar` 0.2 s and `percentage_of_length` 0.5 with `evaluate_offset` optional — [sed_eval docs](https://tut-arg.github.io/sed_eval/sound_event.html)
- sed_eval cites Forman & Scholz (SIGKDD Explorations 2010) for evaluating cross-validation folds together rather than fold-wise — [sed_eval docs](https://tut-arg.github.io/sed_eval/sound_event.html)
- Gururani et al. chose AUC because "a fixed threshold ... is not straightforward", picked per-class thresholds "by computing the class-wise f-measure at all score thresholds and selecting the threshold giving the best f-measure in the validation set", and found the chosen thresholds (0.02-0.55) correlate 0.9 with training-class frequency, "suggesting that the model has learned biases in the dataset" — [Gururani et al. 2018](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf)
- Hung & Yang pick per-instrument thresholds (0.01-0.99 grid) by maximising F1 on the training set, with no smoothing — [Hung & Yang 2018](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1806.09587)
- OpenMIC baseline: 100 random splits of 500 test instances with 3-fold CV inside the training set for hyper-parameters, reporting accuracy relative to the class "bias point" — [Humphrey et al. 2018](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2018/paper/000248.pdf)
- A DCASE-style practice tunes threshold and median window per event class on a validation set over a grid (thresholds 0.1-0.9, odd median sizes 1-31), because "optimal post-processing depends on each event's temporal structure" (secondary source quoting a system report) — [scrapbox note on SED median filters](https://scrapbox.io/main-custard/音響イベント検出におけるメディアンフィルタ)
- Dinkel & Yu show median-filter window choice itself shifts results toward long or short events, motivating the window-free double threshold — [ar5iv 1904.03841](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.03841)
- Cocktail Fork evaluates activity with sed_eval at 1 s segments and a 750 ms collar, threshold 0.5, median 5 — [Petermann et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07327)
- Onset evaluation convention: 50 ms tolerance via mir_eval — [arXiv 2508.07973](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973)

### Inferences
- The v1.7 validation (51 resting bars, 7 disputed stretches, one wrong) is a segment-based evaluation with time_resolution = one bar; reporting it as per-bar P/R/F plus the sed_eval error-rate decomposition (deletions = missed playing bars, insertions = false rests) would make it comparable to the literature.
- To choose constants "inside a band" generically: sweep the ratio (e.g. 0.02-0.10) and the hysteresis pair on all songs pooled, plot per-bar P/R or AUC, and pick the point on the pooled curve; evaluate leave-one-song-out so a constant that only helps one song shows up as a drop on the held-out song. Forman & Scholz's pooled-fold recommendation is the published justification for pooling bars across songs rather than averaging per-song scores.
- Gururani's 0.9 correlation between tuned thresholds and class priors is the formal version of the risk the user describes: a threshold fitted on songs with lots of guitar rest will migrate toward calling rests. Reporting the chosen operating point *with* the pooled curve, and checking that the bars at 0.050/0.062 sit on the flat part of it, is the defence.
- Because activity truth in the literature is itself energy-derived, ear truth is the stricter standard; the one mis-rested pickup bar is a deletion of a playing bar, which sed_eval error rate penalises the same as an insertion, so a tuning objective should weight misses and false rests explicitly rather than optimise F alone.

### Gaps
- No published study evaluates per-bar rest decisions against listening truth, nor reports how many songs are needed before per-song variance of the best threshold stabilises.
- No source gives a recommended ratio of hysteresis thresholds for musical instruments (only speech/SED values).
