# Separating or telling apart two guitar parts on one stem, and handling bass bleed on a guitar stem (state of the art, October 2026)

Scope note: all figures below are quoted from the cited source; where I measured something myself (HTTP HEAD sizes) that is stated. Numbers from different validation sets are not comparable with one another and are flagged as such.

## Key Question 1: Which separators give a guitar stem, what guitar SDR do they report, which split lead from rhythm or report bass bleed, and which are open, pip-installable on Windows, and how large?

### Takeaway
Open models with a guitar stem are essentially one: Demucs `htdemucs_6s` (guitar + piano added, experimental, repo archived January 2025), which scores guitar SDR 3.07 dB mean on MoisesDB and 5.22 dB on MVSep's private guitar set; community BS-RoFormer / Mel-RoFormer guitar checkpoints on MVSep reach 7.0 to 9.05 dB on that same private set but are hosted services or large (hundreds of MB) checkpoints, not a published pip model. No open model separates lead from rhythm guitar; only Moises (paid tier) claims it, and LALAL.AI moved the other way, merging acoustic and electric into one "Guitars" stem because a model "changing its mind midway" split one performance across two stems.

### Cited Findings

**Open models and their guitar stems**

The repository was archived (read-only) on 1 January 2025, the author no longer works at Meta, only important bug fixes are handled in the fork at github.com/adefossez/demucs; licence MIT; install `python.exe -m pip install -U demucs` on Windows. — [facebookresearch/demucs README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)

The page has no notes on bleed or bass. — [MVSep algorithm 17 (Guitar)](https://www.mvsep.com/algorithms/17)

The repo (`pip install msst`, MIT) trains BS-RoFormer, Mel-Band RoFormer, MDX23C, HTDemucs, SCNet and others; no guitar checkpoint is published there. — [ZFTurbo pretrained_models.md](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/blob/main/docs/pretrained_models.md); [repo README](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training)

Stems are the 4 MUSDB stems only; guitar is not addressed. — [BS-RoFormer paper, Sec. 4.4 and Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02612)

- `pip install BS-RoFormer` (lucidrains) installs the architecture only (BSRoformer and MelBandRoformer classes), MIT, no weights; it points to ZFTurbo's repo and Kimberley Jensen's Mel-Band vocal model for weights. — [lucidrains/BS-RoFormer](https://github.com/lucidrains/BS-RoFormer)

**Commercial lead/rhythm and guitar stems**

- AudioShake sells an electric-guitar separator via API/SDK described as covering "crisp rhythm parts to saturated leads" (one stem, not a lead/rhythm split). — [AudioShake electric guitar separation](https://www.audioshake.ai/audioshake-models/electric-guitar-separation)

**Bleed reports**
- MoisesDB's authors expect "other plucked" tracks to "still be difficult to distinguish from 'guitar' tracks", and describe the typical symptom as "the separated 'other' stem bleeds into 'guitar'". — [MoisesDB paper, Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15913)
- The SDX 2023 paper defines bleeding as "the recording contains signals belonging to more than one instrument", ran a dedicated Bleeding leaderboard, and reports that bleeding "was harder to handle than label noise", partly because bleeding affected every stem while label noise affected about 34% of stems. Training the baseline on the bleeding-corrupted set lowered average SDR by 0.83 dB relative to clean MoisesDB. — [SDX 2023 Music Demixing Track paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.06979v4)
- No source found quantifies bass bleed into the Demucs guitar stem; the forum-level evidence is anecdotal (users split vocals first with a RoFormer, then run htdemucs_ft on the instrumental). — [StemSplit, Spleeter vs Demucs (vendor blog)](https://stemsplit.io/blog/spleeter-vs-demucs.md)

### Inferences
- The only guitar stem obtainable with a pip install on Windows and torch is `htdemucs_6s` (~55 MB). The MoisesDB benchmark (3.07 dB mean, below the 5.3 to 5.4 dB oracle-mask ceiling) is the honest number for it; MVSep's 5.22 dB is on a different, undisclosed set.
- The strongest open guitar separators (MVSep's BS/Mel-RoFormer guitar models at 7 to 9 dB) are the same architecture family whose vocal checkpoints weigh ~640 MB; a guitar checkpoint of that family would be a similar size and would need the ZFTurbo or audio-separator inference stack, not a published pip model.
- Every open model's "guitar" target is the MoisesDB stem (acoustic + clean electric + distorted electric summed). Nothing in the open world is trained to put lead and rhythm on different outputs, so a two-part split must happen after separation, on the pipeline's side.
- Because `htdemucs_6s`'s "other" SDR on MoisesDB is near 0 dB and guitar is 3 dB, content assignment between guitar, other and (for low parts) bass is unreliable at the 1 s level; the observed "guitar stem carried the bass melody" case is consistent with this.

### Gaps
- No source gives a MUSDB18 guitar SDR (MUSDB18 has no guitar stem), and no model card publishes guitar SDR on MoisesDB except the HT-Demucs baseline in the MoisesDB paper.
- MVSep does not disclose its guitar validation set, so its 5.22 to 9.05 dB figures cannot be mapped to MoisesDB numbers.
- No evidence found of a Sound Demixing Challenge 2024 edition or of any challenge track with a guitar stem.
- Spleeter fine-tuning for guitar: no documented recipe or result found.
- The PyTorch parameter count of `htdemucs_6s` was not published on any fetched page.

## Key Question 2: Is there research on separating two guitars or lead versus rhythm guitar?

### Takeaway
The only peer-reviewed two-guitar separation work found is GuitarDuets (ISMIR 2024), where an HT-Demucs adapted to two outputs reaches ~4.3 to 5.9 dB SDR on the first guitar but ~0.2 to 1.4 dB on the second, which the authors read as the model treating the second guitar as background; ground-truth note labels help a lot but estimated notes barely do. Lead-versus-rhythm as a role split exists only as detection (Pati and Lerch's guitar-solo detector, Foulon et al.'s chord/solo/bass mode classifier) or in the older pitch-informed solo/accompaniment separation line (Cano), not as a trained open separator.

### Cited Findings
- GuitarDuets provides "approximately three hours of real and synthesized classical guitar duet recordings" and adapts HT-Demucs "to output two stereo signals"; a joint permutation-invariant transcription and separation variant uses predicted notes as side information. — [GuitarDuets abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01172)
- Cross-dataset results on the real-duet test set (SDR G1 / G2): training on real + synthetic gives 5.882 / 0.920 dB; real only 4.952 / 1.014; GuitarSet only 4.632 / 1.378; synthetic only 3.472 / 0.200. With ground-truth note conditioning on the synthetic set G2 rises to 4.863 dB, with estimated notes 1.977 dB, with no conditioning 2.569 dB. The authors suggest "the model may treat G1 as the primary source and G2 as background noise or an artifact". The parts are labelled only "first guitar" and "second guitar"; roles are not defined. — [GuitarDuets full text, Tables 3 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01172)
- Metric caveat from the same paper: in a simulation, guitar-guitar mixtures scored higher SDR and SI-SDR than guitar-piano mixtures at the same mixing ratio (SDR 5 dB reached at mixing ratio about 0.8 multitimbral vs 0.6 monotimbral), so "the timbral similarity between the two guitars introduces a challenge for the metrics". Real recordings also had cross-microphone bleed, addressed with a bleed-free test set. Dataset on Zenodo; no code link. — [GuitarDuets full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01172); [Zenodo 12802440](https://zenodo.org/records/12802440)
- GuitarSet, used as one of GuitarDuets' training sources, has 360 excerpts from 6 players, each playing 30 lead sheets in a comping version and then "later solo on top of his own comping"; so comp and solo are separate recordings of the same player over the same backing. — [GuitarSet site](https://guitarset.weebly.com/)
- Bastas, Katsouros and Maragos (TASLP 2025) separate individual guitar strings with a multi-channel Wave-U-Net and feed them to tablature transcription, using synthesized targets (GS-Aux from GuitarSet, ADGP from DadaGP); this is string-level, not part-level, separation. — [Bastas et al. TASLP 2025 PDF](https://robotics.ntua.gr/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025_Bastas_Deep-Guitar-StringSeparation-TablatureTranscription_TASLP_final.pdf)
- The standard survey of lead/accompaniment separation (Rafii et al., TASLP 2018) treats "lead" primarily as vocals and notes "the particular difficulty of obtaining data for learning lead separation systems". — [HAL record](https://hal-lirmm.ccsd.cnrs.fr/lirmm-01766781); [arXiv 1804.08300](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.08300.pdf)
- Cano's pitch-informed solo/accompaniment separation (Ilmenau 2014; EURASIP JASP 2014) separates "main instruments from music accompaniment regardless of the musical genre"; scope: "only pitched monophonic instruments were considered in a single-channel scenario"; pitch detection front-end plus tone-object spectral estimation; perceptual quality comparable to SiSEC 2011/2013 systems. — [EURASIP thesis record](https://theses.eurasip.org/theses/606/pitch-informed-solo-and-accompaniment-separation); [Springer article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1687-6180-2014-23); [EUSIPCO 2012 paper](https://new.eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2012/Conference/papers/1569588731.pdf)
- Pati and Lerch (AES 2017) detect electric-guitar solos in 60 full rock songs (GSD dataset; 355 min, ~25% solo). A solo is defined as a section where "the guitar is in the foreground compared to other instruments or vocals", plays "improvised melodic phrases which do not generally repeat over measures", and is "longer than 2 measures" (explicitly distinguished from riffs, which "rely on the use of power chords ... and repeat over measures", and licks). Features: 17 baseline (spectral centroid, max amplitude, ZCR, spectral crest, flux, MFCC 2-13), 2 predominant-pitch features (Melodia f0 in MIDI and pitch confidence) and 2 structural-segmentation features (segment repetition count, normalized segment duration); 2 s texture windows; SVM; morphological post-processing with k = 4 s minimum. Best macro-accuracy 78.6%, precision 63.3%, recall 71.8%; pitch features alone (P) score below the baseline (macro 69.5%), which the authors attribute to the fact that "the pitch-based features rely on predominant pitch only and contain no information about which instrument is actually contributing to the predominant pitch". Folds were split by song. — [Pati and Lerch 2017 PDF](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf); [GSD dataset repo](https://github.com/ashispati/GuitarSoloDetection)
- Foulon, Roy and Pachet (2014) classify single-guitar audio into three playing modes, "bass, solo melodic playing, and chords", with supervised learning on recordings from several guitar types; Pati and Lerch report their f-measure as "around 95%" but note "they used sound from a single guitar only and thus, the performance of the algorithm on actual songs involving other instruments is unknown". — [Springer chapter DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-12976-1_4](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-12976-1_4); [Pati and Lerch 2017, Sec. 2](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)
- MoisesDB's guitar stem is defined by instrument type only: "Acoustic Guitar, Clean Electric Guitar, Distorted Electric Guitar"; there is no lead/rhythm track type in the taxonomy. The dataset is "free of charge for non-commercial research use only" (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 per the repo). — [MoisesDB paper, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15913); [moises-ai/moises-db](https://github.com/moises-ai/moises-db)
- The SDX 2023 challenge separated only vocals, bass, drums and other; no guitar leaderboard existed. — [SDX 2023 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.06979v4)

### Inferences
- GuitarDuets is the closest controlled test of "two similar-timbre guitars on one input": even with a modern separator trained on the task, the weaker part sits around 1 dB SDR, and only oracle note information lifts it. For a pipeline that will not train a model, this argues for assigning *detected notes* to parts rather than trying to re-separate audio.
- Pati and Lerch's negative result for predominant pitch alone matches the project's experience that pitch and onset features did not generalize: the pitch track tells you *that* something is in the foreground, not *which instrument*; the useful gains came from combining timbre, pitch and structural repetition and from song-level post-processing.
- Foulon et al.'s chord/solo/bass classifier on single-guitar audio is the closest published analogue to the needed "is this stem content strum, lead or bass?" decision; its 95% is on clean single-guitar input, so it bounds what a timbre+pitch classifier could do *after* a good separation, not on a bleedy stem.

### Gaps
- No paper on separating lead from rhythm guitar in a full mix; no timbre-clustering or query-by-example separation paper specific to two guitars was found in this search.
- Foulon et al.'s feature set and full results could not be read (chapter not downloadable).
- GuitarDuets reports no parameter count and no released code.

## Key Question 3: Voice separation theory: how are multi-pitch notes assigned to voices, and are there Python implementations?

### Takeaway
Classical voice separation rests on four principles (monophonic voices, all voices sound together at least once, pitch proximity, no crossing) and the strongest simple method is Chew and Wu's contig mapping: segment where the number of simultaneous notes changes, order notes within a segment by pitch, then connect segments by minimum pitch-distance matching. On a pop corpus of monophonic MIDI tracks a trivial "closest reference pitch per voice" baseline assigns 89.5% of notes correctly, beating contig mapping on that metric, but all methods fail at voice crossings and none knows about instruments. The only maintained Python implementation found is partitura's `estimate_voices` (contig mapping, with known rough edges).

### Cited Findings
- Chew and Wu's four principles as summarized by Guiomard-Kagan et al.: "(p1) Voices are monophonic; (p2) At least once, all voices must be played simultaneously; (p3) Intervals are minimized between successive notes in the same stream or voice (pitch proximity); (p4) Voices tend not to cross." Contig mapping: split into blocks where the count of simultaneous notes is constant (contigs), order fragments low to high, start from maximal-voice contigs, and connect fragments with weight −K if they are two parts of the same note, else the absolute pitch difference; choose links minimizing the total weight. — [Guiomard-Kagan et al., ISMIR 2015](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/180_Paper.pdf); original: [Chew and Wu, contig mapping (VoSA)](https://infolab.usc.edu/imsc/research/project/vosa/vosa.pdf)
- Their benchmark (48 Bach WTC fugues plus 97 pop MIDI files where each monophonic track is a voice, 3.0 voices average): note precision (NPR) on pop, baseline 89.5%, CW 84.6%, CW-Prioritized 64.8%; transition ratio on pop, baseline 87.1%, CW 88.7%, CW-Prioritized 89.4%. On fugues, CW NPR 83% / 87.8% and TR 95.9% / 95.6%. "On the pop corpus, the baseline NPR is even better than CW and CW-Prioritized." The contig-creation step alone has TR-precision 99.4%, "a very good method that makes almost no error"; "the challenge is thus to do the right connections between the contigs". "None of the algorithms successfully handles the voice crossing"; handling it "would require to have a knowledge of the patterns". The baseline assigns a reference pitch per voice (average pitch over the fullest segments) and gives each note to the closest reference pitch. — [Guiomard-Kagan et al., ISMIR 2015, Table 2 and Sec. 5](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/180_Paper.pdf)
- The same paper summarizes other methods: Temperley's Streamer (pitch proximity, a long rest starts a new stream, no crossing), Rafailidis et al.'s k-NN stream segmentation (synchronicity, pitch and onset proximity), Madsen and Widmer (allows crossing voices), and Kilian and Hoos, which cuts the score into slices of overlapping notes and uses "an optimization method involving several evaluation functions"; "the output voices can contain chords". — [Guiomard-Kagan et al., ISMIR 2015](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/180_Paper.pdf); [Kilian and Hoos 2002 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~hoos/Publ/KilHoo02.pdf)
- Huron (2001) derives voice-leading rules from six perceptual principles: toneness, temporal continuity, minimum masking, tonal fusion, pitch proximity and pitch comodulation; pitch proximity explains why small intervals keep a line coherent and why crossing and overlap are avoided, with an exception noted for octave leaps in the bass. — [SAGE encyclopedia entry on voice-leading rules](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/music-in-the-social-and-behavioral-sciences/chpt/voice-leading-rules); Huron, Music Perception 19(1):1-64, cited in [Guiomard-Kagan et al.](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/180_Paper.pdf)
- Two families of definitions: monophonic non-overlapping voices (Chew and Wu 2004, Temperley 2001) versus perceptual streams that may hold chords (Cambouropoulos 2006 VISA, Kilian and Hoos 2002); Gray and Bunescu's neural models use pitch features that "capture Huron's pitch proximity principle". — [Gray and Bunescu, arXiv 2011.03028](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.03028); [VISA](https://www.academia.edu/92253245/Visa_The_Voice_Integration_Segregation_Algorithm)
- Data-driven alternatives: Jordanous (ICMC 2008) learns voice structure from similar MIDI pieces (12% over a baseline; MATLAB code using "frequency information in the context of a previous note"); de Valk and Weyde (ISMIR 2018) publish a Bach WTC + inventions dataset and neural models; Karystinaios et al. (2024) treat voice and staff assignment as graph clustering. — [Jordanous paper record](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/42367/); [Jordanous code record](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/42883); [de Valk and Weyde ISMIR 2018](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/304_Paper.pdf); [Cluster and Separate, arXiv 2407.21030](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.21030)
- Python implementation: partitura's `estimate_voices(note_info, monophonic_voices=True)` implements Chew and Wu's contig mapping; cost between fragments is `-MAX_COST` for the same note, `MAX_COST` for a stream that skipped a connection, otherwise `abs(pitch difference)`; `MAX_COST` is 1000 (the paper uses 2^31); matching is greedy minimum-cost; in polyphonic mode chords are reduced to their highest note; grace notes are deleted (TODO); the docstring default for `monophonic_voices` disagrees with the signature; a `Contig.duration` line computes `offset - offset` (always 0). Output: a voice number per note, starting at 1. — [partitura voice_separation source](https://partitura.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/partitura/musicanalysis/voice_separation.html); [partitura paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13144)

### Inferences
- The project's register split (notes above/below MIDI 60) is literally the ISMIR 2015 "baseline": one reference pitch per voice, nearest wins. The literature says that baseline is competitive on note accuracy for pop material with mostly non-crossing parts, but its errors cluster exactly where parts cross or share pitches, and it cannot adapt the boundary per song unless the reference pitches are estimated from the song's own fullest segments (the baseline does this: reference pitch = mean pitch of each voice in the maximal-polyphony segments). That per-song reference estimate is the generic form of the fitted MIDI-60 constant.
- Contig mapping needs polyphony counts and note durations; multi-pitch estimates from audio have unreliable offsets and spurious notes, which is why GuitarDuets saw little gain from estimated notes. Contig *creation* (segmenting by simultaneous-note count) is the robust part and could still be used to find "two-part" sections, with pitch-proximity linking only within those sections.
- No HMM-based voice assignment for symbolic music was found; the Markov-flavoured method (Jordanous) conditions on the previous note's pitch. A pitch-proximity cost with a crossing penalty, solved as per-onset assignment, is what the field actually uses.

### Gaps
- Kilian and Hoos's exact cost functions and Chew and Wu's original AVC numbers were not read (PDF text garbled / not fetched).
- Huron's own wording for each rule was not verified against the Music Perception article.
- No evaluation of any of these algorithms on audio-derived (noisy) multi-pitch input was found.

## Key Question 4: Instrument activity and bleed: how do activity-detection models flag that a stem is really bass or drums, and can cross-stem correlation detect bleed?

### Takeaway
Instrument activity detection at 1 s resolution is an established task (Gururani, Summers and Lerch, ISMIR 2018; OpenMIC-2018 for clip-level), but the pretrained-model ecosystem is awkward on Windows (Essentia's TF models are Linux/macOS only; OpenMIC ships VGGish features plus a random-forest baseline, not a ready classifier). For bleed, the multitrack crosstalk literature gives a direct recipe: model the guitar stem's magnitude spectrogram as the clean part plus a weighted sum of the other stems and fit the weights by least squares; the fitted bass weight is a bleed amount, and Seipel and Lerch show the estimates correlate >0.9 with the true mixing at −12 to −18 dB crosstalk but degrade (0.68) at −6 dB and for instruments with overlapping ranges.

### Cited Findings
- Gururani, Summers and Lerch (ISMIR 2018) contrast instrument recognition with instrument activity detection ("when each instrument is present on a fine-grained time scale", one second up to track level), train MLP, CNN and CRNN models for up to 18 instruments on public multitrack datasets, evaluate with AUC-ROC and Label Ranking Average Precision, and propose an instrument-confusion visualization. — [ISMIR 2018 paper PDF](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf); [anthology record](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/2018.ismir_conference-2018.75)
- OpenMIC-2018: the official toolkit fetches VGGish parameters and provides a tutorial to train and evaluate a model; the baseline is a random forest on VGGish embeddings; leaderboard models include PaSST (0.843 mAP) and DyMN-L (0.855 mAP). It is a feature extractor plus labels, not a packaged classifier. — [cosmir/openmic-2018](https://github.com/cosmir/openmic-2018); [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/1432913); [benchmark listing](https://vercel.hyper.ai/en/sota/tasks/instrument-recognition/benchmark/instrument-recognition-on-openmic-2018)
- Essentia's TensorFlow package: "available for Linux and macOS as a separate Python package, essentia-tensorflow". — [Essentia docs](https://essentia.upf.edu/machine_learning.html)
- Activity-gated evaluation: the Spheres orchestral dataset paper computes separation quality per instrument over 1 s frames and takes the median only over frames where that instrument is active. — [Spheres dataset, arXiv 2511.21247](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21247)
- Seipel and Lerch (AES 2018): "the crosstalk on a particular track is modeled as a weighted sum of the remaining tracks of this recording"; the weights λ(l,j) are found by gradient descent on the cost Θ(λ) = (1/N) Σ_n Σ_k [X_mix,l(n,k) − Σ_{j≠l} λ(l,j) X(j,n,k)]², on magnitude spectrograms with a 4096-sample Hamming window and 2048 hop at 48 kHz (~85 ms, chosen so short delays can be neglected); negative λ are clipped to zero; crosstalk is then removed by spectral subtraction. Correlation between the estimated λ matrix and the true demixing matrix averaged 0.957 (−12 dB crosstalk), 0.937 (−18 dB) but only 0.678 (−6 dB); SIR gains 13 to 16 dB. Failure modes: "crosstalk reduction for instruments that have overlapping frequency ranges with a similar tonal character is harder", tutti sections are harder than solos, and sparse percussion tracks yield "relatively high λ values which in turn result in a harsh spectral subtraction". For annotation use, "the amount of separation (compare SIR) is crucial while the actual audio quality is irrelevant". — [Seipel and Lerch 2018 PDF](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2018/06/Seipel-and-Lerch-2018-Multi-Track-Crosstalk-Reduction.pdf)
- Related interference-reduction work estimates per-source power spectral densities and an "interference matrix" of how strongly each source appears in each microphone (Prätzlich et al. KAMIR, ICASSP 2015; Di Carlo et al. Gaussian/NMF frameworks; Kokkinis et al. Wiener filtering). — [KAMIR PDF](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.com/content/05_fau/professor/00_mueller/03_publications/2015_PraetzlichBittnerLiutkusMueller_KAMIR_ICASSP.pdf); [Di Carlo et al. HAL](https://hal-univ-tlse3.archives-ouvertes.fr/INRIA/hal-01713889v1)
- Rajesh and Rajan (WASPAA 2023) release Keras/TensorFlow CAE and truncated-U-Net models for interference reduction trained on MUSDB18HQ with artificial and RIR-based mixes; the repo states no licence and no bleed-estimation metric beyond SDR. — [its-rajesh/IRMR](https://github.com/its-rajesh/IRMR)
- A Tampere thesis proposes "bleeding matrices", a confusion-matrix view of how much interference in each separated stem comes from each other source, noting SIR alone does not say which source causes it. — [Tampere thesis record](https://trepo.tuni.fi/handle/10024/234175)
- SDX 2023 simulated bleeding by adding to every stem a gain-reduced (−7 to −12 dB), low-pass (900 to 9000 Hz cutoff) or band-pass filtered copy of every other stem in the song; label noise means "the recorded instrument and the label identifying it do not agree". Bleeding leaderboard winner kuielab: mean 6.58 dB (bass 6.98, drums 6.65, other 4.96, vocals 7.74). — [SDX 2023 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.06979v4)
- MoisesDB records per-track `has_bleed` flags in each song's `data.json` (exposed as `track.bleedings`, a dict stem → track type → list of booleans; no audio analysis is done), and the paper notes "minuscule bleeding from other stems may be present for some of the tracks". — [moises-db track.py](https://github.com/moises-ai/moises-db/blob/main/moisesdb/track.py); [MoisesDB paper, Sec. 3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15913)

### Inferences
- A separator's guitar and bass outputs are the multitrack case Seipel and Lerch assume (one file per source, all time-aligned, no delay), so their λ estimate transfers directly: fit λ_bass by non-negative least squares of the guitar-stem magnitude spectrogram on the bass-stem spectrogram (~85 ms frames, possibly restricted to the bass register below ~250 Hz) and treat λ_bass, or the fraction of guitar-stem energy explained by λ_bass × bass, as a bleed score per section. Their −6 dB result warns that when the "bleed" is as loud as the content (the blind-song case, where the stem *was* the bass melody) the weights become unreliable, but a very high explained-energy fraction is itself the signal that the stem has no independent guitar content.
- An activity model is the complement: it answers "is a guitar present at all in this second?" from the mixture, independent of the separator. On Windows with torch, a PaSST/AudioSet-style tagger fine-tuned on OpenMIC is the realistic route; Essentia is out.
- Combining the two (activity says no guitar; λ_bass explains most of the stem) gives a per-section "stem is bass, skip riff test" rule that does not depend on a pitch threshold.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that applies cross-stem correlation to *separator outputs* (as opposed to microphone multitracks) to flag bleed; the transfer above is an inference.
- Numerical results of Gururani et al. (per-instrument AUC, dataset names) were not read.
- Whether OpenMIC-2018's class list includes both "bass" and "guitar" as separate classes was not verified in this search.

## Key Question 5: Register-based splitting: what does the literature say about splitting a mixed guitar signal by pitch band, and when does it fail?

### Takeaway
Register (pitch-band) splitting is the oldest and weakest cue in the literature: it is the voice-separation baseline, the "pitch-mismatch" solo detector that Peterschmitt et al. found unreliable for guitar, and the predominant-pitch feature that Pati and Lerch found adds little without timbre. It works when parts occupy disjoint ranges and do not cross; it fails at crossings, shared notes, bass octave leaps, and whenever the low part is the one playing the melody. NMF with pitch-indexed or instrument-constrained bases is the principled version, and even there same-instrument overlapping harmonics are the stated hard case.

### Cited Findings
- The ISMIR 2015 voice-separation baseline is a register split (nearest reference pitch per voice) and reaches NPR 89.5% on pop MIDI but merges voices that play the same notes (a pop file where "two voices play mostly same notes" gives NPR and TR near 50%) and all methods fail at crossings. — [Guiomard-Kagan et al., ISMIR 2015](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/180_Paper.pdf)
- Peterschmitt et al.'s pitch-mismatch-error solo detector "performed well for harmonic solo instruments like saxophone and violin but did not give reliable results for instruments such as piano and guitar which had less sparse and less harmonic spectra"; Smit and Ellis's harmonic-cancellation energy-drop detector reached about 70% precision/recall on folk and classical. — [Pati and Lerch 2017, Sec. 2](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)
- Predominant pitch (Melodia f0 and confidence) on its own scored below spectral/timbral baseline features for solo detection (macro-accuracy 69.5% vs 74.3%), because it "contain[s] no information about which instrument is actually contributing to the predominant pitch"; the combination plus segment-repetition features and 4 s post-processing was best. — [Pati and Lerch 2017, Table 1](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)
- Huron's pitch-proximity principle has an explicit exception for octave leaps in the bass, i.e. bass lines legitimately violate small-interval continuity. — [SAGE encyclopedia entry](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/music-in-the-social-and-behavioral-sciences/chpt/voice-leading-rules)
- Pitch-informed separation (Cano) is restricted to a *monophonic* pitched solo over accompaniment; it tracks the solo's pitch and builds tone objects rather than cutting by band. — [EURASIP thesis record](https://theses.eurasip.org/theses/606/pitch-informed-solo-and-accompaniment-separation)
- NMF with pitch-indexed dictionaries: Bay et al. (ISMIR 2012) build spectral dictionaries indexed by fundamental frequency and instrument and track pitches with a PLCA variant; Bryan, Mysore and Wang (ISMIR 2013) separate polyphonic guitar and piano into pitch tracks using harmonic structure learned from synthesized recordings plus user corrections, noting that "distinct pitches from one instrument share similar timbre and attacks, and their overlapping harmonics make separation hard"; Grindlay and Ellis constrain NMF bases to an instrument subspace ("eigeninstruments"). — [Bay et al. 2012](https://www.mit.edu/~paris/pubs/bay-ismir2012.pdf); [Bryan et al. 2013](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2013/paper/000188.pdf); [Grindlay and Ellis 2009](https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~grindlay/pubs/WASPAA_2009.pdf)
- Harmonic-percussive separation followed by NMF for hexaphonic guitar string separation "performed comparably to plain NMF" (Senvaityte and Pauwels 2019). — [Academia.edu record](https://www.academia.edu/130131931/Guitar_String_Separation_Using_Non_Negative_Matrix_Factorization_and_Factor_Deconvolution)
- GuitarDuets shows that with identical timbre and overlapping registers a trained separator still collapses the second part to ~1 dB SDR. — [GuitarDuets full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01172)

### Inferences
- A fixed MIDI-60 boundary is the literature's baseline with the per-song reference-pitch estimate removed; replacing the constant with per-song reference pitches estimated from the maximal-polyphony segments (the ISMIR 2015 baseline's own rule) is the generic version and is what a reviewer would compare against.
- The literature's failure list maps onto the project's observations: (a) strum and lead share pitches in the middle register (shared notes, crossings); (b) the bass case is the "low part carries the melody" case where any high-register rule follows the wrong part; (c) predominant pitch cannot tell which instrument is sounding, so register-split onset statistics cannot either.
- The cue the literature adds on top of register is *repetition structure* (riffs and strums repeat across bars; solos do not), used both by Pati and Lerch (segment repetition count) and by the riff definition itself. That is a generic, song-relative cue.

### Gaps
- No paper was found on splitting by low/high band pitch-change shares, nor on register-constrained NMF bases for lead-vs-rhythm guitar specifically; Asakawa and Hamanaka's NMF work reports a lead-guitar figure but is in Japanese and was not read. — [IPSJ record](https://ipsj.ixsq.nii.ac.jp/record/98456/files/IPSJ-MUS14102016.pdf)

## Key Question 6: Evaluation: how would a small project measure whether a two-part split works?

### Takeaway
Three complementary measurements are standard and cheap: (1) note-level onset F-measure per part with mir_eval (50 ms onset, 50 cents pitch, `offset_ratio=None`), (2) voice-assignment metrics from the symbolic literature (note precision plus transition precision/recall, which do not require matching voice labels), and (3) for audio splits, BSSEval SDR via museval on synthetic two-guitar mixtures built from GuitarSet comp + solo pairs (CC BY 4.0) or MoisesDB guitar sub-tracks (non-commercial), with the GuitarDuets caveat that SDR is inflated for same-timbre sources. Bleed robustness can be tested with the SDX 2023 bleed-injection recipe.

### Cited Findings
- mir_eval `transcription.precision_recall_f1_overlap` defaults: `onset_tolerance=0.05` s, `pitch_tolerance=50.0` cents, `offset_ratio=0.2`; set `offset_ratio=None` to ignore offsets; `onset_precision_recall_f1` evaluates onsets only (ignores pitch). — [mir_eval transcription API](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/latest/api/transcription.html)
- GuitarSet transcription papers use note-level F50 (pitch match plus onset within ±50 ms); supervised models reach F50 89.7 on the GuitarSet test split, TART reports about 0.70 to 0.84; FretNet uses six-fold cross-validation split by player. Scores are only comparable within the same split and tolerance. — [High Resolution Guitar Transcription, arXiv 2402.15258](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.15258); [TART, arXiv 2510.02597](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02597); [FretNet, arXiv 2212.03023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.03023)
- Voice-assignment metrics: note precision (NPR, share of notes in the right voice, requires a voice correspondence, "deeply penalize[s]" one split in the middle of a piece), transition precision/recall (share of successive-note pairs correctly kept together, no label matching needed; equal to each other when voice counts match), and entropy-based over/under-segmentation scores So/Su. — [Guiomard-Kagan et al., ISMIR 2015, Sec. 4.3](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/180_Paper.pdf)
- museval: `pip install museval`, BSSEval metrics computed framewise with time-invariant distortion filters; `museval.eval_mus_track(track, estimates)` scores a dict of estimate arrays; MIT. — [sigsep/sigsep-mus-eval](https://github.com/sigsep/sigsep-mus-eval)
- GuitarSet: 360 excerpts (~30 s), 6 players × 30 lead sheets × {comping, solo}, solo recorded "later ... on top of his own comping"; files `hex` (6-channel), `hex_cln` (interference removed), `mic` (mono); annotations include pitch contours, string/fret, chords, beats, playing style; CC BY 4.0; 8.2 GB. — [GuitarSet site](https://guitarset.weebly.com/); [Zenodo 3371780](https://zenodo.org/records/3371780)
- MoisesDB guitar stems are built from separate Acoustic / Clean Electric / Distorted Electric tracks that "can combine multiple sources, which are mixed on the fly"; licence CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; per-track bleed flags exist. — [moises-ai/moises-db](https://github.com/moises-ai/moises-db); [MoisesDB paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15913)
- GuitarDuets provides ~3 h of real and synthesized duets with note-level annotations for the synthesized part (Zenodo 12802440) and warns that SDR/SI-SDR read higher for monotimbral mixtures at the same mixing ratio. — [GuitarDuets full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01172); [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/12802440)
- SDX 2023 bleed injection recipe: add every other stem at −7 to −12 dB after a random low-pass (900 to 9000 Hz) or band-pass filter of random order 3 to 9. — [SDX 2023 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.06979v4)
- Pati and Lerch report macro-accuracy rather than micro-accuracy because 75% of frames are non-solo (R1 = 74.7% by predicting all non-solo), use song-level folds, and compute metrics per song. — [Pati and Lerch 2017](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)
- Activity-gated scoring: compute per-instrument scores on 1 s frames and take the median over frames where the instrument is active. — [Spheres dataset](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21247)

### Inferences
- For the project's actual question ("do the printed strokes follow the strum, not the lead?"), the symbolic transition metric is the right one: take the ear-truth strum onsets per section as the reference voice, the detected onsets assigned to "strum" as the estimate, and report onset precision/recall at 50 ms per section plus the share of detected onsets wrongly pulled from the lead part. It needs no SDR and no audio ground truth.
- A reproducible synthetic test set is available for free: GuitarSet comp + solo excerpts from the same player and lead sheet mixed at a few ratios (with the drums+bass backing track as a third source if desired) give a two-part guitar mix with note-level truth for both parts; MoisesDB's acoustic/clean/distorted sub-tracks give realistic multi-guitar mixes but non-commercially. Both allow per-part onset F-measure and, for any audio split, museval SDR.
- Bleed handling should be tested the SDX way: inject the bass stem into the guitar stem at −7 to −12 dB (and, for the "stem is the bass" case, at 0 dB with the guitar removed) and check that the bleed score and the riff test respond.
- Report macro-accuracy and per-song results with song-level folds, following Pati and Lerch; otherwise a rule fitted to one song will look good on frame counts.

### Gaps
- No published protocol exists for evaluating note-to-part assignment on audio-derived notes; the combination above is assembled from the symbolic and transcription literatures.
- The licence of the GuitarDuets dataset is not stated on the pages read.
