# Riff versus strum: texture classification, polyphony estimation, riff discovery and tab transcription (literature and tools, as of 2026-10)

Scope note for the reviewer: the project rule under review flags a riff when (a) chroma entropy is below a ceiling, (b) the single-note share is above a floor, and (c) the pitch-change share between consecutive pyin-named onsets is above 0.26; a flagged riff is then reduced to a 1–2 bar figure by vote and printed as tab only when repeats agree > 0.70, support > 0.75 and named share > 0.6. Each section below ends with a comparison against those rules.

## Q1. What features and models classify monophonic vs polyphonic texture, or lead vs rhythm guitar, and with what accuracy?

### Takeaway
The literature never uses chroma entropy alone for this job. The two audio systems that come closest to "lead vs rhythm" use (i) a pitch tracker's own confidence/inharmonicity statistics as the polyphony cue (Lachambre; Foulon/Roy/Pachet, F ≈ 0.95–0.97 on isolated guitar), and (ii) for mixes, a predominant-pitch confidence plus a structural-repetition count (Pati & Lerch, macro-accuracy 78.6 %, solo precision 63.3 %). On symbolic tab, a single binary "chords present / single notes present" feature already separates rhythm from non-rhythm bars at F1 0.87, and 31 bar-level features reach F1 0.95 — but that work defines "chord" as ≥ 2 simultaneous notes, so a power-chord riff is rhythm guitar, not lead.

### Cited Findings
- Lachambre, André-Obrecht and Pinquier distinguish monophony from polyphony using the short-term mean and variance of the YIN confidence indicator (a low YIN value means a reliable pitch; polyphony makes it unreliable), modelled with bivariate Weibull distributions; the step is a front end for singing-voice detection — [IRIT SAMOVA page](https://irit.fr/SAMOVA/site/?p=454)
- Foulon, Roy and Pachet report that the Lachambre method "only requires short training sets (about 2 minutes of audio), works for many instruments, and achieves good performance (a 6.3% global error rate)" and that "the YIN descriptor yields informative features for polyphony detection" — [Foulon et al. 2013, Automatic classification of guitar playing modes (PDF)](https://www.francoispachet.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/foulon2013classification.pdf)
- Foulon et al. classify isolated guitar audio into three playing modes (bass, melody, chords) from 50 ms frames with 75 % overlap; after feature selection the retained features are YIN pitch (quantised), YIN inharmonicity coefficient, harmonic-to-noise ratio (lowest f0 185 Hz because of the 50 ms frame), spectral spread, spectral centroid and spectral kurtosis; a −13 dB noise gate removes silent frames and an onset/offset detector removes transient frames before classification — [Foulon et al. 2013](https://www.francoispachet.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/foulon2013classification.pdf)
- Foulon et al.: frame-level Bayesian-network classification gives average F-measure 0.87; aggregating frames over a chunk of about one bar (1.5 s, "one 4/4 bar at 160 bpm") with winner-takes-all raises F to 0.96 (Godin) and 0.941 (Cort) but only 0.854 (Ovation) and 0.839 (nylon) when trained on one song; leave-one-out training on six songs gives average F 0.949–0.971 across guitar subsets; the dataset is 84 files, 1 h 39 min, each song recorded three times (melody, bass, chords) — [Foulon et al. 2013](https://www.francoispachet.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/foulon2013classification.pdf)
- Foulon et al. note the degradation across guitar types is because "a feature such as spectral spread is sensitive to timbre" — [Foulon et al. 2013](https://www.francoispachet.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/foulon2013classification.pdf)
- Pati and Lerch (AES 2017) detect electric-guitar solos in full rock mixes with an SVM over 21 features per block: 17 baseline spectral/temporal features, 2 predominant-pitch features (Melodia pitch in MIDI and Melodia "pitch confidence", zero for unvoiced) and 2 structural features (number of repetitions of the block's segment label in the song, and segment duration normalised by song length, from msaf cnmf boundaries + Laplacian labels); features are aggregated as median and std over 2 s texture windows with 50 % overlap; libSVM RBF, C = 1 — [Pati & Lerch 2017 (PDF)](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)
- Pati and Lerch's structural feature exists because "a solo does not usually repeat in a song … a feature which incorporates the structural information of a song may be helpful towards distinguishing between solos and riffs" — [Pati & Lerch 2017](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)
- Pati and Lerch results (Table 1, all in %): baseline features only, macro-accuracy 74.3 / precision 54.1; predominant pitch only, 69.5 / 43.2; structural only, 57.2 / 33.9; all three plus post-processing (erosion/dilation, minimum contiguous solo length k = 4 s), macro-accuracy 78.6, precision 63.3, recall 71.8, F 67.3, specificity 85.4; the majority-class baseline micro-accuracy is 74.7 % — [Pati & Lerch 2017](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)
- Pati and Lerch define a solo by role (guitar in the foreground, improvised melodic phrases that do not repeat, spanning more than a few measures) and explicitly separate it from riffs, citing Kernfeld that a riff is "a short melodic sequence" that repeats, and noting "many guitar riffs rely on the use of power chords"; the dataset is 355 min of audio with mean solo 35 s (min 6 s, max 2 min) — [Pati & Lerch 2017](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf); the project page adds "solos can be very similar to some of the other techniques such as riffs or licks" — [GT Music Informatics: Guitar Solo Detection](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/?p=320)
- Régnier, Martin and Bigo (ISMIR 2021) identify rhythm-guitar bars in symbolic tablature: 31 bar-level features (number of notes, presence of single notes, mean/min/max pitch, pitch ambitus, pitch variety, min/max interval between successive single notes, interval variety, duration variety, presence of chords, number of distinct chords, number of n-note chords for n = 2..6, binary "major/minor triad formable" and "fifth interval formable", min/max/mean fret and string, let-ring, bend, vibrato, whammy flags) fed to an LSTM; F1 0.95 on 102 tablatures with manual function labels; 6051 of 7487 non-empty bars (82 %) are rhythm guitar — [Régnier et al. ISMIR 2021 (PDF)](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000006.pdf)
- Régnier et al.: "chords/single notes presence" alone gives precision 0.86, recall 0.88, F1 0.87; note + chord features 0.94; tab features 0.94; all 0.95. Chords are present in 80 % of rhythm-guitar bars and single notes in 92 % of non-rhythm bars — [Régnier et al. ISMIR 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000006.pdf)
- Régnier et al. define a chord as "a set of at least two notes that are plucked simultaneously" (arpeggios excluded), and remark that lead guitar "appears to be less well-defined as it can be alternately associated with solo parts, riffs and licks, or hybrid accompanying parts"; ambiguous cases include songs that "begin with a guitar riff played as a foreground part, which is then repeated as a background accompaniment of a vocal verse" — [Régnier et al. ISMIR 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000006.pdf)
- Régnier et al. use leave-one-piece-out validation because "bars can highly repeat, in particular in rhythm guitar sections" — [Régnier et al. ISMIR 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000006.pdf)
- A monophonic-vs-polyphonic classifier for Cypriot folk songs using three ANNs on low-level timbre features reports precision 0.88 and recall 0.78 — [SCITEPRESS 2014 paper (via Unpaywall)](https://unpaywall.org/10.5220%2F0005049101440151) (secondary listing; original PDF not read)
- A 2016 De La Salle University study tested the theory "the chroma vector of a chord is more evenly distributed compared to a single-note (which has more intensity focused on a single chroma-bin)"; results supported it in controlled cases (sine, MIDI-rendered) and the best approach reached about 85 % accuracy on 22 audio files — [DLSU Research Congress 2016, TPHS-10](https://www.dlsu.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/pdf/conferences/research-congress-proceedings/2016/TPHS/TPHS-10.pdf) (search-snippet level; the PDF returned 404 on fetch)
- Baelde, Biernacki and Greff classify monophonic and polyphonic audio in real time from the whole normalised power spectrum with a kernel generative model, building polyphonic models from monophonic training data via the spectrum's additivity — [Pattern Recognition 2019 listing](https://datalearner.com/academic/journal-papers/0031-3203/volumes-and-issues/471/paper-detail/24399)
- Chen, Su and Yang (ISMIR 2015) detect five electric-guitar playing techniques on 42 solo tracks with candidate events from the melody contour then timbre/pitch classification, average F-score 74 % — [NTU scholars record](https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/handle/123456789/636366)
- GuitarSet: 360 excerpts of ~30 s, 6 players × 30 lead sheets (5 styles × 3 progressions × 2 tempi); each player records a comping version then a solo version over it; hexaphonic pickup gives per-string audio and 16 annotations per excerpt (6 pitch contours, 6 midi_note, beat, tempo, instructed and performed chords) — [GuitarSet site](https://guitarset.weebly.com/)

### Inferences
- Nobody in the surveyed literature gates on chroma entropy; the texture cue that generalises across guitars is the pitch tracker's own reliability (YIN/pyin confidence or inharmonicity), aggregated over roughly one bar. The project already runs pyin per onset; its voiced-probability output is the Lachambre/Foulon feature and is not currently used as a texture feature.
- Both Foulon and Pati aggregate over windows of 1.5–2 s and apply a minimum-duration rule (Foulon: one bar; Pati: k = 4 s), which is the same instinct as the project's "reduce to a 1–2 bar figure".
- Pati's structural-repetition feature is the mirror of the project's riff test: solos are non-repeating foreground, riffs are repeating foreground. A repeat-count feature therefore should raise riff confidence, not just be a post-hoc tab gate.
- Régnier's symbolic result says the textural split (single notes vs ≥ 2 simultaneous notes) is strong but puts power-chord figures on the rhythm side, which matches the project's ear-verified false positive (two-note palm-muted power-chord hook flagged as a riff) only if the project also treats dyads as accompaniment. The project's chroma-entropy ceiling is doing double duty (dyads out, double-stop riffs out) and that is why double-stop riffs are missed.
- Foulon's 50 ms frames with an explicit transient-rejection step suggest that computing the single-note share only on steady-state frames (after onsets) would be less noisy than over all frames.

### Gaps
- No paper evaluates a lead-vs-rhythm classifier on separated guitar stems (Demucs-style); all audio work is either isolated guitar (Foulon, Kehling) or full mixes (Pati). Stem bleed effects are unquantified.
- The Lachambre TASLP 2011 paper's own accuracy table was not retrieved; only Foulon's citation of a 6.3 % error rate.
- No published accuracy for GuitarSet comp vs solo discrimination as a classification task; GuitarSet labels exist but no one benchmarks comp/solo classification on them in the sources found.
- Chordify's and Moises's internal features are undocumented (see Q4).

## Q2. How do multi-pitch and tab estimators perform on guitar, and which run on CPU with pip on Windows? Sizes and licences.

### Takeaway
On GuitarSet, note-level onset F-measure runs from ~0.79 (Basic Pitch, 16.8 k parameters, Apache-2.0, Windows via ONNX) to ~0.90 (MT3, Riley et al. 2024, TART 2026 zero-shot ~0.81 averaged over four sets); tab-level (string+fret) F1 is lower, 0.75 for TabCNN on GuitarSet and ~0.72 for TART, and collapses to ~0.45 on real electric tone for TabCNN. pyin and CREPE are monophonic by design and documented as unsuitable for polyphony.

### Cited Findings
- Basic Pitch (Bittner et al., ICASSP 2022, "NMP") has "a total of only 16,782 parameters" and predicts frame-wise onset (Yo), note (Yn) and pitch (Yp) posteriorgrams from a harmonic CQT; notes are created by an Onsets-and-Frames-style procedure, tracking through Yn until it falls below threshold τn (tuned on validation to maximise Fno) — [arXiv 2203.09893 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09893)
- Basic Pitch GuitarSet results (Table 2; frame Acc / note F-no-offset Fno / note F with offsets): NMP 0.70 / 0.79 / 0.56 vs baseline MI-AMT 0.43 / 0.59 / 0.27; Molina (solo vocals) 0.63 / 0.52 / 0.35; MAESTRO Fno 0.71 vs Onsets-and-Frames 95.2 %; half of GuitarSet is treated as polyphonic (comp) and half monophonic (solo), and NMP beats the guitar-specific TENT on the solo half — [arXiv 2203.09893](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09893)
- Basic Pitch note metric: pitch within a quarter tone, onset within 50 ms, offset within 20 % of duration (for F); hop 10 ms — [arXiv 2203.09893](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09893)
- Basic Pitch CPU cost: on a 2017 MacBook Pro (3.1 GHz quad i7, 16 GB), a 7 min 45 s file takes 24 s and 951 MB peak memory (MI-AMT 96 s / 3.3 GB; Onsets-and-Frames 5.4 GB; Vocano 8.5 GB) — [arXiv 2203.09893](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09893)
- Basic Pitch package: Apache License 2.0 (Spotify AB 2022); Python 3.7–3.11; macOS, Windows and Linux; backends TensorFlow, CoreML, TFLite, ONNX with ONNX the default on Windows; `pip install basic-pitch`; `predict()` accepts min/max frequency limits; README says "we have improved Basic Pitch beyond what was presented in this paper" — [github.com/spotify/basic-pitch](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch); [Hugging Face README](https://huggingface.co/spotify/basic-pitch/raw/main/README.md)
- Harmonica (BandLab, 2026) is a family of instrument-agnostic transcribers built on multi-depth harmonic convolution; the nano model has 26.3 K parameters, runs at 1,622.5× real time, and reaches frame F1 0.796 on its development set, 14.6 points above Basic Pitch — [arXiv 2609.04640](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04640)
- TabCNN reproduction on GuitarSet (six-fold by player): multi-pitch F1 0.826 ± 0.025, tablature F1 0.748 ± 0.047, tablature disambiguation rate (TDR) 0.899 ± 0.033 — [Leveraging real electric guitar tones … (arXiv 2405.14679)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14679v3); a secondary review of the same DAFx-24 paper reports the baseline tab F1 falling to 0.447 on real electric guitar with the GuitarProFX-augmented model recovering to 0.585 — [themoonlight.io review](https://themoonlight.io/review/leveraging-real-electric-guitar-tones-and-effects-to-improve-robustness-in-guitar-tablature-transcription-modeling) (secondary; not verified against the paper)
- TabCNN repository: no licence file or statement; Keras/TensorFlow; "made for Python 2.7"; default input CQT (options mel, CQT+mel, STFT); trains and tests on GuitarSet folds — [github.com/andywiggins/tab-cnn](https://github.com/andywiggins/tab-cnn)
- Guitar-TECHS (2025) retrains TabCNN with the GuitarSet six-fold scheme and reports modest multi-pitch/tab gains and "notable improvement" on recordings with different hardware — [arXiv 2501.03720](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03720)
- FretNet (Cwitkowitz, Hirvonen, Klapuri; ICASSP 2023, Yousician) estimates continuous-valued pitch contours grouped by string/fret, "significantly improves the resolution of multi-pitch estimation while yielding tablature results competitive with baseline models" — [arXiv 2212.03023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03023v2); Cwitkowitz's `amt-tools` package is listed as MIT — [ClickPy amt-tools](https://clickpy.clickhouse.com/dashboard/amt-tools)
- Riley, Edwards and Dixon (ICASSP 2024) train a guitar transcriber from score-audio pairs via a high-resolution piano model; their GuitarSet table lists MT3 at 90.0 F50 (50 ms onset-only note F) and their own model at 89.7; the paper calls GuitarSet a zero-shot evaluation — [arXiv 2402.15258](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.15258); MT3's Onset F1 counts a note correct when pitch matches and onset is within ±50 ms, ignoring offsets — [MT3 arXiv 2111.03017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03017)
- TART (ISMIR 2026) is a four-stage audio-to-tab pipeline (audio→MIDI with a piano model adapted to guitar, MLP technique classifier, audio-conditioned T5 string-fret assignment, LSTM tab generation); zero-shot over GuitarSet, EGDB and two noisy benchmarks it averages 81.35 % audio-to-MIDI F50, 71.8 % string-fret Tab F1 and 54.08 % end-to-end Tab F1 — [arXiv 2609.11904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11904); earlier version [arXiv 2510.02597](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02597). No code link surfaced in search.
- Kehling, Abeßer, Dittmar and Schuller (DAFx 2014) transcribe isolated polyphonic electric guitar: onset/offset/multipitch accuracy 98 %, string number 82 %, plucking style 93 %, expression style 83 %; the first 15 partials of each note are tracked with a per-frame inharmonicity coefficient; octave errors are removed by comparing odd vs even partial energy ("unlikely fundamentals are eliminated when the total energy of even partials falls below a quarter of the energy of odd partials"); 774 note-level features reduced by IRMFSP then an RBF SVM; a plausibility filter rejects impossible fingerings — [DAFx-14 paper (PDF)](https://www.dafx14.fau.de/papers/dafx14_christian_kehling_automatic_tablature_trans.pdf); dataset IDMT-SMT-Guitar: seven guitars, standard tuning, 44.1 kHz mono, four subsets including one for chord recognition and rhythm-style tasks — [Fraunhofer IDMT dataset page](https://www.idmt.fraunhofer.de/en/publications/datasets/guitar.html), [Zenodo 7544110](https://zenodo.org/records/7544110)
- librosa pyin: "well adapted to signals with a clear and prominent monophonic source … not appropriate for analyzing signals with polyphony or harmony"; returns f0, voiced flag and voiced probability, NaN where unvoiced — [librosa f0 tutorial](https://librosa.org/doc/dev/auto_tutorials/01-intro/03-f0.html); the original pYIN Vamp plugin is a separate GPL codebase — [c4dm pyin](https://code.soundsoftware.ac.uk/projects/pyin)
- CREPE: MIT licence, "monophonic pitch tracker based on a deep convolutional neural network operating directly on the time-domain waveform", requires `tensorflow >= 2.0.0`, 10 ms default hop, outputs time/frequency/confidence — [PyPI crepe](https://pypi.org/project/crepe/); torchcrepe is an MIT PyTorch port with "tiny" and "full" capacities — [PyPI torchcrepe](https://pypi.org/project/torchcrepe)
- Omnizart: `pip install omnizart` then `omnizart download-checkpoints`; TensorFlow-based; MIT per package metadata; "incompatible for ARM-based MacOS"; no Windows-specific notes — [libraries.io omnizart](https://libraries.io/pypi/omnizart), [JOSS paper](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.03391.pdf)
- Essentia's Melodia (PredominantPitchMelodia) outputs a pitch track and per-frame confidence; voicing is set by `voicingTolerance` relative to the mean contour salience; recommended front end is EqualLoudness, 2048-sample frames, 128-sample hop at 44.1 kHz — [Essentia PredominantPitchMelodia](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_PredominantPitchMelodia.html); Melodia characterises pitch contours and derives rules separating melodic from non-melodic contours — [Salamon & Gómez 2012 (PDF)](https://audiolabs-erlangen.de/content/05_fau/professor/00_mueller/02_teaching/2024s_sarntal/01_group_F0/2012_SalamonG_Melodia_TALSP.pdf)

### Inferences
- The reported numbers put a ceiling on any gate: even the best GuitarSet transcribers leave ~10 % of notes wrong at onset level on clean studio acoustic guitar, and tab-level accuracy on real electric tone is ~0.45–0.6. A "named share > 0.6" gate computed from pyin on a stem is therefore demanding something the state of the art barely achieves, which is consistent with only one riff in eleven songs passing.
- pyin names one pitch per onset; on a double-stop or power-chord riff it will return either a low voiced probability or an arbitrary one of the two pitches, which directly explains the "riff with double stops is missed" and the "pitch-change share too low for one-pitch riffs" failures. Basic Pitch is the drop-in polyphonic replacement that fits the stated constraints (pip, Windows, CPU, Apache-2.0, ~17 k parameters), and it exposes min/max frequency limits useful for a guitar stem. The project's own memory notes basic-pitch needs a Python 3.11 interpreter because TensorFlow lacks a 3.12 wheel (project memory, not a web source).
- Kehling's odd/even partial-energy test and Foulon's YIN inharmonicity are the two documented, cheap discriminators between "one string" and "two strings a fifth apart" that work in the harmonic domain rather than on chroma.

### Gaps
- No source gives Basic Pitch's current-model numbers on GuitarSet (the README says the model changed after the paper).
- No GuitarSet evaluation of CREPE-at-onsets or pyin-at-onsets as a polyphonic proxy was found; everyone who evaluates on GuitarSet uses a multi-pitch model.
- TabCNN's repository has no licence, so its reuse status is undefined; FretNet code availability is asserted by an aggregator but no repository was located.
- Model file sizes in MB for Basic Pitch, Omnizart and MT3 were not stated in the fetched pages.

## Q3. How is a repeated figure (riff, ostinato, motif) found in a note sequence or audio, and how is the canonical repeat chosen?

### Takeaway
Symbolic pattern discovery is dominated by the SIA family (maximal translatable patterns on point sets, so chords are handled natively) and n-gram/string methods; audio methods work on beat-synchronous chroma with self-similarity or sparse convolutive NMF. Even the best MIREX entries score only ~0.49 three-layer F1 against human annotation, and moving from symbolic to audio input cuts establishment scores by ~25–50 %. No surveyed method publishes a rule for choosing the "canonical" occurrence; the project's vote is a local invention.

### Cited Findings
- SIA finds all maximal translatable patterns (MTPs) in a point set and SIATEC all translational equivalence classes (TECs) of MTPs; because point sets can represent chords, "the discovery of patterns containing polyphony is also enabled by the use of point-set representations" — [MorpheuS, arXiv 1812.04832](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.04832)
- SIATECCompress "performed best in the 2013 and 2014 MIREX competition on Discovery of repeated themes and sections" — [MorpheuS, arXiv 1812.04832](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.04832); three SIATECCompress variants were submitted to MIREX 2016 and the best reached an average three-layer F1 (TLF1) of 0.490 over the five JKUPDD pieces — [Meredith, AAU record](https://vbn.aau.dk/da/publications/using-siateccompress-to-discover-repeated-themes-and-sections-in-)
- SIATEC-C (ISMIR 2022) discovers repeated patterns and all their occurrences with sub-quadratic space — [ISMIR 2022 paper 6](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2022/paper/000006.pdf)
- The JKUPDD benchmark has 26 motifs/themes/sections in 5 pieces (Bach, Beethoven, Chopin, Gibbons, Mozart) — [arXiv 2001.01720](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.01720); BPS-motif adds note-level motif and occurrence annotations for Beethoven piano sonatas — [ISMIR 2023 paper 32](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2023/paper/000032.pdf)
- Nieto and Farbood's MIREX 2014 entry is a greedy path-finding method meant to run on both audio and symbolic input and to extract monophonic or polyphonic patterns — [MIREX 2014 Nieto-Farbood (PDF)](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/MIREX2014-NietoFarbood.pdf); their 2013 entry finds repetitions then applies "perceptually inspired rules" — [MIREX 2013 Nieto-Farbood (PDF)](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/NietoFarbood-PatDiscoMIREX2013.pdf)
- An ISMIR 2018 study concludes that "state-of-the-art algorithms are not yet able to reproduce human-annotated patterns" and examines why — [ISMIR 2018 paper 75](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/75_Paper.pdf)
- mir_eval's pattern module implements establishment (per-pattern, regardless of how many occurrences were found), occurrence (all repetitions), and three-layer precision/recall/F, with defaults "from the original matlab code by Tom Collins used for MIREX 2013" — [mir_eval pattern API](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/stable/api/pattern.html)
- Weiss and Bello's audio method (as reported by Music Machinery, 2010) applies sparse convolutive NMF to beat-synchronous chromagrams, uses sparsity "to automatically identify the number of patterns and their lengths", targets riff identification for thumbnailing, and is "competitive with state-of-the-art HMM and self-similarity-matrix" segmentation; the post describes no canonical-occurrence rule — [Music Machinery](https://musicmachinery.com/2010/08/10/identifying-repeated-patterns-in-music/)
- A TISMIR article frames audio pattern detection as a fundamental MIR task where "a pattern may vary considerably both in musical aspects and acoustic realization" — [TISMIR 116](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/116); the Wagner leitmotif work builds activity detection from 16 Ring recordings — [AudioLabs leitmotif page](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.de/resources/MIR/2021-TISMIR-TowardsLeitmotifDetection)
- Sears and Widmer study n-gram pattern discovery (on chord sequences) with weighting functions that favour more salient n-grams — [arXiv 1807.06700](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.06700); Wang, Kuo and Su (TISMIR 2025) precede rule-based repeated-pattern discovery with a learned motif-note identifier to reduce spurious output — [TISMIR 250](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/250/files/68d3d8299f059.xml)
- Pati and Lerch's structural features (count of a segment label's repetitions, from msaf) are the only riff-specific audio feature found; their purpose is to reject repeating foreground (riffs) from the solo class — [Pati & Lerch 2017](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)
- Régnier et al. observe that rhythm-guitar bars "can highly repeat", which is why they evaluate leave-one-piece-out — [Régnier et al. ISMIR 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000006.pdf)

### Inferences
- Pattern discovery methods treat repetition as the primary evidence of a riff; the project treats repetition only as a tab gate after a texture-based flag. Reversing the order (find bar-length repeats on beat-synchronous chroma or on the onset/pitch sequence, then ask whether the repeated figure is single-note) would catch one-pitch riffs (their repetition is strong even when pitch-change share is ~0) and would reject non-repeating strums regardless of entropy.
- SIA-family point-set patterns include chords, so a riff with double stops or power chords is a legitimate repeated pattern there; the project's entropy ceiling throws these away before the vote.
- The ~0.49 TLF1 ceiling on symbolic classical data, and the further 25–50 % drop from audio, mean that "repeats agree > 0.70" against pyin-named notes is a stiff test; a chroma-level agreement (transposition-free cosine on beat-synchronous chroma) is the measure the audio pattern literature actually uses.
- No published canonical-occurrence rule exists; the common proxies in the literature are the first occurrence (the "original motif" in evolutionary-chain formulations) or the highest-salience n-gram (Sears & Widmer weighting). A majority vote over occurrences is defensible but unprecedented in the sources.

### Gaps
- The ~25 %/~50 % audio-vs-symbolic drop in establishment scores came from a search summary attributed to a study combining transcription with pattern discovery; its primary source (likely the Springer chapter at [link.springer.com/10.1007/978-3-319-25931-4_13](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/978-3-319-25931-4_13)) was not fetched and the figure should be treated as unverified.
- No paper was found on riff detection in pop/rock audio as a named task; "riff" appears only as a confounder in solo detection and as a function label in symbolic tab.
- No published method for picking which repeat to print was found.

## Q4. How do commercial tools decide tab vs chords, and how do they present uncertainty?

### Takeaway
No commercial tool documents an automatic riff-vs-strum decision. Klangio's Guitar2Tabs makes the user choose "strumming" or "picking" mode up front; Soundslice refuses automatic notation entirely (it only separates stems); Songsterr's AI tab generator emits full tabs and reviewers report technique errors (palm muting ignored, slides missed) and wrong track separation; Moises and Chordify output chords, not note-level tab. None of them publishes a confidence score per bar.

### Cited Findings
- Klangio Guitar2Tabs offers a "strumming mode" for rhythm patterns and a "picking mode" for fingerpicking and lead lines, covers acoustic/electric/bass, alternate tunings and capo, and exports PDF, MIDI, Guitar Pro and MusicXML — [MuseHub Guitar2Tabs](https://musehub.com/app/guitar2tabs); the app analyses an uploaded recording server-side and "generates sheet music", shown with tabs and chords — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1589660528); a MuseHub review quotes a user saying it "even turns the accidental background noises into tabs" — [MuseHub Guitar2Tabs](https://musehub.com/app/guitar2tabs)
- Soundslice's transcription help describes a human workflow ("as you figure out notes, enter them in our editor") — [Soundslice transcribing help](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/creating/transcribing/); its only automation is stem generation (vocals, drums, bass, other) — [Soundslice blog: automatic multitrack stem generation](https://www.soundslice.com/blog/298/automatic-multitrack-stem-generation/)
- heise tested Songsterr's AI tab from users' own recordings and found it "fairly accurate" but "palm muting is often ignored" and slides for quick position changes are missed, while vibrato and harmonics are recognised; "when differentiating between several guitar tracks, the AI does not always make the correct distinction" — [heise.de](https://heise.de/-10356152)
- Moises provides AI chord detection "synced" to the song and an "Advanced Chords" upgrade, lyric transcription and Song Sections; no note-level tab feature is documented — [MusicTech on Moises](https://musictech.com/news/gear/moises-new-ai-tools/), [Moises App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/1515796612)
- Chordify's co-founder estimated chord accuracy at "about 70% to 95%" in 2013 while saying it is "difficult to quantify" — [TNW 2013](https://thenextweb.com/apps/2013/05/02/chordify-taps-soundcloud-and-youtube-to-show-you-the-chords-to-your-favorite-songs/); Chordify presents chord sheets rather than tabs — [GuitarChalk review](https://www.guitarchalk.com/chordify-review/); fingerpicking tablature versions are paid-only per a competitor's comparison — [Hooktheory](https://www.hooktheory.com/blog/chordify-alternatives/); Chordify's Guitar Toolkit adds live chord detection — [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/chordify-lyrics-live-chord-detection)
- Songscription markets a tab generator that lets users edit the output — [Songscription](https://songscription.ai/guitar-tab-generator)

### Inferences
- The commercial pattern is to push the texture decision to the user (Klangio's mode switch) or to show everything and let the user correct (Songsterr, Songscription). None gates output on confidence as the project does; the project's gate is stricter than any product.
- The heise finding that Songsterr confuses multiple guitar tracks is the same failure as the project's "riff over a strum in the same stem is seen as the strum": separation of two guitars inside one stem is unsolved commercially too.

### Gaps
- No product documentation of per-bar or per-note confidence display was found for Klangio, Moises, Soundslice, Songsterr or Chordify.
- Klangio's own site was not fetched; the mode description comes from store listings.

## Q5. What does theory say about distinguishing a power-chord dyad from a single note, and about riffs on one pitch?

### Takeaway
A single guitar note already deposits chroma energy on its fifth (third harmonic) and octave, and a power chord is a root-plus-fifth dyad at ~3:2, so in chroma the two are near-duplicates; distortion makes it worse by adding intermodulation partials. The literature discriminates them in the harmonic domain instead (inharmonicity, odd/even partial energy, pitch-tracker confidence), not in chroma. One-pitch riffs are a repetition phenomenon, not a pitch-change phenomenon, and no surveyed method uses an interval-change ratio as a texture cue.

### Cited Findings
- A power chord "consists of the root note and the fifth, as well as possibly octaves of those notes"; theorists "often call this a dyad"; "the ratio between the frequencies of the root and fifth are very close to the just interval 3:2"; with distortion "additional partials are generated at the sums and differences of the frequencies of the harmonics of those notes (intermodulation distortion)" — [Wikipedia: Power chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_chord)
- Chroma features fold spectral energy into twelve pitch classes and are robust to timbre; partials of a played note distort the chroma distribution, which is why some methods use transcription-like front ends — [Müller, Kurth, Clausen 2005 (PDF)](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.com/content/05_fau/professor/00_mueller/03_publications/2005_MuellerKurthClausen_ChromaAudioFeatures_WASPAA.pdf); a chord-detection README models the second harmonic as half the power and the third as a third of the fundamental's as a crude overtone-subtraction starting point — [chordabra README](https://github.com/High5Apps/chordabra/blob/master/README.md)
- Kehling et al. remove octave confusions "by summing up and comparing the odd and even harmonics of a note" and eliminate a candidate fundamental "when the total energy of even partials falls below a quarter of the energy of odd partials"; they track 15 partials with a per-frame inharmonicity coefficient and classify string number at 82 % — [DAFx-14](https://www.dafx14.fau.de/papers/dafx14_christian_kehling_automatic_tablature_trans.pdf)
- Foulon et al. use the YIN inharmonicity coefficient and harmonic-to-noise ratio as polyphony cues on guitar — [Foulon et al. 2013](https://www.francoispachet.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/foulon2013classification.pdf); Lachambre et al. use YIN confidence mean/variance — [IRIT](https://irit.fr/SAMOVA/site/?p=454)
- Régnier et al. include a binary "fifth interval formable from the bar's notes" feature among rhythm-guitar predictors, and treat two simultaneous notes as a chord — [Régnier et al. ISMIR 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000006.pdf)
- Pati and Lerch: "many guitar riffs rely on the use of power chords" — [Pati & Lerch 2017](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)
- Pitch-class histogram entropy is used in symbolic analysis as a proxy for tonal focus (low entropy = few pitch classes) — [Jazz Transformer, arXiv 2008.01307](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.01307); pitch-class-profile entropy over onset-based or fixed windows is used as a composer-identification feature — [Kaliakatsos-Papakostas et al. 2011](https://www.epitropakis.co.uk/sites/default/files/pubs/KaliakatsosEV2011_MCM.pdf). Neither uses it to separate monophony from polyphony.
- Weiss and Peeters add an auxiliary head predicting the local polyphony count (0–23 active pitches per frame) inside a multi-pitch U-net and find it helps the main task — [arXiv 2202.09198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.09198); a comb-filter autocorrelation method estimates polyphony with 6 % mean error over five instruments — [SCITEPRESS 2005](https://scitepress.org/Papers/2005/11750/pdf/index.html)

### Inferences
- The chroma-entropy ceiling cannot separate a palm-muted power-chord hook from a single-note riff: both are low-entropy (one or two pitch classes plus harmonic leakage onto the fifth). That is exactly the observed false positive. Conversely, a clean single-note riff played with vibrato, bends, or on a distorted stem spreads chroma and can exceed the ceiling.
- A dyad-vs-note test should look where the papers look: at the pyin voiced probability per onset (low for dyads), at inharmonicity, or at the odd/even partial-energy ratio of the candidate fundamental. These are all computable from the existing per-onset pyin pass on a CPU.
- The pitch-change share rule (> 0.26) is unsupported by any source; riffs on one or two pitches are defined by rhythm and repetition. Régnier's "pitch variety" and "interval variety" features exist but as inputs to a trained classifier for rhythm guitar, not as a riff threshold, and his data say non-rhythm bars are characterised by single notes (92 %), not by pitch variety.
- Pati's and Régnier's definitions both put power-chord riffs in the accompaniment/rhythm class; if the project wants them printed as tab it needs a separate "dyad riff" class rather than squeezing them through the single-note rule.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that measures chroma entropy for single notes vs power chords on distorted electric guitar; the DLSU chroma-evenness result (85 %) is on 22 files and controlled sources.
- No source quantifies pyin's voiced-probability distribution on dyads specifically.

## Q6. What evaluation protocol fits?

### Takeaway
Use three layers: section-level precision/recall of riff flags against ear truth with class-imbalance-aware (macro) accuracy and a minimum-segment rule, as in Pati & Lerch; note-level mir_eval transcription F with offsets ignored (onset 50 ms, pitch 50 cents), as every guitar-transcription paper does; and tab-level string/fret F1 or TDR, as TabCNN and TART do. Split by song (leave-one-piece-out) because bars repeat within a song. mir_eval's pattern module gives establishment/occurrence scores if the riff is treated as a repeated pattern.

### Cited Findings
- mir_eval transcription defaults: onset_tolerance 0.05 s, pitch_tolerance 50 cents, offset_ratio 0.2 (offset tolerance max(0.2 × duration, 50 ms)); "if offset_ratio is set to None, offsets are ignored in the matching" — [mir_eval transcription API](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/stable/api/transcription.html)
- MT3 defines Onset F1 as correct pitch within ±50 ms of a reference onset, ignoring offsets — [MT3 arXiv 2111.03017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03017); Riley et al. report GuitarSet as F50 — [arXiv 2402.15258](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.15258); Basic Pitch uses Fno (no offset) as its headline metric because offsets are "less reliable than onsets" — [arXiv 2203.09893](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09893)
- Tab-level metrics: tablature F1 and tablature disambiguation rate (TDR, the share of correctly pitched notes assigned the right string) — [arXiv 2405.14679](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14679v3); TART reports audio-to-MIDI F50, string-fret Tab F1 and end-to-end Tab F1 separately — [arXiv 2609.11904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11904)
- Pati and Lerch evaluate section flags with micro- and macro-accuracy, precision, recall, F and specificity, note the majority-class baseline is 74.7 % so macro-accuracy is the better indicator, and apply erosion/dilation plus a minimum contiguous solo length k = 4 s before scoring — [Pati & Lerch 2017](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)
- Régnier et al. choose F1 because the dataset is unbalanced, discuss precision-vs-recall trade-offs by use case, and use leave-one-piece-out because bars repeat — [Régnier et al. ISMIR 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000006.pdf)
- mir_eval pattern metrics: standard P/R/F, establishment, occurrence at thresholds, and three-layer F — [mir_eval pattern API](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/stable/api/pattern.html); MIREX tables report Pest/Rest/Fest, occurrence at 0.75 and 0.5, and F3 — [MIREX 2014 Nieto-Farbood](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/MIREX2014-NietoFarbood.pdf)
- GuitarSet provides per-string midi_note annotations and both instructed and performed chord labels, enabling note-level and chord-level truth on the same audio — [GuitarSet](https://guitarset.weebly.com/); the Pati & Lerch GSD dataset (solo segment annotations on 40+ rock songs) is public on GitHub — [GuitarSoloDetection dataset](https://github.com/ashispati/GuitarSoloDetection/tree/master/Dataset)

### Inferences
- For the project's eleven-song ear-verified set, report riff-flag precision/recall per section with macro-accuracy and a minimum segment length, and state the majority-class baseline, exactly as Pati does; this makes "only one riff passed the gate" a recall number rather than an anecdote.
- Use `offset_ratio=None` in mir_eval for the note layer; guitar offsets on a stem are unreliable and every cited guitar paper drops them.
- GuitarSet's comp/solo halves give a free, labelled mono-vs-poly texture test for the chroma-entropy and single-note-share rules before touching the project's songs; the Basic Pitch paper already splits its GuitarSet results that way.
- Score the tab gate with TDR-style string/fret accuracy on the ukulele mapping only after the note layer passes, so the three gates can be diagnosed separately.

### Gaps
- No standard protocol exists for "riff section" detection; the closest are solo detection (Pati) and symbolic function labelling (Régnier). Thresholds like 0.70/0.75/0.6 have no published counterpart.
- No public dataset labels riff sections in audio; GSD labels solos only.
