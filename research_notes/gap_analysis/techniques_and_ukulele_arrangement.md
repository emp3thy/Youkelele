# Playing-technique detection and ukulele arrangement / difficulty grading (audio-to-ukulele-tab pipeline)

Compiled 2026-10-01. Scope: (1) detecting and notating guitar/ukulele techniques from audio or transcribed notes; (2) turning a transcribed band arrangement into a playable ukulele part (chord-melody, score reduction, per-section mode selection, difficulty grading, chord substitution). Method note: web search quota was exhausted early in this session; findings below come from direct fetches of primary sources (arXiv API, Crossref API, publisher pages, project READMEs, pedagogy pages) plus local text extraction of downloaded PDFs. Where a source could not be reached it is listed under Gaps rather than paraphrased from memory.

## Key Question 1: Technique detection — datasets, models, what is realistically detectable, symbolic heuristics

### Takeaway
Guitar technique detection is a mature-but-narrow research line: pitch-contour pattern mining (bend = arc, vibrato = sinusoid, slide = staircase, hammer-on/pull-off = adjacent pitch step with no new attack) followed by a timbre+pitch classifier reaches F-scores of roughly 0.6-0.9 for bend/vibrato/pull-off, 0.6-0.7 for hammer-on and 0.4-0.6 for slide on clean monophonic solo audio; performance drops in mixtures. As of 2025-2026 the open end-to-end tools (TART, tabforge) implement exactly this candidate-then-classify design; the largest technique-labelled datasets (GOAT, Guitar-TECHS, IDMT-SMT-Guitar, Mitsou et al. 2024) are electric-guitar, not ukulele, and none of them covers the ukulele-specific "chunk" strum.

### Cited Findings

**Datasets with technique labels**
- IDMT-SMT-Guitar (Kehling, Männchen, Eppler; Fraunhofer IDMT; DAFx 2014): subset 1 has about 4,700 note events from three guitars at 24-bit covering plucking styles finger-style/muted/picked and expression styles normal/bending/slide/vibrato/harmonics/dead-notes; subset 2 = 400 mono+poly events; subset 3 = 5 short recordings; subset 4 = 64 short pieces at two tempi for chord/rhythm tasks; 44.1 kHz mono WAV; XML annotations for subsets 1-3; licence CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; Zenodo record 7544110 — [Fraunhofer IDMT dataset page](https://www.idmt.fraunhofer.de/en/publications/datasets/guitar.html)
- Kehling et al.'s own classifier on that data (RBF-SVM over whole-note-event features) reached "83% average accuracy" over six classes normal/bend/slide/vibrato/harmonics/dead-notes, with recall only 50.9% (slide), 66.7% (vibrato), 71.3% (bend) — as reported in [Chen, Su, Yang, ISMIR 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000119.pdf)
- GuitarSet (Xi, Bittner et al., ISMIR 2018): 360 excerpts of about 30 s, 6 players x 30 lead sheets x comping/soloing, hexaphonic pickup; JAMS annotations include per-string pitch contours and MIDI notes, beats/tempo, instructed and performed chords, keys; the site does not list playing-technique annotations — [GuitarSet site](https://guitarset.weebly.com/)
- EGDB (Chen et al., "Towards Automatic Transcription of Polyphonic Electric Guitar Music: A New Dataset and a Multi-Loss Transformer Model", ICASSP 2022, DOI 10.1109/icassp43922.2022.9747697): 240 electric-guitar tablatures rendered through DI, Marshall, Fender-twin and Mesa tones; Google-Drive download; no technique annotation stated on the demo page — [EGDB page](https://ss12f32v.github.io/Guitar-Transcription/); [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works?query=EGDB+electric+guitar+dataset+transcription+amplifier+rendered+Chen+Yang+ICASSP+2022&rows=5&select=title,author,published-print,container-title,DOI)
- Guitar-TECHS (Pedroza, Abreu, Corey, Roman; ICASSP 2025; arXiv 2501.03720): six techniques — alternate picking, palm mute, vibrato, harmonics, pinch harmonics, bendings; 5 h 12 min total; 1,860 technique recordings per player for players 01-02 plus 12 excerpts from player 03; egocentric + exocentric mics, DI and amp; MIDI at 9,600 ticks/quarter; 48 kHz 32-bit float; CC BY 4.0; guitar-techs.github.io — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03720v1)
- GOAT (Loth, Sarmento, Sarkar, Guo, Barthet, Sandler; arXiv 2509.22655, posted 2025-07): 5.9 h unique DI audio (5.17 h standard tuning + 43.85 min non-standard) plus 29.5 h amp-rendered variants; 172 audio files; 109,869 notes; 13,538 chords; techniques encoded via DadaGP tokens, most prevalent bends, palm mutes, legatos, slides, with tapping and vibrato "much smaller representation"; available on request via Zenodo, CC BY 4.0 but research-only because of cover-song copyright — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22655v1)
- Mitsou et al., "A multimodal dataset for electric guitar playing technique recognition", Data in Brief 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.dib.2023.109842 — exists (Crossref), but the ScienceDirect page returned HTTP 403 so class list and counts could not be verified — [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works?query=multimodal+dataset+electric+guitar+playing+technique+recognition+Data+in+Brief&rows=4&select=title,author,published-print,container-title,DOI,URL,abstract)
- Chen/Su/Yang's "G&N" solo dataset: 42 unaccompanied electric guitar solos, 10-25 notes each; 1,113 note events, 19.5 minutes; the ISMIR-2015 counts were 143 pull-offs, 70 hammer-ons, 143 bends, 74 slides, 61 vibratos — [Chen, Su, Yang ISMIR 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000119.pdf); [TENT, TISMIR 2019](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.23)
- Wang (McGill MA thesis, 2022) built 379 synthesized guitar-solo recordings (rock/metal) from 147 Guitar Pro files collected from Ultimate Guitar (100 dev / 47 test), with ground truth decoded programmatically from the GP files — [Wang 2022 thesis PDF](https://mcgill.scholaris.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/2dc4757b-994e-46b9-9bbf-ba1d9161c98f/content)
- DadaGP (Sarmento et al., ISMIR 2021): 26,181 GuitarPro songs, 739 genres, 116 M tokens, >1,200 h; technique notations are "note effect tokens (nfx)" — most frequent palm mute, bends, vibratos, slides, and hammer-ons/pull-offs "both under nfx:hammer"; effect tokens apply to the preceding `instrument:note:string:fret` token; 960 ticks/quarter; encoder/decoder requires PyGuitarPro 0.6 exactly; MIT code, dataset by request — [DadaGP paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000076.pdf); [DadaGP GitHub](https://github.com/dada-bots/dadaGP)

**Models and reported accuracy (audio)**
- Su, Lin, Yang ISMIR 2014 ("Sparse cepstral and phase codes"): sparse coding of log-cepstrum, group-delay function and instantaneous-frequency deviation gives F-score 71.7% on single-note technique classification; cepstral and phase features matter most for separating pull-off, hammer-on and bend — [ISMIR 2014 paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000213.pdf)
- Chen, Su, Yang ISMIR 2015: two-stage framework; stage 1 "candidate selection" mines the melody (F0) contour for technique-specific patterns — "Bend: arc-like or twisted trajectories. Vibrato: sinusoidal patterns. Slide: upward or downward stair-like patterns. Hammer-on, pull-off: two adjacent parallel horizontal lines resulting from two notes of different F0s" — after median filtering, note tracking, mean filtering and quantisation; four local pitch extrema define a vibrato window; stage 2 is an RBF-SVM on MFCC + timbre + pitch features. Results on the 42 unaccompanied solos (CS+SVM): F-scores bend 87.7, vibrato 84.0, pull-off 74.4, hammer-on 66.3, slide 57.7 (recall 86.2/79.5/73.6/65.7/58.6; precision 89.3/89.1/75.3/66.7/56.8); candidate selection alone has recall about 94% but precision 15-63%. Hammer-on/pull-off scored correct if detected instant within 50 ms of ground truth. Source separation is an optional pre-step for mixtures; on five studio mixtures the authors advise using candidate selection only for bend detection — [Chen, Su, Yang ISMIR 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000119.pdf)
- TENT (Su, Chen, Su, Yang; TISMIR 2(1), 2019): technique-embedded note tracking; techniques Bend, Release, Vibrato, Hammer-on, Pull-off, Slide (incl. slide-in/out) + Normal; contour segmented into sub-melodies, local extrema give trend labels; CNN over MFCC(39) / log-mel(128) / normalised pitch contour + derivatives; best feature set MFCC+PC F=0.797 overall; per-class F: Normal 0.880, Bend/Release 0.767, Pull-off 0.730, Hammer-on 0.681, Slide 0.388; note-tracking F improved 14.7% because detected techniques stop vibrato/bend notes being split into several notes — [TENT](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.23)
- Wang 2022 (McGill): pipeline = monophony detector -> note-event separator -> technique classifier (RBF-SVM on 486-dim vector: MFCC, spectral centroid, F0 sequence with first/second deltas, aggregated by mean/std/max/min/skew/kurtosis). Per-class F1 on synthesized solos: bend 89.2, vibrato 81.7, hammer-on 85.0, pull-off 84.3, slide 71.7 (vs TENT's 76.7 / - / 68.1 / 73.0 / 38.8) — [Wang 2022 thesis](https://mcgill.scholaris.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/2dc4757b-994e-46b9-9bbf-ba1d9161c98f/content)
- TART (Gupta et al., UC Berkeley; arXiv 2510.02597, Oct 2025; revised as arXiv 2609.11904, Sept 2026): four stages — piano-transcription model adapted to guitar for audio-to-MIDI, MLP technique classifier, Transformer (audio-conditioned T5 in 2026 version) string/fret assignment, LSTM tab generator. The MLP classifies nine techniques + other: picking, sweep picking, alternate picking, hammer-on/pull-off/legato, slides, bends, vibrato, palm mute, harmonics; input is a 180-dim vector per onset (MFCC + BFCC + mel-spectrogram + chroma); training unified Magcil (~500 recordings), AGPT (10+ h) and IDMT-SMT-Chords (7,398 segments, 4.1 h) with noise/shift/pitch augmentation; 76% overall accuracy, with bends, slides and vibrato each under 100 training samples and performing much worse; techniques are "encoded symbolically and appended to fret numbers" in ASCII tab. 2026 version: audio-to-MIDI F50 81.35%, string-fret Tab F1 71.8%, end-to-end Tab F1 54.08% on GuitarSet, EGDB and noisy variants — [TART 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02597v1); [TART 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11904)
- FretNet (Cwitkowitz, Hirvonen, Klapuri; arXiv 2212.03023, 2022) estimates continuous-valued per-string pitch contours for tablature transcription specifically so that pitch-modulating techniques (bends, slides, vibrato) are representable; tab results "competitive with baseline models" — [FretNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03023)
- Percussive (body) technique recognition for acoustic guitar: Martelloni, McPherson, Barthet (arXiv 2307.07426, 2023) use causal CNN/VAE for real-time recognition with a taxonomy by hand part and location — [arXiv 2307.07426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07426)
- Related plucked-string work: frame-level multi-label technique detection for Guzheng with multi-scale network + self-attention (Li et al., arXiv 2303.13272, 2023) and FCN-based detection for Erhu with 87.31% accuracy (Wang et al., arXiv 1910.09021, 2019) — [arXiv API listing](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22playing+technique%22+AND+%28abs:guitar+OR+abs:string+OR+abs:plucked%29+AND+%28abs:detection+OR+abs:recognition+OR+abs:classification%29&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending&max_results=30)
- Older: Reboursière et al., "Left and right-hand guitar playing techniques detection", NIME 2012 (title only verified) — [NIME 2012](https://www.nime.org/proceedings/2012/nime2012_213.pdf)

**Symbolic / tablature-side technique prediction**
- D'Hooge, Bigo, Déguernel, "Modeling Bends in Popular Music Guitar Tablatures" (arXiv 2308.12307, 2023): 25 high-level features per note computed from tab context; decision tree predicts where a bend should occur on 932 lead-guitar tabs with F1 0.71 and few false positives; proposed use is "to assist the arrangement of non-guitar music into guitar tablatures" — [arXiv 2308.12307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12307)
- D'Hooge's thesis ("Assisting Western Popular Music Guitar Practice and Tablature Composition with Machine Learning", DOI 10.70675/06264cdbzc47dz475fz85b7z58f1271ca7ef) covers automated tab difficulty estimation, bend suggestion and chord-position recommendation — [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works?query=Personalized+Difficulty+Level+Classification+and+Feature+Analysis+for+Guitar+Tablature&rows=3&select=title,author,published-print,container-title,DOI,abstract)
- tabforge (jqcortes, PR #2 "TabForge P2-P4", 2025-2026): bends/vibrato "inferred from bend curves", hammer-on/slide via a "two-pass legato-aware Viterbi constraint", dead notes via technique inference, written to GP5 through PyGuitarPro and verified to round-trip "with hammer-on and bend effects intact" — [tabforge PR #2](https://github.com/jqcortes/RockSmithTabConvertToMidi_GuitarPro/pull/2)

**Pitch trackers for contour-based heuristics (Python, CPU, Windows-friendly)**
- SwiftF0: 14,386-parameter CNN pitch tracker, 180x real time on one laptop CPU core, 46.875-2093.75 Hz (about F#1-C7), 16 ms hop, outputs pitch Hz + confidence (voiced >= 0.5) + RMS dB, `detect()`, `detect_file()`, `stream()`, and `segment_notes()` which groups the contour into notes with configurable holding penalties; MIT; top pitch-F1 among 19 trackers in its benchmark, tied with RMVPE and above CREPE — [SwiftF0 GitHub](https://github.com/lars76/swift-f0)
- CREPE: tiny/small/medium/large/full capacities, default 10 ms step, returns time/frequency/confidence/activation, 360 bins at 20-cent resolution, optional Viterbi smoothing, auto-resamples to 16 kHz, `pip install crepe`, MIT — [CREPE GitHub](https://github.com/marl/crepe)

**Ukulele-specific techniques (pedagogy)**
- Chunk strum (aka chop, chuck, mute, chick, chock): on the down-stroke the strumming finger pops out and "strikes the strings earlier than the rest of the hand", then the palm presses onto the strings so they do not ring; produces "a popping, snapping, rasping sound ... reminiscent of a snare drum hit", unpitched; used inside patterns such as "D chunk U"; multiple fingers give a bigger sound (Brad Bordessa, updated 2026-02-11) — [Live Ukulele chunk strum](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/strums/chunk-strum/)
- Muting on ukulele: (1) strum-hand palm mute after the strum, (2) "lift muting" by releasing fretting pressure (best for barre chords), (3) pinky mute with the fretting hand; muting controls the note/rest ratio (Bordessa, 2009) — [Live Ukulele muting](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/muting-chords-and-notes/)
- Tremolo picking on ukulele: anchored-finger rocking motion with the index nail, or an advanced m-i-thumb roll; notated as rapid repeated note symbols (beamed/slashed); common in classical uke arrangements (Bordessa, 2026-03-24) — [Live Ukulele tremolo](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/techniques/tremolo-picking/)
- Live Ukulele strum notation: D = down, U = up, parentheses = ghost strum (hand keeps moving, strings missed), e.g. `D(U)D U(D)U D(U)`; Live Ukulele's strum and lesson indexes contain no dedicated triplet-strum, roll-strum or fan-strum lesson — [Live Ukulele strums](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/strums/); [Live Ukulele lessons index](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/)

### Inferences
- For a separated ukulele/guitar stem the realistic 2026 detectable set is: bend (best), vibrato, pull-off, hammer-on, slide (weakest, often confused with legato), palm mute and dead/muted notes (timbre, well represented in GOAT/IDMT), harmonics (IDMT, Guitar-TECHS). Published F-scores of 0.58-0.89 are on clean monophonic electric solos; expect lower numbers on nylon-string ukulele after source separation (Chen et al. explicitly saw degradation in mixtures and recommend contour-only bend detection there).
- A pure-symbolic heuristic from a SwiftF0/CREPE contour is sufficient for a first implementation and matches the published candidate-selection stage: (a) adjacent pitch plateaus on one string with no onset-energy spike between them -> hammer-on (ascending) / pull-off (descending); (b) monotonic staircase of >= 2 semitone steps over tens of ms -> slide; (c) smooth arc rising <= 2 semitones and holding or returning -> bend/release; (d) periodic +/- 20-100 cent oscillation at about 4-8 Hz around a plateau -> vibrato. SwiftF0's `segment_notes()` already yields the plateaus. Precision will be low without a second-stage classifier (CS-only precision was 15-63% in Chen et al.).
- The ukulele "chunk" is not in any surveyed dataset; it is an unpitched broadband percussive strum and is best treated as a drum-like onset class (strong transient, low pitch confidence, short decay) rather than a note-level technique. The acoustic-guitar body-percussion work (Martelloni et al.) is the nearest methodological precedent.
- Ukulele tremolo and roll strums manifest as rapid repeated onsets (>~8/s) on the same pitch set; they can be inferred from onset density rather than timbre.

- No paper or dataset found that detects ukulele techniques specifically; all detection literature is electric or acoustic guitar.

- "SoloDB" and an "expressive guitar technique transformer" could not be located via arXiv/Crossref; may not exist under those names.

## Key Question 2: Encoding techniques in tab formats (alphaTex, Guitar Pro via PyGuitarPro, MusicXML, LilyPond, ASCII)

### Takeaway
Every target format has a direct field for the core technique set; the mapping is mechanical once a technique label sits on a note. ASCII conventions are the least standardised, so emit a legend. Ukulele ASCII tab is conventionally written with the A string on top (A-E-C-G).

### Cited Findings
- alphaTex note properties (alphaTab docs): `h` hammer-on/pull-off (direction decided by the next note's fret on the same string), slides `sl` legato, `ss` shift, `sib`/`sia` slide-in from below/above, `sou`/`sod` slide-out up/down, pick slides `psu`/`psd`, vibrato `v` and wide `vw`, bends `b (values)` or `be (valueAndOffset)` with types bend/release/bendRelease/hold/prebend/prebendBend/prebendRelease/custom and styles default/gradual/fast, e.g. `3.3{b (0 4)}`, harmonics `nh`, `ah value`, `ph value`, `th value`, `sh value`, `fh value`, dead note `x.3` or `3.3{x}`, `lr` let ring, `pm` palm mute, `st` staccato, `tr fret [16|32|64]` trill, `g` ghost, tie `-` or `t`, accents `ac`/`hac`/`ten`, `lht` left-hand tap, fingering `lf n`/`rf n` (1 thumb .. 5 pinky), ornaments turn/iturn/umordent/lmordent, `slur id` — [alphaTex note properties](https://alphatab.net/docs/alphatex/note-properties)
- PyGuitarPro (0.11 docs): `NoteEffect` fields accentuatedNote, bend (`BendEffect`), ghostNote, grace (`GraceEffect`), hammer (bool), harmonic (`HarmonicEffect`), heavyAccentuatedNote, leftHandFinger/rightHandFinger (`Fingering`), letRing, palmMute, slides (list of `SlideType`), staccato, tremoloPicking (`TremoloPickingEffect(duration)`), trill (`TrillEffect`), vibrato. `BeatEffect` fields: stroke (`BeatStroke(direction, value)`), hasRasgueado, pickStroke (`BeatStrokeDirection`), chord, fadeIn, tremoloBar (`BendEffect`), mixTableChange, slapEffect (none/tapping/slapping/popping), vibrato. `BendEffect(type, value, points)` with `BendType` none/bend/bendRelease/bendReleaseBend/prebend/prebendRelease/dip/dive/releaseUp/invertedDip/return_/releaseDown. Harmonic classes NaturalHarmonic, ArtificialHarmonic(pitch, octave), TappedHarmonic(fret), PinchHarmonic, SemiHarmonic. `NoteType` rest/normal/tied/dead — [PyGuitarPro API](https://pyguitarpro.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pyguitarpro/api.html)
- OpenUkulele writes GP5 "with GM ukulele timbre" via PyGuitarPro, and tabforge round-trips hammer-on and bend effects through PyGuitarPro — [OpenUkulele README](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele); [tabforge PR #2](https://github.com/jqcortes/RockSmithTabConvertToMidi_GuitarPro/pull/2)
- MusicXML 4.0 `<technical>` children include `<hammer-on>`, `<pull-off>`, `<bend>` (with bend-alter, pre-bend, release, with-bar), `<harmonic>` (natural/artificial with base/touching/sounding pitch), `<fret>`, `<string>`, `<tap>`, `<fingering>`, `<pluck>`, `<open-string>`, `<thumb-position>`, `<snap-pizzicato>`, `<golpe>`, `<other-technical>`, plus wind/brass/bowed items — [MusicXML technical](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/technical/)
- MusicXML `<slide>` lives under `<notations>` (not `<technical>`), `type` start/stop required, optional `line-type` solid/dashed/dotted/wavy and `number`; it is a continuous pitch slide as opposed to `<glissando>`; vibrato is `<wavy-line>`, tremolo is `<ornaments><tremolo>`, muting is `<play><mute>` — [MusicXML slide](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/slide/)
- LilyPond 2.24 fretted-string notation: `\new TabStaff`, string numbers `c4\5`, `\set TabStaff.stringTunings = \stringTuning <...>` with predefined `ukulele-tuning`, hammer-on/pull-off written as slurs `d4( e\2)`, slides `c'8\3\glissando d'8\3`, bends with `\^` (`f'4\^ g'4\^ f'2`, `\preBend`, `\bendHold`), harmonics `\harmonic`, `\harmonicByFret #5`, `\harmonicByRatio #1/2`; dead notes and palm mute exist but their syntax was not in the fetched excerpt — [LilyPond fretted strings](https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.24/Documentation/notation/common-notation-for-fretted-strings)
- ASCII tab legend (Wikipedia): `h` hammer-on, `p` pull-off, `b` bend, `r` release, `/` slide up, `\` slide down, `s` legato slide, `v` or `~` vibrato, `*` natural harmonic, `[n]` artificial harmonic, `n(n)` tapped harmonic, `t`/`T` tap, `tr` trill, `TP` tremolo picking, `PM` palm mute (also `_` or `.`), `x` muted slash, `N.C.` no chord; line order "is not standardized" so writers label strings — [Wikipedia: Tablature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablature)
- Ukulele ASCII tab per Live Ukulele: lines top-to-bottom A-E-C-G; `h` hammer-on, `p` pull-off, `/` and `\` slides, `b` bend "so that it equals the pitch of the second note shown", `r` release, `~` vibrato, `()` ghost note (play very softly), `<n>` natural harmonic in angle brackets; the guide does not define x/chunk/strum arrows/tremolo/let-ring (Bordessa, 2026-02-11) — [Live Ukulele how to read tab](https://liveukulele.com/tabs/how-to-read-tab/)
- OpenUkulele ASCII format: four-line tab with tempo, tuning and measure lines, files `{stem}-easy.txt` / `{stem}-hard.txt`, plus GP5 and a JSON report — [OpenUkulele README](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele)

### Inferences
- A single internal technique enum {hammer, pull, slide_legato, slide_shift, slide_in, slide_out, bend(value, type), vibrato, palm_mute, dead, natural_harmonic, let_ring, tremolo, chunk} maps one-to-one onto alphaTex and PyGuitarPro; chunk has no native field anywhere and should be emitted as a dead-note strum across all strings (`x` on each string, GP5 `NoteType.dead` + beat stroke) with a text annotation.
- alphaTex and GP5 both need the technique on the *first* note of a legato pair (alphaTex `h` decides direction from the next note); MusicXML needs explicit start/stop pairs for slides.

### Gaps
- LilyPond syntax for `\deadNote` and `\palmMute` was not captured from the fetched page.
- No authoritative ukulele-community ASCII convention for strum direction or chunk was found (Live Ukulele leaves them undefined).

## Key Question 3: Arrangement — ukulele range, octave placement, chord-melody principles, automatic arrangement and score-reduction research

### Takeaway
The ukulele's usable range is small (re-entrant soprano C4-A5, low-G extends to G3, tenor to about D6), so band material must be collapsed to melody-on-top plus 1-2 chord tones below, with bass only on strong beats; this is exactly the rule set used by ukulele pedagogues (Bordessa, McQueen) and the one hard-coded in OpenUkulele. The academic lineage for automating it is HMM/Viterbi fingering with note-dropping (Hori, Kameoka, Sagayama 2013; Song2Guitar 2017) and, for score reduction with difficulty control, Nakamura & Yoshii 2018 and Terao, Nakamura, Yoshii ICASSP 2023 (easier score = subset of harder score, note-importance masks warped by a difficulty parameter).

### Cited Findings

**Range and tuning**
- Standard re-entrant C6 tuning G4-C4-E4-A4; low-G G3-C4-E4-A4 (equals a guitar's top four strings capo 5); D6 tuning A4-D4-F#4-B4; baritone D3-G3-B3-E4. Sizes: soprano 13 in scale, 12-15 frets, range C4-A5; concert 15 in, 15-18 frets, C4-C6; tenor 17 in, 17-19 frets, G3-D6; baritone 19 in, 18-21 frets, D3-A#5 — [Wikipedia: Ukulele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukulele)
- Low-G is G3 = 196 Hz vs high-G G4 = 392 Hz; low-G "extends the range of the ukulele by five extra notes" downward; high-G gives tighter, "piano-like note clusters" suited to campanella and traditional strumming; low-G suits solo fingerpicking arrangements; switching requires re-fingering single-note lines (Bordessa, 2024-01-05) — [Live Ukulele low-G](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/strings/low-g/)
- OpenUkulele models "high-G re-entrant tuning G4-C4-E4-A4" on a 15-fret four-string instrument — [OpenUkulele README](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele)

**Chord-melody arrangement principles (pedagogy)**
- Bordessa (2026-02-06): "the melody always should be higher than the harmony"; put melody on the A or E string so at least two harmony notes fit below; choose the key that places most melody notes there, "sometimes the obvious key isn't the best"; pick chord tones of the prevailing harmony below the melody; find alternative voicings by shifting each string's note "up to the next zone"; put harmony on beats 1 and 3 and keep other beats melody-only; always sound a chord at harmony changes "even if replacing rests, since the melody doesn't tell our ears the harmony"; prefer low-G for richer low voicings; with high-G, roll chords so the melody sounds last — [Live Ukulele solo arrangements](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/solo-fingerpicking-arrangements/)
- McQueen (Ukulele Tricks): four building blocks melody, harmony, rhythm, dynamics; "The melody note is almost always placed as the highest note in the chord", i.e. on the E and A strings; needs triads in root/1st/2nd inversion; strum chords between melody notes and accent chords containing melody notes — [Ukulele Tricks chord melody](https://ukuleletricks.com/arrange-chord-melody-songs/)
- Slash chords on ukulele: bass note should be lowest; "it gets tricky to do slash chords right if you play with a high G string"; with four strings, one chord tone is usually dropped to fit the bass; many slash chords are respellings of another chord (Bordessa, 2009) — [Live Ukulele slash chords](https://liveukulele.com/chords/ukulele-slash-chords/)

**Automatic arrangement systems**
- Hori, Kameoka, Sagayama, "Input-Output HMM Applied to Automatic Arrangement for Guitars", J. Information Processing 21(2), 2013, DOI 10.2197/ipsjjip.21.264: arrangement = HMM decoding where hidden states are left-hand forms on the fingerboard and observations are the input score's notes; Viterbi output gives beginner-friendly fingerings/arrangements; HMM parameters tuned by hand to reflect beginner preferences — [J-STAGE record](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ipsjjip/21/2/21_264/_article/-char/en)
- Song2Guitar (Ariga, Fukayama, Goto; ISMIR 2017): generates difficulty-aware solo-guitar covers from polyphonic audio using MIR-extracted beats, chords and melody; fingering state X_t has four components (string, fret) per left-hand finger, first-order Markov transitions model short-time hand-form changes and avoid long position jumps; difficulty study: in 50 classical-guitar tabs rated by five guitarists on a 7-point scale, average index-finger movement (barre fret if barre, else min fret) correlated r = 0.55 (R^2 0.30) and average number of pressing fingers r = 0.51 (R^2 0.26) with rated difficulty; the system restricts allowed hand forms adaptively to hit a target difficulty; user test showed a guitarist could play the generated covers at different difficulties — [Song2Guitar PDF](https://staff.aist.go.jp/m.goto/PAPER/ISMIR2017ariga.pdf)
- Nakamura & Yoshii, "Statistical piano reduction controlling performance difficulty", APSIPA TSIP 2018 (arXiv 1808.05006): reduction as "optimization of musical fidelity under constraints on difficulty values"; difficulty measures come from probabilistic generative models of piano scores and relate to performance-error rate; prior model of piano scores + model of how ensemble scores are edited; iterative optimisation — [arXiv 1808.05006](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05006); [Crossref DOI 10.1017/atsip.2018.18](https://api.crossref.org/works?query=Nakamura+Sagayama+piano+reduction+performance+difficulty+ensemble+scores&rows=6&select=title,author,published-print,container-title,DOI,URL)
- Terao, Nakamura, Yoshii, "Neural Band-to-Piano Score Arrangement with Stepless Difficulty Control", ICASSP 2023: augment the band score by shifting all notes +/- one octave, then a difficulty-agnostic U-Net estimates each note's importance, warped by a power function of the difficulty level gamma; assumes an easier score is a subset of a harder one; statistic-level losses on note density and polyphony; trained on 184 band/piano pairs (85 elementary, 99 advanced), 138 train / 46 test, 12-measure windows; cites earlier work on guitar arrangement [6-8] and audio-based piano reduction (Takamori et al. 2019; Wang et al. audio-to-symbolic arrangement) — [Terao ICASSP 2023 PDF](https://eita-nakamura.github.io/articles/Terao_BandToPianoScoreArrangement_ICASSP2023.pdf)
- OpenUkulele (xin-pu, 2025-2026): pipeline input (MIDI/audio -> NoteEvent) -> timing quantisation -> arrangement ("difficulty retention strategy") -> fingering (candidates + per-measure dynamic programming) -> independent validation -> per-measure fallback -> render + report. Easy: frets 0-5, at most 2 simultaneous notes, span <= 4 frets, no barre, eighth-note grid, notes = melody + bass on beats 1/3. Hard: frets 0-15, up to 4 notes per beat, span <= 5 frets, same-fret adjacent-string barre allowed, sixteenth grid when source has it, melody + bass + up to 2 harmony notes. Validation checks same-string conflicts, pitch range, left-hand span, barre feasibility, finger allocation; degradation order "remove harmony -> remove bass -> melody only", and unplayable sections are excluded rather than emitted; uses mido + PyGuitarPro, Basic Pitch + onnxruntime for audio, matplotlib for sheets; has `--transpose` (uniform semitone shift) and audio melody filtering PRs — [OpenUkulele README](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele); [PR #4 transpose](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele/pull/4); [PR #6 melody filtering](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele/pull/6)
- MIDI-to-tab fingering models usable for the ukulele fretting step: MIDI-to-Tab (Edwards, Riley, Sarmento, Dixon; arXiv 2408.05024, 2024) encoder-decoder Transformer with masked LM pre-training on DadaGP's 25k+ tabs, minimises hand-position movement, guitarists rated it more playable than competing algorithms; Fretting-Transformer (Hamberger, Murgul, Schmidt, Heizmann; arXiv 2506.14223, 2025) T5 with `CAPO<<#>>` and `TUNING<<#,#,#,#,#,#>>` conditioning tokens, trained with four tunings and capo up to 7; Kaliakatsos-Papakostas et al. (arXiv 2510.10619, 2025) ML MIDI-to-tab preserving "parsimonious motion between successive combinations" — [arXiv API listing](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=all:%22tablature%22+AND+%28all:%22difficulty%22+OR+all:%22playability%22%29&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending&max_results=25); [Fretting-Transformer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14223v1)
- AnimeTAB (Zhou, Ju, Xie; arXiv 2210.03027, 2022): 412 fingerstyle guitar MusicXML tracks + 547 clips with structure labels, and a TABprocessor toolkit for rule-based melody extraction, key detection and chord labelling — [arXiv API listing](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=%28ti:guitar+AND+abs:technique%29+OR+ti:%22Guitar-TECHS%22+OR+ti:EGDB+OR+abs:%22guitar+playing+techniques%22&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending&max_results=40)

### Inferences
- Octave placement rule derivable from the cited ranges: transpose the vocal melody by whole octaves (and optionally +/- a few semitones with a matching chord transposition) until its span fits C4-A5 (high-G soprano) or G3-C6 (low-G tenor), preferring the octave that keeps most notes on the E/A strings (E4-A5 region) per Bordessa/McQueen. Male vocals (typically C3-C5) will usually need +12; female vocals often fit as-is.
- Terao et al.'s "augment by +/- octave then mask notes by importance warped by difficulty" is directly transferable to ukulele: candidate note set = melody + chord tones + bass (each at +/- 12), importance = melody > bass-on-strong-beat > chord 3rd/7th > root duplicate > 5th, and the difficulty knob controls how many survive, subject to OpenUkulele-style hard constraints (<= 4 strings, span, barre).
- Hori-style Viterbi over hand forms (cost = index-finger movement + number of fretting fingers, the two features Song2Guitar validated) is the practical fingering engine; OpenUkulele already does per-measure DP on a 4-string model.

- No ukulele-specific automatic chord-melody arrangement paper was found; everything is guitar or piano.
- Could not locate any Sony/2023-2025 "guitar arrangement from piano" transformer paper, nor "LooPy", "SymPAC" or "Arranger" projects via arXiv/Crossref/Semantic Scholar (the latter rate-limited); treat those names as unverified.

## Key Question 4: Per-section mode selection (strummed accompaniment vs riff vs melody)

### Takeaway
No published work decides "strum vs riff vs melody" for an arrangement; the closest tools are lead-instrument / guitar-solo detectors (Pati & Lerch 2017; Ou, Takahashi, Wang ICASSP 2025) and tabforge's rule-based lead/rhythm split with HMM smoothing. A per-section classifier can be built from stem-level features (vocal activity, guitar predominant-pitch salience, note density, chord-change rate, section label).

### Cited Findings
- Pati & Lerch, "A Dataset and Method for Electric Guitar Solo Detection in Rock Music", AES Semantic Audio 2017: 60 rock songs with solo start/duration annotations (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0); SVM over baseline spectral/temporal features plus predominant-pitch features and structural-segmentation features; best config (all features + post-processing, k = 4 s) macro-accuracy 78.6%, solo precision 63.3%, micro-accuracy 82.6%; post-processing cut false positives (precision +~5 points); confusion matrix non-solo 13216/2228, solo 1410/4001 — [Pati & Lerch PDF](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/ashispati/GuitarSoloDetection)
- Ou, Takahashi (Yamaha), Wang, "Lead Instrument Detection from Multitrack Music", ICASSP 2025 (arXiv 2503.03232): detects the lead instrument per frame from multitrack audio using MERT (m-a-p/MERT-v1-95M) embeddings with track-wise frame-level attention, track classification and permutation augmentation; datasets MedleyDB v1 with new lead annotations (Adobe Audition markers) and an "MJN" set; metrics frame-level accuracy and macro-F1 averaged over 5-s samples; Table I reports track-attention variants with Inst F1 ~83-84 and accuracy ~87-91 on the balanced test set; outperforms SVM and CRNN baselines and generalises to unseen instruments; MIT code and four Hugging Face checkpoints (MedleyDB / MJN / both / segment-level guitar) — [arXiv 2503.03232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03232); [GitHub](https://github.com/Sonata165/LeadInstrumentDetection)
- tabforge P3: "rule-based lead/rhythm scoring with 2-state HMM smoothing and region post-processing" splits a guitar transcription into Lead Guitar and Rhythm Guitar tracks, with "chord-voicing selection and strum generation" for the rhythm part; chord recognition from manual JSON, lazy madmom fallback or an LVCR placeholder — [tabforge PR #2](https://github.com/jqcortes/RockSmithTabConvertToMidi_GuitarPro/pull/2)
- OpenUkulele's easy mode always keeps the melody and adds bass only on beats 1 and 3, i.e. it is a melody-centred arrangement rather than a strummed accompaniment — [OpenUkulele README](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele)
- Vélez Vásquez et al. (ISMIR 2023) note that chord-based playability scoring works from "chord annotations and timings" alone, which are exactly the inputs of a strummed-accompaniment section — [ISMIR 2023 playability](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2023/paper/000086.pdf)

### Inferences
- A workable heuristic per structural section (from a structure segmenter): if vocal stem is active for most of the section -> strummed/fingerpicked chords with vocal melody optional; if vocals absent and the guitar/keys stem has a salient monophonic predominant pitch with high note density -> transcribe as riff/melody (Pati & Lerch's predominant-pitch and segmentation features are the right inputs; Ou et al.'s per-stem lead detector can be run on the separated stems directly); if no lead and only sustained harmony -> strum. Pati & Lerch's 63% solo precision suggests smoothing with section boundaries and a minimum duration (they used 4-s windows) before committing a mode.
- Choosing strummed vs fingerpicked within accompaniment is not covered by any source; chord-change rate and tempo (from the playability rubric) plus a user preference are the natural knobs.

### Gaps
- No work found on "accompaniment vs lead" classification for arrangement purposes or on automatic choice of strum pattern vs fingerpicking pattern.
- The LeadInstrumentDetection README gives no numbers; the per-class F1 table in the paper text was only partially legible.

## Key Question 5: Difficulty grading — published models, features and how easy/hard arrangements are generated

### Takeaway
The best-validated rhythm-guitar model is the ISMIR 2023 seven-criterion rubric (weights 3-3-2-2-2-1-0; quartile cut-offs <8 easy, <12.5 moderate, <18 hard, >=18 expert) scored from chord symbols and timings; for single-line/fingerstyle difficulty the validated features are index-finger movement and number of fretting fingers (Song2Guitar), fret-transition and string-change costs (Fretting-Transformer's 0-18.5 score), and hand-span feasibility losses (2026 diffusion work). OpenUkulele's easy/hard generation is constraint-based (fret ceiling, polyphony, span, barre, grid) with a fixed degradation order.

### Cited Findings
- Vélez Vásquez, Baelemans, Driedger (Chordify), Zuidema, Burgoyne, "Quantifying the ease of playing song chords on the guitar", ISMIR 2023: rubric of seven criteria each scored 0-3 and weighted — uncommonness of chord (3), chord finger positioning (3), chord fingering difficulty (2; 3 points = "mostly chords that require four fingers or barre chords", 1 point = "most chords require three fingers or are A or E barre chords", 0 = two-three fingers), repetitiveness (2), right-hand complexity (2; inner strings not strummed = harder), chord progression time (1; speed of transitions), beat difficulty / syncopes and ghost notes (0; recommended to omit). Billboard Playability Dataset: 200 McGill Billboard songs annotated by a guitar expert; weighting fitted with partial-credit IRT models; playability bins by quartile of weighted score: easy < 8, moderate < 12.5, hard < 18, expert >= 18; mean chords per song rise from 139 (easy) to 175 (expert); rule-based baseline scores each criterion from chord annotations and timings and beats length-based baselines except on progression-time and beat difficulty; LSTM/DeepGRU on chord symbols or tab text did not beat the rules; code at github.com/Marcel-Velez/playability-billboard — [ISMIR 2023 paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2023/paper/000086.pdf)
- Song2Guitar difficulty features and their validation (r = 0.55 index-finger movement, r = 0.51 number of fretting fingers, 50 tabs, 5 raters, 7-point scale) — [Song2Guitar PDF](https://staff.aist.go.jp/m.goto/PAPER/ISMIR2017ariga.pdf)
- Fretting-Transformer playability difficulty score: horizontal fret movement (backward moves penalised more than forward), a locality factor making higher frets costlier, vertical string movement penalising gaps over one string; range 0 (same open string) to 18.5 (24-fret maximum); reported scores 1.95 (GuitarToday, 363 fingerstyle tabs), 2.41 (DadaGP acoustic subset, 2,301 tracks), 4.24 (Leduc, 232 jazz tabs); tab accuracy 98.41 / 81.58 / 72.19% — [Fretting-Transformer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14223v1)
- Simionato & Bigo, "Playability-Aware Audio-to-Tablature Guitar Transcription via Diffusion Models" (arXiv 2608.30854, Aug 2026): auxiliary losses pitch-class distance, positional distance, circle-of-fifths distance, string similarity and hand-span feasibility; evaluated on GuitarSet and GOAT; consistent gains over the standard objective — [arXiv 2608.30854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30854)
- Cwitkowitz, Driedger, Duan (arXiv 2204.08094, 2022): an "inhibition loss" learned from symbolic tab collections discourages co-activation of unlikely string/fret pairs, i.e. a learned playability prior — [arXiv 2204.08094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08094)
- "Personalized Difficulty Level Classification and Feature Analysis for Guitar Tablature": 154 features including chord complexity and playing speed for rating tab difficulty (only the Academia.edu abstract was accessible; venue/year unverified) — [Academia.edu listing](https://www.academia.edu/12848499/Personalized_Difficulty_Level_Classification_and_Feature_Analysis_for_Guitar_Tablature)
- Visual Guitar Tab Comparison (Heyen et al., arXiv 2311.14726, 2023) lets players compare tab versions by "similarity, difficulty, and correctness" with automatic bar alignment — [arXiv API listing](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=all:%22tablature%22+AND+%28all:%22difficulty%22+OR+all:%22playability%22%29&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending&max_results=25)
- Piano-side precedents cited by the ISMIR 2023 paper: a 2012 MusicXML-based method with seven instrument-agnostic criteria on 50 piano pieces, and a GRU+attention / gradient-boosted-trees model using fingering features with levels derived from graded practice books — [ISMIR 2023 paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2023/paper/000086.pdf)
- OpenUkulele easy/hard constraint sets and degradation order (see Q3) — [OpenUkulele README](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele)
- Terao et al.: difficulty as a continuous gamma that selects a subset of notes; losses on note density and polyphony distributions per level — [Terao ICASSP 2023 PDF](https://eita-nakamura.github.io/articles/Terao_BandToPianoScoreArrangement_ICASSP2023.pdf)
- Ukulele hard-chord work-arounds (Dagan Bernstein on Live Ukulele, 2020-07-23): E major use `4447` instead of `4442`, or the triad `442`, or transpose up a half-step to F; Fmaj7 alternative `5500`; Bb and B via barre-position tips or "triad on first three strings only"; general strategies = alternative fingerings, other inversions/positions, 3-note triads instead of 4, transposition by half-steps with capo or chord charts — [Live Ukulele hard chords](https://liveukulele.com/chords/hard-chords/)

### Inferences
- A ukulele adaptation of the ISMIR 2023 rubric is straightforward because it is chord-symbol-driven: replace the guitar "A or E barre" exception with ukulele-specific hard shapes (E, Bb, B, Fmaj7 per Live Ukulele), compute uncommonness from a ukulele chord-frequency table, keep repetitiveness and progression-time (chord changes per bar x BPM) as-is, and drop beat difficulty (weight 0). The published quartile cut-offs give an initial easy/moderate/hard/expert banding.
- For melody/riff sections, use Fretting-Transformer-style per-transition cost (fret jump, backward penalty, high-fret locality, string skips) plus Song2Guitar's two hand-form features; OpenUkulele's hard constraints (span <= 4 or 5 frets, polyphony <= 2 or 4, fret ceiling 5 or 15, barre allowed or not) define the easy/hard envelopes.
- Easy-arrangement generation should follow the Terao/OpenUkulele pattern: generate the richest playable arrangement first, then remove notes in a fixed priority (harmony -> bass -> melody-only) until constraints and the difficulty target are met.

- Could not reach Chordify's "simplify chords", Moises "easy chords", Yousician level or Ultimate Guitar difficulty documentation (search quota exhausted; help-centre pages returned 403/404). Their rules remain unverified.
- No published ukulele-specific difficulty model was found.
- Full feature list of the 154-feature personalized-difficulty paper not retrieved.

## Key Question 6: Chord substitution rules for ukulele and Python chord-theory libraries

### Takeaway
Pedagogy-sourced rules: drop to 3-note triads when a 4-note shape is hard, prefer another inversion/position, treat power chords as 3rd-less chords interchangeable with major/minor, play the top chord of a slash chord (bass only on low-G), and transpose by half-steps when E/Bb/B shapes dominate. music21 `harmony.ChordSymbol` is the most complete parser (kinds, slash bass, explicit degree alterations, figure round-trip); pychord, chordparser and mingus are lighter alternatives.

### Cited Findings
- Hard-shape substitutions (E -> `4447` barre, `442` triad, or transpose to F; Fmaj7 -> `5500`; Bb/B -> three-string triad; general: alternative fingerings, inversions, 3-note triads, transposition) — [Live Ukulele hard chords](https://liveukulele.com/chords/hard-chords/)
- Power chords on ukulele = root + 5th; lowest on low-G is G5 (`02xx`); "X" marks muted strings; because the 3rd is absent power chords "can function interchangeably with major or minor chords" (Bordessa, 2023-11-14) — [Live Ukulele power chords](https://liveukulele.com/chords/power-chords/)
- Slash chords: bass note lowest; hard with high-G; usually drop one chord tone to fit the bass on four strings; many slash chords are respellings of another chord (Bordessa, 2009) — [Live Ukulele slash chords](https://liveukulele.com/chords/ukulele-slash-chords/)
- Transposing (Bordessa, 2009): use scale-degree numbers to move chords and melody to a key that "fits on the ukulele better"; examples in C, G and F — [Live Ukulele transposing](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/theory/transposing/)
- Live Ukulele's chord index also lists barre-chord technique, 7th-chord resolution via circle of fifths, jazz shapes, diminished/augmented and "mandolin-style open chords" lessons — [Live Ukulele chords index](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/chords/)
- Low-G vs high-G affects which voicings are available (see Q3) — [Live Ukulele low-G](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/strings/low-g/)
- Fretting-Transformer handles capo/tuning via `CAPO<<#>>` and `TUNING<<...>>` tokens, trained on standard, half-step down, whole-step down and drop-D with capo up to 7 — [Fretting-Transformer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14223v1)
- OpenUkulele offers `--transpose` as a uniform semitone shift for C-based rendering — [OpenUkulele PR #4](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele/pull/4)

**Python libraries**
- music21 `harmony.ChordSymbol`: parses figures such as `Cm`, `C7`, `CM7`, `Cdim7`, `Csus4`, `C9`, `Cadd2`, slash bass `C/E`, `Cm7/E-` (flats written with `-`), explicit degree alterations `C35b7b9#11b13`; attributes/methods `root()`, `bass()`, `chordKind` (e.g. 'minor', 'dominant-seventh'), `chordKindStr`, `figure`, `findFigure()`, `pitches`, `transpose()`, `writeAsChord`, `realizeChordSymbolDurations()`; module functions `chordSymbolFigureFromChord()`, `inversionIsValid()` — [music21 harmony docs](https://www.music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleHarmony.html)
- pychord: `Chord("Am7").components()` -> ['A','C','E','G']; `transpose(3)` turns "Am7/G" into "Cm7/Bb"; `from_note_index`; `ChordProgression` with collective transpose; `find_chords_from_notes()`; inversion syntax `C/1`, `Cm7/3/F`; MIT; Python >= 3.10 — [pychord GitHub](https://github.com/yuma-m/pychord)
- chordparser: parses chord notation incl. slash chords (`C7add4/E`), transposes, converts to Roman numerals in a key, analyses chord-scale relationships and secondary dominants; MIT; Python 3 — [chordparser GitHub](https://github.com/titus-ong/chordparser)
- mingus: `pip install mingus`, GPL-3.0; chord functions documented at bspaans.github.io/python-mingus (not fetched) — [mingus GitHub](https://github.com/bspaans/python-mingus)

### Inferences
- A rule table consistent with the cited pedagogy: `X5` -> `X` or `Xm` by key context; `Xadd9`/`Xsus2`/`Xsus4`/`X6` -> `X` in easy mode; `Xmaj7`/`X7`/`Xm7` kept in moderate mode (7ths are 4-note shapes that are often *easier* on uke, e.g. Fmaj7 alternatives) but reduced to triads in easy mode; `X/Y` -> `X` on high-G, keep bass on low-G only if it sits on the G string; if the key yields E, B or Bb as I/IV/V, transpose the whole song by +1 or -1 semitone (or more) to C/G/F/D/A families — the Live Ukulele E-major advice ("transpose up a half-step to F major") is the explicit precedent. Eb-tuned (half-step-down) source recordings should be analysed at concert pitch and then transposed, since the ukulele has no practical capo convention for that case in the sources.
- music21 is the right core because it already yields `chordKind` and bass separately, which is what the substitution table keys on; pychord is enough if only components and transposition are needed.
