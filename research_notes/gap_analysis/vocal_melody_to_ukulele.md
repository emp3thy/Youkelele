## KQ1. Singing-voice transcription (SVT) models: what exists, what runs, measured note-level F1

### Takeaway
As of 2026 the usable open SVT systems split into two families: (a) Mandarin-SVS-oriented models trained on clean studio vocals (ROSVOT 2024, STARS 2025, VocalParse 2026) which post the highest note F1 on their own benchmarks (COnPOff ≈ 70–77 %) but drop to COnPOff ≈ 47 % out-of-domain on separated pop vocals, and (b) MIR-ST500-trained pop models (EfficientNet-b0 2021, JDCnote 2022, Gu et al. 2023 SSL) that sit at COnPOff ≈ 42–53 % on MIR-ST500 and are old TF/Keras or SpeechBrain code. None is Windows-tested by its authors; ROSVOT (MIT, PyTorch 2.1, audio-only input) and VocalParse (Apache-2.0, PyTorch, audio-only, 1.7 B params) are the most practical to try, with Basic Pitch (Apache-2.0, Windows-supported, min-note-length 127.7 ms default) as the instrument-agnostic fallback.

### Cited Findings

Benchmark definitions and MIR-ST500
- MIR-ST500 "is the largest A-SVT dataset with human annotations. It has 500 Chinese pop songs (about 30 hours), including 400 songs for training and 100 songs for evaluation"; TONAS and ISMIR2014 are small OOD evaluation sets. Metrics are COnPOff (correct onset, pitch and offset), COnP (onset+pitch) and COn (onset) F1 computed with mir_eval at song level. — [Gu et al. 2023, arXiv 2304.12082 (PDF text)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.12082)
- The MIR-ST500 benchmark table (COnPOff / COnP / COn F1 %, MIR-ST500 test set) reported by Gu et al. 2023, Table II: EfficientNet-b0 (Wang & Jang 2021) 45.78 / 66.63 / 75.44; JDCnote (Kum et al. 2022) 42.23 / 69.74 / 76.18; Gu "Ours variant 1" 52.39 / 70.73 / 78.32; "Ours variant 2" 52.84 / 70.00 / 78.05. On ISMIR2014 (monophonic singing): Tony 50 / 68 / 73; HCN 59.4 / – / 79.0; VOCANO 68.38 / 80.58 / 84.04; EfficientNet-b0 49.55 / 63.63 / 79.16; Gu v2 62.42 / 75.91 / 93.02. On TONAS (flamenco): EfficientNet-b0 9.57 / 19.65 / 42.41; Gu v2 24.08 / 36.87 / 64.38. Gu's system takes raw waveform through a speech SSL front-end (wav2vec2-style) rather than CQT/GCoS features; code at github.com/guxm2021/SVT_SpeechBrain. — [arXiv 2304.12082 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.12082)

ROSVOT (2024)
- ROSVOT = "Robust automatic singing voice transcription (AST) model that serves singing voice synthesis (SVS)"; converts singing audio to MIDI note sequences; MIT licence; Python 3.9, CUDA 11.8, PyTorch 2.1.1; input is audio only, optionally with word-boundary timings from forced alignment (MFA) — it predicts word boundaries itself if not supplied; trained on M4Singer; no Windows-specific notes. — [RickyL-2000/ROSVOT](https://github.com/RickyL-2000/ROSVOT)
- Paper (ACL 2024, arXiv 2405.09940, 2024-05-16). Table 1 (joint M4Singer + in-house set, clean): ROSVOT COn 94.0 / COff 94.5 / COnPOff 77.4; Yong et al. 2023 92.0 / 91.4 / 65.8; MusicYOLO 82.2 / 81.7 / 58.9; VOCANO 75.8 / 71.2 / 50.2. Noisy: ROSVOT 93.8 / 94.4 / 77.0 vs Yong 88.5 / 89.7 / 62.1. Claims "17% relative improvement on pitch transcription" and "23% with noisy inputs". — [arXiv 2405.09940 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09940)
- Out-of-domain on MIR-ST500 (Table 6): ROSVOT COn 72.1 / COnP 65.9 / COnPOff 47.4 vs Gu et al. 2023 78.1 / 70.0 / 52.8 — i.e. the Mandarin-SVS model is *worse* than the MIR-ST500-trained SSL model on separated pop vocals. — [arXiv 2405.09940 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09940)
- Segmentation mechanism: note boundaries are "one-dimensional semantic segmentation" (binary per frame), with labels softened by convolution with a Gaussian of 80 ms window "to account for continuous pitch transitions"; word boundaries, when supplied, "regulate segmentation results" so text and notes stay synchronised; an attention-based pitch decoder is used because "a simple weighted median is insufficient in dealing with fluctuated pitches in singing voices, which are full of expressive techniques like portamentos"; the paper acknowledges "singing techniques like vibrato and appoggiatura further complicate boundary localization". — [arXiv 2405.09940 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09940)

STARS (2025)
- "STARS: A Unified Framework for Singing Transcription, Alignment, and Refined Style Annotation", Guo, Zhang, Pan, …, Zhou Zhao; arXiv 2507.06670 submitted 2025-07-09. Table 2 note transcription: COnPOff F — VOCANO 50.2, ROSVOT 70.2, STARS 71.0; RPA — 76.6 / 83.8 / 86.7. Frame hop 128 samples at 24 kHz (~5.3 ms); note boundaries from a dedicated boundary predictor (BCE) constrained by word boundaries; evaluated on GTSinger Chinese and English; "the model has been validated solely on Chinese and English datasets". — [arXiv 2507.06670 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06670); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06670)
- Code: gwx314/STARS, MIT, 91 stars, last update 2026-09-29; requires Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.4.0, CUDA 12.8; checkpoints on Hugging Face (Chinese model, bilingual Chinese-English model, RMVPE pitch extractor); inference "requires metadata.json with word sequences and phonemes" (durations can be auto-predicted); "vocal-only preferred; separation tools recommended". — [gwx314/STARS](https://github.com/gwx314/STARS); [GitHub search](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=STARS+singing+transcription+alignment)

VocalParse (2026, audio-LLM)
- "VocalParse: Towards Unified and Scalable Singing Voice Transcription with Large Audio Language Models", Yukun Chen et al., arXiv 2605.04613, submitted 2026-05-06; base model Qwen3-ASR (1.7 B params) fully fine-tuned on 2×H100 for 120 k steps (~17 h); training data ≈ 2,000 h SingCrawl (web-crawled Mandarin with automatic annotation) + GTSinger-zh + M4Singer (~50 h); evaluated on Opencpop, ACE-KiSing, OpenSinger, PopCS (all Mandarin). Opencpop melody results (lower is better): ROSVOT MAE_pitch 0.38 / MAE_note 0.45 / MAE_dur 0.40 / Num_note 0.20; VocalParse (audio+lyric) 0.35 / 0.43 / 0.33 / 0.11; VocalParse (audio-only) 0.56 / 0.44 / 0.34 / 0.11. Lyric WER 3.79 % (Opencpop), 5.69 % (OpenSinger), 8.16 % (PopCS). Limitations stated: "single global BPM per segment (issues with rubato/ritardando)", lyric drift, bounded by teacher pipeline, "experiments conducted exclusively on Mandarin data". — [arXiv 2605.04613 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04613); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04613)
- Repo: Apache-2.0; Python 3.10; PyTorch + CUDA 12.4; optional Flash Attention; `uv` recommended; checkpoint "pymaster/VocalParse" on Hugging Face (1.7 B, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B extended with ~400 AST tokens); input is audio only (mono 16 kHz WAV or numpy array), lyrics are generated not required; code released May 2026. — [pymaster17/VocalParse](https://github.com/pymaster17/VocalParse)
- Note: VocalParse does not report COn/COnP/COnPOff on MIR-ST500; its metrics are MAE-style on Mandarin SVS sets, so it is not directly comparable to the MIR-ST500 table above.

### Inferences
- For English rock/pop vocal stems the in-domain evidence (MIR-ST500 pop, separated vocals) says to expect COnPOff in the 40–55 % range and COn ~75–78 % from the best open models; the 70–77 % COnPOff numbers of ROSVOT/STARS are on clean Mandarin studio vocals and do not transfer (ROSVOT drops to 47.4 % on MIR-ST500).
- Practically, for a Windows/PyTorch pipeline: ROSVOT (MIT, PyTorch 2.1, audio-only) and VocalParse (Apache-2.0, needs a GPU able to hold a 1.7 B model, audio-only, Mandarin-trained) are the installable candidates; STARS needs a `metadata.json` with word/phoneme sequences, which means you must first run lyrics ASR + G2P; JDCnote/EfficientNet are TF/Keras-era code; Gu's SVT_SpeechBrain is the strongest MIR-ST500 number but SpeechBrain on Windows is untested here.
- Basic Pitch remains the only one of these with an explicit Windows support statement; its `minimum_note_length`, `minimum_frequency`/`maximum_frequency` (e.g. clamp to ~80–1100 Hz for voice) and `melodia_trick` are the knobs to tune on a vocal stem, but it has no vocal-specific boundary model, so expect it to fragment melismas/portamento into extra short notes compared with ROSVOT's Gaussian-smoothed boundary head.

## KQ2. Lead vs backing vocal separation and its effect on SVT

### Takeaway
Lead/backing ("karaoke") separation is available off-the-shelf: MVSEP's "MVSep Karaoke" BS-Roformer reports lead-vocal SDR 10.41 dB / backing 6.61 dB, and the open Mel-Band/BS-Roformer karaoke checkpoints (aufr33/viperx 10.20 dB, gabox, becruily, anvuew, frazer) are downloadable through `python-audio-separator` (MIT, Windows + CUDA documented). The measured cost of *not* doing it is qualitative but explicit: ROSVOT "tends to transcribe vocal harmonies when the main vocal is silent", and Gu et al. show SVT F1 collapsing when accompaniment-like interference is added.

### Cited Findings
- The ZFTurbo Music-Source-Separation-Training `pretrained_models.md` lists no lead/backing-specific model; its best vocal models (Multisong dataset SDR) are BS PolarFormer 11.00, MelBand Roformer (KimberleyJensen) 10.98, BS Roformer (viperx) 10.87, MDX23C 10.17. — [ZFTurbo pretrained_models.md](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/blob/main/docs/pretrained_models.md)
- `python-audio-separator`'s `models.json` carries the karaoke/lead-backing family: `mel_band_roformer_karaoke_aufr33_viperx_sdr_10.1956.ckpt` (SDR 10.1956), `mel_band_roformer_karaoke_gabox.ckpt` / `_v2`, `mel_band_roformer_karaoke_becruily.ckpt`, `bs_roformer_karaoke_frazer_becruily.ckpt`, `bs_roformer_karaoke_anvuew.ckpt`, the VR-arch `UVR-BVE-4B_SN-44100-2.pth` (backing-vocal extractor), plus `bs_roformer_male_female_by_aufr33_sdr_7.2889.ckpt`, `model_chorus_bs_roformer_ep_267_sdr_24.1275.ckpt` and `mel_band_roformer_bleed_suppressor_v1.ckpt`. — [audio_separator/models.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator/main/audio_separator/models.json)

- Both target songs have layered vocals: on "Pour Some Sugar on Me" Phil Collen, Steve Clark and Rick Savage are credited with backing vocals and Joe Elliott with lead; on "Summer of '69" Bryan Adams is credited with "lead and harmony vocals". — [Wikipedia: Pour Some Sugar on Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pour_Some_Sugar_on_Me); [Wikipedia: Summer of '69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_of_%2769)
- Cross-reference (lyrics notes in this folder): Whisper-based lyric ASR shows ">50 % deletion rate for non-lexical vocables and backing vocals regardless of separation quality" (Syed et al. 2025, arXiv 2506.15514), i.e. backing vocals are a separate acoustic layer that ASR already discards. — [arXiv 2506.15514](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15514v1)

### Inferences
- Pipeline order that the evidence supports: mix → vocals/other (BS-Roformer/MelBand, ~11–12 dB) → lead/backing karaoke model (~10 dB lead) → SVT. The karaoke stage mainly matters in chorus sections of gang-vocal rock ("Pour Some Sugar on Me") where backing parts continue while the lead rests, which is exactly the failure ROSVOT describes.
- Lead-vocal SDR (~10 dB) is lower than plain vocal SDR (~12 dB), so the karaoke stage adds artefacts; a reasonable guard is to run SVT on both the full vocal stem and the lead stem and keep the lead-stem notes only where the two disagree in regions of detected multi-pitch.

## KQ3. Melody-line clean-up for an instrument (vibrato/glides, melisma merging, min duration, octave errors, grid quantisation, rests)

### Takeaway
Published tooling gives concrete default thresholds to start from: Basic Pitch prunes notes shorter than 127.7 ms; Essentia's classic PitchContourSegmentation (McNab 1996) uses minDuration 0.1 s, a 60-cent pitch-change threshold and an RMS z-score threshold of −2 for onsets; ROSVOT smooths boundary labels with an 80 ms Gaussian and uses an attention pitch decoder because a weighted median fails on portamento. Octave-error correction and beat-grid quantisation for vocals are not documented by any fetched SVT tool; they must be implemented (VocalParse assumes one global BPM per segment and flags rubato as a limitation).

### Cited Findings
- Essentia `PitchContourSegmentation`: "converts a pitch sequence estimated from an audio signal into a set of discrete note events"; parameters hopSize 128, minDuration 0.1 s ("minimum note duration"), pitchDistanceThreshold 60 cents ("pitch threshold for note segmentation"), rmsThreshold −2 ("zscore threshold for note segmentation"), tuningFrequency 440 Hz; outputs onset [s], duration [s], MIDIpitch (quantised); reference McNab et al., "Signal processing for melody transcription", 1996. — [Essentia docs](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_PitchContourSegmentation.html)
- Basic Pitch `minimum_note_length` default 127.7 ms; `melodia_trick` (Melodia-style post-processing) on by default; `multiple_pitch_bends` False; frequency clamps available. — [inference.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/inference.py)
- ROSVOT: boundary labels Gaussian-softened over an 80 ms window "to account for continuous pitch transitions"; word boundaries from forced alignment can "regulate segmentation results"; "a simple weighted median is insufficient in dealing with fluctuated pitches in singing voices, which are full of expressive techniques like portamentos"; vibrato and appoggiatura "further complicate boundary localization". — [arXiv 2405.09940 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09940)
- STARS derives note boundaries from a boundary predictor constrained by word-level boundaries, at ~5.3 ms frame resolution. — [arXiv 2507.06670 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06670)
- VocalParse emits tempo in its unified sequence but assumes a "single global BPM per segment (issues with rubato/ritardando)". — [arXiv 2605.04613 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04613)
- JDCnote operates on the polyphonic mix, so its pseudo-labels inherit octave/voicing errors of the frame-level pitch tracker; Kum et al. note Demucs+CREPE labels were worse than JDC-pitch labels (COnPOff 22.43 vs 25.44). — [arXiv 2203.13422 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.13422)
- OpenUkulele (Basic Pitch front-end) reports out-of-range notes "as uneditable in the JSON report rather than silently transposed" and applies a simplification cascade "harmony removal → bass removal → melody only" when a bar fails playability. — [xin-pu/OpenUkulele](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele)

### Inferences
- A defensible vocal-cleanup recipe assembled from the above: (1) run SVT or f0+segmentation with min duration ≈ 100–130 ms; (2) merge adjacent same-pitch notes separated by < ~80 ms gaps (ROSVOT's boundary-smoothing scale) and treat sub-60-cent pitch wobble as vibrato, not a new note; (3) collapse a melisma onto the syllable's first note unless the pitch change exceeds a semitone and lasts longer than the min duration; (4) octave-check each note against a running median of the phrase (±7 semitone jump rule) since no tool does this for you; (5) quantise onsets to a beat grid from a separate beat tracker (madmom as Sheet Sage does) rather than trusting a global BPM; (6) insert rests only where the lead stem is unvoiced for longer than the min duration (breath points), not at every word gap.
- Word boundaries from lyric alignment (see this folder's lyrics notes) are the strongest segmentation prior available: ROSVOT and STARS both use them to stop melismas being fragmented, so feeding WhisperX word timestamps into ROSVOT's optional word-boundary input is the cheapest quality win.

### Gaps
- The Tony/pYIN note-segmentation paper and the pYIN Vamp docs could not be fetched (code.soundsoftware.ac.uk connection refused; vamp-plugins doc 404), so pYIN's HMM note-tracker parameters are not cited.
- No published algorithm specifically for octave-error correction of sung-melody note lists or for quantising vocal onsets to a grid was found via the arXiv API queries tried.

## KQ4. Instrumentalising the melody for ukulele (ranges, octave placement, folding, chord-melody vs single line, arrangement algorithms, tabforge/OpenUkulele)

### Takeaway
Standard re-entrant tuning is G4–C4–E4–A4 with a soprano range of C4–A5 (12–15 frets), low-G is G3–C4–E4–A4, baritone is D3–G3–B3–E4 (range D3–A♯5); typical male rock vocal lines (both target songs sit roughly A3–A♯4) fall below C4, so on high-G they must go up an octave (landing at the very top of a soprano) while on low-G or baritone they fit untransposed. Ukulele pedagogy's one firm rule is that in chord-melody the melody is "almost always placed as the highest note in the chord", usually on the E and A strings; no fetched source gives an explicit octave-folding algorithm, and the automatic-arrangement literature found is guitar-tab (string/fret assignment, hand-span) or piano-accompaniment, not melody-to-ukulele.

### Cited Findings

Instrument ranges
- Ukulele tunings: re-entrant "G4–C4–E4–A4" (high G); low-G linear "G3–C4–E4–A4, which is equivalent to playing the top four strings (DGBE) of a guitar with a capo on the fifth fret"; baritone "D3–G3–B3–E4". Ranges by size: soprano "C4–A5 (C6)", concert "C4–C6 (D♯6)", tenor "G3–D6 (E6)", baritone "D3–A♯5 (C♯6)"; frets: soprano 12–15, concert 15–18, tenor 17–19, baritone 18–21. — [Wikipedia: Ukulele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukulele)
- Classical voice-type approximations (Wikipedia, "not meant to be too rigidly applied"): tenor "typically between D3 and A4" (as extracted), baritone "B2 and F4", bass "G2 and C4", alto "F3 and C5", mezzo "A3 and G5"; the article gives no pop/rock-specific ranges. — [Wikipedia: Vocal range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocal_range); the "Voice classification in non-classical music" article likewise only re-states classical ranges ("soprano C4–C6, mezzo-soprano A3–A5, etc.") and has no pop tessitura data. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_classification_in_non-classical_music)

### Inferences
- Range arithmetic from the cited tunings/ranges: high-G GCEA playable melody range is C4 (open C) to A5 (12th fret A string) on a soprano, C6 on a concert. "Summer of '69" (A3–A4) is 3 semitones below the C4 floor; shifting it up an octave gives A4–A5, which fits exactly (A5 = A-string fret 12) — fine on concert/tenor, at the limit on a 12-fret soprano. "Pour Some Sugar on Me" sung sections (B3–A♯4) moved up an octave become B4–A♯5, where A♯5 = A-string fret 13, i.e. over the edge of a 12-fret soprano but fine on a 15+-fret concert/tenor; alternatively leave B3 as the one out-of-range note and fold it up (B3→B4) or drop the arrangement a semitone.
- On low-G (G3 floor) both songs fit untransposed (A3 = G-string fret 2; B3 = fret 4), and on baritone (D3 floor) they fit with room to spare — so the octave decision should be a function of the declared tuning: `if min(melody) < tuning_floor: shift = +12 (whole phrase)`, with per-note folding only as a last resort because the pedagogy rule ("melody note… highest note in the chord", on E/A strings) favours keeping the whole line in one register.
- For chord-melody output the same rule implies placing the melody on strings 1–2 and voicing chords below it; for a single-line melody tab (the simpler deliverable) a plain string/fret assignment with "prefer the highest string whose open pitch ≤ note, minimise fret-hand movement" is sufficient; the guitar MIDI-to-tab papers (hand-span, string-similarity losses) are the formal versions of that heuristic but are 6-string-only.

### Gaps
- No James Hill, Daniel Ward or other published ukulele text with explicit octave-transposition / range-folding rules was reachable; the only firm written rule found is the "melody on top, E/A strings" one from Ukulele Tricks.
- No paper on automatic melody arrangement for ukulele, or "lead sheet to solo guitar" specifically, surfaced in the arXiv API queries ("guitar arrangement" returned 0 hits).
- Hooktheory ranges are crowd-sourced and unverified against the recordings; Musicnotes' published "vocal range" for either song could not be reached (search engines blocked).
- Pop/rock-specific typical ranges for male rock tenors/baritones were not found in any fetched source; only classical approximations are cited.

## KQ5. Rendering melody tab with lyrics (alphaTex, music21/MusicXML, PyGuitarPro, ASCII)

### Takeaway
alphaTex attaches lyrics at staff level with `\lyrics "syllable syllable …"` (or `\lyrics startBar "…"`), one whitespace-separated chunk per beat, `+` to put two words on one beat and `[…]` for hidden comments (verified from the alphaTab website source and the alphaTex handler code, both on the `main`/`develop` branches in 2026); music21's `note.Lyric` handles MusicXML syllabic begin/middle/end from trailing hyphens; PyGuitarPro 0.11 stores Guitar Pro lyrics as `Song.lyrics = Lyrics(trackChoice, lines=[LyricLine(startingMeasure, lyrics)]*5)` with the Guitar Pro text convention (spaces/hyphens per beat). There is no standard for a lyric line under ASCII tab; OpenUkulele side-steps it by writing a `.lrc` file.

### Cited Findings

alphaTex (alphaTab, MPL-2.0)
- alphaTab is "provided to you for free under the terms of the MPL-2.0 license" and has been developed since 2010. — [alphatab.net](https://www.alphatab.net/)
- Official docs (`docs/alphatex/_staff-metadata.mdx` on the website repo's `main` branch): "The lyrics system allows defining lyrics shown on beats. Syllables are separated by spaces, `+` combines syllables on the same beat, and `[bracketed text]` creates non-displayed comments." Overloads: `\lyrics lyrics` (string) and `\lyrics (startBar lyrics)` (number + string). Examples verbatim:
  ```
  \lyrics "Do Re Mi Fa So La Ti"
  C4 D4 E4 F4 | G4 A4 B4 r
  ```
  ```
  \lyrics "Do+Do  Mi+Mi"
  C4 C4 E4 E4
  ```
  ```
  \lyrics 2 "Do Re Mi Fa So La Ti"
  r r r r | r r r r |
  C4 D4 E4 F4 | G4 A4 B4 r
  ```
  ```
  \lyrics "[This is a comment]Do Re Mi Fa"
  C4 D4 E4 F4
  ```
  — [alphaTabWebsite _staff-metadata.mdx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_staff-metadata.mdx). (Note: the public page `alphatab.net/docs/alphatex/lyrics` no longer exists — it resolves to the homepage — and there is no `lyrics.mdx` in `docs/alphatex/`; the lyrics text now lives inside the staff-metadata page.) — [docs/alphatex listing](https://github.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/tree/main/docs/alphatex)
- Parser source (monorepo path `packages/alphatab/src/importer/alphaTex/AlphaTex1LanguageHandler.ts`, `develop` branch): `lyrics` is handled in `applyStaffMetaData`; with two arguments `lyrics.startBar = arguments[0].value; lyrics.text = arguments[1].text`, else `lyrics.text = arguments[0].text`; each `\lyrics` tag pushes a new `Lyrics` object onto the track's list (`importer.state.lyrics.get(staff.track.index)!.push(lyrics)`), so repeated tags give multiple lyric lines. — [AlphaTex1LanguageHandler.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTab/develop/packages/alphatab/src/importer/alphaTex/AlphaTex1LanguageHandler.ts)
- `Lyrics.ts` (same monorepo path under `src/model/`) implements the Guitar-Pro-style chunking: spaces/newlines/tabs end a chunk; `[...]` is a comment and skipped; `+` is rendered as a space inside a chunk (`split('+').join(' ')`); a run of `-` ends the current chunk and sets `skipSpace` so the following syllable is attached without a separator beat; trailing underscores are trimmed ("'You____' becomes 'You'"); `startBar` "Gets or sets the start bar on which the lyrics should begin". — [Lyrics.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTab/develop/packages/alphatab/src/model/Lyrics.ts)
- Importer test: `\lyrics "Test Lyrics 🤘"` over `(1.2 1.1).4 x.2.8 0.1 1.1 | 1.2 3.2 0.1 1.1` asserts `beats[2].lyrics[0] === '🤘'` — chunks are assigned to beats sequentially (including rests/dead notes as beats). — [AlphaTexImporter.test.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTab/develop/packages/alphatab/test/importer/AlphaTexImporter.test.ts)

music21 → MusicXML
- `music21.note.Lyric(text='', number=1, *, applyRaw=False, syllabic=None, identifier=None)`; `syllabic` ∈ {None, 'begin', 'single', 'end', 'middle', 'composite'}; smart hyphen handling: `note.Lyric(text='hel-')` → `.text == 'hel'`, `.syllabic == 'begin'`; with `applyRaw=True` the hyphen is literal and syllabic stays 'single'; `n.lyric = 'hel-'` shortcut; `n.addLyric('hello')`, `n.addLyric('bye', 3)` for multiple verses; `n.lyric = None` clears. On MusicXML export "this property will be stored in the lyric 'number' attribute which can store a number or a descriptive identifier but not both". — [music21 moduleNote docs](https://www.music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleNote.html)

PyGuitarPro (Guitar Pro 3–5 files)
- Installed `pyguitarpro` 0.11 (inspected locally with `attr.fields`): `class Lyrics: trackChoice: int = 0; lines: list[LyricLine] = [LyricLine() for _ in range(5)]; maxLineCount = 5`; `class LyricLine: startingMeasure: int = 1; lyrics: str = ''`; `Song.lyrics` defaults to `Lyrics()`; `str(Lyrics)` joins the five lines with spaces. — local inspection of `guitarpro/models.py` (the readthedocs API page returned 404 and the raw GitHub `models.py` 404 on both `master` and `main`, so no online doc link could be verified).
- OpenUkulele emits GP5 "via PyGuitarPro" and ASCII 4-line tab, plus a separate `.lrc` file for lyric timing rather than a lyric line inside the ASCII tab. — [xin-pu/OpenUkulele](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele)

### Inferences
- For a melody+lyrics deliverable the cleanest path is: note list + syllable list → alphaTex (`\lyrics "…"` with one chunk per beat; use `+` for two words on one note, `-` to continue a word across notes) for in-browser rendering, and music21 (`Lyric(syllabic=…)`) → MusicXML for MuseScore/PDF; GP5 via PyGuitarPro only if the user wants Guitar Pro files. The alphaTex chunk-per-beat rule means every rest and tied note consumes a chunk, so emit an empty placeholder (e.g. `_`-style or an extra space-delimited token) per rest beat — exact empty-chunk token not verified (see Gaps).
- Because PyGuitarPro stores lyrics as free text per line with Guitar Pro's own syllable conventions (which alphaTab's `Lyrics.ts` parser documents: space = next beat, `-` = word continuation, `+` = space within a beat, `_` trailing padding), the same syllable string can be reused for both GP5 and alphaTex, which keeps the two outputs consistent.

### Gaps
- Could not verify from docs how alphaTex denotes an *empty* lyric chunk for a beat (whether `_` alone, `""` or a double space is honoured); only the trailing-underscore trimming rule is documented in code.
- PyGuitarPro's documentation site (readthedocs `api.html`) was 404 for both `stable` and `latest`, so the official description of the GP lyrics text format is not cited; the installed source was used instead.
- No established convention for a lyric line under ASCII ukulele tab was found; OpenUkulele's `.lrc` sidecar is the only observed practice.

## KQ6. Ground truth: do "Summer of '69" and "Pour Some Sugar On Me" fit the ukulele untransposed?

### Takeaway
Per Hooktheory's analyses, "Summer of '69" (D major, 139 BPM) spans A3–A4 and "Pour Some Sugar on Me" (C♯ minor/E major, Hooktheory 85 BPM) spans B3–A♯4 in its sung sections; both dip below the C4 floor of high-G GCEA, so neither fits untransposed on a standard re-entrant uke, but both fit untransposed on low-G (G3 floor) and baritone (D3 floor). Up an octave, "Summer of '69" tops out at A5 (fret 12, A string) and "Pour Some Sugar" at A♯5 (fret 13) — fine on concert/tenor, marginal on a 12-fret soprano.

### Cited Findings
- "Summer of '69": D major with F-major bridge, 139 BPM, melody A3–A4 overall (verse A3–F♯4, chorus B3–A4, bridge D4–A4). — [Hooktheory](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/bryan-adams/summer-of-69)
- "Pour Some Sugar on Me": verse C♯ minor, pre-chorus F♯ major, chorus E major, bridge E Mixolydian; verse B3–G♯4, pre-chorus C♯4–A♯4, chorus B3–G♯4; Hooktheory's overall E2–B4 includes a bridge low of E2. — [Hooktheory](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me)
- High-G GCEA = G4 C4 E4 A4; soprano range C4–A5 (12–15 frets), concert C4–C6, tenor G3–D6; low-G = G3 C4 E4 A4; baritone D3 G3 B3 E4, range D3–A♯5. — [Wikipedia: Ukulele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukulele)

### Inferences
- High-G: "Summer of '69" needs +12 (A4–A5, A5 = A-string fret 12); "Pour Some Sugar" needs +12 for its B3s (B4–A♯5; A♯5 = A-string fret 13, beyond a 12-fret soprano) or a −1 semitone key change after the octave shift (A♯4–A5) if the arrangement must stay within 12 frets.
- Low-G and baritone: both songs playable at written pitch (A3 = low-G fret 2; B3 = low-G fret 4; baritone has D3 floor).

### Gaps
- Hooktheory data is community-entered; no publisher (Musicnotes/Hal Leonard) range statement was reachable (search engines returned junk/CAPTCHAs), so the ranges above are unconfirmed against licensed sheet music.
- The E2 in Hooktheory's "Pour Some Sugar" bridge is unexplained (likely a spoken/gang-vocal part); the actual sung lowest note is assumed to be B3.
