## Key Question 1: Tempo-grid robustness — do beat trackers drift over a 4-minute song, and how is a non-drifting tempo/bar grid built and represented?

### Takeaway
Modern trackers (Beat This!, madmom DBN) rarely "drift" in the sense of accumulating phase error on steady rock, but they do exhibit (a) locally-correct/globally-unstable "continuity errors" (18 % of SMC tracks), (b) octave errors forced by the DBN's 55–215 BPM window, and (c) chunk-boundary effects from 30 s windowing; the practical fix pattern used in the field is to post-process raw beat times into an explicit tempo map (median/regression tempo, re-anchored to onset peaks) and store it as MIDI tempo events / JAMS / alphaTex `\tempo` with per-bar positions.

### Cited Findings

**Evidence on tracker behaviour and failure modes**
- Beat This! (Foscarin, Schlüter, Widmer, ISMIR 2024) removes DBN post-processing because the DBN imposes "tempo range [55,215] BPM and beat-per-measure restrictions [3,4]" and "fixed tempo variability parameters mismatched to music characteristics"; the authors argue these assumptions "block research from solving corner cases" such as tempo/time-signature changes. Input: 22.05 kHz, 128-bin mel, window 1024, hop 441 → 50 fps; trained on 18 datasets / 4,556 tracks (incl. Ballroom, Beatles, RWC Pop). Minimal post-processing: pick frames that are the local maximum within ±3 frames (±70 ms) and have probability > 0.5; downbeats are snapped to the nearest beat. GTZAN beat F1 89.1, downbeat 78.3; Ballroom beat 97.5, downbeat 95.3 — [arXiv 2407.21658](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21658)
- Beat This! processes long audio in chunks: `split_piece(spect, chunk_size=1500, border_size=6, avoid_short_end=True)`; "`border_size` is the number of frames assumed to be discarded in the predictions on either side (since the model was not trained on the input edges due to the max-pool in the loss)… consecutive chunks overlap by `border_size`"; aggregation uses `overlap_mode='keep_first'`; audio is resampled to 22050 Hz; default postprocessor is `minimal`, with `dbn=True` (madmom) optional — [beat_this/inference.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CPJKU/beat_this/main/beat_this/inference.py). At 50 fps, 1500 frames = 30 s and a 6-frame border = 120 ms. (The paper text says predictions for long pieces concatenate non-overlapping 30 s excerpts; the shipped code uses the 6-frame overlap above.)
- "The SMC Blind Spot" (Ahn, Hwang, Jung, arXiv 2605.12287, May 2026) analysed Beat This!, Beat Transformer and madmom TCN on the 217-track SMC set: 29 % good (F ≥ 0.8), 8 % octave errors (correct phase, wrong metrical level), 18 % "continuity errors" ("locally correct but globally unstable tracking"), 5 % complete failures; 21 % of SMC tracks (45 pieces) lie below the DBN's default 55 BPM minimum, so the DBN forces double-tempo output; lowering `min_bpm` from 55 to 30 improves slow tracks without hurting others; per-track tuning of the tempo-continuity hyper-parameter λ gives F = 0.642 vs 0.627 fixed — [arXiv 2605.12287](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12287)
- madmom `DBNBeatTrackingProcessor` defaults: `min_bpm=55`, `max_bpm=215`, `num_tempi=None`, `transition_lambda=100` ("Lambda for the exponential tempo change distribution (higher values prefer a constant tempo from one beat to the next one)"), `observation_lambda=16`, `threshold=0`, `correct=True` (selects the frame with maximum activation inside each beat region), `fps` required; `RNNBeatProcessor` runs at 100 fps — [madmom/features/beats.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CPJKU/madmom/main/madmom/features/beats.py)
- madmom `DBNDownBeatTrackingProcessor`: `beats_per_bar` required (e.g. `[3, 4]`), `min_bpm=55`, `max_bpm=215`, `num_tempi=60`, `transition_lambda=100`, `observation_lambda=16`, `threshold=0.05`, `correct=True`, 100 fps; based on Böck, Krebs, Widmer, "Joint Beat and Downbeat Tracking with Recurrent Neural Networks", ISMIR 2016 — [madmom/features/downbeats.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CPJKU/madmom/main/madmom/features/downbeats.py)
- librosa `beat_track` is the Ellis (2007) dynamic-programming tracker: (1) onset strength, (2) a single tempo from onset autocorrelation, (3) peak picking "approximately consistent with estimated tempo"; defaults `hop_length=512`, `start_bpm=120`, `tightness=100` (penalty `tightness * (log(interval) - log(expected_frames_per_beat))²`), `trim=True`; `plp` is offered for "when tempo varies significantly" — [librosa/beat.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/beat.py). `librosa.feature.tempo` weights the autocorrelation by a log-normal prior `-0.5*((log2(bpm)-log2(start_bpm))/std_bpm)²` with `start_bpm=120`, `std_bpm=1.0` octave, `ac_size=8.0` s, `max_tempo=320` — [librosa/feature/rhythm.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/feature/rhythm.py)
- A human drummer playing without a click shows "gradual pulse drift" (hi-hat interval 157 ms, SD 8.7 ms, long-range correlations α = 0.72 at 5–8 s scales) — [Räsänen et al. 2015, PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0127902). A single global BPM therefore cannot fit a non-click recording.

**Fixes practitioners use on top of raw beat times**
- beat_this issue #13 (2025-11-30, open): user script post-processes Beat This! beats with (i) an octave-correction heuristic constraining BPM to 78–185 for Trap/DnB, (ii) an "Advanced BPM" from "a phase-aware circular mean and linear regression on the beat grid", filtering out the beginning/end and strong deviations from the median beat interval. GiantSteps results: Acc1 89.31 % / Acc2 95.03 % (single model); ensemble of three small models 92.02 % / 97.74 %. Maintainer f0k: "taking the median across multiple tempo predictions is very reasonable" and asked whether regression ever beats `60 / avg_interval`; maintainer fosfrancesco: the small model may do better because the big model spends capacity on "changes of tempo and time signature" — [CPJKU/beat_this#13](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/issues/13)
- Same thread on 1/3-octave (compound-meter) ambiguity: "all datasets cleaned by Böck, e.g., Ballroom, Hainsworth, GTZAN… plus ASAP, will beat a compound meter every 3 eighth notes… In other datasets, like tapcorrect, harmonix, a 6/8 measure contains 6 beats" — [CPJKU/beat_this#13](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/issues/13)
- Re-anchoring beats to onsets is formalised in Driedger et al., "Towards Automatically Correcting Tapped Beat Annotations for Music Recordings" (ISMIR 2019): taps are "snapped" to nearby beat/onset positions, validated on 101 recordings with listening tests — [OpenAlex / DOI 10.5281/zenodo.3527778](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3527778)
- Drum-stem vs mix for beat tracking (Chiu, Su, Yang, IEEE SPL 2021): Open-Unmix splits drum / non-drum; separate trackers + learnable fusion. F1: HJDB drum-only 0.906, non-drum 0.775, mixture 0.879, ensemble 0.914; Rock drum-only 0.886, mixture 0.901, ensemble 0.911; ASAP (piano) drum-only 0.522, mixture 0.587, ensemble 0.596 — [arXiv 2106.08685](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.08685). Drum-only tracking beats the mixture only on drum-heavy electronic music; on a rock set the mixture tracker is slightly better and the fusion best.
- Beat Transformer (Zhao, Xia, Wang, ISMIR 2022) tracks on demixed multi-stem spectrograms and reports "up to 4 % point improvement in downbeat tracking accuracy" over TCNs — [arXiv 2209.07140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07140). Chiu et al. EUSIPCO 2021 also use drum/non-drum separation as data augmentation for beat/downbeat tracking — [DOI 10.23919/eusipco54536.2021.9616022](https://doi.org/10.23919/eusipco54536.2021.9616022)

**Tempo-map representations**
- MIDI: `set_tempo` meta message, "Tempo is in microseconds per beat (quarter note)", default 500000 (= 120 BPM); `time_signature` has numerator, denominator, clocks_per_click (24), notated_32nd_notes_per_beat (8); `bpm2tempo()/tempo2bpm()` helpers — [mido docs](https://mido.readthedocs.io/en/stable/meta_message_types.html)
- pretty_midi stores tempo as a list of `(tick, tick_scale)` pairs (`_tick_scales`), reads `set_tempo` only from track 0, default resolution 220 ticks/beat and initial tempo 120; `get_tempo_changes()` returns `(times_in_seconds, tempi_qpm)`; `tick_to_time` / `time_to_tick` use a cumulative tick→time array (binary search for the inverse); `get_beats`/`get_downbeats` honour tempo and time-signature changes; `adjust_times(original_times, new_times)` remaps all note/meta timings by interpolation and "recomputes `_tick_scales`" — i.e. it is a ready-made piecewise-linear time-warp — [pretty_midi.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/craffel/pretty-midi/main/pretty_midi/pretty_midi.py)
- JAMS namespaces: `tempo` (time, duration, value = non-negative BPM, confidence ∈ [0,1]; several tempo observations may coexist with confidences summing to 1), `beat` (value = metrical position in bar or null), `beat_position` (structured value with `position` ≥ 1, `measure`, `num_beats`, `beat_units` ∈ {1,2,4,…,256}), `onset`, `chord` (Harte syntax), `segment` — [JAMS namespace docs](https://jams.readthedocs.io/en/stable/namespace.html)
- alphaTex supports mid-score tempo per bar: `\tempo bpm "label"` or `\tempo (bpm "label" position hide)` where `position` is a 0–1 ratio within the bar; multiple tempo changes per bar are allowed; "Tempo changes affect the whole song and not only an individual track… specify the tempo changes only once on the first track/staff"; `\ts numerator denominator` / `\ts common` per bar; no polytempo — [alphaTab bar metadata docs](https://www.alphatab.net/docs/alphatex/bar-metadata)
- Basic Pitch writes note times in absolute seconds and the `midi_tempo` argument (default 120) does not change them — issue #152 (2024-11-18, open): "The generated MIDI file retains the original timing of the audio via time-value compensation… the midi_tempo setting does not influence the playback speed" — [spotify/basic-pitch#152](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/issues/152). So a Basic-Pitch MIDI is a fixed-120-BPM timeline; a separate tempo map must be applied (e.g. via `pretty_midi.adjust_times`) before beat-quantising.

**Commercial-tool BPM errors (Chordify / Moises)**
- No primary user reports could be retrieved (search engines blocked; support.chordify.net and help.moises.ai returned HTTP 403). Related evidence: Chordify's own annotator-subjectivity dataset (CASD, 4 experts × 50 songs) shows only 73 % agreement even on a major/minor vocabulary — [Koops et al. 2019, JNMR](https://doi.org/10.1080/09298215.2019.1613436)

### Inferences
- A 4-minute rock track at ~120 BPM contains ~480 beats; a constant-BPM grid fitted by `60/mean_IBI` will mis-place beats by the accumulated tempo wobble (Räsänen-type drift). The field practice (beat_this #13, Driedger 2019) is: take raw beat times → reject outliers vs median IBI → fit tempo per section (regression or median) → snap each beat to the nearest onset/activation peak → store as MIDI `set_tempo` events at each bar (or JAMS `beat_position`). pretty_midi's `adjust_times` plus `get_tempo_changes` gives this round-trip for free.
- Because Beat This! has no DBN, its output has no tempo-continuity prior; for steady rock, running the drum stem through madmom's DBN (λ = 100) or a simple IBI-median filter restores global stability (the "continuity error" class in SMC Blind Spot). Conversely, for songs with genuine tempo changes, the DBN's λ should be lowered or the piece segmented first.
- Chunk boundaries (30 s in Beat This!) are a plausible source of discrete beat-phase jumps every 30 s; verify by checking IBI outliers at multiples of 30 s.
- The drum-stem-vs-mix evidence says: run the tracker on both and fuse (or at least cross-check tempo and phase); the drum stem alone is safest for drum-heavy rock but not universally better.

### Gaps
- No paper directly measures accumulated phase drift (ms per minute) of Beat This!/madmom/librosa on full-length rock songs; the SMC Blind Spot analysis uses short SMC excerpts.
- No retrievable primary source for Chordify/Moises BPM user complaints (403 / search blocked).
- Hockman & Fujinaga 2010 and Böck 2020 ("Deconstruct, analyse, reconstruct") abstracts were not retrievable (Zenodo "[TODO] Add abstract").

## Key Question 2: Tempo-octave choice — resolving 85 vs 170 BPM so strumming patterns are written at the right level

### Takeaway
Tempo evaluation literature treats the octave as inherently ambiguous (ACC2 forgives ×2, ×3, ½, ⅓ errors; annotators themselves disagree on 2–10 % of tracks), and the best-documented discriminators are the backbeat (snare on 2 and 4 defines the quarter-note level; a snare on 3 only signals half-time), perceptual pulse-salience priors (~700 ms ≈ 86 BPM, librosa's 120-BPM log-normal prior), and genre/BPM-range heuristics; "Pour Some Sugar On Me" is a live example, listed at 176 ("half-time at 88") on one site and 85 on another.

### Cited Findings
- Schreiber, Urbano, Müller, "Music Tempo Estimation: Are We Done Yet?" (TISMIR 2020): ACC1 = within 4 % of reference; ACC2 additionally accepts estimates off by factors 2, 3, ½, ⅓, which "counts some perceptually erroneous estimates as correct". Annotator corrections by Percival & Tzanetakis: Ballroom 32 (4.6 %), ACM Mirum 135 (9.6 %), GTZAN 24 (2.4 %); older systems concentrate errors at −1 tempo octave; high-tempo ballroom genres (Quickstep, Viennese Waltz, Jive) show consistent octave errors; recommendation: report ACC1/ACC2 with error distributions (OE1/OE2) — [TISMIR 10.5334/tismir.43](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.43)
- Henkel et al. 2024, self-supervised tempo estimation: "highly competitive performance when the constraint of finding the precise tempo octave is relaxed" — i.e. octave choice remains the hard, label-dependent part — [arXiv 2401.08891](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=all:tempo%20AND%20all:octave%20AND%20all:estimation&max_results=15)
- SMC Blind Spot 2026: 8 % of SMC tracks are octave errors with correct phase; DBN `min_bpm=55` forces double-tempo output on slow music (21 % of SMC) — [arXiv 2605.12287](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12287)
- Classic octave-selection papers (titles retrieved, abstracts unavailable on Zenodo): Hockman & Fujinaga, "Fast vs Slow: Learning Tempo Octaves from User Data", ISMIR 2010 — [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.1416109](https://zenodo.org/records/1416109); Gkiokas et al., "Reducing Tempo Octave Errors by Periodicity Vector Coding and SVM Learning", ISMIR 2012 — [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.1417438](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1417438); Seyerlehner, Widmer, Schnitzer, "From Rhythm Patterns to Perceived Tempo", ISMIR 2007 — [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.1418372](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1418372); Schreiber & Müller, "A Post-Processing Procedure for Improving Music Tempo Estimates Using Supervised Learning", ISMIR 2017 — [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.1415046](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1415046)
- Perceptual prior: Parncutt (1994) pulse-salience model peaks around 700 ms inter-beat intervals (≈ 86 BPM) — [Music Perception, DOI 10.2307/40285633](https://doi.org/10.2307/40285633). librosa's default tempo prior is centred on 120 BPM with σ = 1 octave — [librosa/feature/rhythm.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/feature/rhythm.py)
- Backbeat: "In a simple 4/4 rhythm these are beats 2 and 4" and "the snare drum is typically used to play the backbeat pattern" — [Wikipedia: Beat (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_(music)). Half-time: the snare "moves to beats 3 of measures (bars) one and two (beats 3 & 7)", "the length of each note is doubled while its frequency is halved", eighths "would sound like 16ths" at the same marking; double-time in jazz "means using note values twice as fast as previously but without changing the pace of the chord progressions" — [Wikipedia: Half-time (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-time_(music))
- Practical heuristic in the wild: beat_this #13 constrains BPM to 78–185 to fix "double/half-time errors found in Trap/DnB" and reports Acc1 89–92 % vs Acc2 95–98 % on GiantSteps — the 6–7 point gap is exactly the octave-ambiguity cost — [CPJKU/beat_this#13](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/issues/13)
- "Pour Some Sugar On Me" (Def Leppard): songbpm.com lists 176 BPM, 4:32, "can also be used half-time at 88 BPM" — [songbpm.com](https://songbpm.com/@def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me); bpmdatabase.com lists 85 BPM (two 2017 Mercury edits) — [bpmdatabase.com](https://www.bpmdatabase.com/music/search/?q=pour+some+sugar+on+me). Note the listings disagree by both octave (85/88 vs 176) and by 3–4 % within the octave (85 vs 88).
- Minimum useful subdivision: in jazz the short swung note stays ≈ 100 ms regardless of tempo ("practical limit on tone duration") — [Friberg & Sundström 2002](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2002.19.3.333); West-African dance drumming shows 80–100 ms subdivisions as a lower limit for meter — [Polak 2018](https://doi.org/10.18061/emr.v12i3-4.4951)

### Inferences
- For a strum-pattern writer the decision rule that follows from the sources: find the snare-dominant onsets in the drum stem; if snare hits alternate with kick at the tracker's beat level (snare on every second beat), the tracker's level is the quarter note (backbeat on 2 & 4). If snare hits fall on every fourth tracked beat only, the tracked level is an eighth and the notated tempo should be halved (half-time feel / tracker double-time error). If snare lands on every tracked beat, the tracker is at the half-note level and tempo should be doubled.
- A sanity window of roughly 70–180 BPM for rock (cf. 78–185 in beat_this #13, librosa's 120 ± 1 octave prior, Parncutt's ~86 BPM salience peak) will select the notation level for most 4/4 rock, with the backbeat test breaking ties.
- At 176 BPM the eighth note is 170 ms and the sixteenth 85 ms — below the ~100 ms practical limit, so D/U patterns written at 176 would need to be quarter-note-only; writing the same song at 88 BPM gives 170 ms eighths for a normal 8-stroke-per-bar D/U pattern. This is the practical argument for the 85–88 BPM listing for ukulele tabs.
- Sites that publish "85 vs 176" are presumably using different automatic estimators; the pipeline should store both the tracked level and the chosen notation level (JAMS `tempo` allows two observations with confidences).

### Gaps
- No retrievable source states how published ukulele/guitar tab sites choose the notation tempo level.
- The onset-density and genre-prior discriminators are described in the ISMIR 2007/2010/2012 papers above, but their abstracts/feature lists could not be fetched; the specific rules are not documented here.

## Key Question 3: Cross-stage alignment — keeping mix, stems, Basic Pitch, chords, Whisper words and the YouTube clock on one timeline

### Takeaway
Each stage has a documented, deterministic offset or truncation (Basic Pitch −188 samples per 2 s window at frame level and a hard-coded 1.8 ms "magic" offset in its note decoder; Demucs 44.1 kHz-only output that silently shortens 48 kHz inputs; audio-separator VR stems 240 samples short; Whisper 20 ms token resolution with word timestamps "inaccurate by several seconds" at utterance level and WhisperX occasionally 10–20 s off after dropped segments; centered STFTs with n_fft//2 padding), so the robust approach is to resample everything to one master clock, correct known offsets analytically, then verify with cross-correlation/DTW and a click-track render.

### Cited Findings

**Basic Pitch**
- Issue #190 (2026-04-15, open, basic-pitch 0.4.0): frame-level `model_output["note"]` "has a growing temporal misalignment… By the end of a ~530-second file the drift reaches −2.7 seconds." Root cause: windows of `AUDIO_N_SAMPLES = 43844` samples, `overlap_len = 30 × 256 = 7680`, so `hop_size = 36164` samples, but after stripping 15 frames each side only `142` frames are kept and `142 × 256 = 36352`, a mismatch of "−188 samples/window"; "Over 321 windows (527-second file) the error accumulates to ~60 000 samples ≈ 2.7 seconds." "The note-level output (`note_events`, `midi_data`) is not affected because it is decoded from seconds-stamped onset/offset detections rather than from the raw frame array." — [spotify/basic-pitch#190](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/issues/190)
- Why note events are unaffected: `note_creation.model_frames_to_time` subtracts a per-window offset `window_offset = (FFT_HOP / AUDIO_SAMPLE_RATE) * (ANNOT_N_FRAMES - (AUDIO_N_SAMPLES / FFT_HOP)) + MAGIC_ALIGNMENT_OFFSET` with `MAGIC_ALIGNMENT_OFFSET = 0.0018` s, and "no explanatory comment precedes it in the code" — [basic_pitch/note_creation.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/note_creation.py)
- Constants: `FFT_HOP = 256`, `AUDIO_SAMPLE_RATE = 22050`, `AUDIO_WINDOW_LENGTH = 2` s, `ANNOTATIONS_FPS = AUDIO_SAMPLE_RATE // FFT_HOP` (integer 86, though 22050/256 = 86.13), `ANNOT_N_FRAMES = 172`, `AUDIO_N_SAMPLES = 22050×2 − 256` — [basic_pitch/constants.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/constants.py)
- Inference: `librosa.load(path, sr=22050, mono=True)`; audio is prepended with `overlap_len/2` zeros; last window right-padded with zeros; `unwrap_output` drops `n_overlapping_frames/2` frames each side and trims to `n_expected_windows = audio_original_length / hop_size` windows; defaults `onset_threshold=0.5`, `frame_threshold=0.3`, `minimum_note_length=127.7` ms, `melodia_trick=True`, `midi_tempo=120` — [basic_pitch/inference.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/inference.py)
- Note amplitude = `np.mean(frames[note_start_idx:i, freq_idx])` (mean frame activation over the note); MIDI `velocity = int(np.round(127 * amplitude))`; `min_note_len=11` frames — [basic_pitch/note_creation.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/note_creation.py)

**Demucs / audio-separator**
- `apply_model(model, mix, shifts=1, split=True, overlap=0.25, transition_power=1., segment=None, …)`; `max_shift = int(0.5 * model.samplerate)`; the mix is padded to `length + 2*max_shift`, shifted by a random offset, and the output cropped `shifted_out[..., max_shift - offset:]` so the shift is undone; `stride = int((1 - overlap) * segment_length)`; segments are blended with a triangular weight `(weight/weight.max())**transition_power` — [demucs/apply.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/demucs/main/demucs/apply.py). API docs: `shifts` "will shift in time wav by a random amount between 0 and 0.5 sec and apply the opposite shift to the output… averaged", gaining "up to 0.2 points" SDR; `split` chunks "predictions will be performed individually on each and concatenated"; `samplerate` is a read-only model property — [demucs docs/api.md](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/blob/main/docs/api.md)
- Issue #563 (2023-10-26, closed) "Shorter Stems than Inputs": user saw stems with fewer samples than input and a "non-linear difference" in the drum stem; resolution: "the sample rate is 44.1kHz but my source is 48K" — [facebookresearch/demucs#563](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/issues/563). Open requests to output at the source rate: #288 (2022) "High-Res Audio Support [24bit/48KHz]", #437 (2023) "Bypassing the 44 100 Hz output files limit", #615 (2024) "change output sample rate" — [demucs issues via gh api](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/issues/437)
- python-audio-separator PR #304 (2026-08-27, open) "Fix VR output truncation at STFT boundaries": "The VR path used centered STFT/ISTFT without preserving the expected waveform length… input 882000 samples, hop 480, remainder 240, output before 881760"; "At 44.1 kHz, both stems were approximately 5.44 ms shorter than the input"; also replaces a hard-coded 44.1 kHz output resample with the configured rate. Related: #299 MDXC `num_overlap` is a divisor (`step = chunk_size / num_overlap`), not seconds; #298 RoFormer tail chunk was "double-weighted" when two start positions overran the end; #307 Demucs `segment` is seconds, default = model-configured — [python-audio-separator issues](https://github.com/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator/pull/304)

**Whisper / WhisperX**
- Whisper constants: `SAMPLE_RATE = 16000`, `N_FFT = 400`, `HOP_LENGTH = 160` (10 ms), `CHUNK_LENGTH = 30` s, `N_FRAMES = 3000`, `TOKENS_PER_SECOND = 50` (20 ms per timestamp token); `load_audio` runs ffmpeg with `-ar 16000` — [whisper/audio.py](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/audio.py)
- Whisper word timestamps: cross-attention weights from `alignment_heads`, z-scored, median-filtered (`medfilt_width=7`), DTW over the attention matrix, indices ÷ 50 tokens/s; `add_word_timestamps` caps word duration at 2× median, truncates at segment edges, merges punctuation — [whisper/timing.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/whisper/main/whisper/timing.py)

- WhisperX #402 (2023-08-02, open), vocals from source separation: WhisperX "fails to detect some lines, and then transcribes the rest… with incorrect time stamps, starting right after the previous lines as if the time spanned by the gap did not exist", resyncing ~20 s later; several users confirm through 2024 — [m-bain/whisperX#402](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX/issues/402). #1276 (2025-10-23): "timestamps are… 10–20 seconds off before the offset eventually resets" on 10-minute files; linked to #1220 (word timestamps "not tightly aligned" since v3.3.3) and #1247 (word boundaries "significantly off" vs Montreal Forced Aligner) — [m-bain/whisperX#1276](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX/issues/1276)

**STFT centring, frame rates, resampling**
- librosa `stft`: "If `True`, the signal `y` is padded so that frame `D[:, t]` is centered at `y[t * hop_length]`" by `n_fft // 2` samples each side (`pad_mode="constant"`); with `center=False` "`D[:, t]` begins at `y[t * hop_length]`"; defaults `n_fft=2048`, `hop_length=win_length // 4`; `center` must be False with `librosa.stream` — [librosa/core/spectrum.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/core/spectrum.py)
- librosa `resample` default `res_type='soxr_hq'`; the docstring makes no statement about delay — [librosa/core/audio.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/core/audio.py)
- Beat This! frames are 441 samples at 22.05 kHz (50 fps) — [arXiv 2407.21658](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21658); madmom beat/downbeat nets run at 100 fps — [madmom beats.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CPJKU/madmom/main/madmom/features/beats.py); Basic Pitch 256 samples at 22.05 kHz; Whisper 160 at 16 kHz.

**YouTube / Opus clock**
- RFC 7845 (Ogg Opus): "The granule position of an audio data page is in units of PCM audio samples at a fixed rate of 48 kHz"; `Pre-skip` is "the number of samples (at 48 kHz) to discard from the decoder output when starting playback", with ≥ 3,840 samples (80 ms) recommended for decoder convergence; muxers "SHOULD take into account the algorithmic delay of the Opus encoder" when setting pre-skip — [RFC 7845](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7845)
- YouTube IFrame API: `player.getCurrentTime()` "Returns the elapsed time in seconds since the video started playing"; `getDuration()` returns 0 until metadata loads; `seekTo` advances "to the closest keyframe before that time unless the player has already downloaded the portion"; playback rates 0.25–2 and cue/load resets rate to 1; the docs give no precision, polling or clock-start statement — [YouTube IFrame API reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/iframe_api_reference)

**Verification tools**
- `scipy.signal.correlate(in1, in2, mode='full', method='auto')` with `correlation_lags` to turn the argmax into a lag; docs include a delay-estimation example — [scipy docs](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.correlate.html)
- `librosa.sequence.dtw(metric='euclidean', step_sizes_sigma=None, weights_add/mul=None, subseq=False, backtrack=True, global_constraints=False, band_rad=0.25)` returns accumulated cost `D` and warping path `wp` (reverse order) — [librosa/sequence.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/sequence.py)
- Sync Toolbox (Müller, Özer, Krause, Prätzlich, Driedger, JOSS 2021): `pip install synctoolbox`; multiscale DTW, memory-restricted MrMsDTW and "high-resolution music synchronization" on chroma features; "state-of-the-art alignment results for a wide range of Western music" — [meinardmueller/synctoolbox](https://github.com/meinardmueller/synctoolbox), [JOSS 10.21105/joss.03434](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.03434)
- Click test: `librosa.clicks(times=…, sr=22050, hop_length=512, click_freq=1000.0, click_duration=0.1, length=None)` "returns a signal with the signal `click` sound placed at each specified time" — [librosa/core/audio.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/core/audio.py)
- `pretty_midi.adjust_times(original_times, new_times)` warps an entire MIDI object to a new timeline — [pretty_midi.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/craffel/pretty-midi/main/pretty_midi/pretty_midi.py)

### Inferences
- Master-clock recipe implied by the sources: decode YouTube audio once (Opus → 48 kHz PCM with pre-skip applied by the decoder), resample once to 44.1 kHz for Demucs, and derive every other input (22.05 kHz for Beat This!/Basic Pitch, 16 kHz for Whisper) from that single 44.1 kHz master so all stages share t = 0. Feeding 48 kHz directly to Demucs or Basic Pitch yields outputs that are 8.2 % shorter or rescaled (demucs #563).
- Use Basic Pitch `note_events` (seconds), never frame indices, unless you re-derive frame times with `hop = 256` and the per-window −188-sample correction from #190; also treat the 1.8 ms `MAGIC_ALIGNMENT_OFFSET` as an unexplained bias and verify it with a click test rather than trusting it.
- Centered STFT (librosa, VR models) places frame t at sample t·hop, so frame-based features are already "centered"; non-centered pipelines (center=False) are late by n_fft/2 (46 ms at n_fft 2048 / 22.05 kHz). Check which convention each model uses before subtracting.
- Demucs with `shifts ≥ 1` re-aligns its output (opposite shift + crop), so stems are time-aligned to the input to within the model's own latency; the known length hazards are sample-rate mismatch (#563) and separator-library STFT truncation (#304, 240 samples), both fixable by `len(stem) == len(mix)` assertions and zero-padding.
- Whisper timestamps are 20 ms-quantised and WhisperX word times can be off by whole segments on sung vocals (#402); align lyrics to the beat grid by cross-correlating the vocal-stem onset envelope with word onsets, or by DTW of the Whisper words against the vocal stem, and flag any segment whose words jump > 1 s.
- For the YouTube player, `getCurrentTime()` is only specified as seconds since playback start; compute the offset between the downloaded audio and the player (e.g. video leading silence vs. extracted audio) once per video by cross-correlating a short clip rendered from the player with the master audio, then store a single `player_offset_s`.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source for the precision/update rate of `getCurrentTime()` or for a systematic offset between YouTube's video start and the audio track in yt-dlp downloads (search engines blocked; yt-dlp issue search returned nothing relevant).
- No published measurement of Basic Pitch's absolute onset bias (the 0.0018 s constant is unexplained in code).
- librosa.resample/soxr delay behaviour is not documented in the docstring; no citation found either way.
- WhisperX's quantitative word-timing error on music (not speech) is unreported; only anecdotal issues exist.

## Key Question 4: Drum-stem groove — swing ratio, straight vs shuffle, and accent/dynamics cues for strum patterns

### Takeaway
Swing ratio is well defined in the literature (long:short duration of consecutive eighths or sixteenths), is tempo-dependent in jazz (≈ 3.5:1 slow → 1:1 fast, short note ≈ 100 ms) but weakly swung and tempo-independent in funk/breakbeats (median 1.2:1 sixteenth swing), and can be read directly from onset intervals on the drum stem; accent cues are best taken from kick/snare placement via a 5-class drum transcriber (ADTOF-trained CRNN, Omnizart, ADTLib, or Riley & Dixon's 7-class stem-based ADT with RMS-derived velocity), while melodic-AMT amplitudes (Basic Pitch) are mean frame activations, not calibrated velocities.

### Cited Findings

**Swing ratio definitions and values**
- Friberg & Sundström (Music Perception 2002), "Swing Ratios and Ensemble Timing in Jazz Performance": "substantial and gradual variation of drummers' swing ratio with respect to tempo", ≈ 3.5:1 at slow tempi down to 1:1 at fast tempi, with the short note constant at ≈ 100 ms ("practical limit on tone duration") — [DOI 10.1525/mp.2002.19.3.333](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2002.19.3.333)
- Honing & de Haas (Music Perception 2008), "Swing Once More": "swing ratio is not kept constant, but is systematically adapted to global tempo" by expert drummers — [DOI 10.1525/mp.2008.25.5.471](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2008.25.5.471)
- Frane (Music Perception 2017), "Swing Rhythm in Classic Drum Breaks from Hip-Hop's Breakbeat Canon": 30 breaks; sixteenth-note swing = "a systematic delay of even-numbered sixteenth divisions"; swing "was common", "median swing ratio = 1.2:1", "uncorrelated with tempo"; introduces "swing density" (proportion of swing-affected events) — [DOI 10.1525/mp.2017.34.3.291](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2017.34.3.291)
- Dittmar, Pfleiderer, Müller, "Automated Estimation of Ride Cymbal Swing Ratios in Jazz Recordings", ISMIR 2015 (title/venue only; Zenodo abstract missing) — [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.1418148](https://zenodo.org/records/1418148). Marchand & Peeters, "Swing Ratio Estimation", 2015 (HAL; title only) — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=title.search:swing%20ratio%20estimation)
- Datseris et al. (Sci. Rep. 2019), "Microtiming Deviations and Swing Feel in Jazz": 12 piano standards, 160 listeners; "quantized versions (without MTDs) were rated slightly higher and versions with expanded MTDs were rated lower" — microtiming beyond the swing ratio is not what makes swing — [arXiv 1904.03442](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=all:swing%20AND%20all:ratio%20AND%20all:music&max_results=15), [DOI 10.1038/s41598-019-55981-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-55981-3)
- Nelias et al. (Commun. Phys. 2022), "Downbeat delays are a key component of swing in jazz": across 456 improvisations, "slightly delayed downbeats synchronized with offbeats" enhance swing — [DOI 10.1038/s42005-022-00995-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-022-00995-z)
- Senn et al. (PLoS ONE 2018), "Groove in drum patterns…": 248 drum patterns; syncopation and event density associate with groove, "microtiming variability demonstrated no significant effect" — [DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0199604](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0199604)

**Accent / dynamics structure in real drum tracks**
- Räsänen et al. 2015 (hi-hat track of a pop recording): timing SD 8.7 ms on a 157 ms interval; "intentional accents on every second 16th note"; amplitude long-range correlations α = 0.63; two-bar-phrase correlation 0.88 for amplitudes vs 0.48 for intervals; drummer played without a metronome — [PLoS ONE 10.1371/journal.pone.0127902](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0127902). Accent patterns are therefore far more repeatable bar-to-bar than microtiming.
- Câmara et al. (Music Perception 2020; JNMR 2022): back-beat performance under "laid-back / on / pushed" instructions shows "systematic differences" in stroke timing and intensity; strategies include timing displacement, flams, and intensity variation — [DOI 10.1525/mp.2020.38.1.1](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2020.38.1.1), [DOI 10.1080/09298215.2022.2150649](https://doi.org/10.1080/09298215.2022.2150649)

**Drum transcription tools and velocity estimation**
- Riley & Dixon (arXiv 2509.24853, Sep 2025), "Enhanced ADT via Drum Stem Source Separation": uses the "Jarredou" drum-kit separation model (plus Demucs v4 for mixtures); expands 5 → 7/8 classes (kick, snare, toms, crash, ride, open/closed hi-hat); velocity = Essentia equal-loudness filter → RMS (1024-sample window, 10 ms hop) → peak within 50 ms of each onset → MIDI 0–127; F-measure (50 ms tolerance) 5-class: MDB 0.89, ENST 0.85, RBMA 0.63; 8-class: MDB 0.84, ENST 0.76 — [arXiv 2509.24853](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24853)
- ADTOF (Zehren, Alunno, Bientinesi 2021): 359 h of real music with crowd-sourced (Clone Hero / Rhythm Gaming World) charts; 5-class vocabulary BD, SD, TT, HH, CY+RD; pretrained CRNN / Frame_RNN models; MIDI/TXT output via notebook — [MZehren/ADTOF](https://github.com/MZehren/ADTOF), [arXiv 2111.11737](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=all:%22drum%20transcription%22&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending&max_results=20). Follow-up on the synthetic-to-real gap: Zehren et al. 2024 — [arXiv 2407.19823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19823)
- ADTLib (Southall et al., ISMIR 2016/2017): kick, snare, hi-hat only; BRNN / soft-attention CNN; outputs onset-time text files and auto-generated drum tab PDF; depends on TensorFlow + madmom — [CarlSouthall/ADTLib](https://github.com/CarlSouthall/ADTLib)
- Omnizart drum model = Wei, Wu, Su, "Improving Automatic Drum Transcription Using Large-Scale Audio-to-MIDI Aligned Data", ICASSP 2021: 1,565 polyphonic mixtures with aligned MIDI, CNN "compares favorably with state-of-the-art ADT systems"; CLI outputs a `.mid` next to the audio — [DOI 10.1109/icassp39728.2021.9414409](https://doi.org/10.1109/icassp39728.2021.9414409), [omnizart drum CLI](https://music-and-culture-technology-lab.github.io/omnizart-doc/drum/cli.html)
- E-GMD (Callender, Hawthorne, Engel 2020): 444 h, 43 kits, "the first with human-performed velocity annotations"; models trained to predict velocity "produce outputs with improved perceptual quality, despite similar results on classification metrics" — [arXiv 2004.00188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00188)
- Recent velocity-aware ADT: "Noise-to-Notes" (Sony, Sep 2025) predicts onset and velocity with diffusion — [arXiv 2509.21739](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=all:%22drum%20transcription%22&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending&max_results=20); "Separate-and-Detect" (Aug 2026) joint stem generation + transcription — [arXiv 2608.01093](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=all:%22drum%20transcription%22&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending&max_results=20)
- Melodic AMT velocity: Transkun (piano only, MAESTRO-trained) reports onset+offset+velocity F1 0.9487 on MAESTRO v3 — [Yujia-Yan/Transkun](https://github.com/Yujia-Yan/Transkun). Basic Pitch velocity is `round(127 × mean frame activation)` — an activation statistic, not a trained loudness target — [basic_pitch/note_creation.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/note_creation.py)
- No madmom drum-transcription module exists in the madmom repository; "DrumFormer" could not be located in arXiv's drum-transcription listing (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Straight-vs-shuffle decision from the drum stem: take hi-hat/ride onsets (or all onsets) within each beat, compute the position of the off-beat onset as a fraction of the beat; a ratio ≈ 1:1 (off-beat at 0.50) is straight, ≈ 2:1 (0.67) is a triplet shuffle, and Frane's 1.2:1 (0.545) is "light sixteenth swing" that should still be written straight. Given Friberg's 100 ms floor and Frane's tempo-independence in breakbeats, test at both eighth and sixteenth levels and report the swing ratio with its level (JAMS `tempo`-style confidence pair).
- Accent cues for D/U patterns: snare positions (backbeat) → accented down-strums; kick positions → bass-emphasis or muted hits; because amplitude accent patterns repeat at 0.88 correlation across two-bar phrases (Räsänen), aggregate kick/snare histograms over 2–4-bar windows rather than per bar before writing an accent pattern.
- Use the stem-based transcriber's RMS-within-50 ms velocity (Riley & Dixon) for accents rather than Basic Pitch amplitudes; Basic Pitch amplitudes are only usable as a relative, within-track ranking.

### Gaps
- Abstracts/method details for Dittmar et al. 2015 (ride-cymbal swing ratio) and Marchand & Peeters 2015 were unavailable (Zenodo "[TODO]"); only titles/venues are confirmed.
- No source found for "DrumFormer"; could not confirm it exists under that name.
- No published accuracy figure for Basic Pitch's amplitude as a velocity estimator.
- No study found that maps drum accents to strumming-pattern accents directly; the mapping above is an inference.

## Key Question 5: "Pushed" chord changes — snapping anticipations without losing musical meaning

### Takeaway
The anticipated ("pushed") change is documented in the theory literature as a chord/bass event arriving an eighth (or a sixteenth) before the beat it belongs to — Wikipedia's "anticipated bass" is placed "on the 2+ and the 4… thus anticipating the third and first beats" — but no retrievable source describes an algorithm for quantising such changes; the defensible design is to quantise chord boundaries on an eighth-note grid, attribute an off-beat change to the following downbeat for the bar-level chord label, and keep the anticipation as a strum-pattern accent.

### Cited Findings
- "Anticipated bass is a bass tone that comes syncopated shortly before the downbeat… timing can vary, but it usually occurs on the 2+ and the 4 of the 4/4 time, thus anticipating the third and first beats" (son montuno / Afro-Cuban tumbao) — [Wikipedia: Syncopation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncopation)
- Classical definition: an anticipation "is basically a note of the second chord played early" — [Wikipedia: Anticipation (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticipation_(music))
- Câmara, "Groove" (Oxford Handbook of Critical Concepts in Music Theory, 2018) lists "swing" and "anticipated beats" among groove components in contemporary styles — [DOI 10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190454746.013.17](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190454746.013.17)
- Chord-estimation systems conventionally work beat-synchronously with meter position as context (Mauch 2010: a DBN over key, "meter position" and beat-synchronous features "results in significant increase in recognition accuracy") — [Mauch 2010 thesis via OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=improving%20automatic%20drum%20transcription%20large-scale%20audio-to-midi%20aligned%20data&per-page=4)
- Chord label boundaries are inherently subjective: 4 expert annotators agree only 73 % on major/minor labels over 50 songs (CASD) — [Koops et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/09298215.2019.1613436)
- JAMS `chord` annotations carry `time` and `duration` in seconds with Harte labels, so a pushed change can be stored at its true sub-beat time while a separate `beat_position`-indexed representation holds the bar-level label — [JAMS namespace docs](https://jams.readthedocs.io/en/stable/namespace.html)
- Drummers realise "pushed" feel partly through intensity and timing displacement of the back-beat — [Câmara et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2020.38.1.1)

### Inferences
- Snapping rule consistent with the sources: quantise chord-change times to the eighth grid derived from the tempo map (Q1); if a change lands on the "and" of 4 (or the "and" of 2 for a mid-bar change), label the next downbeat's bar with the new chord (the push "anticipates the first beat"), and mark the push by placing the chord symbol/accent on that off-beat up-strum in the D/U pattern rather than moving the bar line. This preserves the bar-level harmony while keeping the rhythmic meaning.
- Use the swing/shuffle estimate (Q4) before snapping: with a 2:1 shuffle the "and" sits at 0.67 of the beat, so an eighth-grid snap must use the swung grid or pushes will be misattributed.
- Chord changes detected 40–80 ms early are within annotator noise (CASD disagreement; typical chord-model hop sizes) and should be snapped to the beat, not treated as pushes; a push is a change that is closer to the preceding off-beat than to either adjacent beat.

### Gaps
- No lesson, textbook or paper could be retrieved that uses the exact term "pushed chords" (search engines blocked/CAPTCHA'd); the terminology is attested only indirectly via "anticipated bass/beats" and the Câmara "pushed" timing instruction.
- No published algorithm or evaluation for quantising anticipated chord changes in automatic chord estimation output was found.
