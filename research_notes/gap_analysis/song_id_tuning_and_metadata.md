# Cheap wins before heavy models: song identification, metadata priors, tuning-offset detection, and stereo-field tricks (research notes, compiled 2026-10-01)

Scope: what a Python/Windows 11 pipeline can do *before* Demucs/Basic Pitch/Whisper run, for a YouTube music-video input. Target songs: "Summer of '69" (Bryan Adams, 1984/85) and "Pour Some Sugar On Me" (Def Leppard, 1987). Every fact is dated to the source as found on 2026-10-01 unless otherwise stated. The web-search budget for this session was exhausted part-way through; several items are therefore recorded as gaps rather than guessed.

## Q1. Audio fingerprinting: which service/library identifies the song, at what cost and under what terms?

### Takeaway
Chromaprint/AcoustID (pyacoustid) is free for non-commercial use, returns MusicBrainz recording IDs directly, and is the natural first hop into structured metadata; AudD is the cheapest *commercial* API with published pricing (300 free requests, then $5/1,000) and returns MusicBrainz/Spotify/Apple IDs plus a `timecode` into the song; shazamio is a free reverse-engineered Shazam client with no published terms. All of these are exact-recording fingerprinters: none of the primary sources claims cover or live-version identification, so a music video containing a live take or re-record may fail.

### Cited Findings

**AcoustID / Chromaprint / pyacoustid**
- AcoustID web service: register the application to obtain a client API key; "Do not make more than 3 requests per second"; "This service is provided for free for non-commercial use only"; commercial users must sign up at acoustid.biz; lookup requires `duration`, `fingerprint`, `client`; optional `meta` returns recordings, release groups, artists and MusicBrainz IDs with confidence scores — [AcoustID Web Service docs](https://acoustid.org/webservice)
- Commercial plans (acoustid.biz): Small EUR 50/month for 1 million searches, Medium EUR 100/month for 15 million, Large EUR 500/month for 150 million, no rate limiting; first 10,000 searches free on commercial plans; "We do not provide refunds"; non-commercial use has "unlimited searches" at 3 req/s — [acoustid.biz](https://acoustid.biz/)
- AcoustID database size (stats page, undated snapshot read 2026-10-01): 94,232,371 fingerprints, 22,094,588 recordings, 76,315,405 AcoustIDs — [AcoustID stats](https://acoustid.org/stats)
- pyacoustid requires Python 3.10+ and either the Chromaprint shared library or the `fpcalc` binary on `$PATH` (or `FPCALC` env var); on Windows it "search[es] more shared-library locations for the Chromaprint library"; `acoustid.match(apikey, path)` yields `(score, recording_id, title, artist)`; the library enforces "thread-safe API rate limiting to 3 queries per second" — [beetbox/pyacoustid](https://github.com/beetbox/pyacoustid)
- Chromaprint is "a client-side library that implements a custom algorithm for extracting fingerprints from any audio source"; it "only calculates audio fingerprints from the provided raw uncompressed audio data. It does not deal with audio file formats"; Windows build available as `chromaprint-fpcalc-1.6.1-windows-x86_64.zip` (1.8 MB) — [acoustid.org/chromaprint](https://acoustid.org/chromaprint)
- How Chromaprint works (author's blog, 2011): audio is resampled to 11025 Hz, frame size 4096 (0.371 s) with 2/3 overlap; spectrogram is converted to 12-bin chroma ("only interested in notes, not octaves"); a sliding 16x12 window applies 16 filters producing 2-bit results packed into one 32-bit integer per position; the only robustness test shown is against low-bitrate MP3, with no discussion of time-stretch/pitch-shift robustness — [How does Chromaprint work?](https://oxygene.sk/2011/01/how-does-chromaprint-work/)

**AudD**
- Pricing (audd.io, 2026): "300 requests are free on signup, no card needed"; pay-as-you-go "$5 per 1,000 requests"; volume plans 100K/month $450, 200K $800, 500K $1,800; stream monitoring $45/stream/month (AudD DB) or $25 (own catalogue) — [AudD](https://audd.io/); also summarised at [AudD pricing article](https://audd.io/resources/articles/music-recognition-api-pricing)
- Returned metadata: "artist, title, album, label, release date, and identifiers such as ISRC and UPC", plus links to Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer, MusicBrainz; marketing says fingerprinting is "designed to match recordings that have been altered — remixes, slowed-and-reverb versions, pitch shifts, and edits" but does not explicitly claim cover or live-version matching; ToS at https://audd.io/terms/ (page body could not be retrieved) — [AudD](https://audd.io/)
- API parameters: one of `file` (multipart) or `url` (HTTP(S) file URL, example `https://audd.tech/example.mp3`); `return` accepts `apple_music, spotify, deezer, musicbrainz` (Napster removed); response includes `timecode` — "the time in the recognized song when the fragment you sent is played"; standard endpoint limit 10 MB, "short audio clip", 0.1-1.5 s latency; `/enterprise` endpoint handles "days-long files" with server-side chunking; `/streams` for live monitoring — [AudD docs](https://docs.audd.io/)

**ACRCloud**
- No fixed public price list; "most tiers are quoted per use case"; new accounts start with a free trial; Music Recognition metered per request; Broadcast Monitoring ~ $26/stream/month; Enterprise negotiated — [apis.io ACRCloud plans](https://apis.io/plans/acrcloud/acrcloud-plans-pricing/)
- A third-party comparison states ACRCloud's free tier is a 14-day trial with no card and "free users can send 100 queries per day" (unverified against ACRCloud's own site, which was unreachable) — [autovj.club comparison, 2026](https://autovj.club/en/guide/song-recognition/)
- ACRCloud markets a database of "more than 150 million tracks", a separate **Cover Song Identification** solution, and humming recognition — [ACRCloud](https://www.acrcloud.com/pricing/) (pricing page served homepage content without prices)
- AudD's own comparison page positions itself against ACRCloud on published pricing — [AudD vs ACRCloud](https://audd.io/resources/articles/audd-vs-acrcloud.html) (vendor source; treat as marketing)

**Shazam via shazamio (unofficial)**
- "ShazamIO is a free asynchronous library from reverse engineered Shazam API written in Python 3.10+ with asyncio and aiohttp"; recognises "from a file, a `pathlib.Path`, or the bytes of one"; returns title, artist, artwork, provider links including Apple Music; the README contains no legality/terms statement and no rate-limit documentation — [shazamio/ShazamIO](https://github.com/shazamio/ShazamIO)
- PyPI: shazamio 0.8.1 released 11 June 2025, Python >=3.10,<4.0, MIT licence — [PyPI shazamio](https://pypi.org/project/shazamio/); a search snippet reported shazamio-core 1.2.0 released 29 Aug 2026 (PyPI page itself could not be fetched to verify) — [PyPI shazamio-core](https://pypi.org/project/shazamio-core/)

**Self-hosted / open-source fingerprinters (need your own reference corpus)**
- Dejavu: spectrogram-peak fingerprints with locality-sensitive hashes stored in MySQL (default) or PostgreSQL; test on 45 songs: 100 % from disk, mic: 60 % with 1 s, 95.6 % with 2 s, 100 % with 5+ s; "No performance degradation" for compressed streaming vs lossless; "excels at recognition of exact signals with reasonable amounts of noise"; 109 open issues and no stated Python-version support (effectively unmaintained) — [worldveil/dejavu](https://github.com/worldveil/dejavu)
- audfprint (Dan Ellis): landmark-based fingerprints; needs a database built with `new`/`add`; matches with as few as 5-6 temporally consistent hashes; "random chance will result in fewer than 1% of raw common hashes being temporally consistent"; requires ffmpeg — [dpwe/audfprint](https://github.com/dpwe/audfprint)
- Panako: Java (JDK 11+), AGPL-3.0; "audio queries can be identified reliably and quickly even if they has been sped up, time stretched or pitch shifted with respect to the reference audio"; requires `panako store` of reference audio first; README warns about patents US7627477 B2 and US6990453 — [JorenSix/Panako](https://github.com/JorenSix/Panako)

### Inferences
- For a hobby/non-commercial tab pipeline, the zero-cost path is: decode YouTube audio -> `fpcalc`/pyacoustid -> AcoustID -> MusicBrainz recording/work IDs; AudD's 300 free requests are a cheap second opinion and uniquely give a `timecode` (useful for aligning a music-video edit against the album master).
- Chromaprint's chroma-based design discards octave information but *not* absolute pitch class, so a recording pitch-shifted by a semitone (e.g. a vari-speeded video or a half-step-down live take) will produce a different fingerprint; only Panako (self-hosted, needs the reference audio) explicitly claims pitch-shift/time-stretch robustness. None of the exact-match services claims to identify live versions or covers; ACRCloud sells a separate cover-ID product for that.
- shazamio's lack of any terms makes it a prototype-only tool; Shazam has no public API, so production use carries ToS risk (not quantified here — see gaps).
- Fingerprint a 30-120 s excerpt from the middle of the video rather than the whole file: this skips intro dialogue common in music videos and keeps AudD under the 10 MB limit (Chromaprint's own blog documents 0.371 s frames, so an excerpt of tens of seconds is ample).

### Gaps
- No primary-source accuracy figures for any service on *YouTube music-video audio with intro dialogue, live takes or covers*; search budget ran out before locating ISMIR fingerprint-robustness papers (Semantic Scholar API returned HTTP 429).
- Shazam's terms of service regarding reverse-engineered clients were not retrieved.
- ACRCloud's own pricing page and free-tier numbers could not be fetched (403/redirect); the "100 queries/day" figure is third-party.
- `fpcalc` default `-length` (commonly 120 s) could not be confirmed from the repository page fetched.

## Q2. Metadata sources: which databases give key, BPM, time signature, chords, tuning — and what is their 2026 status?

### Takeaway
No free database reliably supplies all four of key/BPM/time-signature/tuning. AcousticBrainz is dead (dump only), Spotify Audio Features is dead for new apps since 27 Nov 2024 and still returns 403 in 2026, MusicBrainz stores neither key nor BPM. The usable 2026 cheap sources are: GetSongBPM API (free, CC BY 4.0, 3,000 req/h, attribution link required) for tempo/key; Hooktheory TheoryTab pages for per-section key/BPM/chord progressions (but their terms forbid scraping, and the public API only exposes aggregate chord-probability data); Wikidata properties P826 (tonality) and P1725 (BPM) where populated.

### Cited Findings
- MusicBrainz Work attributes are name, type, aliases, lyrics languages, ISWC, MBID, disambiguation, annotation — "Neither 'Key' nor tempo/BPM are listed as Work attributes" — [MusicBrainz Work docs](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/Work)
- MusicBrainz API rate limits: 1 request/second per source IP on average; anonymous/blank user-agents and `python-musicbrainz/0.7.3`, Headphones, beets throttled at 50 req/s globally; global 300 req/s; User-Agent must identify the app and contact ("Application name/<version> ( contact-email )"); no explicit commercial-use prohibition — [MusicBrainz API Rate Limiting](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_API/Rate_Limiting)
- AcousticBrainz shutdown (announced 16 Feb 2022): data "lacked sufficient quality"; "Musical key data was unreliable", "BPM predictions contained errors without confidence level indicators"; submissions stopped within a month, full site shutdown early 2023; a full data dump remains downloadable; MetaBrainz instead planned to put tempo estimation into Picard for local computation and lean on ListenBrainz tagging — [MetaBrainz blog](https://blog.metabrainz.org/2022/02/16/acousticbrainz-making-a-hard-decision-to-end-the-project/)
- beets `acousticbrainz` plugin: "This plugin is now deprecated since the AcousicBrainz project has been shut down"; it fetched `bpm`, `initial_key`, `key_strength`, `chords_key`, `chords_scale`, moods etc.; recommended replacement is `beets-xtractor` (runs Essentia locally) — [beets docs](https://beets.readthedocs.io/en/stable/plugins/acousticbrainz.html)
- Spotify deprecated, on 27 Nov 2024: Related Artists, Recommendations, Audio Features, Audio Analysis, Featured Playlists, Category Playlists, 30-second preview URLs in multi-get, and editorial/algorithmic playlists; apps "with existing extended mode Web API access ... remain unaffected"; applies to new apps and dev-mode apps without pending extension; reason given: "creating a more secure platform" — [Spotify developer blog, 2024-11-27](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2024-11-27-changes-to-the-web-api)
- Community threads through 2025-2026 report Audio Features still returning 403 for new apps with no official replacement — [Spotify community thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Access-to-deprecated-Audio-Features-endpoint-for-personal/td-p/7419715) (page body 403'd on fetch; status from search summary) and [dev.to, 2026](https://dev.to/birrings/spotifys-audiofeatures-api-died-in-2024-heres-what-i-built-to-replace-it-3dn3)
- GetSongBPM: "open source database of beats per minutes (CC BY 4.0)"; API key required (`api_key` URL param or `X-API-KEY` header); "limit of 3000 requests per hour"; free, but "a link back to GetSongBPM.com is REQUIRED (website or store listing), or the account will be suspended without notice"; returns tempo and key — [GetSongBPM API](https://getsongbpm.com/api) (page 403'd on direct fetch; details from search summary and [metacpan WebService::GetSongBPM](https://metacpan.org/pod/WebService::GetSongBPM))
- SongBPM.com (Spotify-derived data): "Summer Of '69" 139 BPM, 3:36; "Pour Some Sugar On Me" 176 BPM (88 half-time), 4:32; key fields present but not populated in the fetched HTML — [songbpm Summer of '69](https://songbpm.com/@bryan-adams/summer-of-69), [songbpm Pour Some Sugar](https://songbpm.com/@def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me)
- Tunebat: pages returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch (no public API found); search snippet reported Summer of '69 as D major, 139 BPM — [Tunebat](https://tunebat.com/Info/Summer-Of-69-Bryan-Adams/0GONea6G2XdnHWjNZd6zt3)
- Hooktheory API: OAuth2 bearer token from `POST users/auth`; tokens now "expire about an hour after it is issued"; endpoints `GET trends/nodes` (next-chord probabilities) and `GET trends/songs?cp=4,1` (songs containing a progression, 20/page, returns artist, song, section, TheoryTab URL); "Requests limited to 10 every 10 seconds"; the API "does **not** provide detailed chord progressions for individual songs" — [Hooktheory API docs](https://www.hooktheory.com/api/trends/docs)
- Hooktheory terms: "no third party may copy, scrape, bulk-download, text-and-data mine, or redistribute the website including the TheoryTab database or any substantial portion of it"; forbids use "to train, fine-tune, or evaluate models" and circumventing rate limits; exceptions only via "a written data license or API terms"; TDM reservation expressed in machine-readable form — [Hooktheory terms](https://www.hooktheory.com/terms)
- Hooktheory TheoryTab, "Summer of 69": D major throughout except bridge in F major; 139 BPM; 4/4; verse I-V, pre-chorus/chorus vi-V-I-IV, bridge I-IV-V-IV; melody range A3-A4 — [TheoryTab Summer of 69](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/bryan-adams/summer-of-69)
- Hooktheory TheoryTab, "Pour Some Sugar on Me": 85 BPM, 4/4; verse C# minor (i(no3)), pre-chorus F# major, chorus E major (I(no3)), bridge E Mixolydian (VII most used); power chords (no 3rd) and suspended chords — [TheoryTab Pour Some Sugar](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me)
- Wikidata property P826 "tonality" — "key of a musical composition", value must be a tonal system or mode; example Piano Sonata No. 31 -> A-flat major — [Wikidata P826](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P826)
- Wikidata property P1725 "beats per minute" — "tempo of a musical work, indicated numerically"; quantity, range 0-320 bpm; for works and tracks, not albums — [Wikidata P1725](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P1725)
- Essentia (the AcousticBrainz backend) is the suggested local replacement: `KeyExtractor` computes HPCP and applies key estimation with 16 profile options (default `bgate`), parameters `tuningFrequency` (default 440) and `averageDetuningCorrection` (default true, "shifts a pcp to the nearest tempered bin"), outputs key, scale, strength — [Essentia KeyExtractor](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_KeyExtractor.html)
- madmom provides `CNNKeyRecognitionProcessor` outputting one of 24 keys (docs page body not retrievable beyond headings) — [madmom key docs](https://madmom.readthedocs.io/en/latest/modules/features/key.html)

### Inferences
- Realistic 2026 metadata chain: fingerprint -> MusicBrainz recording -> (a) MusicBrainz work -> Wikidata (via MB-Wikidata links) for P826/P1725 when present; (b) GetSongBPM lookup by artist+title for tempo/key; (c) Hooktheory TheoryTab consulted *manually* for section-level chords/key (automated scraping violates their terms). Everything else (time signature, tuning) must be estimated locally (Essentia/madmom/librosa) or hand-entered.
- The 176 vs 85/88 BPM disagreement for "Pour Some Sugar On Me" is a classic octave-tempo ambiguity (Spotify-derived data counted eighth notes); treat any database BPM as a prior with a x2/x0.5 ambiguity and resolve against a local beat tracker.
- No database found stores *guitar tuning*; tuning must be inferred locally (Q3) or from tab sites.

### Gaps
- GetSongBPM's returned field list (whether time signature is included) could not be verified because the API page returned 403; the Perl client shows `tempo` and key only.
- Ultimate Guitar and Chordify terms-of-service pages returned 404/403, so their scraping clauses were not captured; Chordify has no public API that I could confirm.
- Discogs was not verified; from the sources read, no evidence that Discogs stores key or BPM.
- Tunebat has no documented API; its anti-bot 403 suggests scraping is blocked.

## Q3. Tuning-offset detection and correction (A != 440, Eb/D standard, drop D, tape drift)

### Takeaway
librosa and Essentia both estimate only the *sub-semitone* deviation from A440 (librosa returns [-0.5, 0.5) of a bin; Essentia returns -35..+65 cents), so they can detect tape-speed/vari-speed drift but cannot by themselves distinguish Eb-standard from E-standard; that is a key/chord-label question answered by comparing detected key/bass notes with the chord shapes a tab implies. Correcting a fractional offset is best done by passing the measured tuning into the analysis (chroma/CQT/HPCP) rather than resampling; whole-semitone transposition for transcription should be a relabel, not a pitch-shift of the audio.

### Cited Findings
- `librosa.estimate_tuning(*, y=None, sr=22050, S=None, n_fft=2048, resolution=0.01, bins_per_octave=12)` returns "estimated tuning deviation (fractions of a bin)" in [-0.5, 0.5); resolution 0.01 "corresponds to measurements in cents"; reference is A440 — [librosa.estimate_tuning docs](https://librosa.org/doc/main/generated/librosa.estimate_tuning.html) (from search summary; docs site returned 404 on direct fetch)
- Implementation: `piptrack` finds dominant frequencies per STFT bin via parabolic interpolation; magnitudes below `threshold = np.median(mag[pitch_mask])` are discarded; `pitch_tuning` computes `residual = np.mod(bins_per_octave * hz_to_octs(frequencies), 1.0)`, maps residuals >0.5 to negative, histograms them across [-0.5, 0.5) and returns the histogram peak — [librosa core/pitch.py](https://github.com/librosa/librosa/blob/main/librosa/core/pitch.py)
- librosa issue #925 documents that CQT-based functions estimate tuning automatically when `tuning=None`, leading to "inconsistent/double calculation of tuning in cqt" if the caller also estimates it — [librosa issue #925](https://github.com/librosa/librosa/issues/925)
- Essentia `TuningFrequency` (adapted from E. Gómez, MIREX 2005 key estimation) takes spectral-peak frequencies/magnitudes and outputs `tuningFrequency` (Hz) and `tuningCents` in the range -35 to 65 cents; default `resolution` 1 cent; used in key estimation and HPCP computation — [Essentia TuningFrequency](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_TuningFrequency.html)
- Essentia `TuningFrequencyExtractor` wraps this over a whole signal with `frameSize` 4096 and `hopSize` 2048 — [Essentia TuningFrequencyExtractor](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_TuningFrequencyExtractor.html)
- Essentia `KeyExtractor` accepts `tuningFrequency` (default 440 Hz) which "adapts semitone crossover frequencies for HPCP computation" and `averageDetuningCorrection` — [Essentia KeyExtractor](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_KeyExtractor.html)
- `librosa.effects.pitch_shift(y, *, sr, n_steps, bins_per_octave=12, res_type="soxr_hq", scale=False)` is implemented as phase-vocoder `time_stretch` at `rate = 2.0 ** (-n_steps / bins_per_octave)` followed by `resample` and `fix_length`; `n_steps` may be fractional — [librosa effects.py](https://github.com/librosa/librosa/blob/main/librosa/effects.py)
- pyrubberband is "A python wrapper for rubberband" providing `time_stretch(y, sr, factor, rbargs={})` and `pitch_shift`; "All processing is done via the command-line through files on disk" and it needs the Rubber Band CLI binary; README gives macOS/brew instructions only, no Windows instructions — [bmcfee/pyrubberband](https://github.com/bmcfee/pyrubberband)
- Rubber Band Library v4.0.0 (released 25 Oct 2024) ships a Windows command-line executable; dual-licensed GPL / paid commercial — [Breakfast Quay Rubber Band](https://breakfastquay.com/rubberband/)
- Guitar tuning definitions: Eb tuning = all strings one semitone down (Hendrix, Nirvana's *In Utero*, Guns N' Roses "Sweet Child o' Mine"); D standard = D-G-C-F-A-D (Elliott Smith, Metallica "The Thing That Should Not Be"); Drop D = only low E to D, giving a DAD open power chord (Beatles "Dear Prudence", Soundgarden, Tool) — [Wikipedia Guitar tunings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar_tunings)
- Wow and flutter: wow <4 Hz, flutter >4 Hz speed irregularities; professional tape machines ~0.02 % weighted flutter ("considered inaudible"); high-end cassette ~0.08 %; digital jitter "far below" audibility — [Wikipedia Wow and flutter measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wow_and_flutter_measurement)
- "Summer of '69" is taught in standard tuning (E-A-D-G-B-E), no capo, key D major with bridge in F — [Jon MacLennan lesson](https://www.jonmaclennan.com/blog/summer-of-69-chords)

### Inferences
- Because both libraries fold tuning into a single semitone, detecting *half-step-down* tuning is a two-stage test: (1) estimate the fractional offset; (2) if the chord/key model and the known tab disagree by exactly one semitone (e.g. tab says E5/A5/B5 shapes but detected roots are Eb/Ab/Bb), conclude Eb standard; drop D shows up as a low-D bass note under otherwise standard-tuned chord roots. For the two target songs: Summer of '69 is documented as standard tuning; no source was found on Def Leppard's tuning for "Pour Some Sugar On Me" (see gaps).
- For fractional offsets (a few cents to ~40 cents, typical of vari-speed or tape drift), pass the measured tuning into `chroma_cqt(tuning=...)`/`KeyExtractor(tuningFrequency=...)` rather than resampling; resampling by `2**(cents/1200)` is also cheap and exact if a model (e.g. Basic Pitch) has no tuning parameter — a plain `resample` with no time-stretch preserves transients better than a phase vocoder, at the cost of a <3 % tempo change.
- For a whole-semitone (Eb) case, do not pitch-shift the audio before transcription: transcribe at the recorded pitch and relabel notes +1 semitone for a standard-tuned ukulele arrangement, or keep concert pitch and transpose the chord symbols (Q4). Pitch-shifting via `librosa.effects.pitch_shift` (phase vocoder) introduces smearing that hurts onset-based transcription; Rubber Band is higher quality but needs the Windows CLI and files on disk.
- Professional 1980s tape flutter of ~0.02 % is far below the 1-cent resolution of the tuning estimators; real-world "80s drift" is more likely deliberate vari-speed (constant offset) than wow, so a single global tuning estimate per track suffices; estimate on a few 30 s windows and compare to detect a non-constant offset.

### Gaps
- No ISMIR tuning-estimation evaluation paper was retrieved (Semantic Scholar API rate-limited; search budget exhausted) — no published accuracy numbers for `estimate_tuning` vs Essentia.
- No source found stating the guitar tuning used on "Pour Some Sugar On Me" (Guitar Pro page lists four guitar tracks but no tuning in the HTML; Ultimate Guitar page 404'd).
- No source found documenting vari-speed or pitch offset on *Hysteria* or *Reckless* masters.
- madmom's tuning handling for key recognition could not be read (docs page truncated).

## Q4. Capo / transposition decision for ukulele

### Takeaway
Libraries exist for the mechanics (pychord transposes chord symbols by semitones; music21 transposes by interval with correct enharmonic spelling), but I found no published algorithm or arranger heuristic for *choosing* a ukulele capo/transposition; the decision rule must be designed in-house (count open-shape chords per candidate transposition, penalise capo height).

### Cited Findings
- pychord: parse chords like "Am7" or "C#/G"; `transpose()` by semitones ("Am7/G" +3 -> "Cm7/Bb"); component notes; enharmonic equality (C# == Db); reverse lookup from notes; `ChordProgression` with transposition; Python 3.10+; `pip install pychord` — [yuma-m/pychord](https://github.com/yuma-m/pychord)
- music21: `pitch.Pitch('d#').transpose('M7')` -> `C##4`, i.e. transposition "preserves enharmonic spelling based on the interval"; `getEnharmonic()` / `getLowerEnharmonic()` available — [music21 User's Guide ch. 3](https://www.music21.org/music21docs/usersGuide/usersGuide_03_pitches.html)
- Known chord vocabularies for the targets (from Q2): Summer of '69 uses D, A, Bm, G (bridge F, Bb, C) — [Jon MacLennan](https://www.jonmaclennan.com/blog/summer-of-69-chords); Pour Some Sugar On Me is built on power chords in C# minor / E major with an E Mixolydian bridge — [TheoryTab](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me)

### Inferences
- A simple scoring rule: for each transposition t in 0..11 (and capo c in 0..5 with sounding key preserved), map every chord to its ukulele shape; score = number of chords playable as open shapes (C, G, Am, F, D, A, Em, Dm, E7, G7, C7 etc.) minus a penalty per barre chord and per capo fret; prefer t=0/c=0 on ties so the arrangement stays in the recording's key for play-along. For Summer of '69 (D, A, Bm, G) this already scores fully open on GCEA; for Pour Some Sugar (E/C#m/F#/B) a +1 transposition to F or a -4 to C would trade play-along fidelity for open shapes.
- Use pychord for the transposition arithmetic and music21 only if you need spelled-correct notation output.

### Gaps
- No ukulele-arranger source on capo/transposition heuristics was reachable (ukutabs returned 403; search budget exhausted). The scoring rule above is an inference, not sourced practice.
- No Python library found that encodes ukulele chord-shape difficulty.

## Q5. Stereo-field tricks for isolating rhythm guitars (80s double-tracking, M/S, centre-cancel, ADRess/DUET, stereo as Demucs input)

### Takeaway
Classical panning-based separation (ADRess 2004, DUET) is cheap and works only when sources sit at distinct, static pan positions in a dry mix — true of some 80s hard-panned double-tracked rhythm guitars but defeated by reverb and by sources sharing a position. Centre-cancel (L-R) removes centred vocal/bass/kick but leaves stereo reverb and anything off-centre; it is a reasonable pre-filter to expose hard-panned guitars. Demucs already consumes stereo and outputs 44.1 kHz stereo stems, so the cheapest exploitation of panning is to feed L-only / R-only / side-only mixes to the chord/pitch models as extra views rather than to build a separate separator.

### Cited Findings
- ADRess (Barry, Lawlor, Coyle, DAFx 2004): operates in the frequency domain using the "intensity ratio between audio signals input to left and right ears"; builds an azimuth-frequency plane and resynthesises a source from a chosen azimuth; constraints: "the recording must be made in a specific fashion and the sources do not move position within the stereo field"; "co-located sources cannot be separated"; reverberant mixes degrade performance; the method "assumes relatively anechoic or dry recording conditions" and was evaluated mainly on prepared mixes — [DAFx 2004 paper P_240](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2004/P_240.PDF)
- DUET: "Blind Source Separation algorithm for stereo audio that depends on an amplitude-phase 2D histogram built from the differences between the two channels"; requires W-disjoint orthogonality (sources do not overlap in time-frequency), approximately true for speech; the search summary of the CQT-DUET paper states "musical signals do not adhere to the W-Disjoint Orthogonality condition, so DUET is not suitable for the separation of musical instruments" — [DUET (Rickard)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227143748_The_DUET_blind_source_separation_algorithm); [DUET using CQT, IEEE 2011](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5946379/) (abstract page body not retrievable; claim from search summary)
- Audacity "Vocal Reduction and Isolation": centre cancellation uses the "invert and mix" method to remove audio common to both channels; "stereo reverberation will not be fully removed because it is independently distributed over the whole stereo image"; off-centre sources remain; an "Isolate Center" mode extracts centred material as mono with a Strength control — [Audacity manual](https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/vocal_reduction_and_isolation.html)
- Demucs: outputs "44.1 kHz in stereo WAV" by default; handles stereo and mono inputs with automatic resampling; models `htdemucs` (default), `htdemucs_ft` (4x slower), `htdemucs_6s` adds guitar and piano stems — "Quick testing seems to show okay quality for `guitar`, but a lot of bleeding and artifacts for the `piano` source"; stems are rescaled to prevent clipping, which "may affect relative volume balance" — [facebookresearch/demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)
- Hybrid Demucs paper: "end-to-end hybrid source separation, letting the model decide which domain is best suited for each source"; won Sony's Music Demixing Challenge 2021; +1.4 dB SDR across sources on MusDB HQ — [arXiv 2111.03600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03600)
- *Hysteria* production facts relevant to the stereo field: guitars "recorded first to a LinnDrum click track"; the Rockman was chosen because other amps' "crunchy" tone was unsuitable for "layering guitars" (Mike Shipley); "All of the album's drum sounds were samples recorded by Lange and the engineers, then programmed and sequenced on the Fairlight CMI"; mixed by Mike Shipley at Lange's Hindhead studio over three months — [Wikipedia Hysteria (album)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hysteria_(Def_Leppard_album))
- On the album's title track "the clean guitar chords were recorded one note at a time ... in effect 'building' a chord by recording the notes that make them up" — [Wikipedia Hysteria (song)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hysteria_(Def_Leppard_song))

### Inferences
- A cheap, model-free "panning scan" is a sensible first pass: compute per-band L/R intensity-ratio histograms (the ADRess azimuth plane) over the song; peaks at hard-left/right with guitar-range spectral content indicate double-tracked rhythm guitars; then feed a narrow-azimuth resynthesis (or simply L and R channels separately) to the chord estimator and take the per-frame agreement as a confidence feature.
- Because Mutt Lange's productions stacked many guitar layers and used sampled, sequenced drums, the stereo image is dense and reverberant; expect ADRess/centre-cancel to give a *cleaner view*, not a clean stem — Demucs `htdemucs_6s` remains the real separator, with the panning-derived signals used as extra inputs/sanity checks.
- Centre-cancel (L-R) is a near-free way to attenuate centred lead vocal, bass and kick before chord estimation, exposing side-panned guitars; the M (L+R) channel conversely emphasises bass for root detection.

### Gaps
- No primary source describing the actual pan positions of the rhythm guitars on "Pour Some Sugar On Me" (the Sound on Sound "Classic Tracks" and Mix articles returned 410/404); the "hard-panned doubled guitars" claim is folklore not confirmed here.
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of ADRess/DUET on commercial 80s rock recordings was retrieved; the overview paper (arXiv 1804.08300) PDF could not be parsed.
- No paper found that feeds explicit panning features into Demucs; Demucs simply ingests stereo.

## Q6. Loudness normalisation, YouTube codec effects, and per-model sample rates

### Takeaway
Pipeline housekeeping that is cheap and sourced: download the best YouTube audio (itag 251 Opus, <=160 kbps, is the practical ceiling for non-Premium), decode once to float, optionally loudness-normalise with pyloudnorm (BS.1770-4) or ffmpeg `loudnorm` (two-pass, beware its 192 kHz upsampling), and then resample per model: 22,050 Hz mono for Basic Pitch, 44.1 kHz stereo for Demucs, 16 kHz mono for Whisper. No measured study of Opus/AAC effects on chord/pitch accuracy was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube audio-only itags (gist last updated April 2021): 140 = MP4/AAC-LC 128 kbps stereo; 251 = WebM/Opus VBR up to ~160 kbps stereo; 250 = Opus ~70 kbps; 249 = Opus ~50 kbps; 139 = AAC-HE 48 kbps (rare); 141 = 256 kbps AAC "only to Premium users"; "251 represents the highest-quality audio generally accessible" — [YouTube itag gist (AgentOak)](https://gist.github.com/AgentOak/34d47c65b1d28829bb17c24c04a0096f)
- yt-dlp FAQ: YouTube serves "higher quality formats as separate audio and video" streams, so ffmpeg is needed for merging — [yt-dlp FAQ](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/FAQ)
- pyloudnorm implements ITU-R BS.1770-4; `meter = pyln.Meter(rate); loudness = meter.integrated_loudness(data)`; supports peak normalisation and loudness normalisation to a LUFS target plus `loudness_range()` (EBU Tech 3342); depends on SciPy/NumPy; README does not warn about clipping after normalisation — [csteinmetz1/pyloudnorm](https://github.com/csteinmetz1/pyloudnorm)
- ffmpeg `loudnorm`: EBU R128 AGC plus true-peak limiter; targets I (LUFS), LRA (LU), TP (dBTP), e.g. -16 LUFS / 11 LU / -1.5 dBTP; two-pass workflow (`print_format=json` then `measured_I/LRA/TP/offset`) enables linear gain where possible; the true-peak limiter upsamples to 192 kHz and "Users must manually downsample to their target sample rate afterward" — [loudnorm author's page](http://k.ylo.ph/2016/04/04/loudnorm.html)
- Basic Pitch: "all audio will be resampled to 22050 Hz before processing"; stereo is downmixed to mono; Python 3.7-3.11; outputs MIDI with pitch bends, optional NPZ/CSV/WAV — [spotify/basic-pitch](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch)
- Demucs: 44.1 kHz stereo output; stereo/mono inputs resampled automatically — [facebookresearch/demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)
- Whisper: `SAMPLE_RATE = 16000`, `N_FFT = 400`, `HOP_LENGTH = 160`, `CHUNK_LENGTH = 30`; `load_audio` shells out to ffmpeg with `-ac 1 -acodec pcm_s16le -ar 16000 -f s16le` and divides by 32768.0 — [whisper/audio.py](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/audio.py); `transcribe()` uses "a sliding 30-second window" — [openai/whisper README](https://github.com/openai/whisper)
- Dejavu reports "No performance degradation" for fingerprinting compressed streaming audio vs lossless (fingerprinting only, not pitch/chord accuracy) — [worldveil/dejavu](https://github.com/worldveil/dejavu)
- Chromaprint's design test compared low-bitrate MP3 against the original and still matched — [How does Chromaprint work?](https://oxygene.sk/2011/01/how-does-chromaprint-work/)

### Inferences
- Loudness normalisation is primarily for consistent thresholds (onset/magnitude gates such as librosa's median-magnitude gate in `estimate_tuning`) and for Whisper's level expectations; neural separators and transcribers are largely gain-invariant, so a simple peak or LUFS normalisation in NumPy (pyloudnorm) is adequate and avoids ffmpeg loudnorm's 192 kHz round-trip. Apply it once on the decoded master and derive all per-model resamplings from that.
- Keep one decoded 44.1/48 kHz stereo float array as the source of truth; resample copies with `soxr_hq` (librosa default) to 22.05 k mono (Basic Pitch) and 16 k mono (Whisper). Do not chain lossy encodes between stages.

### Gaps
- No measured study of Opus/AAC (128-160 kbps) effects on chord-recognition or pitch-transcription accuracy was retrieved (the ISMIR 2014 "effect of audio quality on robustness of MFCCs and chroma" work was targeted but the Semantic Scholar API returned 429 and the search budget was exhausted).
- The YouTube itag table is from 2021; current availability of 251/140 should be confirmed with `yt-dlp -F` at run time.
