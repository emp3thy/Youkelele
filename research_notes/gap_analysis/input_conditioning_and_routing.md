## Key question 1 — Robustness: how recording conditions, loudness, resampling and lossy codecs shift transcription/chord accuracy, and what preprocessing mitigates it

### Takeaway
The best-controlled evidence (Marták, Hu & Widmer, Dec 2025) shows that for piano AMT the *acoustic* ("sound") shift alone costs about 20 F1 points, genre a further ~14, and that onset-only detection is almost unaffected (2.4 pp) while offsets (-16 pp from sound) and velocity/dynamics (-22 pp from genre, up to -50 pp on random material) are the fragile parts; for lossy codecs the 2013–2014 literature finds chroma/chord features essentially codec-insensitive at YouTube-class bitrates while MFCC/timbre features need roughly >=160 kbps MP3, and the real killers are reverb/live impulse responses and high-pass/small-speaker colouration rather than compression. Demucs normalises its own input (mean/std) and Basic Pitch min-max normalises each 2-s CQT chunk, so pre-normalisation is mainly for your own feature code (chroma, beat tracking, RMS-based section logic) and for consistent thresholds, not for those two models.

### Cited Findings

**AMT distribution shift (the Dec 2025 study)**
- "Sound and Music Biases in Deep Music Transcription Models: A Systematic Analysis" (Marták, Hu, Widmer, JKU Linz), arXiv 2512.14602, submitted 2025-12-16. Abstract: "we measure a note-level F1 performance drop of 20 percentage points due to sound, and 14 due to genre. Generally, we find that dynamics estimation proves more vulnerable to musical variation than onset prediction." — [arXiv 2512.14602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14602)
- Systems evaluated (all trained on classical piano, mostly MAESTRO): Onsets and Frames (Hawthorne 2018), High-Resolution Piano Transcription (Kong 2021), T5 seq2seq (Hawthorne 2021), hFT-Transformer (Toyama 2023), and Edwards 2024 (Kong architecture retrained with augmentations for sound robustness). Evaluation used the authors' own checkpoints and inference code. — [arXiv 2512.14602 PDF, Sec. 2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14602)
- MDS corpus: (1) Genre set, 100 pieces / 5.25 h / 118,388 notes across 10 genres (Classical, Blues, Rock, Soul, Jazz, Latin, Pop, Country, Folk, World); (2) Random set, 72 pieces, synthetic non-musical sequences; (3) MAEtest, 20 MAESTRO test pieces re-recorded on the authors' Disklavier so that the *sound* shift can be isolated from the *music* shift. — [arXiv 2512.14602 PDF, Table 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14602)
- Isolated sound shift: Note F1 (On&Off&Vel) on MAEtest = 84.03% with MAESTRO audio vs 63.35% with the re-recorded audio, i.e. "Sound Distribution Shift: 20.68%". — [arXiv 2512.14602 PDF, Fig. 7 caption](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14602)
- Per-metric deltas (percentage points lost; columns Sound / Genre / Random): Frame F1 8.33 / -6.57 / 26.99; Note F1 (Onset only) 2.43 / 1.62 / 20.46; Note F1 (On&Off) 16.02 / -1.99 / 47.79; Note F1 (On&Vel) 9.58 / 22.05 / 49.55; Note F1 (On&Off&Vel) 20.68 / 14.17 / 51.68. Conclusion text: "offset detection seems to be much more impacted by changes in sound — decrease of 16 p.p. — while velocity detection appears to be the most brittle under musical distribution shift — decrease of 22 p.p. due to varying genres, and up to 50 p.p. when confronted with non-musical material", with "surprising robustness of onset-only detection". — [arXiv 2512.14602 PDF, Table 3 & Sec. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14602)
- The paper notes that augmentation-based robustness training "seem[s] to alleviate this issue to certain extent, [but] these gains seem to come at the expense of reduced ID performance [2, 18, 19]". — [arXiv 2512.14602 PDF, Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14602)
- Precursor workshop paper: "Quantifying the Corpus Bias Problem in Automatic Music Transcription Systems" (same authors), arXiv 2408.04737, 2024-08-08: identifies "music" and "sound" (timbre, recording conditions) as the two distribution-shift axes and reports "a stark performance gap". — [arXiv 2408.04737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04737)
- The cited sound-robustness work is Edwards, Dixon, Benetos, Maezawa, Kusaka, "A Data-Driven Analysis of Robust Automatic Piano Transcription" (IEEE SPL 2024; arXiv 2402.01424, 2024-02-02): models "severely overfit to acoustic properties of the training data"; re-recording MAESTRO through a Disklavier in a studio plus augmentation gives 88.4 note-onset F1 on MAPS with no MAPS training data. — [arXiv 2402.01424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01424)
- The microphone-mismatch benchmark referenced for piano multipitch is IDMT-PIANO-MM (Abeßer et al., DMRN+16, 2021), "a benchmark dataset to study microphone mismatch conditions for piano multipitch estimation on mobile devices". — [cited in arXiv 2512.14602 PDF ref. 21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14602)

**Lossy codecs vs chroma/chord/MIR features**
- The "2014 paper on audio compression vs chord recognition" is A. Uemura, K. Ishikura, J. Katto, "Effects of audio compression on chord recognition", MMM 2014. As summarised by Urbano et al.: they "examined the effect of bitrate on chord recognition using chroma features with an SVM classifier. They observed no obvious correlation between encoding and estimation results; the best results were even obtained with very low bitrates for some codecs." — [Urbano et al. ISMIR 2014, Sec. 6 and ref. 23](https://zenodo.org/records/1416276)
- Urbano, Bogdanov, Herrera, Gómez, Serra, "What is the Effect of Audio Quality on the Robustness of MFCCs and Chroma Features?", ISMIR 2014 (Taipei, 2014-10-27). 400 tracks, Essentia 2.0.1 and QM Vamp plugins; conditions: WAV 1411 kbps; MP3 CBR at 44.1 kHz 64/96/128/160/192/256/320 kbps; MP3 CBR at 22.05 kHz 64–160 kbps; MP3 VBR 0/2/4/6; frame sizes 23–743 ms. Conclusion: "MFFCs are robust to frame/hop sizes and lossy encoding provided that a minimum bitrate of approximately 160 Kbps is used. Chroma features were shown to be even more robust, as the codec and bitrates had virtually no effect on the computed descriptors ... lossy compression does not alter the perceived tonality." — [Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.1416276 (PDF)](https://zenodo.org/records/1416276)
- Earlier compression studies cited there: Sigurdsson et al. ISMIR 2006 (MFCC robustness to MP3; high-frequency distortions), Jacobson/Davies/Sandler AES 2008 (lossy encoding vs onset detection), Casey et al. AES 2008 (lossy encoding vs genre classification), Hamawaki et al. MMM 2008 (feature normalisation for bitrate robustness). — [Urbano et al. 2014 refs 4, 10, 11, 21](https://zenodo.org/records/1416276)
- Audio Degradation Toolbox (Mauch & Ewert, ISMIR 2013, Curitiba, 2013-11-04): 14 degradation units; six "Real World" presets: Live Recording (Great Hall impulse response + light pink noise), Radio Broadcast (dynamic-range compression + 2% speed-up), Smartphone Playback (Nexus One speaker IR with high-pass cutoff ≈500 Hz + noise), Smartphone Recording (mic IR + DRC auto-gain + 3% clipping + medium pink noise), Strong MP3 Compression (64 kbps CBR), Vinyl (IR + crackle + wow at 33 rpm + noise). — [Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.1415862 (PDF), Sec. 2](https://zenodo.org/records/1415862)
- ADT chord results (180 Beatles songs, MIREX maj/min overlap): both Chordino and HPA are "relatively robust to the Live Recording degradation, with medians dropping less than 10 percentage points: Chordino 0.74 (original: 0.80), HPA: 0.75 (original: 0.84). Instead, they falter on the Smartphone Playback degradation: Chordino 0.67 (original: 0.80), HPA: 0.36 (original: 0.84)". Under pure high-pass filtering HPA falls to 0.60 (200 Hz), 0.29 (400 Hz), 0.05 (800 Hz) while Chordino's worst median is 0.73 at 400 Hz. The 64 kbps MP3 preset is among the degradations shown with no notable drop in Fig. 6. — [Mauch & Ewert 2013, Sec. 3.4](https://zenodo.org/records/1415862)
- ADT beat-tracking results (±70 ms F-measure): "for the original audio and most of the Real World Degradations, both beat-tracking methods show good performance, with median F-measures always exceeding 0.85. The obvious exception is the Live Recording degradation ... BeatRoot: 0.65 (original: 0.92); Davies's: 0.77 (original: 0.94)" — cause is "spurious onsets introduced by the impulse response". — [Mauch & Ewert 2013, Sec. 3.3](https://zenodo.org/records/1415862)
- ADT audio-ID result relevant to live-detection: the EchoNest fingerprint service identified 100/100 originals and 100/100 of the 64 kbps MP3 versions but 0/100 of the Live Recording versions (and ≤5% of radio/phone/vinyl versions). — [Mauch & Ewert 2013, Table 2](https://zenodo.org/records/1415862)
- Koops, Micchi, Quinton, "Robust Lossy Audio Compression Identification", arXiv 2407.21545 (2024-07-31): a detector of lossy compression trained naively gets "near perfect performance metrics on the held-out test set" but generalises poorly across codec parameters — evidence that codec artefacts are learnable, i.e. a non-trivial domain feature. — [arXiv 2407.21545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21545)

**What the models themselves do with level / sample rate / channels**
- Demucs `Separator.separate_tensor` normalises the input before inference and de-normalises after: `ref = wav.mean(0); wav -= ref.mean(); wav /= ref.std() + 1e-8` ... `out *= ref.std() + 1e-8; out += ref.mean()`; input "will be resampled if it doesn't match the model"; defaults `shifts=1`, `overlap=0.25`. — [demucs/api.py](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/blob/main/demucs/api.py)
- Demucs `apply_model` "shifts" = test-time augmentation: `max_shift = int(0.5 * model.samplerate)`, random shifts applied, outputs accumulated and `out /= shifts`. — [demucs/apply.py](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/blob/main/demucs/apply.py)
- Demucs README: output is 44.1 kHz stereo WAV; `--shifts` does "multiple predictions with random shifts" and averages them; `--overlap` default 0.25; `--segment` (HT models capped at 7.8 s); Demucs "automatically rescales each output stem so as to avoid clipping" (`--clip-mode rescale`, alternative `clamp`); `htdemucs_6s` adds guitar and piano stems but "piano source is not working great". — [Demucs README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/blob/main/README.md)
- Basic Pitch README: "all audio will be resampled to 22050 Hz before processing"; stereo input is down-mixed to mono. — [basic-pitch README](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/blob/main/README.md)
- Basic Pitch inference loads with `librosa.load(str(audio_path), sr=AUDIO_SAMPLE_RATE, mono=True)`; no amplitude/loudness normalisation is applied in `inference.py`; defaults `DEFAULT_ONSET_THRESHOLD = 0.5`, `DEFAULT_FRAME_THRESHOLD = 0.3`, `DEFAULT_MINIMUM_NOTE_LENGTH_MS = 127.7`, `melodia_trick=True`, `multiple_pitch_bends=False`, `DEFAULT_OVERLAPPING_FRAMES = 30`. — [basic_pitch/inference.py](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/blob/main/basic_pitch/inference.py)
- Basic Pitch PR #166 (f90, 2025-03-27, draft, unmerged, "Add a variant of basic pitch with global CQT normalization"): the current model applies "min-max normalization of the CQT" to each 2-second chunk independently, so "relative volume differences between audio chunks don't get reflected in the output"; the PR proposes a fixed global normalisation instead. No evaluation numbers are given. — [spotify/basic-pitch#166](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/pull/166)
- A GitHub issue search of facebookresearch/demucs for "normalize loudness" returned no maintainer discussion of input loudness; nothing authoritative found there. — [GitHub issue search](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=repo:facebookresearch/demucs+normalize+loudness)
- Whisper: `SAMPLE_RATE = 16000`, `N_FFT = 400`, `HOP_LENGTH = 160`, `CHUNK_LENGTH = 30`; `load_audio` decodes via ffmpeg with `-ac 1` (mono), 16 kHz, float32 /32768. — [whisper/audio.py](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/audio.py)
- PaSST expects 32 kHz input, 128 mel bins, ~10 s clips. — [PaSST README](https://github.com/kkoutini/PaSST/blob/main/README.md)
- LAION CLAP requires 48 kHz input. — [LAION-AI/CLAP README](https://github.com/LAION-AI/CLAP/blob/main/README.md)
- AST (MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593): 16 kHz input, max 10.24 s, 527 AudioSet classes, BSD-3-Clause. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593)

**Loudness / peak normalisation tooling**
- pyloudnorm implements ITU-R BS.1770-4; `meter = pyln.Meter(rate); loudness = meter.integrated_loudness(data)`; `pyln.normalize.loudness(data, loudness, -12.0)` normalises to a target LUFS; `pyln.normalize.peak(data, -1.0)` does peak normalisation; `block_size` default 0.400 s; MIT licence. — [csteinmetz1/pyloudnorm](https://github.com/csteinmetz1/pyloudnorm)
- ffmpeg `loudnorm`: "Normalize loudness using the ITU-R BS.1770 standard"; `I` range -70 to -5 LUFS default -24; `LRA` 1–50 LU default 7; `TP` -9 to 0 dBTP default -2; `measured_I/LRA/TP/thresh` for two-pass; `linear` enables linear (gain-only) normalisation; `dual_mono`; `print_format` json/summary/verbose; the filter upsamples to 192 kHz internally and "a two-pass approach is recommended". — [ffmpeg-filters loudnorm](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#loudnorm)
- ffmpeg-normalize (slhck) defaults: -23 LUFS, -2 dBTP, LRA 7; types ebu/rms/peak; two-pass loudnorm by default; `--keep-loudness-range-target`/`--dynamic` control whether loudnorm applies dynamic compression, and linear mode can be forced to avoid it; output sample rate should be set with `-ar` because of the 192 kHz upsampling. — [ffmpeg-normalize README](https://github.com/slhck/ffmpeg-normalize/blob/master/README.md)
- ffmpeg also ships `highpass`, `dcshift` (DC offset correction), `aresample` (with `soxr` engine option and dithering) and `silencedetect`/`silenceremove`. — [ffmpeg-filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html)

**Resampling**
- librosa 0.11 `librosa.resample` default `res_type='soxr_hq'`; options `soxr_vhq/hq/mq/lq` (bandlimited), `soxr_qq` (cubic, "very fast, but not bandlimited"), `kaiser_best`/`kaiser_fast` (resampy), `fft`/`scipy`, `polyphase` (integer rates only), `linear`, `zero_order_hold`, `sinc_*` (samplerate); non-bandlimited modes risk aliasing. — [librosa.resample 0.11.0](https://librosa.org/doc/0.11.0/generated/librosa.resample.html)
- python-soxr benchmark (10 s 48→44.1 kHz on Colab): soxr HQ 10.8 ms vs resampy kaiser_fast 108 ms vs scipy.signal.resample 21.3 ms; LGPL-2.1-or-later; quality presets are documented in libsoxr docs. — [python-soxr README](https://github.com/dofuuz/python-soxr/blob/main/README.md)

### Inferences
- For a YouTube pipeline the dominant acoustic risk is not the codec but room/mic/EQ colouration: ADT shows a 64 kbps MP3 preset is harmless for chord estimation and beat tracking, while a reverberant "live" IR drops beat F-measure by ~0.2–0.3 and a speaker-like high-pass can halve chord accuracy for a context-heavy model (HPA). Treat live/phone-recorded videos as a separate quality tier (see Q2 live detection).
- For Basic Pitch, a pre-normalisation of global loudness changes nothing inside the model (per-chunk min-max CQT) except the behaviour of very quiet chunks (noise gets amplified); the useful knob is instead the frame/onset thresholds, and a stem that is quiet relative to the mix benefits from being separated first because the chunk normalisation is then applied to the stem alone.
- For Demucs, external loudness normalisation is redundant (mean/std standardisation) but harmless; what is not redundant is clipping avoidance: feed float32 and keep `--clip-mode rescale`.
- Recommended chain (derived from the above, not from one source): ffmpeg decode → float32 → DC removal (`dcshift`/high-pass at ~20–30 Hz) → BS.1770 integrated-loudness normalisation to a fixed target (-14 to -18 LUFS, linear/gain-only, no limiter) with pyloudnorm → keep stereo 44.1 kHz for Demucs → per-model resample with `soxr_hq` (22.05 k mono Basic Pitch; 16 k mono Whisper/AST/inaSpeechSegmenter; 32 k PaSST; 48 k CLAP). Apply normalisation *before* Demucs so stems and the mix share one reference level for the stem-RMS routing in Q3. Dither is only relevant if you re-quantise to 16-bit integer files; keep everything float32 in memory.
- Marták's numbers imply the pipeline should rely on onset/pitch (robust) and treat velocity/dynamics and offsets as low-confidence; for tablature this argues for quantised durations from the beat grid rather than from AMT offsets.
- Pitch-shift TTA/ensembling for chord models: no published MIR study found (see Gaps). The analogous documented TTA is Demucs' random time-shift averaging, which is cheap to replicate for chord models (shift by ±k semitones with a high-quality shifter, run, un-shift the label vocabulary, vote); Marták's genre-shift results suggest the main benefit would come from averaging over *timbre* rather than pitch, so time-shift and mild EQ ensembles are at least as justified.

### Gaps
- No study found quantifying Opus or AAC at 128–160 kbps specifically against MIR features (only MP3 CBR/VBR in Urbano 2014 and 64 kbps MP3 in ADT). The Semantic Scholar and arXiv queries for Opus/AAC vs tagging/chord returned nothing relevant.
- No authoritative statement from Demucs or Basic Pitch maintainers on whether loudness normalisation "helps or hurts"; only the code and the unmerged PR #166 are available.
- No paper found on test-time augmentation over pitch shifts for chord recognition or AMT (arXiv and OpenAlex queries returned only unrelated works; the only related 2026 item, arXiv 2608.14819, defines a pitch-transposition *equivariance* measure for foundation-model layers rather than a TTA method).
- pyloudnorm true-peak (oversampled) measurement support was not confirmed from the README; ffmpeg `loudnorm` does true-peak via 192 kHz upsampling.
- Uemura et al. 2014 (MMM) full text was not retrieved; its result is known only via Urbano et al.'s summary.

## Key question 2 — Detecting and trimming non-music content and structural edges (spoken intros, applause, silence, fade-outs, ritardando, live versions)

### Takeaway
Use metadata first (YouTube chapters and SponsorBlock's `music_offtopic`/`intro`/`outro` segments, both exposed by yt-dlp), then a speech/music/noise segmenter (inaSpeechSegmenter, MIT, MIREX-2018 winner) or an AudioSet tagger (AST/CLAP: "Speech", "Applause", "Music") to cut dialogue and crowd noise, then signal-level RMS-envelope logic for silence and fade-outs, and a beat tracker that tolerates tempo drift (madmom DBN with lower `transition_lambda`, or Beat This! which drops the DBN tempo prior) so a ritardando ending does not corrupt the grid. Live versions are best flagged by fingerprint failure (ADT: audio-ID went from 100% to 0% identification under a live-room impulse response) combined with applause/crowd tags.

**Silence, fade-outs, tempo drift**
- `librosa.feature.rms(y=None, S=None, frame_length=2048, hop_length=512, center=True, ...)` returns shape (…,1,t); computing from a spectrogram `S` "will give a more accurate representation of energy over time because its frames can be windowed". — [librosa.feature.rms](https://librosa.org/doc/0.11.0/generated/librosa.feature.rms.html)
- ffmpeg `silencedetect` reports silent intervals against a noise threshold; `silenceremove` strips them. — [ffmpeg-filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html)
- madmom `DBNBeatTrackingProcessor` (Krebs, Böck, Widmer, ISMIR 2015 state space): `min_bpm`, `max_bpm`, `num_tempi` (log spacing if set), `transition_lambda` = "Lambda for the exponential tempo change distribution (higher values prefer a constant tempo from one beat to the next one)", default 100; `observation_lambda`, `threshold`, `correct` (snap beats to activation peaks), `fps`. — [madmom/features/beats.py](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/blob/main/madmom/features/beats.py)
- "Beat This! Accurate Beat Tracking Without DBN Postprocessing" (Foscarin, Schlüter, Widmer, ISMIR 2024, arXiv 2407.21658): removes "the commonly used Dynamic Bayesian Network (DBN) postprocessing, which introduces constraints on the meter and tempo", handles time-signature changes and "classical music exhibiting high tempo variations", state-of-the-art F1 but "performs worse on continuity metrics" and "can still fail, especially for difficult and underrepresented genres". — [arXiv 2407.21658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21658)
- Radio Broadcast preset in ADT includes a 2% speed-up "commonly applied to music in commercial radio stations" — a reminder that the tempo of a found recording may be offset from the canonical version. — [Mauch & Ewert 2013, Sec. 2](https://zenodo.org/records/1415862)

**Live-version / cover detection**
- Under ADT's Live Recording degradation (room IR + pink noise) the EchoNest audio-ID service identified 0 of 100 songs (vs 100/100 for the original and for 64 kbps MP3). — [Mauch & Ewert 2013, Table 2](https://zenodo.org/records/1415862)
- AcoustID lookup: `https://api.acoustid.org/v2/lookup` with `client` (API key), `duration` (s), `fingerprint` (Chromaprint), optional `meta`; returns `score` (0–1) and MusicBrainz recording ids / release groups; limit 3 requests/s; non-commercial use without a commercial agreement. — [AcoustID web service](https://acoustid.org/webservice)
- Chromaprint is "the core component of the AcoustID project ... a client-side library that implements a custom algorithm for extracting fingerprints"; `fpcalc` produces JSON and is "the recommended way to use Chromaprint if all you need is generate fingerprints for AcoustID". — [acoustid.org/chromaprint](https://acoustid.org/chromaprint)
- yt-dlp exposes `live_status` ('was_live', 'post_live') for streams that were broadcast live. — [yt_dlp/extractor/common.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/common.py)

### Inferences
- Trimming order of precedence: (1) SponsorBlock `music_offtopic`/`intro`/`outro` + YouTube chapters whose titles match /intro|outro|credits|talk|interview|skit/ (cheap, crowd-sourced, exact timestamps); (2) inaSpeechSegmenter `speech`/`noise`/`noEnergy` runs ≥2 s at the beginning/end of the file; (3) AudioSet tagger (AST or CLAP zero-shot with labels like "applause", "crowd cheering", "speech", "music") on 10-s windows as a second opinion; (4) RMS silence gating. Keep "speech over music" as music (inaSpeechSegmenter labels speech-over-music as speech, so a sung intro should be checked against the `voice_instrumental`/AudioSet "Singing" tag before cutting).
- Fade-out detection: fit a line to the frame-level RMS in dB over the last 10–20 s; a monotonic slope of roughly -1 dB/s or steeper sustained ≥5 s with no onset-density change is a fade-out (threshold is an engineering choice; no paper found). Trim the tab at the point where RMS drops ~12 dB below the chorus median rather than at file end.
- Ritardando: with madmom, lower `transition_lambda` (e.g. 100 → 20–50) for the final 15 s or run Beat This! in parallel and accept its beats where the DBN's inter-beat intervals start growing monotonically; mark those bars "rit." instead of forcing them onto a constant grid.
- Live-version rule: flag as live if (a) `live_status` in {was_live, post_live}, or (b) AcoustID score < ~0.5 while the title/artist metadata says the song is a known studio release, or (c) AudioSet "Applause"/"Crowd" probability exceeds the "Music" probability in >1 window at the edges, or (d) inaSpeechSegmenter reports `noise` runs >3 s at the edges. Live flags should widen beat-tracker tempo tolerance and lower chord-confidence expectations (ADT: live IR cost beat trackers 0.15–0.27 F-measure but chords <0.1).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed method for fade-out or final-ritardando detection was found on arXiv or OpenAlex (zero hits for "fade-out"/"ritardando" with music/tempo). The RMS-slope and DBN-tolerance approaches above are inferences.
- No dedicated "live vs studio recording" classifier paper was found; the evidence is indirect (fingerprint failure under live-room IR, broadcast applause tracking).
- SponsorBlock category *definitions* page could not be fetched (anti-bot wall); category semantics are taken from yt-dlp's display names.
- inaSpeechSegmenter's internal resampling rate and Windows wheel availability were not confirmed from the README (it only documents a Linux install).

## Key question 3 — Instrument presence/predominance and routing: which stem drives the ukulele arrangement, and which sections are solos

### Takeaway
Clip-level instrument presence is a solved-enough problem (IRMAS CNN micro-F1 0.60; OpenMIC-2018 20-class weak labels; PaSST/AST/CLAP taggers; Essentia's 40-tag MTG-Jamendo instrument head), and Banquet (ISMIR 2024) shows a PaSST instrument-recognition model can directly condition a separator that beats 6-stem HT-Demucs on guitar and piano; the practical routing rule is to combine (a) tagger probabilities for guitar vs piano/synth, (b) per-bar RMS of the Demucs `guitar`/`piano`/`other` stems relative to the normalised mix, and (c) vocal-stem activity to label sections as sung vs instrumental, with the Pati & Lerch rock-guitar-solo dataset/SVM method as the only solo-specific reference found.

### Cited Findings

**Predominant / multi-instrument recognition**
- Han, Kim, Lee, "Deep convolutional neural networks for predominant instrument recognition in polyphonic music", IEEE/ACM TASLP, Jan 2017 (arXiv 1605.09507): CNN trained on single-instrument-labelled excerpts (IRMAS, ~10,000 excerpts, 11 instruments), sliding-window aggregation; micro-F1 0.602, macro-F1 0.503, "19.6% and 16.4%" over the prior state of the art; two aggregation strategies (average vs instrument-wise sum + normalisation). — [arXiv 1605.09507](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.09507)
- OpenMIC-2018: 20,000 ten-second clips from the Free Music Archive, 20 instrument classes, crowd-sourced labels in three states (positive/negative/unknown; `Y_true`/`Y_mask`), VGGish features shipped (`openmic-2018.npz`), CC-BY, Humphrey/McFee/Durand ISMIR 2018; Spotify + NYU MARL. — [cosmir/openmic-2018](https://github.com/cosmir/openmic-2018)
- PaSST ("Efficient Training of Audio Transformers with Patchout", Interspeech 2022, arXiv 2110.05069): AudioSet mAP 0.476 (base `passt_s_swa_p16_128_ap476`), ensemble 0.4956; 32 kHz input; repo includes fine-tuning recipes for OpenMIC-2018, FSD50K, ESC-50; `pip install hear21passt` for embeddings/inference. — [PaSST README](https://github.com/kkoutini/PaSST/blob/main/README.md), [arXiv 2110.05069](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05069)
- Essentia `mtg_jamendo_instrument` (40 tags: accordion, acoustic guitar, bagpipe, banjo, bassoon, bell, bendir, bongo, brass, cello, clarinet, drums, dulcimer, flute, fx, gong, guitar, harmonica, harp, harpsichord, horn, keyboard, koto, latin percussion, organ, pad, piano, piccolo, pipe, pizzicato strings, recorder, saxophone, sitar, speech, string, synth, tabla, trombone, trumpet, violin) on discogs-effnet embeddings; also `nsynth_instrument` and `voice_instrumental`. — [Essentia models](https://essentia.upf.edu/models.html)
- Gururani, Sharma, Lerch, "An Attention Mechanism for Musical Instrument Recognition" (ISMIR 2019, arXiv 1907.04294): weakly-labelled OpenMIC training with attention; "overall improvement in classification accuracy metrics across all 20 instruments"; also uses MedleyDB per-frame instrument activity annotations. — [arXiv 1907.04294](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04294)
- Hung & Lerch, "Multitask learning for instrument activation aware music source separation" (ISMIR 2020, arXiv 2008.00616): uses instrument-activation prediction to improve separation of six instruments on MedleyDB/Mixing Secrets, beating Open-Unmix on the combined set. — [arXiv 2008.00616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00616)
- Sechet et al., "A Hierarchical Deep Learning Approach for Minority Instrument Detection" (arXiv 2506.21167, 2025-06-26): Hornbostel-Sachs hierarchical instrument-activity detection on MedleyDB, "more reliable coarse-level instrument detection". — [arXiv 2506.21167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21167)

**Instrument-conditioned separation and joint models**
- Banquet — Watcharasupat & Lerch, "A Stem-Agnostic Single-Decoder System for Music Source Separation Beyond Four Stems", ISMIR 2024 (arXiv 2406.18747): bandsplit separator + query conditioning from "a PaSST music instrument recognition model"; 24.9 M parameters; on MoisesDB it "approached" 6-stem Hybrid Transformer Demucs on VDBO stems "while outperforming it specifically on guitar and piano separation", and extracts narrow classes such as clean acoustic guitar, reeds and organs; code public, paper CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. — [arXiv 2406.18747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18747)
- Jointist (Cheuk et al., arXiv 2206.10805, 2022-06-22): "instrument-aware multi-instrument framework" with instrument recognition, transcription and separation modules; instrument conditioning drives transcription; demonstrated on downbeat and chord downstream tasks. The 2023 revision (arXiv 2302.00286) reports joint training improves separation by ~5 dB SDR and outperforms MT3 on popular music. — [arXiv 2206.10805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10805), [arXiv 2302.00286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00286)
- MuS3D, "Music Source Separation via Stem Discovery" (Kallinen, Moliner, Juvela, Välimäki, arXiv 2609.30912, 2026-09-25): iteratively discovers active sources with audio queries; notes "query-based separation remains cumbersome due to the need to provide audio examples", and outperforms text-query baselines. — [arXiv 2609.30912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30912)
- Demucs `htdemucs_6s` provides `guitar` and `piano` stems in addition to vocals/drums/bass/other, with the README caveat that "piano source is not working great". — [Demucs README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/blob/main/README.md)

**Solo detection**
- Pati & Lerch, "A Dataset and Method for Guitar Solo Detection in Rock Music", AES International Conference on Semantic Audio, 2017 (published 2017-06-13): dataset of "60 popular rock songs" with solo start-time/duration annotations per text file; SVM-based detector; code and annotations CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. — [ashispati/GuitarSoloDetection](https://github.com/ashispati/GuitarSoloDetection), [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=title.search:guitar%20solo%20detection)
- Related but not solo-detection: Gorlow, Ramona, Pachet, "Decision-Based Transcription of Jazz Guitar Solos" (arXiv 1611.06505, 2016) — melody transcription of improvised guitar solos, +34% F-measure over reference, +19% over pYIN. — [arXiv 1611.06505](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06505)
- Cano, Schuller, Dittmar, "Pitch-informed solo and accompaniment separation towards its use in music education applications", EURASIP JASP 2014. — [DOI 10.1186/1687-6180-2014-23](https://doi.org/10.1186/1687-6180-2014-23)

### Inferences
- Routing rule (derived): after loudness-normalising the mix (Q1) and separating with htdemucs_6s (or Banquet for guitar/piano), compute per-bar RMS (librosa.feature.rms on the beat grid) for `vocals`, `guitar`, `piano`, `bass`, `other`, each relative to the mix. Song-level driver = argmax over bars of time-weighted {guitar, piano+other-with-"synth"/"keyboard" tag} energy, tie-broken by the clip-level tagger (Essentia `mtg_jamendo_instrument` or PaSST/CLAP probabilities for "acoustic guitar", "guitar", "piano", "synth", "keyboard"). Guitar-driven → chord/riff arrangement from the guitar stem's Basic Pitch output and chroma; piano/synth-driven → chords from the mix/`other` chroma, with the ukulele voicing chosen from the chord labels rather than from transcribed piano notes.
- Section rule: a bar window is "instrumental" when vocal-stem RMS is >12–15 dB below its song median for ≥2 bars; it is a "solo to transcribe as melody" when additionally one pitched stem (guitar or piano) carries most of the non-bass/non-drum energy and its Basic Pitch output is mostly monophonic (≤2 simultaneous notes) with high note density — this mirrors the Pati & Lerch SVM setup (hand-crafted features over segments) but uses stems instead. Everything else instrumental is treated as a riff/chord section.
- Prefer Banquet or a PaSST/OpenMIC tagger over Essentia only if GPU is available; Essentia's TensorFlow models run on CPU on Windows but the `discogs-effnet` embedding is the heaviest step.
- Marták's finding that onset-only AMT is robust while velocity is not implies the "solo" melody line can be extracted reliably from onsets+pitch of the stem, but dynamics markings should not be emitted.

### Gaps
- Pati & Lerch's reported F-measure and feature set could not be read (no abstract in OpenAlex, DBLP blocked, PDF not located); only the dataset size (60 songs) and SVM method are confirmed.
- No published, reusable "which stem drives the song" decision rule exists; the rule above is inferred.
- PaSST's OpenMIC-2018 fine-tuned mAP was not confirmed (README only says fine-tuning recipes exist).
- Jointist's instrument set and recognition accuracy were not extracted (abstract only).

## Key question 4 — YouTube-specific: music-video vs album audio, chapters/SponsorBlock via yt-dlp, multiple audio tracks and DRC variants

### Takeaway
yt-dlp gives everything needed at download time: chapters and SponsorBlock segments in the info JSON, `live_status`, per-format `language`/`language_preference` with "(original)"/"(default)" marking for multi-audio-track videos (select with `-S lang` or `ba[language=xx]`), and `-drc` audio format variants (dynamic-range-compressed) that should be avoided; the pipeline should expect music-video audio to differ from the album master in the ways a radio edit does (shortened intros/outros, removed sections, fades replaced by cold endings) plus inserted dialogue.

### Cited Findings
- Radio edit (Wikipedia): "a modification, typically truncated or censored, intended to make a song more suitable for airplay"; typical changes are shortened or removed intros/outros, fade-outs replacing long instrumental endings, removal of verses/choruses/bridges/interludes, profanity edits; examples: Bowie's "Heroes" single fades in partway and ends before the final vocal section; the Beatles' "Revolution" single is a different recording; some edits are longer (Radiohead "Creep" adds a count-off). — [Wikipedia: Radio edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_edit)
- yt-dlp YouTube extractor marks audio tracks: if the track displayName contains "original" the format gets `language_preference = 10` and `format_note` "(original)"; `audioIsDefault` tracks get `language_preference = 5` and "(default)"; "descriptive" tracks get `-10` and language code suffixed `desc`; audio formats with `isDrc` get `format_id` suffix `-drc`, `format_note` "DRC", and their `quality` is lowered by 0.5; audio-only quality labels are `audio_quality_ultralow/low/medium/high`. — [yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/_video.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/_video.py)
- yt-dlp's default format sort order is `lang,quality,res,fps,hdr:12,vcodec,channels,acodec,size,br,asr,proto,ext,hasaud,source,id`; `lang` = "The language preference as determined by the extractor (e.g. original language preferred over audio description)". `--audio-multistreams` allows merging several audio streams; examples `-f "bv*+mergeall[vcodec=none]" --audio-multistreams` and `-f "bv*+ba+ba.2" --audio-multistreams`; yt-dlp by default "prefers higher resolution and better codecs rather than higher bitrates" (use `--format-sort` or `--compat-options format-sort` to change). — [yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/README.md)
- Extractor arg `youtube:lang` prefers translated metadata for a language code; `--extractor-args` also takes `player_client` and `formats` (`missing_pot`, `duplicate`, `incomplete`, `dashy`). — [yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/README.md)
- Format fields in the info JSON: `language` ("e.g. de or en-US"), `audio_channels`, `format_note`, `preference`, `language_preference` (10 = matches the URL's language, -1 unknown, -10 otherwise). — [yt_dlp/extractor/common.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/common.py)
- Chapters and SponsorBlock: see Q2 citations (info-JSON `chapters` list; `--sponsorblock-mark`/`--sponsorblock-remove`; `music_offtopic` = "Non-Music Section"; duration-match check in the post-processor). — [yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/README.md), [sponsorblock.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/postprocessor/sponsorblock.py)
- yt-dlp exposes `heatmap` ("most replayed" normalized values per time span), which can locate the chorus/hook when chapters are absent. — [yt_dlp/extractor/common.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/common.py)
- Codec robustness context for YouTube's lossy audio: chroma unaffected by MP3 codec/bitrate; MFCC/timbre features need ≥~160 kbps MP3 (Urbano 2014); 64 kbps MP3 did not visibly hurt Chordino/HPA or beat trackers (ADT 2013). — [Zenodo 1416276](https://zenodo.org/records/1416276), [Zenodo 1415862](https://zenodo.org/records/1415862)

### Inferences
- Download recipe: `yt-dlp -f "ba[language_preference>=5]/ba" -S "lang,quality,acodec:opus,abr" --no-format-sort-force --write-info-json --sponsorblock-mark all --extractor-args "youtube:formats=missing_pot"`, then reject any chosen `format_id` ending in `-drc`, read `chapters`, `live_status`, `heatmap` and the SponsorBlock chapters from the info JSON, and cut before decoding. Use `--force-keyframes-at-cuts` only if you cut video; for audio-only you can cut on sample boundaries after decoding.
- Because music-video audio is often a radio edit or a video-specific mix, align the YouTube audio to a reference (AcoustID/MusicBrainz duration mismatch, or chroma DTW against a reference if one exists) before trusting structure labels like "intro"/"outro" taken from external sources; a duration mismatch >5 s is a strong hint the video is an edit or live version.
- Prefer the Opus (webm) audio over AAC (m4a) when both are offered, because Opus is YouTube's higher-quality track for most videos (see Gap below — bitrate figures not verified here), and prefer non-DRC variants for any dynamics-sensitive stage (stem RMS routing, fade detection).

### Gaps
- Exact YouTube audio bitrates per itag (e.g. 140 AAC 128 kbps, 251 Opus ~130–160 kbps, 141/774 higher-bitrate Premium) could not be verified from a primary source in this session: yt-dlp no longer carries a static itag table (bitrates come from the player response), and the Wikipedia YouTube article has no formats section. Running `yt-dlp -F` on a sample video is the quickest way to confirm.
- No academic or official source was found that systematically describes how music-video audio differs from album audio; the radio-edit article is the closest documented analogue.
- SponsorBlock coverage for music videos (how many have `music_offtopic` segments) is unknown; no statistics source was found.
