## Key question 1: Bass stem as an anchor for chord roots, inversions/slash chords, and major/minor fixes

### Takeaway
A separated bass stem is now the most reliable stem in the Demucs/RoFormer family (bass SDR 10.4-13.3 dB on MUSDB18-HQ, higher than vocals for BS-RoFormer), and the chord-recognition literature has used a "bass chroma" from the low octaves since Mauch & Dixon (2010) specifically to recover inversions; modern models (Jiang et al. 2019, Poltronieri et al. 2025, BACHI 2025) decompose chords into root + bass + quality, which is exactly the structure a bass-f0 histogram can re-score. The main engineering caveats are octave handling and pitch-tracker floor frequencies (SwiftF0's band starts at 46.9 Hz, above the bass low E at ~41 Hz).

Bass chroma / bass features in chord recognition
- NNLS Chroma/Chordino (Mauch): "Bass Chromagram: a 12-dimensional chromagram, restricted to the bass range. At each frame the Semitone Spectrum is multiplied by a bass pattern and then mapped to the 12 chroma bins"; the ordinary chromagram uses "a mid-range pattern". Chordino smooths "frame-wise chord similarities" from a user-editable chord.dict with either "a simple chord change method, and a standard HMM/Viterbi approach"; parameters include "Boost N" (raises the no-chord profile), "Spectral Roll-on" (zeroes low bins below an energy quantile), chroma normalisation, and local/global tuning. Citation: Mauch & Dixon, "Approximate Note Transcription for the Improved Identification of Difficult Chords", ISMIR 2010. — [isophonics.net/nnls-chroma](http://www.isophonics.net/nnls-chroma)
- Pauwels et al. (ISMIR 2019 review): "The octave-invariance also prevents the chroma feature from distinguishing between different inversions of a given chord. A bass chroma calculated from a window on the lower octaves of the pitch feature was introduced in [39] in order to capture inversions. Extending this, a three windowed chroma with Gaussian models was proposed in [9]". Also: deep models now use "one-hot feature targets representing the bass note [40, 64], root note [40], highest pitch note [64]" as auxiliary targets ("target label engineering"); and "Bass notes, for instance, can be strongly indicative of the chord being played." — [Pauwels, O'Hanlon, Gómez, Sandler, "20 Years of Automatic Chord Recognition from Audio", ISMIR 2019, Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3527739)
- McFee & Bello (ISMIR 2017): "Systems like Chordino [17] and HPA [20] extend this idea by introducing additional latent variables to model key, bass, and metrical position. In these systems, bass is modeled by weighting or partitioning the frequency range to produce distinct bass and treble chroma observations." Their own structured model infers "root, bass, and chord quality ... jointly ... from the entire spectrum". — [McFee & Bello, "Structured Training for Large-Vocabulary Chord Recognition", ISMIR 2017, Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1414880)
- Mauch & Dixon, "Simultaneous Estimation of Chords and Musical Context From Audio", IEEE TASLP, Aug 2010 (the DBN that jointly models key, chord, bass and metric position; reference [39] above). — [DOI 10.1109/tasl.2009.2032947](https://doi.org/10.1109/tasl.2009.2032947)

Evaluation of inversions
- mir_eval.chord grammar is `Root:Quality(Extensions)/Bass`; `encode()` returns `(root_number, semitone_bitmap, bass_number)`; the `_inv` comparators (`majmin_inv`, `thirds_inv`, `triads_inv`, `tetrads_inv`, `sevenths_inv`) "additionally require bass note matching" and return -1 when "the bass note doesn't exist in the triad". — [mir_eval/chord.py](https://github.com/mir-evaluation/mir_eval/blob/main/mir_eval/chord.py)

### Inferences
- Recipe (inference synthesised from the above): (1) run Demucs/BS-RoFormer bass stem; (2) run a monophonic tracker with a floor at or below ~41 Hz (E1) — SwiftF0's 46.875 Hz floor would miss open-E and low-F bass notes, so either pitch-shift the stem up an octave before tracking or use CREPE/Basic Pitch with `minimum_frequency` set near 30 Hz; (3) fold f0 to pitch class, weight by voicing confidence and beat-position (downbeat heavier), and build a 12-bin bass-chroma histogram per beat/bar; (4) treat Jiang-style decomposed posteriors as P(root), P(triad), P(bass); re-score each candidate chord with log P_chord + λ·log P_bass(bass_pc | root, quality) where the bass histogram supplies P_bass; (5) emit a slash chord only when the bass pitch class is a chord tone (mir_eval's `_inv` rule) that differs from the root and persists for ≥1 bar, otherwise treat it as a passing bass note.
- Because bass SDR exceeds vocals SDR for BS-RoFormer and all Demucs variants, the bass stem is the stem least likely to carry bleed-induced wrong pitch classes, which supports using it as a hard prior on the root rather than a soft feature.
- E1 ≈ 41.2 Hz and B0 ≈ 30.9 Hz follow from A4 = 440 Hz equal temperament (standard physics; no single page fetched states both numbers).

## Key question 2: Which chord errors models make most on rock, and heuristics to fix them

### Takeaway
Published confusion analyses show the dominant errors are simplifications toward maj/min (min7/minmaj7→min, maj7/7→maj), weak handling of sus and 6th chords, and power chords/1-chords (2.2% of the 1217-track corpus) being forced into maj or min; root/inversion labelling is itself subjective (76% inter-annotator root agreement on a Billboard subset). Key-aware decoding and bass-root agreement are the standard remedies, but one 2025 symbolic ablation found adding key detection slightly hurt full-chord accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- McFee & Bello 2017 (170-class model, 1217 tracks): "the first obvious trend is a bias toward min and maj, in accordance with the natural bias in the training set (13.6% and 52.5% of the data, respectively, by duration) ... the confusions are generally understandable as simplifications: e.g., (min7,minmaj7)→min and (maj7,7)→maj. The model still appears to struggle with 6th and suspended chords, which account for 1.5% and 2.5% of the data"; "out-of-gamut X-chords ... map overwhelmingly to maj and min ... There are 4557 instances of such chords in the corpus (2.2% of the data), and of these, 2091 are 1-chords (only the root) and 2365 are power chords (root+fifth)". Best model CR2+S+A median weighted recall: Root 0.861, Thirds 0.836, Triads 0.812, Sevenths 0.729, Tetrads 0.671, Maj-Min 0.855, MIREX 0.852; "63.6% accuracy of correctly identifying the simplified chord label (root and quality) at the frame level". — [McFee & Bello, ISMIR 2017](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1414880)
- Humphrey & Bello 2015 insights: "one, music recordings that invalidate tacit assumptions about harmony and tonality result in erroneous and even misleading performance; two, standard lexicons and comparison methods struggle to reflect the natural relationships between chords; three, conventional approaches conflate the competing goals of recognition and transcription ...; and four, the perception of chords in real music can be highly subjective". Inversion example: "the other human annotators mark the A:min/b3 instead as a root position C:maj. Given how subjective it might be for human experts to agree on possible inversions, typical evaluation strategies may place too much emphasis on the root of a chord." Tetrad/triad flip-flop: "the human perspectives each report related tetrads and triads, e.g. E:7 and E:maj, causing low annotator agreement, while the machine estimation alternates between the two". — [Humphrey & Bello, "Four Timely Insights on Automatic Chord Estimation", ISMIR 2015](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1417549)
- Poltronieri et al. 2025 citing Koops et al.: "Koops et al. [8] report an inter-annotator agreement rate of 76% for the root note on four different annotations of a 50-song subset of the Billboard dataset"; the Chordify Annotator Subjectivity Dataset (CASD) is "50 tracks with 4 annotations each". — [arXiv:2509.01588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01588); Koops, de Haas, Burgoyne, Bransen et al., "Annotator subjectivity in harmony annotations of popular music", JNMR, 20 May 2019 — [DOI 10.1080/09298215.2019.1613436](https://doi.org/10.1080/09298215.2019.1613436)
- Pauwels 2019: "complex chords are still recognised less well than major and minor triads. Nonetheless, ACR results have improved to the point where the ambiguity in chord labels is now becoming a" limiting factor; key-chord coupling "can be derived from musical knowledge [39, 54] or data-driven [26, 35, 54]"; "it has not been proven that using key-independent relative chord representations lead to improvements in actual chord recognition". — [Pauwels et al., ISMIR 2019](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3527739)
- FMP (Müller) Beatles notebook: reference annotations "reduced to 24 chord labels corresponding to the major and minor triads ... augmented chords are mapped to major chords and diminished chords to minor chords"; warns "there is not much room for improving chord recognition results on the Beatles dataset without the imminent danger of overfitting". — [FMP C5S3_ChordRec_Beatles](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.de/resources/MIR/FMP/C5/C5S3_ChordRec_Beatles.html)
- BACHI ablation: "BACHI w/. key detection" 77.4 / 78.6 / 76.4 / 67.6 (Root/Quality/Bass/Full) vs plain BACHI 77.8 / 79.0 / 77.0 / 68.1 — adding key detection did not help this symbolic model. — [arXiv:2510.06528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06528)
- Key-chord joint models: Mauch & Dixon 2010 (DBN with key, chord, bass, metric position) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tasl.2009.2032947); Pauwels, Martens, Leman, "Improving the key extraction performance of a simultaneous local key and chord estimation system", ICME, Jul 2011 — [DOI 10.1109/icme.2011.6012146](https://doi.org/10.1109/icme.2011.6012146); HPA (Ni et al. 2012) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tasl.2012.2188516).

- Jiang 2019's confusion matrix explicitly contains inversion classes (maj/3, maj/5, min/b3, min/5, maj/2, maj/b7, min/2, min/b7) alongside sus2/sus4, dim, aug, 7, maj7, min7, hdim7, dim7, 9, 11, 13 — i.e. a model that can output slash chords natively. — [Jiang et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3527892)

### Inferences
- For rock, the maj/min confusion is frequently not a model error but an ill-posed label: power chords (root+5th, no third) are forced to maj or min by 25-class or 170-class models. Resolving the third from a guitar/"other" stem (presence of major vs minor third above the bass root) and otherwise emitting a "5" chord (mir_eval QUALITIES includes "5" and "1") is consistent with McFee's finding that X-chords are mostly 1-chords and power chords.
- Bass-root agreement is a cheap check: if the bass-stem pitch class over a bar equals the predicted root, keep the label; if it equals the third or fifth, consider a slash chord; if it matches neither, down-weight that chord in Viterbi re-scoring.
- Key-aware relabelling should be applied with low weight: the 2025 BACHI ablation and Pauwels' "not proven" comment both suggest key priors can hurt when modulations or borrowed chords occur; use it mainly to break ties between enharmonically or diatonically plausible labels, not to override strong acoustic evidence.

- The quantitative results of Cho & Bello 2014 (which smoothing/feature component matters most) could not be retrieved (IEEE page blocked; no abstract in Crossref).

## Key question 3: Local (section-wise) key estimation and modulation detection

### Takeaway
Local key estimation (LKE) is an active but small research line centred on Weiß/Schreiber/Müller (ICASSP + TASLP 2020), Ding & Weiß (EUSIPCO 2024, code-released ISMIR 2025 follow-up) and older Papadopoulos & Peeters (TASLP 2012); available off-the-shelf tools (madmom, Essentia KeyExtractor, music21 Krumhansl-Schmuckler) are global-key estimators that must be run in sliding windows or per structural section. The characteristic errors are relative (C:maj vs A:min), parallel and fifth confusions, which MIREX scores credit at 0.3/0.2/0.5; final-chorus lifts are a real but now rare phenomenon (~25% of 1960s-1990s US No. 1 hits, one in the 2010s).

Modulations / key lifts in pop
- "In every decade from the 1960s to the 1990s, about 25% of No. 1 hits on the Billboard Hot 100 had a key change; however, in the 2010s, only one Billboard No. 1 hit had a key change" (cited to NPR All Things Considered, 25 Nov 2022); examples: "Because the Night" has a "modulation up a whole step at the end"; BTS "Dynamite" is "the lone single that shifts keys late in the song" to top the Hot 100 since 2020. — [Wikipedia: Modulation (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation_(music))
- Hooktheory TheoryTab has "80,781 songs" and groups progressions as "Beginner", "Intermediate I (inverted chords)", "Intermediate II (secondary dominants and inverted 7 chords)", "Advanced (borrowed chords)", with slash/inversion notation such as "57/4". — [Hooktheory common chord progressions](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/common-chord-progressions)

### Inferences
- Practical LKE for a chord-sheet pipeline: compute beat-synchronous chroma, correlate against a profile set per structural section (or a sliding 8-16 bar window), and run a 24-state HMM/Viterbi over sections with a high self-transition so that only sustained changes register; a +1/+2 semitone jump confined to the last chorus is the "key lift" case (Wikipedia's "Because the Night" whole-step example).
- Relative-key ambiguity (the "C# minor vs E major" case) is the MIREX "relative error" class; since profile methods differ in bias (KS toward dominant, TKP toward relative major), decide mode with chord-level evidence: the pitch class of the first/last chord of the section and the chord occupying the most downbeats, which is also how Korzeniowski derived mode from Billboard tonic+chord annotations.
- Mapping to ukulele: express each section's key relative to the first section; a lift of n semitones can be rendered either as transposed chord names or as "capo n" for that section — music21's `Key.transpose(n)` and `sharps` give the spelled key and whether to prefer flats (negative sharps → flats, e.g. Bb not A#).

### Gaps
- Full text/abstracts of the three Weiß/Schreiber/Müller/Ding LKE papers could not be fetched (IEEE pages blocked; not on arXiv/Zenodo), so their exact architectures, window lengths and accuracies are not recorded here; the ISMIR 2025 paper's code repo (suncerock/cvc-lke-ismir25) is the only confirmed open implementation and is trained on classical cross-version datasets, not rock.
- No pop/rock local-key dataset with modulation annotations was found; Billboard annotates only tonic (per Korzeniowski 2017), Isophonics Beatles has "key changes" per the FMP notebook but their format was not inspected.
- The original NPR/Tedium key-change statistics page could not be fetched; the figure is quoted via Wikipedia.

## Key question 4: Chord-sequence post-processing (smoothing, beat/bar snapping, harmonic rhythm, key-aware relabelling, ukulele-friendly names)

### Takeaway
The literature's consensus is HMM/Viterbi smoothing of chord posteriors (not blind median filtering, which smears boundaries and deletes short chords); beat-synchronous resampling cuts processing to 0.33-1 Hz but was shown not to beat HMM smoothing, and downbeat-dependent transition probabilities are the standard way to snap changes to bars without forbidding anticipations. Label conversion and vocabulary reduction are covered by mir_eval (Harte parsing/encoding, `_inv` and triad/tetrad comparators), music21 (ChordSymbol parsing of 'C/E', flats as '-', Key.sharps for spelling) and pychord (slash chords, transpose).

### Cited Findings
Smoothing and decoding
- Pauwels 2019, Problem 3 ("mismatch between processing rate and chord rate"): "the high frame rate compared to the chord rate leads to a high risk of fragmented chord output ... Fujishima himself used a mean filter, but median [47] filters have also been used. These smoothing filters can be applied either to chord probabilities [48] or to the features themselves [2]"; "The drawback of blindly applying a smoothing filter is that chord transitions may be smeared ... and will cause short chords to be smoothed out even if they are very apparent in the signal. A better method is therefore to consider the chord matching outputs as the observations of an HMM and smooth them with the Viterbi algorithm"; beat-synchronous grids assume "chords only change at beat times ... It has been shown, however, that beat-synchronous processing may not be advantageous compared to smoothing the chord output with an HMM [10]"; "the processing rate of systems built around them is lower (0.33-1 Hz for 60-180 BPM)"; "In HMM-based approaches, the off-diagonal elements of the transition matrix determine where a chord change will lead to, whereas the diagonal elements influence when it takes place"; "chords are more likely to change on downbeats than on other beats ... making chord transition probabilities depend on the metric position of a beat in a measure ... a joint downbeat-chord system [50] that can deal with different time signatures and added or deleted beats ... [39] ... limited to the 4/4 time signature". — [Pauwels et al., ISMIR 2019](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3527739)
- Papadopoulos & Peeters, "Joint Estimation of Chords and Downbeats From an Audio Signal", IEEE TASLP, Jan 2011 (ICASSP 2008 precursor). — [DOI 10.1109/tasl.2010.2045236](https://doi.org/10.1109/tasl.2010.2045236); [DOI 10.1109/icassp.2008.4517561](https://doi.org/10.1109/icassp.2008.4517561)
- FMP HMM notebook: transition matrices can be (1) learned from Beatles bigrams, (2) made transposition-invariant by "considering all twelve cyclic chroma shifts", or (3) "Uniform with self-bias: Large diagonal values emphasizing self-transitions"; Viterbi yields "an optimal state sequence (consisting of chord labels) that best explains the chroma sequence" and the transition model "introduces a kind of context-aware postfiltering". — [FMP C5S3_ChordRec_HMM](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.de/resources/MIR/FMP/C5/C5S3_ChordRec_HMM.html)
- BTC: "Performance improvements due to the introduction of CRFs are evident in CNN but not in BTC and CRNN. This indicates that outputs of CNN were fragmented and an additional decoder training is necessary". — [arXiv:1907.02698](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02698)
- BACHI uses "a supervised boundary detection module that predicts chord-change likelihoods" on "beat-synchronous MIDI tokens" before labelling — boundary-first decoding. — [arXiv:2510.06528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06528)
- Jiang 2019 mentions their MIREX 2018 submission was evaluated "without beat alignment post-processing", implying beat alignment was a post-processing option in their pipeline. — [Jiang et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3527892)
- Oudre, Grenier, Févotte, "Chord Recognition by Fitting Rescaled Chroma Vectors to Chord Templates", IEEE TASLP, Sep 2011 (template-fitting with explicit temporal smoothing). — [DOI 10.1109/tasl.2011.2139205](https://doi.org/10.1109/tasl.2011.2139205)

Label syntax, conversion and vocabulary reduction
- Harte, Sandler, Abdallah, Gómez, "Symbolic Representation of Musical Chords: A Proposed Syntax for Text Annotations", ISMIR 2005. — [Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.1415114](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1415114)
- mir_eval.chord: `split(chord_label, reduce_extended_chords=False)` → `[root, quality, scale_degrees, bass]`; `encode(chord_label, reduce_extended_chords=False, strict_bass_intervals=False)` → `(root_number, semitone_bitmap, bass_number)`; `reduce_extended_quality(quality)` "Maps extended qualities (9ths, 11ths, 13ths) to simpler base qualities with scale degree extensions"; `join(chord_root, quality, extensions, bass)`; comparators `root`, `majmin` (non-maj/min → -1), `thirds`, `triads` ("root and quality through the fifth"), `tetrads`, `sevenths` ([maj, maj7, 7, min, min7, N]), `mirex` ("share at least 3 pitch classes"), plus `_inv` variants; `QUALITIES` has 23 entries including `1`, `5`, `sus2`, `sus4`, `maj9`, `13`; `merge_chord_intervals()` "Combines consecutive identical chords"; `weighted_accuracy()` weights by duration. — [mir_eval/chord.py](https://github.com/mir-evaluation/mir_eval/blob/main/mir_eval/chord.py)
- music21 `harmony.ChordSymbol` parses lead-sheet figures such as 'C/E', 'Cm7/E-', 'Dm7', 'Bb', 'sus2', 'sus4', 'add2', '7omit3', 'power', 'pedal'; "Due to the relative diversity of lead sheet chord syntax, not all expressions are supported"; flats in roots use '-' ("use '-' notation (e.g., 'D-')") while alterations use 'b'/'#'; methods `bass()`, `inversion()`, `findFigure()`, `chordSymbolFromChord()`, `transpose()`; `writeAsChord` is False by default for ChordSymbols. — [music21 moduleHarmony](https://www.music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleHarmony.html)

- FMP Beatles notebook reduction rule for a 24-triad sheet: "consider only the first two intervals of each chord in root position, where augmented chords are mapped to major chords and diminished chords to minor chords". — [FMP C5S3_ChordRec_Beatles](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.de/resources/MIR/FMP/C5/C5S3_ChordRec_Beatles.html)
- McFee 2017 shows the data-driven simplification direction models already learn: "(min7,minmaj7)→min and (maj7,7)→maj". — [McFee & Bello 2017](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1414880)
- ChordMiniApp (TypeScript, 401 stars, updated 2026-09-29) advertises "Music Analysis, Chord Recognition, Beat Tracking, Guitar Diagrams, Piano..." — an open reference for a beat-aligned chord-sheet UI. — [ptnghia-j/ChordMiniApp](https://github.com/ptnghia-j/ChordMiniApp)

### Inferences
- Snapping without destroying anticipations: implement the Pauwels/Papadopoulos idea as a Viterbi over beat frames where the chord-change penalty depends on metric position (lowest on the downbeat, moderate on beat 3 and on the "and of 4" for push anticipations, highest elsewhere); do not hard-quantise to bars.
- Minimum chord duration: enforce via the self-transition probability (diagonal) rather than a post-hoc deletion, per Pauwels' observation that blind smoothing removes "short chords ... very apparent in the signal"; then apply `mir_eval.chord.merge_chord_intervals` to collapse repeats.
- Harmonic-rhythm estimate (chords per bar) can be read directly from the decoded sequence: count distinct labels per bar after merging; choose the per-section mode (1 or 2 chords/bar) as the sheet's grid and flag bars that deviate.
- Ukulele-friendly naming: reduce with `mir_eval.chord.split(..., reduce_extended_chords=True)` then map qualities to a small set (maj, min, 7, maj7, min7, sus4, dim, aug; add9 → maj; min7 → min when the model's seventh confidence is low), and spell roots using the section key's `Key.sharps` sign (flats for ≤0, sharps for >0) — so Bb in F major but A# in B major.

### Gaps
- No audio-domain "harmonic rhythm estimation" paper was found via Crossref/arXiv (the only hits were symbolic generation work and Oxford Music Online definitions); the per-bar counting approach above is my inference.
- Cho & Bello 2014's exact numbers on smoothing vs. beat-sync could not be retrieved (only Pauwels' summary).
- Chordify's "simplify chords"/capo/transposition behaviour and Chord ai's slash-chord display could not be verified (help pages, App Store, Play Store and archived copies were all blocked).

## Key question 5: Chord datasets for rock with bass/inversion annotations (for evaluation)

### Takeaway
McGill Billboard is the primary rock/pop set with explicit inversion variants (chords_majmininv, chords_majmin7inv) and NNLS bass-capable chroma, released CC0; Isophonics (Beatles etc.) uses Harte syntax (which encodes bass via "/"); CASD (Chordify, 50 songs × 4 annotators) and POP909-CL (909 Chinese pop MIDI with corrected chord/beat/key labels, symbolic) cover annotator subjectivity and key labels; FiloBass is the only fetched dataset pairing bass stems with chord symbols (jazz).

### Cited Findings
- McGill Billboard (mirdata loader): "890 slots from a random sample of Billboard chart slots"; chord variants "chords_full", "chords_majmin7", "chords_majmin7inv", "chords_majmin", "chords_majmininv"; includes "Tuning estimates from the Chordino Vamp plug-in" and "Non-negative-least-squares (NNLS) chroma vectors"; sections labelled both as letters (A, B, A') and names (intro, verse, chorus); tonic and metre from the Salami LAB metadata; "released under a Creative Commons 0 license". Citation: Burgoyne, Wild, Fujinaga, "An Expert Ground Truth Set for Audio Chord Recognition and Music Analysis", ISMIR 2011. — [mirdata billboard.py](https://github.com/mir-dataset-loaders/mirdata/blob/master/mirdata/datasets/billboard.py)
- Billboard key annotation caveat: "only the tonic, and not the mode (major or minor), is annotated for each piece". — [Korzeniowski & Widmer 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02921)
- Billboard vocabulary statistics: "230 different chord qualities ... 2,749 distinct chord classes"; "the first 10% chord qualities cover 93.86% of the data, the last 50% chord qualities only cover 0.35%". — [Jiang et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3527892)
- Isophonics: reference annotations for The Beatles, Carole King, Queen (and Zweieck); "chord, onset and segmentation annotations"; Harte's PhD thesis "describes the chord syntax, transcription process and verification process for the Beatles collection". — [isophonics.net/datasets](http://isophonics.net/datasets); mirdata lists a Beatles loader with "beat, chord, key, section annotations for 179 songs" — [mirdata docs](https://mirdata.readthedocs.io/en/stable/source/mirdata.html); the FMP notebook describes "twelve Beatles albums comprising 180 audio tracks" with "reference annotations for chords, beats, key changes and music structures" — [FMP C5S3_ChordRec_Beatles](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.de/resources/MIR/FMP/C5/C5S3_ChordRec_Beatles.html)
- Humphrey & Bello 2015 corpus: Isophonics (180 Beatles + 20 Queen used), Billboard ("over 1000 annotations, of which more than 700 have been made public"), MARL (295 annotations: 195 USPop + 100 RWC-Pop); merged 1235 tracks, 18 duplicates removed → 1217 unique tracks; 157-class vocabulary (13 qualities × 12 + N). — [Humphrey & Bello 2015](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1417549)
- CASD: "Chordify Annotator Subjectivity Dataset (CASD) ... the largest available dataset for assessing chord annotation agreement", "50 tracks with 4 annotations each"; root agreement 76%. — [Poltronieri et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01588); [Koops et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/09298215.2019.1613436)
- POP909-CL: "an enhanced version of POP909 with human-corrected labels ... of chords, beats, keys, and time signatures across all 909 tracks" (piano-arrangement MIDI of Chinese pop; symbolic, not audio). — [arXiv:2510.06528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06528)
- FiloBass: 48 jazz bass transcriptions with "audio stems ... performance-aligned MIDI ... beats, downbeats, chord symbols, and musical form markers". — [arXiv:2311.02023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02023)
- Hooktheory TheoryTab: "80,781 songs", Roman-numeral with inversion notation. — [Hooktheory](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/common-chord-progressions)
- Evaluation tooling: mir_eval `majmin_inv`/`sevenths_inv` score inversions; `evaluate()` returns all metrics. — [mir_eval/chord.py](https://github.com/mir-evaluation/mir_eval/blob/main/mir_eval/chord.py)

### Inferences
- For a bass-anchored pipeline the cleanest evaluation is Billboard `chords_majmininv` + its own Chordino NNLS (bass) chroma: compare `majmin_inv` before and after bass re-scoring, and separately report the root metric to isolate root fixes from inversion fixes.
- Because inter-annotator root agreement is only 76% and inversion labelling is admitted to be subjective (Humphrey & Bello), target metrics should be reported with a human-agreement ceiling in mind; gains above ~0.85 root / ~0.80 majmin_inv are within annotator noise.
