# Tablature theory, ukulele arrangement and strumming-pattern extraction (notes for a YouTube-to-ukulele-tab pipeline)

Scope: (1) pitch-to-fretboard mapping / fingering optimisation for gCEA (high-G), low-G and baritone DGBE; (2) arranging guitar/piano rock songs for ukulele; (3) separating strummed chords vs riffs vs melody in transcribed data; (4) detecting and notating strumming patterns; (5) notation conventions and file formats producible from Python; plus rhythm quantisation, section detection, and ground truth for "Summer of '69" and "Pour Some Sugar On Me".

Research date: 2026-10-01. Several tab sites (UkuTabs, Ultimate Guitar direct, e-chords, ozbcoz, tunebat) returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher; Ultimate Guitar pages were read through the r.jina.ai reader proxy and are flagged as such.

## Q1. Algorithms for optimal string/fret assignment (fingering) and how they adapt to a 4-string re-entrant ukulele

### Takeaway
Every serious tablature-arrangement algorithm since 1989 is a shortest-path / Viterbi search over a layered DAG whose nodes are candidate (string, fret[, finger]) positions per note or chord and whose edge weights encode biomechanical difficulty (fret distance, string change, hand span, high-fret penalty, open-string bonus). The ukulele version is the same graph with 4 strings, ~12-15 usable frets, 1-3 candidates per pitch, and a special rule for the re-entrant G string (which duplicates pitches of the C/E strings an octave above a low-G).

### Cited Findings
- Sayegh (1989) "Fingering for String Instrument with the Optimum Path Paradigm", Computer Music Journal 13(6):76-84, is the founding formulation: fingering is cast as a shortest-path problem; "the global optimization approach of Sayegh computes the optimum (i.e. the easiest) fingering considering the whole piece" — [Radicioni, Anselma & Lombardo, "An algorithm to compute fingering for string instruments" (AISC 2004)](http://pgp.di.unito.it/~anselma/pdf/AISC04.pdf)
- Radicioni et al. build, per phrase segment, a graph with "a node for each possible fingered position, labeling it with the triple <string, fret, finger>, and linking it to the nodes related to the following note"; edge weights "estimate the cost of the transition from a fingered position to another" based on "physical features of the instrument and morphology of the hand"; the graph is a DAG and the shortest path "can be found in O(n) time, where n is the number of notes in the fragment". They add a segmentation principle (Lerdahl & Jackendoff grouping structure) so phrases are fingered separately and chained by fixing the last node of one segment as the first of the next — [Radicioni et al. 2004](http://pgp.di.unito.it/~anselma/pdf/AISC04.pdf)
- On guitar "each note could be accessed at most in four positions by the four available fingers: in the worst case we will have a total of sixteen possible fingered positions for a note" — [Radicioni et al. 2004](http://pgp.di.unito.it/~anselma/pdf/AISC04.pdf)
- Burlet & Fujinaga's arrangement algorithm (used in Burlet & Hindle 2017) "operates by using Dijkstra's algorithm to search for the shortest path through a directed weighted graph, in which the vertices represent candidate string and fret combinations for a note or chord". "Three biomechanical complexity factors are aggregated to form each edge weight: the fret-wise distance required to transition between notes or chords, the fret-wise finger span required to perform chords, and a penalty of one if the fretting hand surpasses the seventh fret." When a note is followed by a chord the fret distance is |f - (max(g)+min(g))/2| where f is the note's fret and g the chord's fret vector. Penalty values "were determined through subjective analysis of the resulting tablature arrangements." — [Burlet & Hindle 2017, PeerJ CS 3:e109 (PDF)](https://softwareprocess.es/pubs/burlet2017PeerJ.pdf)
- Burlet & Hindle list the family of solutions: "graph-search algorithms (Radicioni & Lombardo, 2005; Radisavljevic & Driessen, 2004; Burlet & Fujinaga, 2013), neural networks (Tuohy & Potter, 2006), and genetic algorithms (Tuohy & Potter, 2005; Burlet, 2013)"; a good arrangement is one where "transitions between notes do not require excessive hand movement and the performance of chords require minimal stretching of the hand (Heijink & Meulenbroek, 2002)" — [Burlet & Hindle 2017](https://softwareprocess.es/pubs/burlet2017PeerJ.pdf)
- Burlet & Hindle's full pipeline converts MIDI to MusicXML with MuseScore's command-line tools, then appends string/fret to each note in the MusicXML — [Burlet & Hindle 2017](https://softwareprocess.es/pubs/burlet2017PeerJ.pdf)
- Hori & Sagayama (ISMIR 2016) model fingering as HMM decoding: hidden states are left-hand forms q_i = (string s_i, fret f_i, finger h_i); for a 6-string, 19-fret guitar there are 6 x (19 x 4 + 1) = 462 forms; the output probability is 1 iff note(q_i) = open-string MIDI number + fret, else 0, so decoding is pure transition-cost optimisation — [Hori & Sagayama, "Minimax Viterbi algorithm for HMM-based guitar fingering decision", ISMIR 2016](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2016/paper/000285.pdf)
- Their hand-designed transition probability is a(q_i -> q_j) ~ (1/2d_t) exp(-|ifp(q_i) - ifp(q_j)| / d_t) x 1/(1 + |s_i - s_j|) x p_H(h_j), where ifp = index-finger position = fret - finger + 1 (fingers assumed on consecutive frets), d_t = inter-onset interval (longer gaps make moves easier), the Laplace term models "the left hand of a guitarist usually stays at a fixed position for several notes and then leaps a few frets to a new position", and p_H(1..4) = 0.35, 0.3, 0.25, 0.1 (index easiest, pinky hardest). Parameters are designed, not learned, because tab-annotated training data was scarce — [Hori & Sagayama 2016](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2016/paper/000285.pdf)
- The minimax Viterbi variant replaces delta_it x a_ij with min(delta_it, a_ij) in the recursion so that the path maximises the minimum transition probability ("make the most difficult move easier"), which they argue matters more for beginners than minimising total difficulty; prior HMM work: Hori, Kameoka & Sagayama, "Input-Output HMM Applied to Automatic Arrangement for Guitars", J. Information Processing 21(2):264-271, 2013 — [Hori & Sagayama 2016](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2016/paper/000285.pdf); [Semantic Scholar entry](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Minimax-Viterbi-Algorithm-for-HMM-Based-Guitar-Hori-Sagayama/0a67d89bf25567495955b4785ae6379bda7b88d9)
- Learned alternative: MIDI-to-Tab (2024) trains a BART encoder-decoder with masked language modelling to predict string tokens from pitch/velocity/duration/time-shift tokens; pre-trained on DadaGP (27,619 Guitar Pro tablatures) and fine-tuned on 281 pro transcriptions + GuitarSet; 94.35% next-note accuracy with ground-truth context, 73.58% agreement over full pieces; guitarists rated it 6.04/10 vs TuxGuitar 3.32, MuseScore 4.67, Guitar Pro 4.69; limitations: "Guitar tuning is never explicitly encoded" and it "does not handle the use of capos" — [MIDI-to-Tab, arXiv 2408.05024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.05024)
- Practical open-source Python implementation: tabforge uses a Viterbi fingering model whose state is "fingering + hand position" with penalties `high_fret_penalty`, `open_string_bonus`, `move_penalty`, `stretch_penalty`, `max_stretch`; tunings include standard, drop D, Eb, DADGAD, open G, 4/5-string bass and ukulele; exports .gp5 via PyGuitarPro, MusicXML via music21, MIDI and ASCII; MIT licence — [tabforge GitHub](https://github.com/rayBastard/tabforge)
- Ukulele fret counts: soprano 12-15 frets, concert and tenor 15-20 — [Ukulele Tricks, "Ukulele Sizes"](https://ukuleletricks.com/ukulele-sizes-soprano-concert-tenor-baritone/)
- Standard ukulele tuning in MIDI numbers: G4 C4 E4 A4; chords-db's C-major entry has frets [0,0,0,3] with midi [67,60,64,72], i.e. open strings 67, 60, 64, 69 — [tombatossals/chords-db ukulele.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tombatossals/chords-db/master/lib/ukulele.json)
- Re-entrant vs low-G: "everything you play on a high-g string is an octave above a low-G string", so notes that must sound low have to be relocated to lower strings; chord shapes usually transfer directly even though voicing changes — [Live Ukulele, "Converting Guitar Tabs for Ukulele"](https://liveukulele.com/tabs/converting/)

### Inferences
- Ukulele adaptation of the DAG: per pitch p (MIDI), candidates are {(s, p - open[s]) : 0 <= p - open[s] <= maxFret} over the 4 strings. With gCEA (open = [67,60,64,69]) and maxFret = 12, the playable range is C4 (60) to A5 (81); the G string only adds pitches 67-79, all of which are also on C/E/A strings, so it is mostly useful for open-string G and for "campanella" effects. With low-G (open G3 = 55) the range extends down to 55 and each pitch 55-59 has exactly one candidate. For baritone DGBE (D3 G3 B3 E4 = 50, 55, 59, 64, linear), the range is 50-76 at 12 frets.
- Because a ukulele has at most 3-4 candidates per note and 12-15 frets, the per-note state space (<= 4 strings x 4 fingers) is ~4x smaller than guitar, so exhaustive chord-candidate enumeration (all combinations of per-note candidates with distinct strings) is cheap: for a 4-note chord it is at most 4! = 24 string permutations before pruning by span.
- A sensible edge cost for ukulele, combining Burlet (fret distance, span, high-fret penalty), Hori (Laplace on index-finger position, 1/(1+|ds|) string change, finger-difficulty prior, inter-onset scaling) and tabforge (open-string bonus, stretch cap): cost = w1 * |ifp_i - ifp_j| / IOI + w2 * |s_i - s_j| + w3 * max(0, span - 3 or 4) + w4 * [maxFret > 7] - w5 * [open string] with hard constraints span <= 4-5 frets and no two notes on one string. Weights must be tuned by listening/looking, as Burlet did; there is no published ukulele-specific weight set.
- For chord-strumming songs the arrangement problem collapses to chord-shape selection (Q2), and the fingering DAG is only needed for riffs/melody segments (Q4).
- Hori's minimax criterion is attractive for a beginner-oriented uke tab generator because it avoids one impossible jump at the cost of slightly harder average moves.

### Gaps
- Sayegh's original CMJ 1989 article was not retrievable (paywalled); its cost details are known here only via Radicioni and Hori's descriptions.
- Burlet & Fujinaga (2013, ISMIR "Robotaba") conference paper with the full graph formulation and its evaluation was not fetched; only the PeerJ summary.
- No paper specifically about ukulele fingering/arrangement was found; everything above is guitar work adapted by inference.
- Radisavljevic & Driessen 2004 ("Path difference learning") was only seen as a search hit ([ICMC 2004 PDF](https://www.mistic.ece.uvic.ca/publications/2004_icmc_pdl.pdf)); not read.

## Q2. Choosing ukulele chord voicings; chord-shape data sources and Python libraries

### Takeaway
Use a dictionary of standard first-position shapes (chords-db's ukulele.json, ChordPro's ukulele config, or algorithmic generation via ukechords/pychord) and pick among a chord's positions by minimal movement from the previous shape, preference for open/low positions and barre avoidance — the same criteria a 2024 DadaGP study found human guitarists use.

### Cited Findings
- tombatossals/chords-db ukulele.json: 4 strings, 4 frets per diagram, tuning G4 C4 E4 A4, keys C Db D Eb E F Gb G Ab A Bb B, ~45 suffixes (major, minor, dim, dim7, sus2, sus4, 7sus4, aug, 6, 69, 7, 7b5, 9, 11, 13, maj7, maj9, m6, m7, m7b5, m9, mmaj7, add9, madd9, ...); each position has frets, fingers, baseFret, barres, midi (e.g. C major position 1: frets [0,0,0,3], fingers [0,0,0,3], baseFret 1, midi [67,60,64,72]) — [chords-db ukulele.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tombatossals/chords-db/master/lib/ukulele.json); README describes positions with frets/fingers/barres/capo and says data is "packed in a JSON library" — [chords-db README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tombatossals/chords-db/master/README.md)
- tabsy-gr/chords-db publishes instruments as JSON with JSON Schemas, including ukulele in G C E A, with frets/fingers/MIDI per voicing — [tabsy-gr/chords-db (search result)](https://github.com/tabsy-gr/chords-db)
- ChordPro ships "JSON config files with chord definitions for guitar and ukulele" — [ChordPro groups.io thread (search result)](https://groups.io/g/ChordPro/topic/database_ro_library_of_chord/77034936); `{define-ukulele Dm base-fret 1 frets 2 2 1 0}` is a valid ukulele chord definition — [ChordPro define directive (search result)](https://chordpro.org/chordpro/directives-define/)
- ukechords (Python, MIT): "identifying what chords are made from a particular fret/shape on a stringed instrument, and determining what shapes can be used to produce a given chord"; supports "several/arbitrary tunings, with any number of strings" with presets "ukulele" and "guitar"; shapes are computed algorithmically with caching, and it does "heuristically determining the difficulty of different shapes, and ranking them" (or sorting by position minimisation); uses pychord for chord naming; CLI `ident` — [nickurak/ukechords](https://github.com/nickurak/ukechords)
- pychord ("A library to handle musical chords in Python") is on PyPI — [pychord on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pychord/0.5.1/); chordsketch is a ChordPro/iReal parser-renderer with "guitar" and "ukulele" presets — [chordsketch on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/chordsketch/0.7.0/)
- music21 has `tablature.UkeleleFretBoard` (sic) with tuning G C E A, 4 strings, built from `FretNote(string, fret, fingering)`, plus `ChordWithFretBoard(figure, numStrings, fretNotes, displayFrets)` — [music21.tablature docs](https://music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleTablature.html)
- LilyPond ships `predefined-ukulele-fretboards.ly` and `#ukulele-tuning`; the ukulele predefined table "contains these chords plus an additional three chords (major sixth, suspended second, and suspended fourth)" — [LilyPond 2.25 Notation Reference, fretted strings](https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.25/Documentation/notation/common-notation-for-fretted-strings)
- Human voicing choice (DadaGP study): musicians favour "minimal movement" between successive chords, prefer open voicings, and avoid barre chords unless necessary; the model conditions on previous diagram, genre and difficulty — [Guitar Chord Diagram Suggestion for Western Popular Music, arXiv 2407.14260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.14260)
- Guitar chord shapes are often reused on uke: "identical fingering shapes often work between tunings, though harmonic voicing changes"; prioritise "melody accuracy while treating filler notes (drones, arpeggios) flexibly" — [Live Ukulele, Converting](https://liveukulele.com/tabs/converting/)

### Inferences
- Voicing selection as a second, chord-level Viterbi: states = chord positions from the dictionary; emission = position matches the detected chord label (and, optionally, how many of the transcribed pitch classes it covers); transition cost = sum of per-string fret moves + barre penalty + baseFret penalty. This directly implements the "minimal movement / open / no barre" preferences.
- Commonly published first-position gCEA shapes for the two target songs (string order G C E A; verify against chords-db before use): C 0003, D 2220, A 2100, Bm 4222 (barre at 2), G 0232, F 2010, Bb 3211, Am 2000, Em 0432, E 4442 or 1402. ukulelearn confirms Bm is "a barre chord ... (fret 2)" for Summer of '69 — [ukulelearn](https://ukulelearn.com/summer-of-69-ukulele-chords/). The rest are from general knowledge and are not individually sourced here.
- chords-db is JS/JSON but trivially loaded with `json.load` in Python; it is the most complete ready-made ukulele shape dictionary found.

### Gaps
- An npm/PyPI package literally named "ukulele-chords" was not verified.
- chords-db's licence text and exact ukulele chord count were not visible in the fetched README.
- No published, quantitative "difficulty score" for ukulele shapes was found; ukechords' heuristic is undocumented in the README.

## Q3. Transposing / arranging guitar- or piano-based rock for ukulele (key, capo, chord simplification)

### Takeaway
Treat the ukulele as a guitar capoed at the 5th fret (subtract 5 from guitar frets; baritone = guitar's top four strings; GCEA = baritone + capo 5). Keep the original key when it yields open shapes; otherwise transpose to a uke-friendly key (C/G/D/A/F families) and say "capo n" to restore the original pitch. Simplify power chords to major/minor by context, drop extensions to 7ths, drop slash-chord basses and "add" notes.

### Cited Findings
- "the ukulele is tuned the same as a guitar capod on the 5th fret", so "any number on the tab higher than 5 can be used straight on ukulele by subtracting 5" (guitar fret 8 -> uke fret 3); guitar E A D G B E maps to uke G C E A (top four strings) — [Live Ukulele, Converting](https://liveukulele.com/tabs/converting/)
- Notes below uke range: (1) "Moving the note up to the next octave will often get you out of trouble", (2) transpose everything up until the lowest note fits (may sit uncomfortably high), (3) retune down a half-step or two or "use a baritone ukulele for extended low range"; sometimes abandoning the conversion is best ("playing Cochise an octave and a half above Tom Morello's riff") — [Live Ukulele, Converting](https://liveukulele.com/tabs/converting/)
- Baritone is tuned D-G-B-E "like the bottom four strings of a guitar"; "By placing the capo at the 5th fret, you transpose your baritone ukulele's tuning to G-C-E-A", so a standard-tuned chord chart played on baritone sounds in a different key — [Ukulele Tricks, baritone article](https://ukuleletricks.com/using-your-baritone-ukulele-to-follow-along-with-my-standard-tuned-lessons/)
- "the baritone ukulele shares the same tuning as the top four strings of the guitar, most guitar chords can be played on the baritone ukulele without modification" — [Kraftgeek (search result)](https://kraftgeek.com/blogs/musician-guide/how-to-translate-guitar-chords-to-ukulele)
- Dr. Uke: playing a baritone chart's "dots" on a GCEA uke without capo "lowered the key by seven half-steps" (equivalently raised by five) — [doctoruke.com transposing PDF (search result)](https://www.doctoruke.com/transposewithdots.pdf)
- Capo on ukulele: "clip it on the first fret and every chord you play sounds one semitone higher" — [UkuTabs transpose guide (search result)](https://ukutabs.com/ukulele-guides/transpose-ukulele-chords/)
- Chord simplification rules (Brad Bordessa): remove extensions (G9 -> G7, F#13 -> F#7); undo substitutions (maj7 -> major, m6/m7/m9 -> minor, 9/11/13 -> 7); power chords "made up of only the root and 5th" can replace major, minor, sus and 7th (Cm -> C5, Asus4 -> A5); ignore "add" chords (Fadd9 -> F); ignore slash chords ("Em/B becomes Em, A/B becomes A"); ignore most parenthetical notes; play major instead of 7th "since major chords are contained within 7th chords" — [Live Ukulele, "Make Ukulele Songs Easier by Simplifying the Chords"](https://liveukulele.com/songs/how-to-make-songs-easier/); same rules in [UkuTabs, "Simplify Difficult Ukulele Chords" (search result)](https://ukutabs.com/ukulele-guides/simplify-difficult-ukulele-chords-5-quick-tips/)
- "The absence of the 3rd that characterizes major and minor chord means a power chord can be used for either" — [UkuTabs simplify guide (search result)](https://ukutabs.com/ukulele-guides/simplify-difficult-ukulele-chords-5-quick-tips/)
- Rock songs on uke: "Almost every easy rock and roll song can be played with just three or four chords, typically ... C, F, G, and Am" — [Ukuleles Unanimous (search result)](https://www.ukulelesunanimous.com/uke-blog/rock-and-roll)
- Worked example of key choice: Def Leppard's original is in E with C#5/F#5/B5 power chords and an E-A-B chorus ([UG 863681 via jina](https://r.jina.ai/https://tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/tab/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me-chords-863681)), while the popular "simplified and more musical" acoustic chart uses capo 3 with Am-D-F verse, D-C-D-C-F-C-F-C pre-chorus and C-F-G-F chorus, and a commenter recommends capo 4 "for better alignment with the original" ([UG 2281783 via jina](https://r.jina.ai/https://tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/tab/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me-chords-2281783)); the ukulele charts use Am, D, F, C, G, A with Am-D-F verses and C-F-G choruses ([search summary of e-chords/ukulele-tabs uke pages](https://www.e-chords.com/ukulele/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me))
- Summer of '69 stays in its original D major on ukulele; chords D, A, Bm, G, F, Bb, C; "Electric guitar players can substitute power chords to avoid barre chords" — [Lauren Bateman chord chart](https://www.laurenbateman.com/summer-of-69-chord-chart/); [ukulelearn](https://ukulelearn.com/summer-of-69-ukulele-chords/)

### Inferences
- Interval arithmetic for the pipeline: baritone DGBE is 5 semitones (a perfect fourth) below GCEA on the C/E/A strings (C4 60 vs G3 55, E4 64 vs B3 59, A4 69 vs E4 64); the Ukulele Tricks fetch summary called this "a perfect fifth lower", which contradicts both the capo-5 statement on the same page and Dr. Uke's seven-half-steps statement; treat it as a fourth. Low-G replaces G4 (67) with G3 (55), extending the range 5 semitones below C4.
- Power chord -> triad: decide major/minor from the detected key and scale degree (diatonic default: I, IV, V major; ii, iii, vi minor), overriding with any detected third from the chroma/pitch data (e.g. C#5 in E major -> C#m, consistent with the capo-4 Am-shape chart of PSSOM).
- Key-choice heuristic: score each of the 12 transpositions by the number of chord labels that have an open (baseFret 1, no barre) shape in the dictionary, plus a penalty for barre count; emit "capo n" if the chosen playing key differs from the original. For PSSOM this yields the C-major/A-minor shape set with capo 4 as the "sounds like the record" option, or no capo for a singable lower key.
- For riffs/melody (not chords), apply the subtract-5 rule to any guitar-derived tab and octave-displace notes below C4 (high-G) or G3 (low-G); for piano-derived material, take the skyline (top voice) and bass only if it fits.

### Gaps
- No source quantified how much of a rock catalogue is playable in original key on uke; the key-choice heuristic above is an inference.
- The UkuTabs transposing guide and ozbcoz chart pages returned 403; their content is known only from search snippets.

## Q4. Distinguishing strummed chords vs single-note riffs vs melody in transcribed note data

### Takeaway
No dedicated paper on "strum vs riff vs melody" classification was found; the practical approach combines (a) a ~20 ms simultaneity threshold to group onsets into chord events, (b) per-window polyphony and note-density statistics, (c) chord-label stability from a chord recogniser, and (d) skyline/highest-voice heuristics for melody — informed by the finding that note-level transcribers fail on dense strumming while strum detectors treat the chord as the unit.

### Cited Findings
- A patent on virtual string instruments uses "a preselected time threshold (on the order of about 20 milliseconds)" to distinguish "events within a chord (approximately simultaneous events)" from events of different chords; shorter differences are treated as part of a "strum" — [US Patent 5,393,926 "Virtual music system" (search result)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5393926)
- "Classical note-tracking models perform well for fingerpicking but are not designed to predict strumming directions ... and often struggle with the dense polyphony and rhythmic structure of strumming, where the emphasis lies on chord-level articulation" — [Murgul, Schimper & Heizmann, "Joint Transcription of Acoustic Guitar Strumming Directions and Chords", arXiv 2508.07973 / ISMIR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973v1)
- "Strumming is a guitar playing technique where the player strokes over the strings of a chord in an up- or downwards direction. Conventional note-based transcription methods cannot distinguish strumming direction due to the very short time difference between string hits" — [ISMIR 2022 LBD, Murgul & Heizmann](https://ismir2022program.ismir.net/lbd_393.html)
- Skyline algorithm: "takes the highest note at each point in time", justified because "melodies are often carried by the highest voice" in Western pop/art/folk; weaknesses: the extracted line may "leap" between accompaniment and melody, so filtering heuristics are needed; one system quantises to 16ths before applying skyline — [A Convolutional Approach to Melody Line Identification in Symbolic Scores, arXiv 1906.10547 (search summary)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.10547); [Melody Track Identification in Music Symbolic Files, FLAIRS 2006](https://cdn.aaai.org/FLAIRS/2006/Flairs06-049.pdf)
- Voice-separation research links "vertical links in lower pitches (e.g., accompaniment)" and "horizontal links in higher pitches (e.g., melody)" to bass-note density — [Learning note-to-note affinity for voice segregation, ISMIR 2021 (search summary)](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000035.pdf)
- Electric-guitar technique detection (bend, vibrato, hammer, slide, etc.) is treated as "a time sequence pattern recognition problem" on note-level features — [Electric guitar playing technique detection in real-world recordings, ISMIR 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000119.pdf)
- TENT ("Technique-Embedded Note Tracking") exists for real-world guitar solos (riff/melody side) — [TISMIR](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.23)
- Yousician's rhythm-guitar system separates the guitar stem (HTDemucs "other") and then detects strums as events, implicitly assuming the track is accompaniment — [Lukoianov & Klapuri, arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756)

### Inferences
- Event grouping: cluster note onsets within 20-30 ms into one "event"; an event with >= 3 distinct pitch classes spanning >= 2 strings is a chord strum; 1-2 pitches is a single-note event.
- Window features per bar (or per beat): mean polyphony per event, fraction of chord events, onset rate (notes per beat), pitch range (semitones), pitch-class set match to the detected chord label, inter-onset regularity (coefficient of variation of IOIs). Rules: strummed accompaniment = high chord fraction, onsets on a regular 8th/16th grid, pitch set == chord label; riff = low polyphony, repeated 1-2-bar pitch sequence (high autocorrelation), narrow range, often on lower strings; melody = monophonic, highest voice (skyline), longer durations, follows vocal phrasing. Use the chord recogniser's label stability as a tie-breaker: bars where the pitch content matches a stable chord label are chords; bars where it doesn't are riffs.
- Because audio-to-MIDI transcribers (Basic Pitch, MT3) under-detect notes inside dense strums, the chord/strum decision should also use audio features (spectral flux onset density, chroma stability) rather than transcribed notes alone — this is the reason the strum-transcription literature works from spectrograms.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper was found that explicitly classifies guitar passages into strummed/riff/melody; the heuristics are inferences from adjacent work.
- GuitarSet ([ISMIR 2018](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2018/paper/000188.pdf)) reportedly contains "comp" and "solo" recordings that could serve as training data for a strum-vs-lead classifier, but this was not verified in the fetched material.

## Q5. Detecting and notating strumming patterns (D/U/x, accents) from audio or onsets; what is realistically detectable

### Takeaway
Strum onsets are reliably detectable (F1 ~93-97%); up/down direction from audio alone is harder (per-class F1 ~79-86% with a CRNN trained on IMU-labelled data) and was considered impossible for note-based methods as recently as 2022; muted strums and accents are not handled by any published audio model. The robust engineering path is: detect strum onsets on the separated guitar stem, snap them to a beat/8th grid, infer direction by position (on-beat = down, off-beat = up) with optional spectral refinement, and decode against a small vocabulary of common patterns with Viterbi — which is exactly what the Yousician system does.

### Cited Findings
- ISMIR 2022 (Murgul & Heizmann): "The audio signal is used for strumming event detection and the motion signal is used for direction classification" (6-axis gyro/accelerometer on the hand); F1 85% up / 92% down; "conventional note-based transcription methods cannot distinguish the direction of the strumming movement"; a manually labelled 5-minute test set was released — [ISMIR 2022 LBD 393](https://ismir2022program.ismir.net/lbd_393.html)
- ISMIR 2025 (Murgul, Schimper & Heizmann): CRNN on log-mel spectrograms (229 bins, 30 Hz min, 16 kHz audio), BiGRU 256 units; three outputs: strum onset (regressing time-to-next-onset with a triangular distribution), direction (up/down), chord (24 maj/min); trained on 4 h synthetic (GuitarPro renderings with randomised effects) + 90 min real recordings from three guitarists labelled semi-automatically with an ESP32 wrist accelerometer; on microphone audio: strum detection F1 92.75% (up 85.51%, down 79.02%), chord accuracy 90.06%; a spectral-flux onset baseline scored 79.49% F1; "the system currently excludes muted strumming events and restricts chord vocabulary to major/minor" — [arXiv 2508.07973](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973v1); [ISMIR 2025 poster page](https://ismir2025program.ismir.net/poster_89.html)
- Yousician (Lukoianov & Klapuri, 2025): three stages — HTDemucs "other" stem, fine-tuned MERT + MLP strum-onset detector, Viterbi decoding of detected strums against "a vocabulary defined by expert musicians" of 924 one-to-two-bar patterns in slash notation with transition penalties; data: 931 recordings of 410 pop/rock songs at four difficulty levels; strum detection F1 96.9% on the "other" stem, 94.7% F1 reconstructing strums from decoded patterns; bar-line discontinuities cut from 4.88% to 0.41%; limitations: assumes steady tempo, performance drops on human vs synthetic audio, guitar separation SNR "below 0 dB" in mixes — [arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756)
- Strum notation conventions used by uke sites: D = down, U = up (also arrows); muted strums "(d) or (u)"; "x" = chunk/chuck ("a percussive stroke with no ringing notes"); bold/capital = accent; "-" = a missed strum/pause used to show timing; the "island strum" / "old faithful" is D-DU-UDU — [UkuTabs beginner strumming guide](https://ukutabs.com/ukulele-guides/ukulele-strumming-patterns-beginners/); [MasterClass, How to Strum a Ukulele](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-strum-a-ukulele); [Klos Guitars, 7 patterns](https://klosguitars.com/blogs/stories/7-ukulele-strumming-patterns-every-beginner-should-know) (all three from search summaries; pages not fetched directly)
- Published strum patterns for Summer of '69: "↓-↓↑-↓↑-↓↑" (D DU DU DU) with advice to "simplify to downstrokes while you learn the changes" — [ukulelearn](https://ukulelearn.com/summer-of-69-ukulele-chords/); Lauren Bateman gives an easier "1 2 3+ 4+ / D D DU D U" and a syncopated 16th-feel version "1+ 2+ 3+ 4+ / DD D**D** DD D**D**" with accents on 1, the "+" after 2, and 4 — [Lauren Bateman](https://www.laurenbateman.com/summer-of-69-chord-chart/); the UG ukulele tab says "There is no strumming pattern for this song yet" — [UG 1378082 via jina](https://r.jina.ai/https://tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/tab/bryan-adams/summer-of-69-ukulele-1378082)
- For Pour Some Sugar On Me, the UG acoustic chart states "No official strumming pattern was documented" — [UG 2281783 via jina](https://r.jina.ai/https://tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/tab/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me-chords-2281783)
- GuitarPro/PyGuitarPro can store stroke direction (`BeatStroke.direction` in {none, up, down}, `BeatStroke.value` = duration), dead notes (`NoteType.dead`), palm mute, accents (`NoteEffect.accentuatedNote`, `heavyAccentuatedNote`) — verified by local introspection of PyGuitarPro 0.11 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/PyGuitarPro/)); alphaTex has `{bu}`/`{bd}` brush with optional duration and `{ds}` dead-slap beats and `{dy f}` dynamics — [alphaTex beat properties](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_beat-properties.mdx)

### Inferences
- Realistic detectability from a YouTube mix: strum onsets and their beat-grid positions: yes (with stem separation). Direction: inferable mostly from metric position (down on beats and on-beat 8ths, up on off-beat 8ths/16ths) — this convention is exactly how the published D/U patterns above are laid out, so position-based inference will match human tabs most of the time; an audio direction classifier adds only modest certainty (~80% per class) and needs training data. Muted/chuck strums: partially detectable as broadband onsets with low chroma energy (spectral flatness high, chroma energy low) on a strum position; no published model. Accents: onset-strength peak relative to neighbours gives a usable accent flag but no literature validates it for guitar strums.
- Pattern decoding: build a vocabulary of ~20-50 common 4/4 patterns (D-DU-UDU, D-DU-DU, DDUUDU, all-down 8ths, etc.) encoded as 8 or 16 slots per bar; score each bar's quantised onset vector against each pattern; Viterbi with a switching penalty across bars (as in Lukoianov & Klapuri) gives a stable per-section pattern, which is what tab sites print (one pattern per song/section).
- Output notation: per section, print one line like "Strum: D - D U - U D U" aligned to "1 + 2 + 3 + 4 +", using (d)/(u) for muted and x for chuck, matching UkuTabs conventions.

### Gaps
- The ISMIR 2025 paper's released code/dataset availability was not checked.
- No source quantifies how well metric-position-based direction inference matches human-written D/U patterns.
- No published method for accent or muted-strum detection in guitar strumming was found (the 2025 paper explicitly excludes muted strums).
- Ukulele Hunt's "Ukulele Strumming Notation" post (2009) returned 404.

## Q6. Rhythm quantisation (onsets -> 8ths/16ths/triplets given tempo and downbeats; swing)

### Takeaway
Snap onsets to a beat-anchored grid (not a fixed-tempo grid), test multiple subdivisions (straight 8/16 vs triplet) per beat or bar and choose by error plus a complexity prior, preserve swing by representing it as a triplet feel rather than as literal 16ths; this is the consensus from Cemgil's Bayesian quantisers through recent beat-based work. Tempo/beat/downbeat come from allin1 or librosa.

### Cited Findings
- Cemgil & Desain frame quantisation as Bayesian inference where "expressive deviations are modelled by a probabilistic performance model from which the corresponding optimal quantizer is derived by Bayes theorem"; it "operates on short groups of onsets" and balances timing fit against notation complexity via priors — [Rhythm Quantization for Transcription (PDF)](https://www.snn.ru.nl/v2/serve.php?doc=cemgil-quantization.pdf); Cemgil & Kappen also did tempo tracking + quantisation by sequential Monte Carlo — [NeurIPS 1999](http://papers.neurips.cc/paper/1999-tempo-tracking-and-rhythm-quantization-by-sequential-monte-carlo.pdf)
- A 2025 beat-based quantiser uses beat annotations as anchors, supports 16th and triplet subdivisions, and preserves swing rather than forcing rigid timing; recommended practice: anchor to detected beats, support multiple subdivisions, keep intentional deviations beyond a tolerance, allow tempo flexibility within phrases, use perceptual snap thresholds — [Beat-Based Rhythm Quantization of MIDI Performances, arXiv 2508.19262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19262)
- A transformer-based rhythm quantiser using beat annotations also exists (2026) — [arXiv 2604.22290](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22290v1)
- librosa: `librosa.beat.beat_track(y, sr)` returns tempo and beat frames; `librosa.onset.onset_detect` gives onset frames; `librosa.beat.plp` gives a locally stable pulse; `librosa.feature.tempogram` gives local onset autocorrelation; beat tracking supports time-varying tempo — [librosa tutorial](https://librosa.org/doc/0.11.0/tutorial.html); [librosa.beat.plp](https://librosa.org/doc/main/generated/librosa.beat.plp.html); [dynamic beat example](http://librosa.org/doc/0.11.0/auto_examples/plot_dynamic_beat.html)
- allin1 returns `bpm`, `beats` (s), `downbeats` (s), `beat_positions` (1,2,3,4,...) and `segments` — [mir-aidj/all-in-one](https://github.com/mir-aidj/all-in-one)
- tabforge detects "tempo, key, time signature" including compound time rendered as 12/8, mid-track meter changes and swing notation, with a beat-grid ensemble and a "120 BPM fallback" — [tabforge](https://github.com/rayBastard/tabforge)
- Swing can be notated as a global triplet feel in Guitar Pro (`MeasureHeader.tripletFeel`, PyGuitarPro 0.11, local introspection) and in alphaTex (`\tf triplet8th`, `\tf triplet16th`, `\tf dotted16th`, `\tf none`) — [alphaTex bar metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_bar-metadata.mdx)

### Inferences
- Simple implementable quantiser: for each onset, find the surrounding beats b_k, b_{k+1} (from allin1), compute fractional position phi in [0,1); candidate grids G = {1/2, 1/4 (16ths), 1/3 (triplets)}; choose per bar the grid minimising total |phi - nearest| with a penalty favouring 8ths over 16ths over triplets; if the best triplet grid wins consistently and off-beat 8ths sit near phi ~ 0.66, set a swing/triplet-feel flag and notate straight 8ths with `\tf triplet8th` rather than literal triplets.
- Downbeats from allin1 give the bar origin, so strum patterns can be expressed as 8- or 16-slot vectors per bar directly.

### Gaps
- No ukulele- or guitar-strum-specific quantisation study; the above is generic MIDI quantisation.
- Exact metrics and baselines of arXiv 2508.19262 were not captured (summary only).

## Q7. Section/structure detection for compact tabs

### Takeaway
allin1 (Kim & Nam, WASPAA 2023) gives tempo, beats, downbeats and functional segments labelled intro/verse/chorus/bridge/solo/etc. in one call; its output maps directly onto tab sections ("[Verse] D A ..."). msaf and librosa segmentation were not investigated in depth.

### Cited Findings
- `allin1.analyze(path)` returns an `AnalysisResult` with `bpm`, `beats`, `downbeats`, `beat_positions`, and `segments` (each with `start`, `end`, `label`); label set: 'start', 'end', 'intro', 'outro', 'break', 'bridge', 'inst', 'solo', 'verse', 'chorus'; depends on PyTorch, NATTEN (must be compiled on Linux/Windows), Demucs (also used for MP3 decoding), ffmpeg optional; `--keep-byproducts`, `--demix-dir`, `--embed`, `--activ` keep demixed stems / embeddings; cite Kim & Nam, "All-In-One Metrical And Functional Structure Analysis With Neighborhood Attentions on Demixed Audio", WASPAA 2023 — [mir-aidj/all-in-one](https://github.com/mir-aidj/all-in-one); `pip install allin1` — [libraries.io](https://libraries.io/pypi/allin1)
- An Apple-Silicon MLX port exists (all-in-one-mlx) — [ssmall256/all-in-one-mlx](https://github.com/ssmall256/all-in-one-mlx)
- alphaTex supports `\section "Intro"` / `\section "S" "Solo"` markers and `\ro ... \rc 2` repeats — [alphaTex bar metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_bar-metadata.mdx); Guitar Pro has `MeasureHeader.marker` (`Marker.title`), `isRepeatOpen`, `repeatClose`, `repeatAlternative` (PyGuitarPro 0.11, local introspection; [API docs](https://pyguitarpro.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pyguitarpro/api.html))

### Inferences
- Pipeline: run allin1 once (its Demucs stems can be reused for the strum detector), snap segment boundaries to the nearest downbeat, then collapse repeated chord/strum content inside identically labelled segments into "Verse (x2)" style blocks, as tab sites do.
- NATTEN compilation on Windows is the main install risk; running allin1 in WSL/Linux or Docker is likely simpler.

### Gaps
- msaf and `librosa.segment` were not researched (no fetch); their suitability vs allin1 is unknown from this session.
- allin1's accuracy numbers were not captured.

## Q8. Tab notation conventions and file formats; what Python can generate

### Takeaway
ASCII tab (4 lines A/E/C/G top-to-bottom), ChordPro with `{define-ukulele ...}` chord diagrams, alphaTex text (rendered by alphaTab in a browser), Guitar Pro .gp5 via PyGuitarPro, LilyPond TabStaff with `#ukulele-tuning`, and MusicXML (hand-written or via music21 with caveats) are all producible from Python; .gpx/.gp (GP6-8) is not writable with PyGuitarPro.

### Cited Findings
- Ukulele tab layout: "four horizontal lines to represent the four strings of the ukulele, with the first string on the top and the fourth on the bottom, and numbers refer to the frets"; default is re-entrant G C E A, alternate tunings are stated before the notation — [Ukulele Magazine notation guide (search summary)](https://ukulelemagazine.com/uke-basics/notation); [Live Ukulele, How to read tab](https://liveukulele.com/tabs/how-to-read-tab/)
- ChordPro: `{define: name base-fret offset frets pos ... fingers pos ...}`; frets use 0 = open, x/N/-1 = muted; "there must be exactly as many positions as there are strings, which is 6 by default" (so ukulele needs the ukulele instrument config or `define-ukulele`); `{chord: ...}` has identical syntax but prints the diagram inline; `copy`, `copyall`, `diagram off`, `display`, `format` options; keyboard form `{define: D keys 0 4 7}` — [ChordPro define directive](https://www.chordpro.org/chordpro/directives-define/); ukulele example `{define-ukulele Dm base-fret 1 frets 2 2 1 0}` and instrument configuration — [ChordPro directives (search result)](https://chordpro.org/chordpro/directives-define/), [Defining an instrument](https://www.chordpro.org/chordpro/chordpro-configuration-instrument/)
- alphaTex (alphaTab, MPL-2.0): note syntax `fret.string.duration`, chords `(1.1 3.2 2.3 0.4)`, rest `r`, dead note `x.3` or `3.3{x}`, tie `-.3` or `{t}`, duration `:8 3.3`, palm mute `{pm}`, hammer `{h}`, slides `{sl} {ss} {sib} {sia} {sou} {sod}` — [alphaTex note properties](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_note-properties.mdx); beat effects `{bu}`/`{bd}` (brush, optional duration e.g. `{bd 360}`), `{su}`/`{sd}` pick strokes, `{ch "C"}` chord name ("If the staff has a definition for this chord it will be shown in the chord diagram list"), `{tu 3}`, `{dy ppp..fff}`, `{txt "..."}`, `().4 {ds}` dead-slap beat, `{tempo 120}` — [alphaTex beat properties](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_beat-properties.mdx); staff-level `\tuning (A1 D2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4)` (list of pitched note names, so a 4-note list defines a 4-string staff), `\capo 5`, `\chord ("D#" 6 8 8 8 6 x) {firstfret 6}` for chord diagrams, `\lyrics "..."` — [alphaTex staff metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_staff-metadata.mdx); bar-level `\ts 4 4`, `\ks Dmajor`, `\tempo 120`, `\ro`/`\rc 2`, `\section "Intro"`, `\tf triplet8th` — [alphaTex bar metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_bar-metadata.mdx); structure `\track "Name" "abbr"`, `\staff{tabs}` / `\staff{score}` / `slash` / `numbered`, `\voice`, `\instrument "Acoustic Steel Guitar"` — [alphaTex structural metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_structural-metadata.mdx); `\title ("Song Title" "Title: %TITLE%" left)`, `\artist "..."` — [alphaTex score metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_score-metadata.mdx); alphaTab loads "Guitar Pro 3-8, MusicXML, Capella or ... alphaTex" — [alphaTab](https://www.alphatab.net/)
- PyGuitarPro: reads/writes "GP3, GP4 and GP5 files" (`guitarpro.parse`, `guitarpro.write`); GPX not supported; originated as a port of alphaTab/TuxGuitar — [PyGuitarPro README](https://github.com/Perlence/PyGuitarPro); object model verified by local introspection of PyGuitarPro 0.11: `Song(title, artist, tempo, key, measureHeaders, tracks, ...)`, `Track(name, fretCount, strings=[GuitarString(number, value)], measures, channel, isPercussionTrack, ...)`, `MeasureHeader(number, start, keySignature, timeSignature(numerator, denominator), marker(title), isRepeatOpen, repeatClose, repeatAlternative, tripletFeel, ...)`, `Beat(voice, notes, duration(value, isDotted, tuplet(enters, times)), text, effect, status in {empty, normal, rest})`, `BeatEffect(stroke=BeatStroke(direction in {none, up, down}, value), pickStroke, chord, hasRasgueado, ...)`, `Chord(name, firstFret, strings, barres, fingerings, root, type in {major, minor, seventh, ..., power}, ...)`, `Note(value, string, velocity, type in {rest, normal, tie, dead}, effect=NoteEffect(palmMute, hammer, staccato, letRing, accentuatedNote, heavyAccentuatedNote, slides, bend, leftHandFinger, ...))`; `Fingering` enum {open, thumb, index, middle, annular, little} — [PyGuitarPro API docs](https://pyguitarpro.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pyguitarpro/api.html)
- music21: `tablature.FretBoard(numStrings, fretNotes, displayFrets)`, `FretNote(string, fret, fingering)`, `ChordWithFretBoard`, `UkeleleFretBoard` (G C E A) — [music21 tablature](https://music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleTablature.html); the MusicXML exporter has `chordWithFretBoardToXml` / `fretBoardToXml` for chord diagrams — [music21.musicxml.m21ToXml](https://music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleMusicxmlM21ToXml.html); but issue #778 reports the MusicXML writer "produces invalid tablature files" (spurious `<fret>0</fret>` tags replacing techniques) and the issue appeared still open with PRs #780/#1169 referenced — [music21 issue 778](https://github.com/cuthbertLab/music21/issues/778); PR 1169 "Handling Staff-tuning in MusicXML" exists — [PR 1169](https://github.com/cuthbertLab/music21/pull/1169)
- MusicXML tablature "requires rhythmic information to be specified along with fret and string information"; staff details use `staff-lines` and `staff-tuning`/`capo` — [MusicXML 3.0 tutorial (search summary)](https://www.musicxml.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/musicxml-tutorial.pdf)
- LilyPond: `\set Staff.stringTunings = #ukulele-tuning` in a TabStaff; `\include "predefined-ukulele-fretboards.ly"` for diagrams; TabStaff line count and automatic FretBoard string count equal the number of elements in stringTunings; custom tunings via `\stringTuning <chord in absolute mode, highest-numbered string first>`; string numbers as `\2`; `ChordNames` + `FretBoards` contexts with `\chordmode` show names and diagrams together — [LilyPond 2.25 fretted strings](https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.25/Documentation/notation/common-notation-for-fretted-strings)
- tabforge demonstrates the Python export trio in practice: ".gp5 via PyGuitarPro with verified round-trip validation", MusicXML via music21 "compatible with MuseScore", MIDI and ASCII — [tabforge](https://github.com/rayBastard/tabforge)
- Burlet & Hindle used MuseScore's CLI to go MIDI -> MusicXML and then injected string/fret into the MusicXML — [Burlet & Hindle 2017](https://softwareprocess.es/pubs/burlet2017PeerJ.pdf)

### Inferences
- Recommended Python outputs for a uke pipeline, in order of effort: (1) ASCII tab + chord-over-lyrics text (strings A E C G top to bottom, bar lines, a "Strum:" line per section); (2) ChordPro with `{define-ukulele}` diagrams and `{comment: Strum D DU UDU}`; (3) alphaTex string with `\tuning (G4 C4 E4 A4)` (ordering convention of the tuning list vs alphaTab string numbering must be checked in the playground), `\chord` diagrams, `{bd}/{bu}` brushes and `{ds}` chucks, rendered by alphaTab in the browser; (4) .gp5 via PyGuitarPro with `Track.strings = [GuitarString(1,69), (2,64), (3,60), (4,67)]` (string 1 = highest pitch, as in Hori's and Guitar Pro's convention), stroke directions and `Chord` diagrams; (5) LilyPond text for PDF; (6) MusicXML written by hand (string/fret in `<technical>`, `<staff-details><staff-lines>4` + `<staff-tuning>`) rather than via music21's exporter until issue 778 is confirmed fixed.
- Chord diagrams for ukulele are conventionally 4 vertical strings G C E A left to right, 4-5 frets tall, dots with finger numbers, "x" above muted strings and a base-fret number for shapes above position 1 (chords-db `baseFret`, ChordPro `base-fret`, alphaTex `{firstfret}` all encode this).

### Gaps
- Whether alphaTex's `\tuning` list order is low-to-high or string-1-first for a 4-string staff was not confirmed from the docs excerpt (the 7-string example is listed low-to-high: A1 D2 ... E4).
- Current status of music21 tab-staff MusicXML export (post-PR 1169) is unverified.
- Guitar Pro 6-8 (.gpx/.gp) writing from Python: no library found in this session (PyGuitarPro stops at GP5).

### Inferences
- Consistency check: Am-shape chart with capo 4 sounds C#m-F#-A / E-A-B, matching the original-key chart (C#5 -> C#m, F#5 -> F#, B5 -> B, E-A-B chorus). So for a uke tab that matches the recording: play C-F-G / Am-D-F shapes with capo 4, or transpose shapes to E-A-B / C#m-F#-A (E 4442, A 2100, B 4322, C#m 1444, F# 3121) without capo.
- The 176/88 BPM ambiguity is a half-time/double-time octave error typical of tempo estimators; the strum grid should be built on the 88 BPM (half-time, backbeat on 2 and 4) interpretation, where the famous riff falls in 8th notes; any pipeline should expose the beat-octave choice as a parameter.
- For Summer of '69 use 139 BPM, 4/4, straight 8ths; the UG ukulele slash chords (D/A, G/D, Bb/D, C/E) are exactly the kind the simplification rules say to drop, so a generated tab should print D, G, Bb, C.
- Verse 1 of Summer of '69 on the record is a palm-muted driving 8th-note D-A figure and the chorus is open-chord strumming (inference from the published "D D DU D U" pattern and the chart structure; the record itself was not analysed here).

### Gaps
- No ukulele-specific published strum pattern for PSSOM was found; the e-chords/ukulele-tabs uke pages could not be fetched (403/geo-block).
- Key databases disagree for PSSOM (C# minor vs E major vs others); the relative pair C#m/E is consistent with the charts, but no authoritative key statement (e.g. from the published sheet music) was retrieved.
- Summer of '69's exact studio tempo is reported as 139 vs 136 BPM by different aggregators; no primary source.
- The intro riff of PSSOM (single-note/power-chord figure) was not retrieved in tab form; only chord labels.
