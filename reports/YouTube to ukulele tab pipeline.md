# Transcribe Generically, Arrange for Ukulele, Decode Strums

No single Python library turns a YouTube rock video into ukulele tablature, and the research converges on one design: a chain of generic, off-the-shelf audio models feeding a ukulele-specific symbolic layer that does all the fingering, voicing, simplification and strum-pattern work. For chords, the 2019 music-x-lab Chord-CNN-LSTM (MIT, PyTorch, git-runnable) scored **Root 78.61 / MajMin 76.39 on Billboard 2013 at MIREX 2025**, within three points of the best 2025 submission and seven to ten points above Chordino, and ChordMini (2026) ships an improved BTC student on the same stack ([MIREX 2025 ACE Results](https://music-ir.org/mirex/wiki/2025:Audio_Chord_Estimation_Results)); madmom's PyPI release is broken on Python 3.10+, so the beat grid should come from Beat This! instead. For notes, Transkun v2 (piano), YourMT3+ (**91.65% onset F1 on GuitarSet**) and Basic Pitch (**80.1%**, but a single pip install on Windows CPU) are the realistic choices, while end-to-end audio-to-tablature research still tops out at **54% tab F1** and collapses on distorted electric guitar, which argues for a two-stage "notes first, fingering second" design in which only the symbolic stage knows about four re-entrant strings ([TART](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11904); [arXiv 2405.14679](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.14679)). Separation should run through python-audio-separator with BS-RoFormer-class 6-stem weights (guitar SDR about 9.0 versus 7.0 for 2024 models), but chord recognition is better fed a pitched-instrument-boosted re-mix than a lone artifact-laden stem, although the measured re-mix gain is only +0.20 WCSR points ([APSIPA 2025 PDF](https://webdev.apsipa.org/proceedings/2025/papers/APSIPA2025_P307.pdf)). The decisive finding is that **no shipping product outputs a detected strumming pattern** (Chordify calls it "near-impossible"), yet two 2025 papers show strum onsets on a separated guitar stem are detectable at **93-97% F1** and can be Viterbi-decoded against a small expert pattern vocabulary; that capability, plus riff-versus-strum section classification and ukulele-aware arrangement, is where a new project should concentrate. alphaTab is the only open renderer that draws tab, in-score chord diagrams and a playback cursor synced to a YouTube iframe, so alphaTex is the natural output format.

## A 2019 PyTorch chord model still matches the 2025 state of the art

There is no "pip install and go" state-of-the-art chord recogniser in 2026, and the options sort into three tiers whose accuracy and installability pull in opposite directions. The DSP tier (Chordino via chord-extractor, librosa chroma templates, Essentia) is easiest to understand but weakest; the 2016-2019 deep-model tier (madmom, crema, BTC, Chord-CNN-LSTM, Omnizart) offers the best accuracy for the effort but is mostly unmaintained; the 2025-2026 research tier (ChordFormer, consonance-trained conformers, Mamba, ChordMini) is largely unpackaged, and some of it has no code at all. Papers With Code, the traditional leaderboard, was shut down in July 2025, so the **MIREX 2025 Audio Chord Estimation results are the best current cross-system benchmark** ([Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/articles/papers-with-code); [MIREX 2025](https://music-ir.org/mirex/wiki/2025:Audio_Chord_Estimation_Results)).

That benchmark is the key evidence. The music-x-lab ISMIR 2019 Chord-CNN-LSTM, run as a baseline, posted **Billboard 2013 Root 78.61 / MajMin 76.39 / Sevenths 64.15** and **Yamaha Balanced 82.00 / 81.16 / 66.97**, the best Sevenths score on that set; the strongest 2025 entry (MD1) reached only 81.35 / 79.15 / 66.40 on Billboard, while the Chordino baseline trailed at **71.06 / 67.18 / 48.88** ([MIREX 2025](https://music-ir.org/mirex/wiki/2025:Audio_Chord_Estimation_Results)). The model is MIT-licensed, ships pretrained weights, runs with `python3 chord_recognition.py audio out [chord_dict]`, writes .lab files and supports MIREX or full MARL vocabularies ([GitHub](https://github.com/music-x-lab/ISMIR2019-Large-Vocabulary-Chord-Recognition); [ISMIR 2019 paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2019/paper/000078.pdf)). ChordMini (Phan et al., 2026) takes a pretrained BTC transformer as teacher, pseudo-labels more than 1,000 hours of audio, and distils a student that **beats the supervised baseline by 2.5% and its own teacher by 1.55%** averaged across metrics; these are relative gains only, since the DAFx26-accepted abstract reports students reaching about 97-99% of the teacher before fine-tuning and then surpassing it, and publishes no absolute WCSR; the MIT repo is active (216 commits, last push June 2026) and includes the checkpoints ([arXiv 2602.19778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19778); [ChordMini GitHub](https://github.com/ptnghia-j/ChordMini); [GitHub search API](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=ChordMini+user:ptnghia-j)). Its companion ChordMiniApp integrates Chord-CNN-LSTM, BTC and ChordMini for chords with Beat-Transformer and madmom for beats, and is the closest existing reference integration of this exact pipeline ([ChordMiniApp](https://github.com/ptnghia-j/ChordMiniApp)). ChordFormer reports the best published numbers (MIREX 83.62 versus 81.52 for CNN+BLSTM on the 1,217-song Isophonics/Billboard/MARL collection) but the paper carries no code link, so it is not usable without trusting a third-party reimplementation ([arXiv 2502.11840](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11840)).

The practical friction is as decisive as the accuracy. madmom's last PyPI release (0.16.1) dates from 2018-11-14; it imports `MutableSequence` from `collections` (removed in Python 3.10) and uses `np.float` (removed in NumPy), so the only working path is `pip install git+https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom` with a Cython build, and the maintainer "doesn't use Windows" ([madmom issue #502](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/issues/502); [madmom discussion #536](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/discussions/536)); the main branch has declared Python 3.10-3.12 and NumPy 2 since August 2024, but that build fails under Cython 3.1 or newer (`numpy/math.pxd` not found), the fix has sat unmerged since May 2025 and no release is planned, so pin `cython<3.1` ([madmom PR 540](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/pull/540); [madmom issue 547](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/issues/547); [madmom PR 548](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/pull/548)). Its model files are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, which blocks commercial use without permission ([PyPI madmom](https://pypi.org/project/madmom/)). Chordino needs the Vamp Plugin Pack installed per machine with `VAMP_PATH` set, and chord-extractor bundles Chordino only for Linux ([chord-extractor GitHub](https://github.com/ohollo/chord-extractor)). The `vamp` host package on PyPI has shipped no wheel since its 2015 cp27 build and has no conda-forge package, so on Python 3.11 or 3.12 it compiles from the sdist against the Vamp SDK ([PyPI vamp](https://pypi.org/pypi/vamp/json)). Essentia has no Python bindings on Windows at all ([Essentia installing](https://essentia.upf.edu/installing.html)). Pure-PyTorch models have none of these problems and run a four-minute song on CPU, which is why the ISMIR 2019 model and ChordMini are the right primary engines for a Windows pipeline.

For the beat grid, **Beat This!** (CPJKU, ISMIR 2024) is the cleanest option: `pip install beat-this`, MIT, PyTorch 2.0+, automatic GPU with CPU fallback, outputs beats and downbeats, with madmom's DBN post-processing optional rather than required; version 1.1.0 shipped on 2026-04-14 ([beat_this GitHub](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this); [PyPI beat-this](https://pypi.org/project/beat-this/)). BeatNet inherits madmom and PyAudio as hard dependencies and needs manual wheels on Windows ([BeatNet GitHub](https://github.com/mjhydri/BeatNet)). Key detection is available on Windows only through madmom's CNN key model via the git install ([madmom key.py](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/blob/main/madmom/features/key.py)); for chord simplification, `mir_eval.chord` and `madmom.evaluation.chords` provide the standard triad/tetrad reductions that collapse 7ths, extensions and inversions to playable triads ([mir_eval chord docs](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/latest/api/chord.html)). Both target songs are built from power chords and major triads, so a maj/min vocabulary is sufficient; the real risk is that a power chord has no third, which the pipeline should resolve from the detected key's diatonic quality rather than from the model's guess.

Nothing in the literature benchmarks chord recognition on distorted-guitar rock specifically. Chroma features are "considerably robust to changes in timbre" ([Jiang et al. 2011](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.de/content/05_fau/professor/00_mueller/03_publications/2011_JiangGroscheKonzMueller_ChordRecognitionEvaluation_AES42-Ilmenau.pdf)), but chroma-CQT "does comparatively poorly at identifying thirds and sevenths" and classifiers "perform poorly on rare chords" ([arXiv 2512.22621](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22621v1)). The deep models were trained on Billboard and Isophonics, which include 1980s rock production, whereas Chordino was never trained at all and suffers most from distortion.

## Note transcription tops 95% on piano but 54% end-to-end on tab

Monophonic pitch tracking has quietly been overtaken by small 2025 models. On the only large cross-corpus benchmark found (19 trackers, 10 corpora, noisy and reverberant variants), **SwiftF0 leads at 0.781 pitch F1 while running 179.6x real time on one CPU core**, followed by RMVPE (0.768), FCPE (0.728), CREPE (0.689 at 0.4x real time) and PESTO (0.680); the author notes SwiftF0's lead over RMVPE "is not statistically resolved", and SwiftF0 and the benchmark share an author ([lars76/pitch-benchmark](https://github.com/lars76/pitch-benchmark)). SwiftF0 depends only on numpy and onnxruntime, weighs 135 KB and covers G1-C7 ([swift-f0 PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/swift-f0/0.2.0/)); PESTO is LGPL, 130k parameters and about 12x real time on CPU ([SonyCSLParis/pesto](https://github.com/SonyCSLParis/pesto)). No tracker has been benchmarked on distorted electric guitar, and all of them fail on double-stops and power chords, so monophonic trackers belong only on genuinely single-line riffs.

For polyphonic note transcription the picture splits by instrument. On piano, Transkun v2 (MIT, `pip install transkun`, 2.0.1 released 2024-09-28) reports MAESTRO v3 note-onset F1 of **0.983** (0.935 with offsets, 0.930 with velocity) with velocity and pedal ([transkun PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/transkun/); [Transkun GitHub](https://github.com/yujia-yan/transkun)), and the Bytedance/Kong model remains a proven fallback at 96.72% onset F1, although its training repo was archived on 2025-12-08 ([arXiv 2010.01815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01815); [bytedance/piano_transcription](https://github.com/bytedance/piano_transcription)). For guitar, ukulele, vocals and mixed stems, **YourMT3+ is the strongest open multi-instrument model: 96.98% onset F1 on MAESTRO, 91.65% on GuitarSet, and 71.6% on MIR-ST500 vocals without any separation pre-processor**, but it is research code with no pip package and a GPU requirement, and it scores below 10% on non-main instruments in commercial pop ([YourMT3+ arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04822v1)); its GitHub repository is a GPL-3.0 placeholder holding only a licence and README, while the code and checkpoints live in an Apache-2.0 Hugging Face Space (Python 3.9+, PyTorch 2.2+, `transformers==4.45.1`) whose author calls the weights "Apache 2.0", with no inference VRAM figure and no Windows notes anywhere ([YourMT3 issue 2](https://github.com/mimbres/YourMT3/issues/2#issuecomment-2255643217); [YourMT3 issue 12](https://github.com/mimbres/YourMT3/issues/12); [HF Space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/mimbres/YourMT3)). Spotify's Basic Pitch is the opposite trade: Apache-2.0, one pip install, CPU, explicit Windows support, pitch-bend output, but only **80.1% note F1 on GuitarSet and 70.9% on MAESTRO**, and no release since 0.4.0 in August 2024 ([ar5iv 2203.09893](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.09893); [basic-pitch PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/basic-pitch/)). Harmonica (September 2026) claims a nano variant 14.6 points above Basic Pitch on frame F1 at 1,622x real time, but no public repository was found ([arXiv 2609.04640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04640)). Onsets and Frames and MT3 are legacy TF1/JAX stacks; the Onsets and Frames README describes the repository as inactive ([magenta O&F README](https://github.com/magenta/magenta/blob/main/magenta/models/onsets_frames_transcription/README.md)).

Direct audio-to-tablature models are not ready for production, and their failure pattern is instructive. TabCNN and FretNet reach only **0.717-0.727 tablature F1 on clean acoustic GuitarSet** ([FretNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.03023)), and when TabCNN is evaluated on EGSet12, twelve real electric-guitar performances, tab F1 **drops from 0.748 to 0.447**, recovering only to 0.557 after training on effects-augmented data ([ar5iv 2405.14679](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.14679)). The 2026 preprints improve on clean data: Noise2Fret's diffusion model reaches 0.795 tab F1 on the GOAT electric-DI dataset versus 0.656 for TabCNN, but explicitly restricts its experiments to recordings "without effects processing" ([Noise2Fret](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30854v1)). TART, the most complete modular system, gets **81.35% audio-to-MIDI F1, 71.8% string/fret F1 given oracle MIDI, and only 54.08% end-to-end tab F1** ([TART](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11904)). That gap between 81% notes and 54% tab means string/fret assignment, not pitch detection, is the dominant error; and since every dataset (GuitarSet, EGDB, GAPS, GOAT, SynthTab) is six-string guitar and no ukulele audio-tab dataset exists, the sensible architecture is to let a generic note model produce MIDI and solve fingering symbolically for four strings. Symbolic MIDI-to-tab is a well-developed problem: the 2024 MIDI-to-Tab BART model achieves 94.35% next-note accuracy with ground-truth context and was rated 6.04/10 by guitarists against 4.69 for Guitar Pro ([arXiv 2408.05024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.05024)), and Fretting-Transformer conditions on tuning and capo ([arXiv 2506.14223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14223)).

Realistic expectations on separated stems are sobering. The only quantified separation-versus-no-separation result is YourMT3+'s **71.60% versus 71.07% vocal F1 with and without separation**, a marginal gain ([YourMT3+](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04822v1)). Combining that with the EGSet12 collapse, a reasonable planning figure is 60-75% note F1 on clean guitar and vocal stems and perhaps 45-60% on distorted lead guitar pulled from a mix, versus 90%+ on solo studio recordings. The pipeline should therefore treat transcribed riffs as drafts that need a correction interface, and should A/B Basic Pitch against YourMT3+ on the two target songs before committing.

## RoFormer stems lead by 2 dB, but chords want a re-mix, not a stem

Getting the audio is no longer trivial. yt-dlp (Unlicense, roughly 195k stars, actively developed) requires Python 3.10+, ffmpeg for extraction, and since 2025-2026 an external JavaScript runtime plus the `yt-dlp-ejs` package to solve YouTube's challenges; the documented install is `pip install -U "yt-dlp[default]"` with Deno (minimum 2.3.0) on PATH ([yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp); [yt-dlp EJS wiki](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/EJS)). Some clients additionally require a Proof-of-Origin token from a provider plugin such as `bgutil-ytdlp-pot-provider`, and cookies and PO tokens solve different problems ([PO Token Guide](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/PO-Token-Guide)). YouTube's own audio is lossy (format 140 AAC about 130 kbps, format 251 Opus about 110 kbps), so `-f bestaudio -x --audio-format wav` merely avoids a second lossy generation ([Spacebar](https://www.spacebar.news/how-to-download-audio-only-media-files-with-yt-dlp/)); `--write-info-json` supplies title, artist, album and channel metadata ([yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp)). pytubefix is a maintained pure-Python fallback with `get_audio_only()` and OAuth, but documents no PO-token handling ([pytubefix](https://github.com/JuanBindez/pytubefix)). The legal position is unambiguous at the ToS level: YouTube's terms (effective 2025-03-17) prohibit downloading content except as permitted by the Service, with written permission, or "as permitted by applicable law", so a personal transcription tool breaches the ToS even where local private-copy law might apply ([YouTube ToS](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms)).

On separation, the open-weights frontier has moved from Demucs to the RoFormer family. Demucs v4 remains MIT, needs 3-7 GB of VRAM, runs at roughly 1.5x track duration on CPU and 9.0 dB average SDR on MUSDB18-HQ, but the Meta repository was archived on 2025-01-01 and its 6-stem variant has "okay quality for guitar, but a lot of bleeding and artifacts for the piano source" ([facebookresearch/demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)); development continues at adefossez/demucs, whose 4.1.0 release (PyPI, 11 July 2026; Python 3.10+, torch 2.1+) drops torchaudio from inference in favour of `sphn`, hosts the pretrained models on the Hugging Face hub and so removes the old Windows torchaudio pin ([PyPI demucs 4.1.0](https://pypi.org/pypi/demucs/4.1.0/json); [Demucs release notes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adefossez/demucs/main/docs/release.md)). BS-RoFormer won the SDX23 music-separation track and a small version reaches 9.80 dB without extra data ([arXiv 2309.02612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02612)). On MVSEP's guitar leaderboard the **"BS Roformer SW" 6-stem checkpoint tops the table at guitar SDR 9.012**, essentially tied with Apple's Logic Pro 11.2 stem splitter at 9.002, while the 2024 MelBand RoFormer guitar model sits at 7.021 ([MVSEP guitar leaderboard](https://mvsep.com/quality_checker/leaderboard/guitar)); MVSEP reports the same model at piano 7.83 and bass 14.62 on its own set ([MVSEP news](https://mvsep.com/en/news)). The catch is provenance: "SW" stands for shared weights, the checkpoint was trained collectively by unnamed contributors rather than by MVSEP or any uploader, its original Hugging Face host (jarredou) has been deleted, and the re-hosts disagree about its licence, lumabeat's card saying unknown while the Blakus and elicwhite re-uploads self-assert an MIT licence the trainers never granted ([MSST issue 248](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/issues/248); [lumabeat card](https://huggingface.co/lumabeat/bs-roformer-sw); [HF elicwhite](https://huggingface.co/elicwhite/bs-roformer-sw-6stem-onnx)). The practical runtime is `python-audio-separator` (MIT; `pip install "audio-separator[gpu]"`, PyTorch 2.3+, CUDA 11.8/12.2), which loads MDX, VR, Demucs and RoFormer checkpoints through one API and defaults to a BS-RoFormer vocal model at 12.9 dB ([python-audio-separator](https://github.com/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator)). Lead-versus-rhythm guitar separation exists only as hosted services: Moises launched it in April 2024, Music.ai charges $0.10/min for "Guitar parts (Rhythm, Solo)", and MVSEP added a lead/rhythm model in March 2026, none with open weights ([Moises blog](https://moises.ai/blog/moises-news/new-guitar-separation-models/); [Music.ai pricing](https://music.ai/pricing/); [MVSEP news](https://mvsep.com/en/news)).

Whether separation helps chord recognition is the question the notes answer most carefully, and the answer is "only if you re-mix". A UW-Madison study that fed Demucs "other" plus bass stems into a CNN-BiLSTM chord model found that "on average across all metrics, the Demucs model performed worse" than the full-mix model, blaming artifacts; it trained the model on stems, so it measures a distribution shift rather than stems fed to a mix-trained model, and reports plots only ([Ko, chord-transcription](https://ko28.github.io/chord-transcription/)). An APSIPA 2025 paper instead used HTDemucs stems to re-mix the song with pitched instruments boosted before feeding a BTC recogniser, and "successfully corrected more than 2,000 misrecognized chord frames", which on inspection is a net gain of **+0.20 WCSR points on triads (75.52 to 75.72) over 485 songs**, 13,675 frames fixed against 11,390 broken, significant by sign test but small, and doubling the vocals or the bass stem alone made things worse ([APSIPA 2025](http://www.apsipa.org/proceedings/2025/papers/APSIPA2025_P307.pdf); [APSIPA 2025 PDF](https://webdev.apsipa.org/proceedings/2025/papers/APSIPA2025_P307.pdf)). Harmonic/percussive separation is likewise reported to improve chord accuracy in complex recordings ([IJACSA 2025](https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume16No4/Paper_75-Intelligent_Guitar_Chord_Recognition.pdf)), and a 2025 pipeline that routes separation, key, chord and beat outputs through GPT-4o chain-of-thought gains 1-2.77% on the MIREX metric ([arXiv 2509.18700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18700)). For transcription, guitar-string separation "improves transcription performance in comparison to the original soundtrack, though performance is still sub-optimal due to artifacts" ([IEEE TASLP](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11179987/)). The design that follows: run the chord recogniser on both the original mix and a re-mix (guitar, piano, other and bass at full level, vocals and drums attenuated) and reconcile; run note transcription on individual bass and guitar stems, where SDR is high enough for pitch tracking to be reliable; expect the piano and "other" stems (6-8 dB) to be the least trustworthy.

## Tablature is a shortest-path problem, and four strings shrink the graph

Every serious fingering algorithm since 1989 is a shortest-path search over a layered graph. Sayegh's "optimum path paradigm" cast fingering as a global shortest-path problem over the whole piece; Radicioni, Anselma and Lombardo build a DAG with a node per candidate (string, fret, finger) triple, edge weights that "estimate the cost of the transition" from hand morphology and instrument geometry, and show the shortest path is found in O(n) in the number of notes, segmenting phrases so each is fingered separately and chained ([Radicioni et al. 2004](http://pgp.di.unito.it/~anselma/pdf/AISC04.pdf)). Burlet and Hindle's arrangement step runs Dijkstra over candidate string/fret combinations with three aggregated biomechanical factors (fret-wise travel distance, finger span within chords, a penalty when the hand passes the seventh fret) and candidly report that penalty values "were determined through subjective analysis of the resulting tablature arrangements" ([Burlet & Hindle 2017](https://softwareprocess.es/pubs/burlet2017PeerJ.pdf)). Hori and Sagayama formalise the same thing as HMM decoding over 462 left-hand forms, with a Laplace-distributed cost on index-finger position scaled by inter-onset interval, a 1/(1+|string change|) term and a finger prior of 0.35/0.30/0.25/0.10 for index through pinky; their minimax Viterbi variant maximises the easiest worst-case move, which suits beginners better than minimising total difficulty ([Hori & Sagayama 2016](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2016/paper/000285.pdf)). tabforge implements a Viterbi over "fingering + hand position" with `high_fret_penalty`, `open_string_bonus`, `move_penalty` and `stretch_penalty`, already lists a ukulele tuning, and exports GP5, MusicXML, MIDI and ASCII under MIT ([tabforge](https://github.com/rayBastard/tabforge)).

The ukulele version of this graph is smaller and has one twist. Standard gCEA tuning is G4 C4 E4 A4, MIDI 67/60/64/69 ([chords-db ukulele.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tombatossals/chords-db/master/lib/ukulele.json)), sopranos carry 12-15 frets ([Ukulele Tricks](https://ukuleletricks.com/ukulele-sizes-soprano-concert-tenor-baritone/)), so the playable range at twelve frets is C4 to A5 and each pitch has at most three or four candidates, meaning exhaustive chord-candidate enumeration is cheap. The re-entrant G string duplicates pitches available on the C, E and A strings an octave above a low-G, so "everything you play on a high-g string is an octave above a low-G string" and notes that must sound low have to be relocated ([Live Ukulele, Converting](https://liveukulele.com/tabs/converting/)). The solver must therefore be parameterised by tuning (gCEA, low-G, baritone DGBE) rather than hard-coded, and must not assume monotonic string pitch; LilyPond's automatic string assignment makes exactly that assumption and fails on re-entrant ukuleles ([lilypond-user](https://lists.gnu.org/archive/html/lilypond-user/2015-08/msg00586.html)).

For strummed sections the problem collapses to chord-shape selection. chords-db's ukulele.json provides roughly 45 suffixes per root with frets, fingers, baseFret, barres and MIDI per position ([chords-db](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tombatossals/chords-db/master/lib/ukulele.json)); ukechords generates shapes algorithmically for arbitrary tunings and ranks them by heuristic difficulty, using pychord for naming ([ukechords](https://github.com/nickurak/ukechords)). A 2024 DadaGP study found that human guitarists choose voicings by minimal movement from the previous shape, preference for open voicings and avoidance of barres unless necessary ([arXiv 2407.14260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.14260)), which maps directly onto a chord-level Viterbi whose transition cost is per-string fret movement plus barre and baseFret penalties. Simplification follows a stable set of folk rules: strip extensions (G9 to G7), undo substitutions (maj7 to major, m7 to minor), ignore "add" notes and slash-chord basses, and treat power chords as either major or minor since "the absence of the 3rd ... means a power chord can be used for either" ([Live Ukulele, simplifying](https://liveukulele.com/songs/how-to-make-songs-easier/); [UkuTabs](https://ukutabs.com/ukulele-guides/simplify-difficult-ukulele-chords-5-quick-tips/)). Transposition is interval arithmetic: the ukulele "is tuned the same as a guitar capod on the 5th fret", so guitar frets above 5 convert by subtracting 5 and baritone DGBE equals the guitar's top four strings ([Live Ukulele, Converting](https://liveukulele.com/tabs/converting/); [Ukulele Tricks, baritone](https://ukuleletricks.com/using-your-baritone-ukulele-to-follow-along-with-my-standard-tuned-lessons/)). The two target songs illustrate the key-choice decision the pipeline must make automatically. Summer of '69 stays in D on ukulele with D, A, Bm, G plus F, Bb, C in the bridge, Bm being a barre at fret 2 ([Lauren Bateman](https://www.laurenbateman.com/summer-of-69-chord-chart/); [ukulelearn](https://ukulelearn.com/summer-of-69-ukulele-chords/)). Pour Some Sugar On Me is in E on the record, with C#5, F#5 and B5 power chords and an E-A-B chorus ([UG 863681](https://r.jina.ai/https://tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/tab/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me-chords-863681)), but the popular acoustic chart plays Am-D-F verses and C-F-G choruses with a capo, and a commenter recommends capo 4 "for better alignment with the original" ([UG 2281783](https://r.jina.ai/https://tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/tab/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me-chords-2281783)); the arithmetic checks out, since Am shapes at capo 4 sound C#m, which is the diatonic reading of C#5 in E. A key-choice heuristic that scores each transposition by the count of open, barre-free shapes and emits "capo n" reproduces this human decision.

Rhythm has to be quantised against detected beats, not a fixed tempo. Cemgil and Desain framed quantisation as Bayesian inference balancing timing fit against notation complexity ([Cemgil & Desain](https://www.snn.ru.nl/v2/serve.php?doc=cemgil-quantization.pdf)), and a 2025 beat-based quantiser anchors onsets to beat annotations, tests 16th and triplet subdivisions, and preserves swing rather than forcing rigid timing ([arXiv 2508.19262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19262)). Swing should be represented as a triplet feel, which Guitar Pro stores as `MeasureHeader.tripletFeel` and alphaTex as `\tf triplet8th` ([alphaTex bar metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_bar-metadata.mdx)). allin1 returns bpm, beats, downbeats, beat positions and functional segments labelled intro/verse/chorus/bridge/solo in one call, which maps directly onto tab sections, but depends on NATTEN, which must be compiled on Windows, although a pure-PyTorch replacement is pending as PR #39 ([all-in-one](https://github.com/mir-aidj/all-in-one); [all-in-one PR 39](https://github.com/mir-aidj/all-in-one/pull/39)). Tempo-octave ambiguity is real for the target material: Pour Some Sugar On Me is listed at 176 BPM "with a half-time option at 88 BPM" on SongBPM, while Tunebat gives the album cut 85 BPM in C# minor and only the extended mix 170, and Hooktheory and BPMDatabase sit at 85, so the 176 is a doubled count and the beat-octave must be an exposed parameter ([songbpm](https://songbpm.com/@def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me); [Tunebat search](https://tunebat.com/Search?q=pour%20some%20sugar%20on%20me%20def%20leppard); [TheoryTab PSSOM](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me)).

Output formats are all reachable from Python. alphaTex encodes a 4-string staff with `\tuning (G4 C4 E4 A4)`, chord diagrams via `\chord`, brushes `{bu}`/`{bd}`, dead-slap chucks `{ds}`, sections, repeats and capo ([alphaTex staff metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_staff-metadata.mdx); [alphaTex beat properties](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_beat-properties.mdx)). PyGuitarPro 0.11 writes GP3-GP5 with `BeatStroke.direction`, dead notes, accents and `Chord` diagrams, but not GP6-8, and the maintainer declined a working GP6-8 reader in 2026 ("I don't plan to introduce support for Guitar Pro 6 and later"), leaving the kaizenman `gpif-support` fork and alphaTab as the GP6-8 readers ([PyGuitarPro](https://github.com/Perlence/PyGuitarPro); [PyPI pyguitarpro](https://pypi.org/project/pyguitarpro/); [PyGuitarPro PR 58](https://github.com/Perlence/PyGuitarPro/pull/58)). ChordPro supports `{define-ukulele Dm base-fret 1 frets 2 2 1 0}` ([ChordPro define](https://www.chordpro.org/chordpro/directives-define/)); LilyPond ships `ukulele-tuning` and `predefined-ukulele-fretboards.ly` ([LilyPond 2.25](https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.25/Documentation/notation/common-notation-for-fretted-strings)). music21 has `UkeleleFretBoard` and `ChordWithFretBoard`, but its MusicXML tablature export has a long-standing invalid-output issue, so MusicXML tab is safer written by hand ([music21 tablature](https://music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleTablature.html); [music21 issue 778](https://github.com/cuthbertLab/music21/issues/778)).

## Strum onsets are detectable at 97%; direction mostly follows the beat

No peer-reviewed paper classifies guitar passages into strummed, riff and melody, so this component must be engineered from adjacent findings. A virtual-instrument patent uses a roughly 20 ms threshold to separate "approximately simultaneous events" within one strum from separate chords ([US 5,393,926](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5393926)); classical note-tracking models "often struggle with the dense polyphony and rhythmic structure of strumming, where the emphasis lies on chord-level articulation" ([Murgul et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973v1)); and melody extraction in Western pop defaults to the skyline rule, "the highest note at each point in time", with filtering to stop it leaping into accompaniment ([FLAIRS 2006](https://cdn.aaai.org/FLAIRS/2006/Flairs06-049.pdf)). Together these support a per-bar classifier over event polyphony (events clustered within 20-30 ms, three or more pitch classes meaning a strum), onset regularity on an 8th/16th grid, pitch-set agreement with the chord recogniser's label, pitch range and 1-2 bar autocorrelation for riffs; because note transcribers under-detect notes inside dense strums, the decision should also use audio features such as spectral-flux onset density and chroma stability rather than transcribed notes alone.

Strumming itself is the component with the largest gap between what the market says and what research shows. Chordify states it "is not able to accurately detect strumming patterns, as this is near-impossible to detect accurately with algorithms and unlikely to be added in the future", and instead sells human-authored strums in paid Song Lessons ([Chordify support](https://support.chordify.net/hc/en-us/articles/360019489618-Does-Chordify-show-the-strumming-patterns)); UkuTabs' 20,000 suggested patterns are user-submitted ([UkuTabs on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ukutabs.songs&hl=en_US)). Meanwhile Murgul, Schimper and Heizmann's ISMIR 2025 CRNN, trained on four hours of synthetic strumming plus 90 minutes of real recordings labelled by a wrist accelerometer, reaches **strum-detection F1 92.75% on microphone audio, with per-direction F1 of 85.51% up and 79.02% down and 90.06% chord accuracy**, against 79.49% for a spectral-flux baseline; it excludes muted strums ([arXiv 2508.07973](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973v1)). Yousician's 2025 system is the better template for mixes: it separates the HTDemucs "other" stem, detects strums with a fine-tuned MERT backbone, and Viterbi-decodes them against an expert vocabulary of 924 one-to-two-bar patterns with switching penalties, achieving **96.9% strum-detection F1 on the stem, 94.7% F1 reconstructing strums from decoded patterns, and cutting bar-line discontinuities from 4.88% to 0.41%**; it assumes steady tempo and notes that guitar SNR in mixes is below 0 dB ([arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756)). The only ukulele-specific work, Phithak, Angskun and Angskun's 2015 study, predicted strum types (d-, du, -u, xu) per beat with F-measure around 82.6% on ten songs ([ProQuest](https://www.proquest.com/openview/e55f121cf94bb00eb34e7ec8ba34ec7b/1?pq-origsite=gscholar&cbl=936334)). None of the three has public code or weights: Murgul's paper carries no availability statement, and Yousician's companion repository holds only eight ten-second excerpts and a downbeat script ([arXiv 2508.07973 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07973); [YousicianGit repo](https://github.com/YousicianGit/rhythmic-pattern-transcription)).

The engineering consequence is that a ukulele project does not need to solve the hard problem. Published ukulele patterns such as Summer of '69's "D DU DU DU" ([ukulelearn](https://ukulelearn.com/summer-of-69-ukulele-chords/)) or Lauren Bateman's "D D DU D U" ([Lauren Bateman](https://www.laurenbateman.com/summer-of-69-chord-chart/)) place downstrokes on beats and on-beat 8ths and upstrokes on off-beats, so **inferring direction from metric position will match human tabs most of the time**, and an audio direction classifier would add only about 80% per-class confidence at the cost of training data; the exception to plan for is the muted straight-eighth "chug", which Ultimate Guitar's own ukulele chart prints for the same song as sixteen downstrokes with accents on beat 1 and the "and" of 2 at 138 BPM, while UkuTabs gives "d - d - d u d u", so published sources disagree and the decoder should treat them as a vocabulary rather than a single truth ([UG S69 ukulele](https://tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/tab/bryan-adams/summer-of-69-ukulele-1326626); [UkuTabs S69](https://ukutabs.com/b/bryan-adams/summer-of-69/)). The tractable design is: detect strum onsets on the separated guitar stem, quantise to an 8- or 16-slot bar vector using the downbeat grid, assign D/U by position, flag muted strums where a broadband onset has high spectral flatness and low chroma energy, and decode each section against a vocabulary of 20-50 common 4/4 patterns with a Viterbi switching penalty so one pattern is printed per section, exactly as tab sites do. Output should follow the conventions readers already know: D and U, (d)/(u) for muted, x for chuck, bold for accents, aligned to "1 + 2 + 3 + 4 +" ([UkuTabs strumming guide](https://ukutabs.com/ukulele-guides/ukulele-strumming-patterns-beginners/)).

## Every open pipeline is the same chain, and none emits strums

The survey of existing implementations is remarkably uniform: nearly every open-source end-to-end project is yt-dlp into Demucs into Basic Pitch (occasionally CREPE or YourMT3+) into a MIDI-to-tab heuristic into LilyPond or PyGuitarPro, built by one person and starred by almost nobody. tabforge (created 2026-08-24, 2 stars) is the most complete: Demucs stems, Basic Pitch plus optional GAPS and YourMT3+ transcribers, a beat-grid ensemble, Krumhansl-Schmuckler key detection, Viterbi fingering tuned on GuitarSet, a ukulele tuning preset, and GP5/MusicXML/ASCII output, yet it self-reports **guitar polyphonic F1 of only 0.41 in a full mix**, uses one tempo grid per track and has no YouTube input ([tabforge](https://github.com/rayBastard/tabforge); [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/rayBastard/tabforge)). OpenUkulele (created 2026-09-13, 0 stars) is the only ukulele-specific audio-to-tab project: high-G re-entrant, 15 frets, easy mode (melody plus bass on beats 1 and 3, frets 0-5, no barre) and hard mode (up to four notes, frets 0-15), dynamic-programming fingering with graceful degradation so "no invalid tabs are output", but it has no strumming support, no YouTube input, and admits audio input "struggles with vocal-heavy, percussion, or heavily-mixed commercial recordings" ([OpenUkulele](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele)). Sheet Sage (459 stars) is the best lead-sheet research artefact, with madmom beats, Jukebox-feature melody transcription and chord recognition to LilyPond, but needs Linux and Docker, is tuned to 24-second segments, reports beat detection that "occasionally doubles/halves" tempo, and its weights are CC BY-NC-SA ([sheetsage](https://github.com/chrisdonahue/sheetsage)); openmirlab's sheetsage-infer repackages it for Windows with PyTorch 2 ([sheetsage-infer](https://github.com/openmirlab/sheetsage-infer)). Smaller efforts confirm the ceiling: IrdiZ/guitar-tabs self-reports about 70% monophonic F1 and 25% on chords ([IrdiZ](https://github.com/IrdiZ/guitar-tabs)), and tab-gener8or advises that the tool "works best analyzing one instrument at a time" ([tab-gener8or](https://github.com/mgd1984/tab-gener8or)).

Commercial tools split into chord-timeline products and note-level transcribers, and none outputs a detected strum. Chordify, born as a Utrecht University research project, claims **75-90% chord accuracy "depending on the style of the music and the sound quality"** ([Utrecht University](https://www.uu.nl/en/news/utrecht-university-and-chordify-introduce-revolutionary-interactive-application)), takes YouTube links, offers no tab or strumming, and users report chord changes "out of place" ([Guitar Chalk](https://www.guitarchalk.com/chordify-review/)); its website-only editor lets users fix chords, toggle 3/4 or 4/4 and shift bar lines when "the automatic beat-tracker does not put the bar lines in the right place" ([Chordify edit chords](https://support.chordify.net/hc/en-us/articles/360002148717-How-to-edit-chords)). Moises Chord Finder adds Easy/Medium/Advanced voicing levels, capo mode, manual chord editing and iReal Pro or MusicXML export, but neither its help centre nor the feature page mentions a ukulele profile at all ([Moises](https://moises.ai/features/chord-finder/); [Moises chord detection](https://help.moises.ai/hc/en-us/articles/6569274648220-How-do-I-use-Chord-Detection)); Ultimate Guitar's AI chords accepts a YouTube link, returns chords only, and caps users at ten attempts per day ([UG help](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/14771579-mobile-what-are-the-ai-chords)). On the note side, Songsterr AI produced tabs that matched published versions of Enter Sandman and Sweet Child O' Mine in a heise test but missed palm muting and slides and confused separate guitar tracks ([heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Songsterr-uses-AI-to-create-fairly-accurate-guitar-tabs-from-your-own-recordings-10356152.html)); Klangio's Guitar2Tabs draws app-store ratings of 2.9-3.4 with reviewers saying notes are correct but "laid out in a way that doesn't reflect how you'd actually play" ([Klangio review](https://agent-finder.co/reviews/klangio)); AnthemScore makes no tab and no separation ([Songscription](https://www.songscription.ai/blog/anthemscore-alternatives)). Songscription's own blog concedes that "a clean solo line transcribes far better than a dense chord progression buried under distortion" ([Songscription](https://www.songscription.ai/blog/best-ai-guitar-tab-generators)). Riffstation, which once claimed about 85% chord accuracy, was discontinued in 2018-2019 ([RoaringApps](https://roaringapps.com/app/riffstation)).

Three lessons follow. First, the better systems (Sheet Sage, tabforge, TART, Chordify's "chord network plus beat network") share one architecture, separation into a beat/meter grid into chord or note transcription into a symbolic arrangement with playability constraints into GP5/MusicXML, so a new project should adopt it rather than reinvent it. Second, the recurring failure modes are beat and downbeat errors, dense distorted mixes, wrong string/fret placement despite correct pitches, and missing technique annotations; a chord-and-strum sheet with misaligned bars is unusable, which makes the downbeat tracker a first-order risk. Third, the open niches are precisely the ones the user's question targets: no system distinguishes riff sections from strummed sections (note transcribers tab everything, chord tools label everything), no system outputs strums, and only OpenUkulele has any ukulele-aware arrangement logic. Licensing is a quiet fourth lesson: Basic Pitch (Apache), Omnizart, Demucs, tabforge and OpenUkulele (MIT) are safe, while Chordino/chord-extractor (GPL), madmom models and Sheet Sage weights (CC BY-NC-SA) and tabforge's optional MuScriptor weights (CC BY-NC) would block a commercial product.

## alphaTab alone syncs tab, chord diagrams and a YouTube cursor

Among web renderers, alphaTab is the only open-source option that combines a tab staff, standard notation, in-score chord diagrams, a smooth playback cursor and a documented external-media sync API. It is MPL-2.0, released 1.8.4 on 2026-07-05 with a monthly-ish cadence, loads Guitar Pro 3-8, alphaTex and MusicXML, and added in-score chord diagrams in 1.8.0 ([alphaTab releases](https://github.com/CoderLine/alphaTab/releases); [alphaTab README](https://github.com/coderline/alphaTab)). The sync mechanism is `IExternalMediaHandler` with `seekTo`, `play`, `pause` and an `updatePosition()` callback the docs recommend driving every 50 ms, enabled through `PlayerMode.EnabledExternalMedia`; it works with HTML5 audio and the YouTube iframe API, with the caveats that alphaTab cannot mix its synthesiser with external audio, cannot pitch-shift the backing track, and YouTube's `seekTo()` autoplays when not paused ([alphaTab Audio & Video Sync](https://alphatab.net/docs/guides/audio-video-sync)). Its Media Sync Editor creates sync points at tempo changes and masterbars and exports either a GP8 file or source code ([Media Sync Editor](https://alphatab.net/docs/guides/media-sync-editor)). Because the Python side already knows beat and downbeat times, emitting one sync point per bar lets the cursor follow real tempo drift. It's MyTabs is an MIT, Docker-deployable Songsterr clone built on alphaTab with MP3/OGG/YouTube sync and three cursor modes, and is the fastest reference to copy ([its-mytabs](https://github.com/ticket-wisp/its-mytabs)).

The alternatives each miss a piece. OpenSheetMusicDisplay (BSD-3, 2.1.3 on 2026-09-19) draws tab with bends and glissandi from MusicXML but has no chord diagrams (an open feature request), no playback, and a cursor you must drive from your own timestamps ([OSMD](https://github.com/opensheetmusicdisplay/opensheetmusicdisplay); [OSMD issue #1010](https://github.com/opensheetmusicdisplay/opensheetmusicdisplay/issues/1010)). VexFlow 5 (MIT) renders tab programmatically with no sync ([npm vexflow](https://www.npmjs.com/package/vexflow)); abcjs 6.7.1 supports custom tunings and a built-in synth cursor but no external sync ([abcjs tablature docs](https://docs.abcjs.net/visual/tablature)); Verovio's tablature import is still "preliminary" and lute-driven ([Verovio CHANGELOG](https://github.com/rism-digital/verovio/blob/develop/CHANGELOG.md)). ChordSheetJS parses and formats ChordPro with ukulele `{define}` support but is GPL-2.0 and jumped from v16 to v18 within one week of September 2026, so pin it ([ChordSheetJS releases](https://github.com/martijnversluis/ChordSheetJS/releases)). For standalone chord diagrams, svguitar (MIT, 2.6.2) has configurable string counts and headless Node rendering ([svguitar](https://github.com/omnibrain/svguitar)) and @chordbook/charts explicitly supports ukulele ([chordbook/charts](https://github.com/chordbook/charts)). **Strum-arrow rendering has no maintained library at all**: strumvg is a zero-star CLI with no license file ([strumvg](https://github.com/edonv/strumvg)), so arrows should be hand-rolled SVG driven by the same beat clock. Soundslice is the polished commercial route, syncing notation to YouTube on the free plan, but unlimited embedding and API access cost $100 per month ([Soundslice plans](https://www.soundslice.com/plans/)).

For static output, MuseScore Studio 4.7.5 provides a headless converter mode (`-o`) that exports PDF, PNG, SVG, MIDI, MusicXML and audio, supports fretboard diagrams for any string count, and can batch jobs from JSON ([MuseScore command line](https://handbook.musescore.org/appendix/command-line-usage); [MuseScore releases](https://github.com/musescore/MuseScore/releases)), although ukulele diagrams come from a community palette and need manual chord-name labelling ([musescore.org](https://musescore.org/en/node/283424)). LilyPond 2.26 produces publication-quality tab if string numbers are set explicitly per note, sidestepping its descending-pitch assumption ([LilyPond releases](https://github.com/lilypond/lilypond/releases)). Pure-Python diagram packages are stale or copyleft (`fretboard` last released 2016; `fretboardgtr` is AGPL), so generate SVGs once and cache them ([PyPI fretboard](https://pypi.org/project/fretboard/); [PyPI fretboardgtr](https://pypi.org/project/fretboardgtr/)). For debugging, librosa 1.0.0's `specshow` chromagram against the chord track, pretty_midi piano rolls against pYIN f0, and Sonic Visualiser 5.2.1 with the Chordino and pYIN Vamp plugins cover inspection without extra tooling ([PyPI librosa](https://pypi.org/project/librosa/); [Sonic Visualiser releases](https://github.com/sonic-visualiser/sonic-visualiser/releases)). If a Streamlit shell is wanted, `st.html` embedding a self-contained page that loads alphaTab from a CDN is the only route; no Streamlit notation component exists on PyPI and `st.components.v1.html` is deprecated since 1.56.0 ([Streamlit docs](https://docs.streamlit.io/develop/api-reference/custom-components/st.components.v1.html)).

## Recommended architecture: generic ears, a ukulele brain, alphaTab eyes

The architecture below keeps every audio model generic and swappable, and concentrates all ukulele knowledge in stages 7-9, which are pure Python over symbolic data. Everything runs on Windows 11 in a single PyTorch virtual environment except madmom, which should be isolated in its own environment or dropped entirely in favour of Beat This! and a chroma-based key estimate.

| Stage | Primary choice | Fallback / alternative | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Ingest | `yt-dlp[default]` + Deno + ffmpeg; `-f bestaudio -x --audio-format wav`; `--write-info-json` for title/artist | pytubefix; local file upload | Only maintained path; source is already lossy so WAV just avoids a second generation |
| 2. Separation | `audio-separator[gpu]` running htdemucs_ft (MIT) for 4 stems and BS-RoFormer-SW for guitar/piano stems | htdemucs_6s (MIT) when a clean license matters | RoFormer-SW guitar SDR 9.0 vs 7.0; no licence (collectively trained "shared weights", authors unnamed), so keep MIT fallback |
| 3. Re-mix | Build a "harmonic re-mix": bass + other + guitar + piano at full level, vocals and drums attenuated | Original mix only | APSIPA 2025 shows boosted-pitched re-mix helps ACR by only +0.20 WCSR points; raw stems hurt it |
| 4. Grid and structure | Beat This! for beats/downbeats; allin1 for sections (run in WSL if NATTEN will not build); expose a beat-octave switch | librosa beat_track and onset_detect | Beat errors are the top-cited failure; PSSOM's 176/88 ambiguity needs a user override |
| 5. Chords and key | Chord-CNN-LSTM or ChordMini on both the original mix and the re-mix; reconcile; snap to beats; reduce with mir_eval to triads; key from madmom CNN (git) or Krumhansl on librosa chroma | Omnizart chord (maj/min) | Near-SOTA at MIREX 2025, MIT, PyTorch, no Vamp |
| 6. Notes | Basic Pitch on the guitar and bass stems (CPU, Windows); SwiftF0 or PESTO on confirmed monophonic riffs for bend/vibrato resolution | YourMT3+ (GPU; Apache-2.0 HF Space) or Transkun for a piano stem | Two-stage design: generic notes, ukulele fingering later |
| 7. Section classifier | Per bar: event polyphony (20-30 ms clustering), onset regularity, chord-label stability, pitch-set match, riff autocorrelation, spectral-flux density; label strum / riff / melody | Manual override in the UI | No existing system does this; it decides which renderer path each bar takes |
| 8. Strums | Onset detection on the guitar stem (spectral flux first, MERT/CRNN later); quantise to 8/16-slot bar vectors; D/U by metric position; muted flag via spectral flatness; Viterbi over a 20-50 pattern vocabulary with a switching penalty, one pattern per section | Print "all downstrokes" when confidence is low | Yousician-style decoding sidesteps the "near-impossible" exact-strum problem |
| 9. Arrangement | Key/capo chooser scoring open, barre-free shapes; chords-db voicings via chord-level Viterbi (minimal movement, open preference, barre penalty); riff/melody fingering via Burlet/Hori-style Viterbi parameterised by tuning (gCEA, low-G, baritone); power-chord quality from key; easy/hard tiers with OpenUkulele-style graceful degradation | ukechords for shape generation | All ukulele intelligence lives here and is cheap to iterate |
| 10. Output and render | alphaTex (tuning, chord diagrams, brushes, sections, per-bar sync points) rendered by alphaTab with a YouTube `IExternalMediaHandler`; strum arrows and chord timeline as custom SVG on the same clock; ASCII tab and ChordPro as text | GP5 via PyGuitarPro; MusicXML by hand into MuseScore CLI for PDF | Only renderer with tab + diagrams + sync; MuseScore for print |

The prioritised focus areas follow directly from where the evidence shows gaps rather than where libraries already exist.

| Priority | Focus area | Why it earns the slot |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strumming-pattern decoding per section | Zero shipping products do it; Chordify calls it impossible; 2025 research shows 93-97% strum-onset F1 on stems and a small-vocabulary Viterbi makes it readable. This is the differentiator. |
| 2 | Strum / riff / melody section classification | Unaddressed by every system found; without it the pipeline cannot decide whether a bar becomes a chord-and-strum line or a fretted tab line. |
| 3 | Ukulele arrangement layer (voicing Viterbi, re-entrant fingering, key/capo chooser, simplification, difficulty tiers) | Only OpenUkulele has any of this; string/fret assignment is the dominant tab error (81% notes vs 54% tab); the symbolic layer is cheap to iterate and is where the ukulele lives. |
| 4 | Beat, downbeat and tempo-octave robustness | Cited as the first failure by Sheet Sage, tabforge and user forums; bars misaligned by one beat make the whole sheet useless. Build a ground-truth grid for the two target songs and test against it. |
| 5 | Separation-aware chord recognition evaluation | Evidence is mixed (raw stems hurt, boosted re-mix helps by only +0.20 points); A/B original versus re-mix on hand-annotated chords for the two songs before trusting either. |
| 6 | Riff transcription on distorted guitar plus a correction UI | Expect 45-60% note F1 on distorted lead; the honest product treats riffs as drafts and makes fixing them fast. Compare Basic Pitch and YourMT3+ on real material. |
| 7 | Rendering and sync details | Verify alphaTex sync-point syntax, brush-arrow rendering and 4-string chord diagrams in a browser; copy It's MyTabs' YouTube handler. |
| 8 | Licensing hygiene | Decide early whether commercial use is possible; if so, exclude Chordino, madmom models, Sheet Sage weights and unlicensed RoFormer checkpoints. |

Explicitly deprioritised: training new audio models (no ukulele dataset exists and guitar-trained generic models plus a symbolic solver cover the need), lead/rhythm guitar separation (commercial API only, and a single guitar stem suffices for chords), and GP6-8 export (no Python writer exists and PyGuitarPro's maintainer has declined to add one; GP5 and alphaTex are enough).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. The hard-looking parts, chord recognition, note transcription and source separation, are solved well enough by generic, MIT-licensed PyTorch models that the correct engineering choice is to treat them as replaceable commodities and spend no effort improving them. The parts that look like polish, deciding whether a bar is strummed or picked, choosing a voicing that a beginner's hand can reach, printing "D DU UDU" under a verse, are the parts nobody has built, and they are symbolic problems that run in milliseconds and can be tuned by ear against two reference songs rather than by training on data that does not exist. A project that ships the generic chain with a mediocre arrangement layer will look like the dozen zero-star repos already on GitHub; one that ships a competent strum decoder and a ukulele-aware arranger has no open-source competitor and, on strumming specifically, no commercial one either.

Two uncertainties should shape the first sprint. Nobody has measured modern chord models or note transcribers on distorted-guitar rock extracted from a mix, so the first deliverable should be a tiny ground-truth set for Summer of '69 and Pour Some Sugar On Me (beats, downbeats, chords, section labels, published strum patterns) against which every stage is scored; and the best 6-stem separation weights turn out to have no licence at all (a collectively trained "shared weights" checkpoint by unnamed authors), so the pipeline should be written to swap separation models without touching anything downstream. Both are cheap to address now and expensive to discover later.
