# Ten Gaps Reshape the Ukulele Pipeline

The first-round design (generic audio models feeding a ukulele-specific symbolic layer, rendered by alphaTab) survives this second pass intact, but it was missing three whole subsystems and a layer of plumbing that decides whether any of it lines up. The biggest omission is a **timing spine**: no stage in round one owned the master clock, and the notes show every model introduces its own offset or drift (Basic Pitch's frame array slips **2.7 s over a 530 s file**, Demucs silently shortens 48 kHz input, WhisperX word times can be **10-20 s off** after a dropped segment), while the beat tracker chosen in round one has no tempo prior and produces "locally correct but globally unstable" grids on **18% of hard tracks** ([basic-pitch #190](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/issues/190); [SMC Blind Spot](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12287)). The second omission is the **vocal path**: for a sung rock song the melody and the words come from the vocal stem, and the evidence says to expect roughly **20% word error** from Whisper on separated pop vocals and only **40-55% note-level F1** from the best open singing-voice transcribers on English pop, which makes "fetch reference lyrics and force-align them" and "treat the vocal melody as a draft" the only honest designs ([arXiv 2506.15514](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15514v1); [arXiv 2304.12082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.12082)). The third is **harmonic correction**: the bass stem is now the best-separated stem of all (**13.3 dB SDR versus 12.7 dB for vocals** with BS-RoFormer), chord models decompose labels into root, bass and quality, and inter-annotator root agreement is only **76%**, so a bass-anchored re-scoring step with a soft local-key prior is both feasible and bounded by human disagreement ([arXiv 2309.02612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02612); [arXiv 2509.01588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01588)). Around those three sit cheap pre-stages the first round skipped (song identification, loudness and codec conditioning, non-music trimming, instrument routing) and an evaluation reality: **no public ukulele transcription dataset exists**, so ground truth must be synthesised from CC0 soundfonts and self-recorded takes ([Zenodo ukulele search](https://zenodo.org/api/records?q=ukulele&size=25); [FreePats ukulele](https://freepats.zenvoid.org/GuitarFamily/ukulele.html)). The engineering envelope is narrower than it looked: Python 3.11 is the only version inside every library's stated range, several of the best weights are non-commercial or unlicensed, and LLMs belong strictly after the audio models, where they score near ceiling on symbolic input but **13-52% on the same chord-quality task from audio** ([arXiv 2510.22455](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22455v1)).

## Timing is the spine, and round one never measured it

### Rhythm quantisation and the MIDI input path

**Known.** Round one picked Beat This! for the grid and a beat-based quantiser in principle, but it never specified how raw note events become note values. The off-the-shelf Python quantisers are blunt: note_seq's `quantize_note_sequence` snaps to a fixed steps-per-quarter grid with a `QUANTIZE_CUTOFF` of 0.5, forces every note to at least one step, has no tuplet or swing support and last shipped in July 2022 ([note_seq sequences_lib.py](https://github.com/magenta/note-seq/blob/main/note_seq/sequences_lib.py); [PyPI note-seq](https://pypi.org/pypi/note-seq/json)); music21's `Stream.quantize` is the only one that handles triplets, through a divisor set whose default **(4, 3) snaps to sixteenths and eighth triplets, whichever is closer**, with a look-ahead since v7 and a recorded per-note quantisation error ([music21 stream/base.py](https://github.com/cuthbertLab/music21/blob/master/music21/stream/base.py)). MuseScore 4's handbook says the MIDI-import quantisation panel is "not currently implemented", so there is no GUI fallback either ([MuseScore 4 handbook](https://musescore.org/en/handbook/4/midi-import)). The research frontier treats quantisation as sequence prediction conditioned on beats: Nakamura's metrical HMM avoids the "grammatically wrong" triplet fragments a note-level HMM produces and beat its rivals by **more than 12 points on polyrhythmic performances** ([Nakamura SMC 2016](https://eita-nakamura.github.io/articles/Nakamura_etal_RhythmTranscriptionOfPolyphonicMIDIPerformances_SMC2016.pdf); [arXiv 1701.08343](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.08343)), PM2S (MIT, Python 3.8, PyTorch 1.12) predicts on-beat notes and fills missing beats by minimising tempo-ratio cost ([PM2S paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2022/paper/000047.pdf); [cheriell/PM2S](https://github.com/cheriell/PM2S)), and the 2026 T5 model of Wachter et al., which takes beat annotations as input exactly as this pipeline would, reaches **97.3% onset F1 but only 83.3% note-value accuracy** on ASAP ([arXiv 2604.22290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22290)). Every released model is piano-trained; only Murgul and Heizmann's 2025 MIDI beat tracker saw guitar data ([arXiv 2507.00466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00466)).

The weak link is duration. Basic Pitch ends a note when frame activation drops below `frame_threshold` (0.3), which is well before the note stops sounding; one practitioner measured a bass stem at **68% silence** and a melody stem at 50%, and fixed it with a `--legato` extension to the next onset capped at 0.25 beats, a velocity floor of 15 and a two-beat duration cap ([swwallowws/coming-undone](https://github.com/swwallowws/coming-undone)). The hidden gap-bridging parameter `energy_tol` (11 frames, about 128 ms) is not exposed by `predict()`, and the 127.7 ms minimum note length equals 11 frames at 86 fps, already close to a sixteenth at 117 BPM ([basic_pitch/note_creation.py](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/blob/main/basic_pitch/note_creation.py); [basic_pitch/inference.py](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/blob/main/basic_pitch/inference.py)). Basic Pitch's `midi_tempo` argument only writes a header; note times stay in absolute seconds, so its MIDI is a fixed-120-BPM timeline that must be warped onto the tempo map before snapping ([basic-pitch PR #193](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/pull/193)). Time signature is a further hole: Beat This! "does not output time signature or explicit tempo values", its optional madmom DBN enumerates only `beats_per_bar=[3, 4]` inside a 55-215 BPM window, and that 55 BPM floor forces double-tempo output on **21% of the SMC set** ([arXiv 2407.21658](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21658); [beat_this postprocessor.py](https://github.com/CpJKU/beat_this/blob/main/beat_this/model/postprocessor.py); [arXiv 2605.12287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12287)). The precision budget is tight but workable: mir_eval's beat tolerance is 70 ms, Beat This! quantises to 20 ms frames, and a sixteenth at 120 BPM is 125 ms, so raw beats suffice for eighth-level strums and need smoothing for sixteenths ([mir_eval/beat.py](https://github.com/mir-evaluation/mir_eval/blob/main/mir_eval/beat.py)).

For MIDI and Guitar Pro input the parsing side is settled. symusic 0.6.0 (April 2026) parses **200-500 times faster than mido** with Windows wheels for Python 3.8-3.12, pretty_midi 0.2.11.post0 (July 2026) is the convenient choice for beats and instrument metadata, mido 1.3.3 is the low-level base, and miditoolkit has not shipped since 2023 ([symusic docs](https://yikai-liao.github.io/symusic/); [PyPI pretty_midi](https://pypi.org/pypi/pretty_midi/json); [PyPI miditoolkit](https://pypi.org/pypi/miditoolkit/json)). Track identification rests on GM program numbers (guitars 24-31 zero-based, bass 32-39, drums on channel 9), track names and polyphony heuristics; midi-miner offers a random-forest melody/bass/chord/drum classifier whose features and accuracy are undocumented ([Wikipedia General MIDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_MIDI); [ruiguo-bio/midi-miner](https://github.com/ruiguo-bio/midi-miner)). PyGuitarPro 0.11 (May 2026, LGPL-3.0, Python 3.10+) reads and writes GP3-GP5 with string and fret per note; two 2026 issues about GP6-8 support were closed but the README still lists GP3-5 ([PyPI PyGuitarPro](https://pypi.org/pypi/PyGuitarPro/json); [PyGuitarPro issues](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=repo:Perlence/PyGuitarPro+gpx)). The guitar-to-ukulele mapping follows from the capo-5 identity: ukulele GCEA equals the guitar's top four strings capoed at fret 5, so guitar strings 1-4 map to ukulele A E C G with `fret_uke = fret_guitar - 5`, and anything on strings 5-6 or below fret 5 must be re-fingered ([Wikipedia Ukulele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukulele)). Symbolic MIDI-to-tab models are guitar-only (Fretting-Transformer 98.41% on simple fingerstyle, 72.19% on jazz chord-melody), and the only ukulele-aware open tool is OpenUkulele's per-measure DP with playability validation ([arXiv 2506.14223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14223); [xin-pu/OpenUkulele](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele)).

**Recommendation.** Keep the two-stage structure the Wachter design validates: build the beat grid from audio first, then snap onsets against it with a music21-style divisor set, choosing (4, 3) for straight material and (4, 6) when the drum-stem swing test (below) reports a shuffle. Discard Basic Pitch offsets entirely for strummed material and sustain each event to the next onset with a gap cap near 0.25 beats, cutting to a rest only beyond the cap; lower `minimum_note_length` toward 60-80 ms when hunting sixteenth strums, and patch the call to `output_to_notes_polyphonic` if `energy_tol` needs changing. Infer beats-per-bar as the mode of beats between consecutive downbeats and resolve 6/8 versus 3/4 with an onset-subdivision test, since no tool does it. For file input, route GP3-5 straight past quantisation (string, fret, tuplets and time signatures are already explicit), parse MIDI with pretty_midi keyed on program and channel, pick melody and chord tracks by mean pitch and polyphony, and verify PyGuitarPro 0.11 against a real `.gp` file before promising GP6-8 support.

**Unverified.** The Cemgil-Kappen and Raphael Bayesian tempo-tracking papers could not be fetched; the Friberg and Sundberg timing-JND numbers are uncited; whether Wachter et al. released code is unknown; no tool for anacrusis or hypermeter detection was found; and no study relates strum-pattern accuracy to beat precision.

### Tempo maps, the octave decision and cross-stage alignment

**Known.** Modern trackers rarely accumulate phase error on steady rock, but the failure modes that remain are the ones that break a tab. The 2026 SMC analysis of Beat This!, Beat Transformer and madmom classifies tracks as **29% good, 8% octave errors, 18% continuity errors and 5% complete failures**, with per-track tuning of the tempo-continuity parameter lifting F from 0.627 to 0.642 ([arXiv 2605.12287](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12287)). Beat This! removes the DBN precisely because its 55-215 BPM window and [3, 4] meter prior "block research from solving corner cases", and processes long audio in 30 s chunks with a six-frame border, which makes multiples of 30 s a plausible site of phase jumps ([arXiv 2407.21658](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21658); [beat_this/inference.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CPJKU/beat_this/main/beat_this/inference.py)). Practitioners post-process: a Beat This! user script constrains BPM to 78-185, computes an "advanced BPM" from a phase-aware circular mean and linear regression after rejecting outliers, and reaches **GiantSteps Acc1 89.3% versus Acc2 95.0%**, the six-point gap being the octave-ambiguity cost; the maintainers endorse the median across predictions ([beat_this #13](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/issues/13)). Running the tracker on the drum stem helps only on drum-heavy material: on a rock set Chiu et al. report **drum-only 0.886, mixture 0.901, fused 0.911** ([arXiv 2106.08685](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.08685)). A human drummer without a click drifts measurably (hi-hat SD 8.7 ms on a 157 ms interval, long-range correlation 0.72), so a single global BPM cannot fit a non-click recording ([Räsänen 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0127902)). The representations exist: MIDI `set_tempo` events, pretty_midi's `adjust_times` piecewise time-warp, JAMS `tempo` with multiple weighted observations, and alphaTex `\tempo` per bar with a 0-1 position ([pretty_midi.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/craffel/pretty-midi/main/pretty_midi/pretty_midi.py); [JAMS namespaces](https://jams.readthedocs.io/en/stable/namespace.html); [alphaTex bar metadata](https://www.alphatab.net/docs/alphatex/bar-metadata)).

The tempo octave is a notation decision, not a measurement. The field's ACC2 metric forgives factors of 2 and 3 because annotators themselves disagree on 2-10% of tracks ([TISMIR tempo](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.43)), Parncutt's pulse-salience model peaks near 700 ms (about 86 BPM) ([Parncutt 1994](https://doi.org/10.2307/40285633)), and the backbeat convention places the snare on beats 2 and 4 at the quarter-note level ([Wikipedia Beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_(music))). For the target song the listings disagree by octave and by a few percent within it (176 BPM "half-time at 88" on one site, 85 on another), and the arithmetic settles the notation level: at 176 BPM a sixteenth is 85 ms, below the roughly **100 ms practical floor** jazz drummers hold for the short swung note, so a D/U pattern is only writable at 88 ([songbpm](https://songbpm.com/@def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me); [Friberg & Sundström 2002](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2002.19.3.333)).

Cross-stage alignment is where the documented hazards pile up. Basic Pitch's frame array drifts 188 samples per 2 s window (2.7 s over 530 s) while its note events, decoded from seconds-stamped detections, do not; its decoder also subtracts an unexplained `MAGIC_ALIGNMENT_OFFSET` of 1.8 ms ([basic-pitch #190](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/issues/190); [note_creation.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/note_creation.py)). Demucs outputs 44.1 kHz only and a 48 kHz input yields stems "shorter than inputs" ([demucs #563](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/issues/563)); python-audio-separator's VR path returned stems **240 samples (5.44 ms) short** until an August 2026 fix ([audio-separator PR #304](https://github.com/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator/pull/304)). Whisper timestamps are quantised to 20 ms tokens, and on separated vocals WhisperX "fails to detect some lines, and then transcribes the rest ... as if the time spanned by the gap did not exist", resyncing tens of seconds later ([whisper/audio.py](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/audio.py); [whisperX #402](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX/issues/402); [whisperX #1276](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX/issues/1276)). Ogg Opus carries a 48 kHz granule clock and a pre-skip the decoder must apply ([RFC 7845](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7845)), and the YouTube IFrame API specifies `getCurrentTime()` only as seconds since playback started, with no precision statement ([YouTube IFrame API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/iframe_api_reference)). Groove is measurable from the drum stem: swing ratio runs from 3.5:1 at slow jazz tempi to 1:1 at fast ones, breakbeat sixteenth swing has a tempo-independent median of 1.2:1 ([Frane 2017](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2017.34.3.291)), and a stem-based drum transcriber reaches **F 0.89 on MDB with RMS-derived velocities**, which are far more trustworthy than Basic Pitch's velocity, defined as `round(127 × mean frame activation)` ([arXiv 2509.24853](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24853); [note_creation.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/note_creation.py)). Anticipated ("pushed") changes are documented as a chord or bass arriving on the "and of 4" to anticipate the next downbeat, and no algorithm for quantising them was found ([Wikipedia Syncopation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncopation)).

**Recommendation.** Decode once to a 44.1 kHz stereo float master and derive every per-model input from it, so all stages share t = 0; assert `len(stem) == len(mix)` after separation and zero-pad any shortfall; consume Basic Pitch `note_events`, never frame indices. Turn raw beats into an explicit tempo map by rejecting intervals outside roughly 0.6-1.6 times the local median, fitting tempo per section by regression, snapping each beat to the nearest onset peak and storing the result as per-bar tempo events and one alphaTab sync point per bar. Run the tracker on both the mix and the drum stem and fuse. Pick the notation level with a backbeat test (snare alternating with kick at the tracked level means quarter notes; snare on every fourth tracked beat means halve the tempo) inside a 70-180 BPM window, and store both the tracked and notated levels. Read the swing ratio from the off-beat onset position within each beat (0.50 straight, 0.67 triplet, 0.545 light swing written straight) before snapping. Quantise chord boundaries on the eighth grid, attribute an "and of 4" change to the following bar's label and keep the push as an accent on the off-beat up-strum. Validate the whole chain once with a click-track file rendered by `librosa.clicks` and cross-correlated through every stage, and calibrate a single `player_offset_s` per video against the YouTube player.

**Unverified.** No paper measures accumulated drift in milliseconds per minute on full-length rock songs; no source documents a systematic offset between yt-dlp audio and the YouTube player clock; `librosa.resample` delay is undocumented; WhisperX word-timing error on music is anecdotal only; and the ride-cymbal swing-ratio estimation papers are known only by title.

## Words and melody come from the vocal stem, not the guitar

### Lyrics transcription and alignment

**Known.** Automatic lyrics transcription is measurably worse than speech recognition and separation barely helps. The QMUL/AudioShake study with Whisper large-v2 reports short-form WER of **20.99% on the Jam-ALT mix and 21.08% on the separated stem**; on MUSDB-ALT separation lowers 23.59% to 20.00% and a true clean stem reaches 14.19%, while RMS-VAD "cut and merge" segmentation improves long-form WER by 2.3-3.1 points, non-lexical vocables and backing vocals suffer a **deletion rate above 50%** regardless of separation, and separation artefacts trigger hallucinations of ten or more sequential words ([arXiv 2506.15514](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15514v1)). LyricWhiz's recipe of sampling several Whisper runs with a "lyrics:" prompt and letting GPT-4 choose cut Jamendo WER from 33.13% to 24.25% ([arXiv 2306.17103](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17103v4)); AudioShake claims a 57% relative reduction over Whisper v2 but as a vendor ([AudioShake blog](https://www.audioshake.ai/post/lyrics-transcription-for-humans-revisiting-audioshakes-transcription-benchmark)). The runtime choices are faster-whisper (up to 4x faster, MIT, CUDA 12 with cuDNN 9) and WhisperX (under 8 GB for large-v2, BSD-2, Windows install notes) ([SYSTRAN/faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper); [m-bain/whisperX](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX)); `alt-eval` implements the Jam-ALT metrics for scoring your own output ([audioshake/alt-eval](https://github.com/audioshake/alt-eval)).

Reference lyrics are the better source, and only one is freely usable. LRCLIB is explicitly free and keyless, returns line-synced LRC when the supplied duration matches within ±2 s, and has a Python wrapper, but its synced lyrics are line-level only and the content is user-contributed copyrighted text ([tranxuanthang/lrclib](https://github.com/tranxuanthang/lrclib); [lrclibapi docs](https://lrclibapi.readthedocs.io/en/latest/examples/fetch_lyrics.html)). Genius' API never returns lyric text and lyricsgenius admits its scraping "violates Genius' terms of service" ([lyricsgenius](https://lyricsgenius.readthedocs.io/en/stable/how_it_works.html)); Musixmatch's free tier returns 30% of each lyric ([publicapis.io](https://publicapis.io/musixmatch-api)); Spotify lyrics need a logged-in cookie ([syrics](https://pypi.org/project/syrics/)). For alignment, speech-trained CTC aligners all run on Windows: WhisperX's `align()` accepts any segment text, ctc-forced-aligner windows audio in 30 s chunks (its default MMS model is CC-BY-NC), and torchaudio's `forced_align` is deprecated in 2.8 and removed in 2.9 ([ctc-forced-aligner](https://github.com/MahmoudAshraf97/ctc-forced-aligner); [torchaudio MMS_FA](https://docs.pytorch.org/audio/stable/tutorials/forced_alignment_for_multilingual_data_tutorial.html)). Singing-trained aligners are better on paper, Stoller's **0.35 s mean error** and Durand's **under 0.2 s** on Jamendo, but Durand released no weights and only Huang et al. 2022 ships MIT checkpoints ([arXiv 1902.06797](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06797); [arXiv 2306.07744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07744); [LyricsAlignment-MTL](https://github.com/jhuang448/LyricsAlignment-MTL)). On output, ChordPro needs no library, ChordSheetJS is GPL-2.0, the only PyPI ChordPro package was removed, pyphen handles syllabification, music21's `Lyric` carries MusicXML syllabic flags, and alphaTex takes `\lyrics "..."` with one whitespace chunk per beat, `+` to join words on a beat and `-` to carry a word across notes ([chordpro.org](https://www.chordpro.org/chordpro/chordpro-introduction/); [music21 note](https://www.music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleNote.html); [alphaTab staff metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTabWebsite/main/docs/alphatex/_staff-metadata.mdx)).

**Recommendation.** Fetch first, transcribe second: query LRCLIB `/api/get` with the YouTube title, artist and the decoded audio's duration, fall back to `/api/search` filtered by duration, and only then run faster-whisper large-v3 or turbo on the vocal stem with VAD segmentation and a hallucination guard; adopt the LyricWhiz multi-sample-and-select trick if ASR is the only source. Force-align the reference text to the vocal stem with WhisperX or ctc-forced-aligner, windowing each lyric line to its LRC timestamp so repeated choruses cannot align to the wrong occurrence, and keep Huang's MIT checkpoints as the upgrade path. Place each chord by snapping its beat-grid time to the nearest aligned word onset within half a beat; tag sections with allin1 labels; treat parenthesised reference lines as backing vocals. Write ChordPro and chords-over-words from Python directly, and keep every fetched lyric local and out of any public repository.

**Unverified.** No WER for Whisper large-v3, turbo, Distil-Whisper or Parakeet on singing was found; no measurement of speech-trained aligner error on sung vocals exists; LRCLIB's rate limits and data licence could not be read; and the alphaTex token for an empty lyric chunk on a rest beat is unconfirmed.

### Vocal melody to ukulele

**Known.** Singing-voice transcription splits into two families with very different numbers. Mandarin-SVS models post the highest in-domain scores (ROSVOT COnPOff **77.4%** clean, STARS 71.0 on GTSinger) but ROSVOT falls to **47.4% COnPOff** out of domain on MIR-ST500's separated pop vocals, below the MIR-ST500-trained SpeechBrain model of Gu et al. at 52.84 / 70.00 / 78.05 (COnPOff / COnP / COn), and that model itself collapses to 21.13% under strong accompaniment perturbation ([arXiv 2405.09940](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09940); [arXiv 2507.06670](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06670); [arXiv 2304.12082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.12082)). VocalParse, a 1.7 B-parameter audio LLM under Apache-2.0, reports only Mandarin MAE metrics and assumes a single global BPM per segment ([arXiv 2605.04613](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04613)). ROSVOT is MIT with PyTorch 2.1 and audio-only input, STARS needs a `metadata.json` of words and phonemes, VOCANO depends on CUDA 10.1 and NVIDIA apex, Omnizart's vocal mode lists Linux and Intel macOS only, and Basic Pitch is the sole candidate with an explicit Windows statement ([RickyL-2000/ROSVOT](https://github.com/RickyL-2000/ROSVOT); [gwx314/STARS](https://github.com/gwx314/STARS); [B05901022/VOCANO](https://github.com/B05901022/VOCANO); [PyPI omnizart](https://pypi.org/project/omnizart/)). Backing vocals are the specific failure: ROSVOT "tends to transcribe vocal harmonies when the main vocal is silent", and both target songs carry credited harmony or gang vocals ([arXiv 2405.09940](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09940)). Lead/backing separation is available: MVSEP's karaoke BS-RoFormer reports **lead SDR 10.41 dB and backing 6.61 dB**, and python-audio-separator ships the aufr33/viperx karaoke MelBand checkpoint (SDR 10.20) among several ([mvsep algorithms](https://mvsep.com/en/algorithms); [audio_separator models.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator/main/audio_separator/models.json)). Cleanup thresholds exist in prior art: Essentia's PitchContourSegmentation uses a 100 ms minimum duration and a 60-cent pitch threshold, ROSVOT softens boundaries with an 80 ms Gaussian and uses word boundaries to regulate segmentation ([Essentia PitchContourSegmentation](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_PitchContourSegmentation.html)).

Range placement is arithmetic once the tuning is declared. High-G soprano runs C4-A5 over 12-15 frets, low-G extends the floor to G3, baritone to D3 ([Wikipedia Ukulele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukulele)); per Hooktheory, Summer of '69's melody spans A3-A4 and Pour Some Sugar On Me's sung sections B3-A#4, so neither fits untransposed on high-G but both fit on low-G or baritone, and an octave up puts A5 at the A-string's twelfth fret and A#5 at the thirteenth, over the edge of a 12-fret soprano ([Hooktheory Summer of 69](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/bryan-adams/summer-of-69); [Hooktheory PSSOM](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me)). The one firm pedagogical rule is that "the melody note is almost always placed as the highest note in the chord", on the E and A strings ([Ukulele Tricks](https://ukuleletricks.com/arrange-chord-melody-songs/)). For rendering, PyGuitarPro stores Guitar Pro lyrics as five `LyricLine` strings with a starting measure and alphaTab's `Lyrics.ts` parses the same space, hyphen and plus conventions, so one syllable string serves both GP5 and alphaTex ([Lyrics.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoderLine/alphaTab/develop/packages/alphatab/src/model/Lyrics.ts)).

**Recommendation.** Chain mix to vocals stem to karaoke lead stem to transcriber, and run the transcriber on both the full vocal stem and the lead stem, keeping lead-stem notes where they disagree in regions of detected multi-pitch. Try ROSVOT first, with WhisperX word boundaries fed into its optional word-boundary input, and tune Basic Pitch (frequency clamp around 80-1100 Hz, minimum note length) as the Windows-native fallback. Clean up with a 100-130 ms minimum duration, merge same-pitch notes across sub-80 ms gaps, treat sub-60-cent wobble as vibrato, collapse melismas onto the syllable's first note unless the change exceeds a semitone for longer than the minimum, octave-check against a running phrase median, quantise to the tempo map and insert rests only at breath points. Shift the whole line up an octave whenever its minimum falls below the declared tuning's floor rather than folding individual notes, and expose tuning (high-G, low-G, baritone) as the parameter that decides it. Emit melody tab plus lyrics via alphaTex and music21 MusicXML.

**Unverified.** No English-pop note F1 exists for STARS, VocalParse, Omnizart or Basic Pitch; none of the three modern transcribers states Windows support; no paper quantifies SVT gain from a karaoke stage; no published octave-folding algorithm for ukulele was found; and Hooktheory's ranges are crowd-sourced, not checked against licensed sheet music.

## Harmony needs a bass anchor, a local key and a tuning check

### Bass-anchored chord roots and local key

**Known.** The bass stem is the best-separated stem in every model family: HT Demucs fine-tuned reports bass 10.39 dB against vocals 9.20, and BS-RoFormer with extra data reaches **bass 13.32 dB versus vocals 12.72** ([arXiv 2211.08553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08553); [arXiv 2309.02612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02612)). Chord recognition has exploited a low-octave "bass chroma" to recover inversions since Mauch and Dixon's 2010 DBN, and the ISMIR 2019 review notes that bass notes "can be strongly indicative of the chord being played" ([isophonics NNLS Chroma](http://www.isophonics.net/nnls-chroma); [Pauwels 2019](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3527739)). Modern models decompose labels into components a bass histogram can re-score: Jiang et al. split every chord into root, triad, bass and extensions and note that in Billboard the first 10% of 230 qualities cover **93.86% of the data**, with a confusion matrix that includes maj/3, maj/5 and min/b3 inversions ([Jiang 2019](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3527892)); Poltronieri's 2025 conformer estimates root, bass and note activations separately ([arXiv 2509.01588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01588)). The dominant errors on rock are not model noise but ill-posed labels: McFee and Bello found a bias toward maj and min matching the training distribution, and that the 2.2% of out-of-gamut chords are overwhelmingly single-note and power chords forced into maj or min ([McFee & Bello 2017](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1414880)); four expert annotators agree on the root only **76%** of the time and on maj/min 73% ([Koops 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/09298215.2019.1613436)). The pitch-tracker detail matters: SwiftF0's band starts at 46.875 Hz, above the bass low E at about 41 Hz, while Basic Pitch exposes a minimum-frequency parameter ([arXiv 2508.18440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18440); [basic-pitch README](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/blob/main/README.md)).

Local key estimation is a small research line with no pop implementation. The Weiß/Schreiber/Müller line and Ding's 2025 cross-version evaluation show that most LKE errors are fifth, relative and parallel confusions (scored 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2 by MIREX), that inter-rater agreement "can be as low as 75%", and that the only confirmed open code is trained on classical cross-version sets ([Ding ISMIR 2025](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17811342)). The off-the-shelf tools are global: madmom's CNN emits one of 24 keys per piece, Essentia's `Key` offers a dozen profiles including "shaath" tuned to popular music, and music21's `WindowedAnalysis` slides Krumhansl-style profiles whose biases are documented (KS toward the dominant, Temperley toward the relative major) ([madmom key.py](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/blob/main/madmom/features/key.py); [Essentia Key](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_Key.html); [music21 analysis](https://www.music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleAnalysisDiscrete.html)). Key priors cut both ways: BACHI's ablation found adding key detection slightly lowered full-chord accuracy (67.6% versus 68.1%) ([arXiv 2510.06528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06528)). Final-chorus key lifts were common (about 25% of US No. 1 hits per decade from the 1960s to the 1990s) and are now rare ([Wikipedia Modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation_(music))). On post-processing, the consensus is HMM/Viterbi over blind median filtering, which "smears" transitions and deletes short chords; beat-synchronous processing was shown not to beat HMM smoothing; and downbeat-dependent transition probabilities are the standard way to favour bar-line changes without forbidding anticipations ([Pauwels 2019](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3527739)). Label plumbing is covered by mir_eval's `split`, `encode` and `_inv` comparators, music21's `ChordSymbol` (flats written as `-`), and pychord's slash-chord transposition ([mir_eval chord.py](https://github.com/mir-evaluation/mir_eval/blob/main/mir_eval/chord.py); [music21 harmony](https://www.music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleHarmony.html); [pychord](https://github.com/yuma-m/pychord/blob/main/README.md)). McGill Billboard ships `chords_majmininv` and NNLS bass chroma under CC0, the natural evaluation set ([mirdata billboard.py](https://github.com/mir-dataset-loaders/mirdata/blob/master/mirdata/datasets/billboard.py)).

**Recommendation.** Track the bass stem with a floor at or below 41 Hz (Basic Pitch with `minimum_frequency` near 30 Hz, or pitch-shift the stem up an octave for SwiftF0), fold to pitch class weighted by confidence and metric position, and build a per-beat bass chroma. Re-score chord candidates as log P(chord) + λ log P(bass | root, quality), keep the label when the bass pitch class equals the root, consider a slash chord only when it is a chord tone that persists for a bar or more, and down-weight the candidate when the bass matches neither. Emit "5" for power chords and resolve the third from the guitar stem or, failing that, the local key's diatonic quality. Estimate local key with beat-synchronous chroma over 8-16 bar windows or structural sections, decode with a 24-state HMM biased strongly to self-transition, and use key only to break ties, not to override acoustic evidence. Decode chords with a metric-position-dependent Viterbi (cheapest change on the downbeat, moderate on beat 3 and the "and of 4", expensive elsewhere), merge repeats, count distinct labels per bar for the harmonic rhythm, and spell roots from the section key's `Key.sharps`. Report `root` and `majmin_inv` before and after bass re-scoring on Billboard, remembering that gains above about 0.85 root are inside annotator noise.

**Unverified.** No paper ablates a chord model with and without a separated bass stem on rock; the three core LKE papers were unreadable, so their windows and accuracies are unknown; no pop dataset annotates modulations; and the Chord ai and Chordify slash-chord and simplification behaviours could not be inspected.

### Song identification, tuning offsets and metadata priors

**Known.** Fingerprinting is cheap and exact-match only. AcoustID is free for non-commercial use at three requests per second against **94 million fingerprints**, returns MusicBrainz IDs, and needs the `fpcalc` binary on Windows via pyacoustid; commercial plans start at EUR 50 per month ([AcoustID web service](https://acoustid.org/webservice); [acoustid.biz](https://acoustid.biz/); [beetbox/pyacoustid](https://github.com/beetbox/pyacoustid)). AudD offers 300 free requests then $5 per 1,000 and uniquely returns a `timecode` into the recognised recording ([AudD](https://audd.io/); [AudD docs](https://docs.audd.io/)); shazamio is a reverse-engineered client with no stated terms ([shazamio](https://github.com/shazamio/ShazamIO)); Panako (AGPL, Java) is the only fingerprinter claiming robustness to pitch shift and time stretch ([JorenSix/Panako](https://github.com/JorenSix/Panako)). Chromaprint's chroma design discards octave but not pitch class, so a vari-speeded video fingerprints differently ([Chromaprint internals](https://oxygene.sk/2011/01/how-does-chromaprint-work/)), and under a live-room impulse response a commercial fingerprinter identified **0 of 100 songs** that it had matched perfectly as 64 kbps MP3 ([Mauch & Ewert 2013](https://zenodo.org/records/1415862)). Metadata for key and tempo has collapsed: MusicBrainz stores neither, AcousticBrainz shut down because its key data "was unreliable", Spotify's Audio Features were deprecated for new apps on 27 November 2024, and the survivors are GetSongBPM (free, CC BY, 3,000 requests per hour, attribution link mandatory), Wikidata's P826 and P1725 properties, and Hooktheory's TheoryTab pages, whose terms forbid scraping and model evaluation ([MusicBrainz Work](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/Work); [MetaBrainz blog](https://blog.metabrainz.org/2022/02/16/acousticbrainz-making-a-hard-decision-to-end-the-project/); [Spotify blog](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2024-11-27-changes-to-the-web-api); [Hooktheory terms](https://www.hooktheory.com/terms)). For the target songs TheoryTab gives D major at 139 BPM with an F-major bridge, and C# minor verses into E major choruses at 85 BPM ([Hooktheory Summer of 69](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/bryan-adams/summer-of-69); [Hooktheory PSSOM](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/def-leppard/pour-some-sugar-on-me)).

Tuning estimators measure only sub-semitone deviation: librosa's `estimate_tuning` returns a fraction of a bin in [-0.5, 0.5) from a histogram of pitch residuals, Essentia's `TuningFrequency` spans -35 to +65 cents, and neither can tell Eb standard from E standard ([librosa pitch.py](https://github.com/librosa/librosa/blob/main/librosa/core/pitch.py); [Essentia TuningFrequency](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_TuningFrequency.html)). librosa's CQT functions re-estimate tuning when `tuning=None`, a documented double-counting trap ([librosa #925](https://github.com/librosa/librosa/issues/925)). Pitch shifting is a phase vocoder in librosa and a GPL-or-paid Rubber Band CLI otherwise ([librosa effects.py](https://github.com/librosa/librosa/blob/main/librosa/effects.py); [Rubber Band](https://breakfastquay.com/rubberband/)); professional 1980s tape flutter of about 0.02% sits far below the one-cent resolution of the estimators, so a real offset is almost certainly a deliberate vari-speed constant ([Wikipedia wow and flutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wow_and_flutter_measurement)). Summer of '69 is taught in standard tuning without a capo ([Jon MacLennan](https://www.jonmaclennan.com/blog/summer-of-69-chords)); Phil Collen's description of the Pour Some Sugar On Me riff on the A and E strings and a bridge alternating frets 7 and 6 on the G string is consistent with E standard but no source states it ([Guitar Player](https://www.guitarplayer.com/players/it-took-us-to-a-whole-other-level-in-the-states-phil-collen-reveals-the-short-sweet-creation-of-def-leppards-pour-some-sugar-on-me)). Stereo-field tricks are limited: ADRess needs static, dry pan positions and "co-located sources cannot be separated", DUET's disjointness assumption fails for music, and Audacity-style centre-cancel leaves stereo reverb and off-centre sources ([ADRess DAFx 2004](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2004/P_240.PDF); [Audacity manual](https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/vocal_reduction_and_isolation.html)). Hysteria's guitars were layered through a Rockman over Fairlight-sequenced sampled drums, so expect a dense, reverberant image rather than clean hard-panned doubles ([Wikipedia Hysteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hysteria_(Def_Leppard_album))).

**Recommendation.** Fingerprint a 30-120 s excerpt from the middle of the video (skipping intro dialogue and staying under AudD's 10 MB limit) through pyacoustid to AcoustID and MusicBrainz, then pull tempo and key from GetSongBPM and Wikidata as priors that carry an explicit x2/x0.5 ambiguity for the local beat tracker to resolve; consult TheoryTab by hand only. Estimate the fractional tuning offset once per track on a few 30 s windows and pass it into `chroma_cqt(tuning=...)` or `KeyExtractor(tuningFrequency=...)` instead of resampling; when the detected roots and the known chord shapes disagree by exactly one semitone, conclude a half-step-down tuning and relabel notes rather than pitch-shifting audio before transcription. Score capo and transposition candidates by counting open, barre-free ukulele shapes with a per-capo-fret penalty and prefer the recording's key on ties. Use L, R and side mixes only as extra chord-model views whose agreement becomes a confidence feature; Demucs remains the separator.

**Unverified.** No accuracy figures exist for any fingerprint service on music-video audio with dialogue or live takes; Shazam's terms were not retrieved; GetSongBPM's returned fields (time signature in particular) are unconfirmed; the tuning and any pitch offset of the Hysteria master are undocumented; and no evaluation of ADRess or centre-cancel on 1980s rock was found.

## Input conditioning and routing decide what the models hear

**Known.** The strongest robustness evidence comes from a December 2025 systematic study of piano transcribers: an isolated change of recording conditions costs **20.68 F1 points**, a genre shift 14.17, while onset-only detection loses only 2.43 points; offsets lose 16 points to sound and velocity loses 22 to genre and up to 50 on non-musical material ([arXiv 2512.14602](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14602)). Lossy codecs are not the problem: the ISMIR 2014 study of 400 tracks found chroma features "even more robust" than MFCCs, with "virtually no effect" from codec or bitrate, and summarised the 2014 chord study as showing no obvious correlation between encoding and estimation ([Urbano 2014](https://zenodo.org/records/1416276)). The Audio Degradation Toolbox makes the real risks concrete: a live-room impulse response cost chord estimators under 10 points but **dropped beat tracking from 0.92 to 0.65**, a smartphone-speaker high-pass took a context-heavy chord model from 0.84 to 0.36, and 64 kbps MP3 showed no notable drop ([Mauch & Ewert 2013](https://zenodo.org/records/1415862)). The models condition their own input: Demucs standardises by mean and standard deviation and reverses it after, Basic Pitch min-max normalises each 2 s CQT chunk independently (an unmerged PR proposes global normalisation), Whisper wants 16 kHz mono, PaSST 32 kHz, CLAP 48 kHz ([demucs/api.py](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/blob/main/demucs/api.py); [basic-pitch PR #166](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/pull/166); [LAION CLAP](https://github.com/LAION-AI/CLAP/blob/main/README.md)). pyloudnorm implements BS.1770-4 in NumPy; ffmpeg's `loudnorm` upsamples to 192 kHz internally and needs a two-pass workflow ([pyloudnorm](https://github.com/csteinmetz1/pyloudnorm); [ffmpeg loudnorm](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#loudnorm)); librosa 0.11 resamples with `soxr_hq` by default ([librosa.resample](https://librosa.org/doc/0.11.0/generated/librosa.resample.html)).

Non-music edges have metadata-level and signal-level answers. yt-dlp's info JSON carries `chapters`, `live_status`, a replay `heatmap`, and SponsorBlock segments including `music_offtopic` ("Non-Music Section"), accepted only when the recorded duration matches the download within a second ([yt_dlp common.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/common.py); [sponsorblock.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/postprocessor/sponsorblock.py)); it also marks multi-track audio by `language_preference` and tags dynamic-range-compressed variants with a `-drc` suffix and lowered quality ([yt-dlp youtube/_video.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/_video.py)). inaSpeechSegmenter (MIT, MIREX 2018 winner) labels speech, music, noise and no-energy, and AST or zero-shot CLAP can tag applause and crowd noise ([ina-foss/inaSpeechSegmenter](https://github.com/ina-foss/inaSpeechSegmenter); [AST model card](https://huggingface.co/MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593)). For routing, clip-level instrument recognition is solved well enough (Han et al. micro-F1 0.602 on IRMAS; OpenMIC-2018's 20 classes under CC-BY; Essentia's 40-tag Jamendo instrument head), and Banquet's PaSST-conditioned separator "outperform[s]" six-stem HT Demucs on guitar and piano ([arXiv 1605.09507](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.09507); [cosmir/openmic-2018](https://github.com/cosmir/openmic-2018); [arXiv 2406.18747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18747)). Solo and lead detection has two references: Pati and Lerch's 60-song rock solo detector at **78.6% macro-accuracy but 63.3% solo precision**, and Ou et al.'s 2025 MERT-based lead-instrument detector with MIT code and checkpoints ([Pati & Lerch 2017](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf); [arXiv 2503.03232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03232)).

**Recommendation.** Download with language preference and a sort that favours Opus, reject any `-drc` format, and read chapters, SponsorBlock marks, `live_status` and `heatmap` before decoding. Decode to float32, remove DC, apply a linear BS.1770 gain to a fixed target around -14 to -18 LUFS with pyloudnorm (no limiter), keep the 44.1 kHz stereo master, and resample per model with `soxr_hq`; normalise before separation so stems and mix share one reference level. Trim in order of precedence: SponsorBlock and chapter titles, then inaSpeechSegmenter runs of two seconds or more at the edges, then a tagger second opinion, then RMS gating, keeping sung intros by checking a singing tag before cutting speech. Detect fade-outs from a sustained RMS slope over the last 10-20 s and end the tab where level drops about 12 dB below the chorus median; mark final bars "rit." instead of forcing them onto the grid. Flag a live version when `live_status` says so, when AcoustID scores below about 0.5 for a known studio title, or when applause tags dominate the edges, and widen tempo tolerance accordingly. Route by per-bar stem RMS relative to the mix with the tagger as tie-breaker: a guitar-driven song takes chords and riffs from the guitar stem, a keyboard-driven one takes chords from the mix or "other" chroma and voices them from labels; call a section instrumental when the vocal stem sits 12-15 dB below its median for two bars, and a solo when one pitched stem carries the energy with mostly monophonic, dense notes. Rely on onsets and pitch, and do not print dynamics from transcriber velocities.

**Unverified.** No study measures Opus or AAC at 128-160 kbps against MIR features; no maintainer guidance exists on whether loudness normalisation helps Demucs or Basic Pitch; no published method for fade-out, ritardando or live-versus-studio detection was found; SponsorBlock coverage of music videos is unknown; and the current per-itag bitrates must be confirmed with `yt-dlp -F`.

## Techniques and arrangement are symbolic problems with guitar-only evidence

**Known.** Guitar technique detection is a mature but narrow line built on contour pattern mining followed by a classifier. Chen, Su and Yang's candidate-selection stage reads bends as arcs, vibrato as sinusoids, slides as staircases and hammer-ons or pull-offs as adjacent plateaus without a new attack, and their full system scores **F 87.7 for bends, 84.0 vibrato, 74.4 pull-off, 66.3 hammer-on and 57.7 slide** on clean unaccompanied solos, with candidate selection alone at 94% recall but only 15-63% precision ([Chen, Su, Yang 2015](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000119.pdf)); TENT's technique-embedded note tracking improved note-tracking F by 14.7% because bends and vibrato stopped being split into several notes ([TENT 2019](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.23)); TART's MLP classifies nine techniques at 76% overall with bends, slides and vibrato each under 100 training samples ([TART 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02597v1)). The datasets are all guitar: IDMT-SMT-Guitar is CC BY-NC-ND, Guitar-TECHS (5 h 12 min, six techniques) is CC BY 4.0, GOAT (5.9 h with DadaGP technique tokens) is research-only on request ([Fraunhofer IDMT](https://www.idmt.fraunhofer.de/en/publications/datasets/guitar.html); [arXiv 2501.03720](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03720v1); [arXiv 2509.22655](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22655v1)). SwiftF0's `segment_notes()` already yields the pitch plateaus such heuristics need ([SwiftF0](https://github.com/lars76/swift-f0)). The ukulele's signature "chunk" strum is an unpitched, snare-like percussive stroke that appears in no dataset ([Live Ukulele chunk](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/strums/chunk-strum/)). Encoding is mechanical: alphaTex has `h`, `sl`/`ss`, `b (0 4)`, `v`, `pm`, `x` and `lr`; PyGuitarPro's `NoteEffect` carries hammer, slides, bend, vibrato, palmMute and harmonics; MusicXML puts slides under `<notations>` and the rest under `<technical>`; ukulele ASCII tab is written A-E-C-G top to bottom and leaves strum direction and chunk undefined ([alphaTex note properties](https://alphatab.net/docs/alphatex/note-properties); [PyGuitarPro API](https://pyguitarpro.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pyguitarpro/api.html); [MusicXML technical](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/technical/); [Live Ukulele tab](https://liveukulele.com/tabs/how-to-read-tab/)).

Arrangement principles come from pedagogy and from guitar and piano research. Bordessa's rules are explicit: the melody "always should be higher than the harmony", on the A or E string so two harmony notes fit below, with harmony on beats 1 and 3, a chord sounded at every harmony change, and low-G preferred for richer voicings ([Live Ukulele solo arrangements](https://liveukulele.com/lessons/solo-fingerpicking-arrangements/)); slash chords get "tricky" with a high G, and one chord tone is usually dropped to fit the bass ([Live Ukulele slash chords](https://liveukulele.com/chords/ukulele-slash-chords/)). The automation lineage runs from Hori's input-output HMM over left-hand forms ([J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ipsjjip/21/2/21_264/_article/-char/en)) through Song2Guitar, which validated **index-finger movement (r = 0.55) and number of fretting fingers (r = 0.51)** as difficulty predictors across 50 rated tabs ([Song2Guitar](https://staff.aist.go.jp/m.goto/PAPER/ISMIR2017ariga.pdf)), to Terao's band-to-piano reduction, which augments by ±octave and masks notes by importance warped by a difficulty parameter so that an easier score is a subset of a harder one ([Terao ICASSP 2023](https://eita-nakamura.github.io/articles/Terao_BandToPianoScoreArrangement_ICASSP2023.pdf)). OpenUkulele hard-codes the constraint envelopes: easy mode frets 0-5, two voices, span of four frets, no barre, eighth grid; hard mode frets 0-15, four notes, span of five, barre allowed, sixteenth grid; degradation drops harmony, then bass, then everything but melody ([xin-pu/OpenUkulele](https://github.com/xin-pu/OpenUkulele)). For chord-sheet difficulty the ISMIR 2023 Chordify rubric scores seven criteria weighted 3-3-2-2-2-1-0 from chord symbols and timings, with quartile cut-offs of **under 8 easy, under 12.5 moderate, under 18 hard**, and rule-based scoring beat LSTM and GRU baselines ([ISMIR 2023 playability](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2023/paper/000086.pdf)); Fretting-Transformer's biomechanical score runs 0-18.5 ([arXiv 2506.14223](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14223v1)). Substitution practice is documented piecemeal: E major as `4447` or the triad `442` or transposed to F, Fmaj7 as `5500`, power chords interchangeable with major or minor, three-note triads when four-note shapes are hard ([Live Ukulele hard chords](https://liveukulele.com/chords/hard-chords/); [Live Ukulele power chords](https://liveukulele.com/chords/power-chords/)). music21's `ChordSymbol` yields `chordKind` and bass separately, which is what a substitution table keys on; pychord and chordparser are lighter alternatives ([music21 harmony](https://www.music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleHarmony.html); [chordparser](https://github.com/titus-ong/chordparser)).

**Recommendation.** Implement the candidate-selection stage from a SwiftF0 contour as symbolic rules (adjacent plateaus without an energy spike for hammer-on and pull-off, monotonic staircases for slides, arcs under two semitones for bends, 4-8 Hz oscillation of 20-100 cents for vibrato), treat chunk as a drum-like onset class with low pitch confidence and short decay, infer tremolo from onset density above about eight per second, and accept low precision until a second-stage classifier is trained. Carry one internal technique enum that maps one-to-one onto alphaTex and PyGuitarPro, emit chunk as a dead-note brush across all strings with a text annotation, and ship a legend with any ASCII output. Arrange by generating candidates (melody, chord tones, bass, each at ±12), ranking importance as melody, then bass on strong beats, then thirds and sevenths, then root doubling, then fifths, and letting a continuous difficulty knob choose how many survive under OpenUkulele-style hard constraints; finger with a Hori-style Viterbi whose cost is index-finger movement plus fretting-finger count. Grade chord sheets with the ISMIR 2023 rubric adapted to ukulele hard shapes (E, Bb, B, Fmaj7) and a ukulele chord-frequency table; grade melody sections with Fretting-Transformer-style transition costs. Encode substitutions as a rule table (X5 to X or Xm by key, add9 and sus to X in easy mode, sevenths kept in moderate mode, slash chords to their top chord on high-G, whole-song transposition when E, B or Bb dominate), and choose strum versus riff versus melody per section from stem activity, lead detection and note density with a minimum four-second window.

**Unverified.** No ukulele technique dataset or detection paper exists; the Mitsou multimodal dataset's class list was unreadable; no sourced description of roll or triplet strums was reachable; no ukulele chord-melody arrangement paper or difficulty model was found; and the Chordify, Moises, Yousician and Ultimate Guitar simplification rules could not be inspected.

## Evaluation needs home-made ground truth and a correction loop

**Known.** Every stage except fingering and strumming has a standard metric with fixed tolerances: mir_eval scores chords by duration-weighted accuracy across vocabularies, beats at ±70 ms with continuity variants, notes at ±50 ms onset, ±50 cents and 20% offset, multipitch at ±0.5 semitone and melody at ±50 cents ([mir_eval chord.py](https://github.com/mir-evaluation/mir_eval/blob/main/mir_eval/chord.py); [mir_eval transcription.py](https://github.com/mir-evaluation/mir_eval/blob/main/mir_eval/transcription.py)). Tablature uses TabCNN's frame-level string/fret precision, recall and the **Tablature Disambiguation Rate (TDR = correct string-fret pairs / correctly identified pitches), 0.899 for TabCNN**, implemented in amt-tools ([TabCNN](https://zenodo.org/records/3527800); [amt_tools evaluate.py](https://github.com/cwitkowitz/amt-tools/blob/master/amt_tools/evaluate.py)); 2024-2026 papers add playability proxies (chord stretch, KL divergence of the fret-string distribution, hand-span feasibility), and the only gold standard is human rating, where guitarists scored ground-truth tabs 7.45/10 and the best automatic fingering 6.04 ([arXiv 2408.05024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05024); [arXiv 2608.30854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30854)). Strumming is scored as per-direction onset F1 at 50 ms and, for readability, as pattern discontinuity per bar, which Yousician's constraints cut from 27.8% to 15.4% ([arXiv 2508.07973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07973); [arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05756)). No published threshold defines a "usable" tab.

The dataset picture forces in-house ground truth. Exhaustive searches of Zenodo, Hugging Face and GitHub found **no ukulele audio dataset with timing or tab annotations**, only a 1,960-clip isolated-chord set under CC BY-NC 4.0 ([Zenodo search](https://zenodo.org/api/records?q=ukulele&size=25); [HF ukulele chords](https://huggingface.co/datasets/FasaiRakphakdee/Basic-Chord-Ukulele-Data)). Guitar sets split by licence: GuitarSet, EGDB, EGSet12, Guitar-TECHS and Slakh are CC BY 4.0, while GOAT, MoisesDB, MAESTRO, MedleyDB and CASD are non-commercial and IDMT is NC-ND ([GuitarSet Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/3371780); [GOAT Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/15690894); [Slakh Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/4599666)). Human tabs are off limits as references: Ultimate Guitar's terms allow only personal printing and forbid reproduction, and Songsterr's documented API endpoints return 404 ([UG ToS](https://www.ultimate-guitar.com/about/tos.htm); [Songsterr help](https://www.songsterr.com/a/wa/help)). The synthesis route is open: FreePats publishes a **CC0 ukulele soundfont** with chuck and body-tap articulations, musical-artifacts lists ten more under public-domain or CC licences, SynthTab renders tabs through string-specific MIDI channels, and Murgul rendered four hours of strumming with DAWDreamer ([FreePats ukulele](https://freepats.zenvoid.org/GuitarFamily/ukulele.html); [synthtab.dev](https://synthtab.dev/)). Real takes can be labelled by EGDB's method of snapping per-string onsets to tab-expected positions, by synctoolbox's multiscale DTW between a synthetic render and the human take, and by manual fix-up in Sonic Visualiser or Tony ([arXiv 2202.09907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09907); [synctoolbox](https://github.com/meinardmueller/synctoolbox); [Tony](https://www.sonicvisualiser.org/tony/)). For regression testing, Basic Pitch asserts `assert_allclose(..., atol=1e-4)` against saved posteriorgrams on a fixture WAV, mirdata validates dataset checksums, and pytest-regressions supplies golden-file fixtures ([basic-pitch tests](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/blob/main/tests/test_inference.py); [pytest-regressions](https://github.com/ESSS/pytest-regressions)). Shipping products assume correction: Moises lets users "manually edit chords", Songsterr edits notes by typing digits with revision history, Soundslice has a sync-point editor, and the open tools (Sonic Visualiser layers, Audacity label tracks, Label Studio's read-only `predictions` that annotators convert into `annotations`) cover the research side ([Moises chord detection](https://moises.ai/features/chord-detection/); [Label Studio predictions](https://labelstud.io/guide/predictions); [Audacity labels](https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/label_tracks.html)).

**Recommendation.** Score every stage with `mir_eval.*.evaluate` and add a four-string TDR on a 4 × (frets + 1) frame matrix, reporting `majmin` alongside the lenient `mirex` vocabulary because expert annotators disagree. Build a ukulele "EGSet12" of about twelve pieces: write tabs in the ukulele tuning, render them through the FreePats soundfont with per-string channels for exact labels, record human takes to a click, transfer labels by DTW and onset snapping, and reserve two full rock mixes (the target songs) with hand-annotated beats, chords, sections and published strum patterns. Wrap it in a pytest harness that caches stems by audio hash, keeps golden `.lab`, beat, note and tab outputs per stage, enforces per-metric minimums (note F without offset at or above 0.8, TDR at or above 0.9, chord majmin in the 0.8s, strum direction F1 of 0.85 down and 0.79 up, zero chords beyond the hand-span limit) and checks posteriorgram determinism. Make the correction loop a file round-trip first (Audacity labels for chords, beats and strums; MIDI for notes) so every human fix becomes a mir_eval reference and a training example, and keep the beat grid attached to edits so timing survives.

**Unverified.** No study defines a usable-tab accuracy threshold or a "corrections needed" metric; CASD's quantitative agreement figures and Chordify's edit features could not be read; no evidence of MLflow or Weights & Biases use in the reference MIR repositories was found; and commercial ukulele sample libraries and their EULAs were unreachable.

## Windows, licences and LLMs set the engineering envelope

**Known.** The platform constraints are tighter than the first-round table implied. PyTorch 2.14.1 (30 September 2026) ships Windows wheels for Python 3.10-3.14 in cu126, cu130 and cu132 variants, and uv's PyTorch guide routes `torch` to an explicit index with platform markers ([PyPI torch](https://pypi.org/project/torch/); [uv PyTorch guide](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/guides/integration/pytorch/)); onnxruntime-gpu builds against CUDA 13 by default from 1.27 and coexists with torch only when CUDA and cuDNN majors match and `preload_dlls()` or an early `import torch` fixes DLL order ([ONNX Runtime CUDA EP](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/CUDA-ExecutionProvider.html)). Against that, Basic Pitch officially tops out at **Python 3.11** with ONNX as its Windows backend, madmom's PyPI release is from 2018 with no Windows wheels, Essentia has no Windows wheels at all, torchaudio's 2.8-2.10 migration to TorchCodec initially lacked Windows support, and Demucs' own Windows document still says torchaudio must not exceed 2.1 ([PyPI basic-pitch](https://pypi.org/project/basic-pitch/); [PyPI essentia](https://pypi.org/project/essentia/); [pytorch/audio releases](https://github.com/pytorch/audio/releases); [demucs windows.md](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs/blob/main/docs/windows.md)). yt-dlp has required an external JavaScript runtime, Deno 2.0 or newer by default, since November 2025 ([yt-dlp #15012](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/15012)). WSL2 passes the Windows driver through but offers no unified memory and only `--gpus all` ([NVIDIA WSL guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/wsl-user-guide/index.html)). Demucs needs about 7 GB of VRAM with default arguments and crashes on out-of-memory rather than falling back to CPU, so sequential model loading is the caller's job ([PyPI demucs](https://pypi.org/project/demucs/); [theDAW #205](https://github.com/gantasmo/theDAW/issues/205)); Snakemake's between-workflow cache and Prefect 3's `INPUTS`/`TASK_SOURCE` policies both support content-addressed stages, and JAMS is the established container for beat, chord and segment annotations ([Snakemake caching](https://snakemake.readthedocs.io/en/stable/executing/caching.html); [Prefect caching](https://docs.prefect.io/v3/concepts/caching); [JAMS paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000355.pdf)).

Licensing bites at the weights, not the code. madmom's models and any pickled processor are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, Sheet Sage's models are CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, Beat This! and Transkun are MIT including weights, Basic Pitch is Apache-2.0, YourMT3+ is GPL-3.0, chord-extractor and ChordSheetJS are GPL-2.0, Essentia is AGPL, alphaTab is MPL-2.0, and the best six-stem RoFormer checkpoint has "no license ... stated with the checkpoint" ([madmom_models](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom_models); [sheetsage](https://github.com/chrisdonahue/sheetsage); [lumabeat/bs-roformer-sw](https://huggingface.co/lumabeat/bs-roformer-sw)). On the output side, publishers shut OLGA down by takedown letter in 2006 on the position that even inaccurate transcriptions infringe, while conceding they were "not interested in pursuing people who are writing chords on a napkin"; Ultimate Guitar later licensed through the Harry Fox Agency; UK fair dealing covers only "limited extracts" for private study; and YouTube's terms prohibit downloading content without permission ([Wikipedia OLGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-line_Guitar_Archive); [Computerworld 2007](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1679484/publishers-no-heroes-to-aspiring-guitarists-in-ip-fight.html); [gov.uk exceptions](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright); [YouTube Terms](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms)).

LLMs have a clear place and a clear exclusion. On core perception tasks Gemini 2.5 Pro identifies chord quality at **97-100% from MIDI but 13-52% from audio**, with syncopation at 95-100% versus 20-65%, and the authors conclude current systems "do not yet 'listen' reliably from audio" ([arXiv 2510.22455](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22455v1)); MuChoMusic finds "an over-reliance on the language modality" in open audio LLMs ([arXiv 2408.01337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01337)); a ReAct agent with deterministic music21 tools beats LLM-only prompting by 5-7 points on chained score analysis ([arXiv 2601.11556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11556)). Audio input is cheap where it is useful (Gemini bills 32 tokens per second of audio, about $0.007 per 3.5-minute song on 2.5 Flash), Claude accepts no audio content block, and Claude's structured outputs guarantee schema-valid JSON with enums and `additionalProperties: false` ([Gemini audio](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/audio); [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing); [Claude structured outputs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs.md)). music21 MCP servers already expose key, harmony and form analysis as tools ([music21-mcp-server](https://github.com/brightlikethelight/music21-mcp-server)).

**Recommendation.** Standardise on Python 3.11 in a uv-locked venv with explicit cu126 or cu130 PyTorch indexes, keep onnxruntime-gpu's CUDA major aligned with torch, install Deno and ffmpeg as prerequisites, drop madmom in favour of Beat This! unless its DBN is wanted in an isolated environment, and reserve WSL2 for Essentia, Sheet Sage and Chordino. Run models sequentially with explicit unload, implement the Demucs CPU fallback yourself, key every artefact on a hash of the decoded PCM plus model checkpoint, parameters and code version, and store annotations as JAMS. Treat GPL and non-commercial components as optional, user-installed, subprocess-invoked extras; never vendor madmom, Sheet Sage or unlicensed RoFormer weights; default to local-file input with YouTube as the user's responsibility; and publish only public-domain or self-written example tabs. Use an LLM only after the audio models, through structured outputs over fixed vocabularies, for chord-spelling normalisation, ukulele-friendly substitution, section labelling from lyric and chord repetition, strum-pattern selection from a fixed list, and performance notes, validating every answer deterministically and logging model ID and schema hash into the cache key.

**Unverified.** The GPL FAQ on subprocess versus import was not fetched, so the separation argument is interpretation; madmom's NumPy 2 and Python 3.12 build status is unknown; Chordify's and Moises' publisher licences are unconfirmed; no benchmark evaluates LLMs on chord simplification, section labelling or strum selection specifically; and OpenAI's audio tokens-per-minute rate was not retrieved.

## Conclusion

The second pass changes the shape of the project more than the first did. Round one concluded that the audio models are commodities and the ukulele intelligence lives in a symbolic layer; this pass shows the symbolic layer cannot be built on top of the audio layer without a timing spine between them, because every model speaks its own clock and the beat tracker that defines the grid has no opinion about tempo continuity, metre or octave. Owning a single 44.1 kHz master, an explicit tempo map and a documented offset for each stage is not plumbing, it is the precondition for a chord-and-strum sheet whose bars land where the listener hears them. The same reasoning elevates the bass stem from an afterthought to the harmonic reference, since it is now cleaner than the vocals and the chord literature has wanted a bass observation for fifteen years.

The vocal path and the ground-truth problem together set the project's honest ambition level. Lyrics and vocal melody are deliverable only as fetch-and-align and transcribe-and-review products, and nothing can be scored until a small ukulele test set is rendered from a CC0 soundfont and recorded by hand, because no one else has made one. That is a modest week of work, and it converts most of the twelve ranked unknowns from opinions into numbers; the click-track alignment test and the bass re-scoring experiment in particular should run before any more stages are added, since they decide whether the first-round architecture needs a tempo-map stage and a different chord model at its core.
