# Replace Fixed Thresholds With Musical Priors

Across all eight weak areas of the Youkulele pipeline, the research literature and the few products that work converge on one lesson: the quantities the pipeline measures with a fixed constant (an onset threshold, a majority vote, a decay-dB cut, a chroma-entropy ceiling, a MIDI 60 register split, a 5 % energy floor, a profile-correlation margin) are measured elsewhere as scale-invariant signals decoded against a structured prior with hysteresis. The strongest evidence is Yousician's 2025 strumming system, which reaches **96.9 F1 on strum onsets from a Demucs "other" stem versus 83.0 for a librosa peak-picker** and then decodes whole one- or two-bar patterns by Viterbi search with a switch penalty that **halves spurious pattern changes (27.8 % to 15.4 %)** ([arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756v1)); nothing in it is a per-onset grid decision, and none of it predicts stroke direction. The same shape recurs everywhere: onset detectors are made loudness-independent by log-flux or whitening and thresholded against a local moving mean, not a file-wide max ([Böck, Krebs, Schedl ISMIR 2012](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2012/paper/000049.pdf)); activity detectors normalise to the song's own envelope and use two thresholds plus a minimum duration ([medleydb activation_conf.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/marl/medleydb/master/medleydb/annotate/activation_conf.py), [pyannote/segmentation](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/segmentation)); texture classifiers use the pitch tracker's own confidence and repetition structure, never chroma entropy ([Foulon et al. 2013](https://www.francoispachet.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/foulon2013classification.pdf), [Pati & Lerch 2017](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)); key estimators' dominant error is exactly the fifth confusion the pipeline hit on Badge, at 5.6–13.6 % even for the best CNN ([Korzeniowski & Widmer 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.05340)). Two areas have no good answer anywhere: cut-versus-ring stroke length (every transcription model's offset is a decay-threshold crossing, and no product prints let-ring from audio) and lead-versus-rhythm separation inside one stem (the one trained two-guitar separator leaves the second guitar at about 1 dB SDR). For those the honest move is the one the pipeline already took in 1.7 for ring flags: print the safe default and say what was not measured. Most of what does transfer needs no new dependency: librosa's `max_size`, `normalize=False`, `onset_strength_multi`, `decompose.hpss`, `autocorrelate` and `pyin`'s voiced probability are already installed; Basic Pitch (Apache-2.0, ONNX on Windows) and mir_eval (MIT) are the only pip additions the evidence supports; madmom, essentia and aubio fail the pip-on-Windows constraint. Evaluation is the other constant: every area has a published protocol (mir_eval at 50 ms, sed_eval at a fixed grid, macro-accuracy with the majority baseline, leave-one-song-out folds, pooled parameter sweeps) that converts the current ear-truth clips into numbers a constant can be chosen against without fitting one song.

## Reading guide: eight areas, five questions each, snippet claims flagged

The report follows the eight weak areas in the order the reviewers' code map lists them, and within each area answers the same five questions: what other software and research systems do, what the measurement theory behind the quantity says, what transfers to a pip-on-Windows librosa-and-torch pipeline that must stay generic across songs, what does not transfer and why, and what evaluation protocol the literature uses. Where a finding bears on a named module or constant, the code map is cited as (code_map §N); the code itself was not reviewed. Numbers are quoted from the primary source wherever the research notes reached it. Claims the notes could only obtain from a search-engine snippet or a secondary summary are marked **[snippet-only]** or **[secondary]** inline, and claims the notes themselves flagged as inferences rather than published methods are stated as such. Every citation URL that the notes contain is kept: those not needed in the argument appear in a closing "further sources" paragraph for each area so a reviewer can follow any of them.

## Area 1: Onset detection and strike density hinge on scale invariance and whole-passage grid choice

### What others do

The pipeline's detector (code_map §1: `detect_onsets`, librosa `onset_strength` with mean aggregation, hop 512, default `onset_detect` peak picking) sits on a specific, documented default. In the installed librosa 1.0.0 source, `onset_detect` applies `normalize=True`, which shifts the whole-file envelope to minimum 0 and divides by its maximum, and then runs `peak_pick` with `delta=0.07` against a moving mean of ±100 ms; at 22050 Hz and hop 512 the nominal 30 ms `pre_max` and `wait` windows evaluate to **a single 23 ms frame**, so the default will emit two peaks 46 ms apart ([librosa onset.py](https://github.com/librosa/librosa/blob/main/librosa/onset.py), [librosa util/utils.py](https://github.com/librosa/librosa/blob/main/librosa/util/utils.py)). The envelope is a rectified log-mel spectral flux; passing `max_size>1` turns it into SuperFlux, which the librosa gallery describes as "less susceptible to vibrato" ([librosa superflux example](https://librosa.org/librosa_gallery/auto_examples/plot_superflux.html)). The SuperFlux reference script's defaults are 200 fps, 24 bands per octave from 30 Hz to 17 kHz, `log(X+1)` compression, `max_bins=3`, threshold 1.1, `pre_avg` 0.15 s, `post_max` 0.05 s and a 30 ms combine window, with the paper claiming up to 60 % fewer false positives on heavy vibrato "without missing any additional events" ([CPJKU/SuperFlux SuperFlux.py](https://github.com/CPJKU/SuperFlux/blob/master/SuperFlux.py), [ajaysmurthy/SuperFlux README](https://github.com/ajaysmurthy/SuperFlux), [PHENICX entry](https://phenicx.upf.edu/node/88.html)); the companion local-group-delay variant (madmom's `complex_flux`) reports up to 50 % fewer false positives ([ISMIR 2013 paper 175](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2013/paper/000175.pdf)).

madmom offers the full ODF family (`superflux`, `complex_flux`, phase and complex-domain variants) plus CNN and RNN onset networks whose peak-picker defaults reduce to a plain 0.5 threshold with 30 ms combining; its own evaluator uses a 25 ms window and merges annotations within 30 ms ([madmom onsets docs](https://madmom.readthedocs.io/en/v0.16/modules/features/onsets.html), [madmom onsets source](https://madmom.readthedocs.io/en/v0.16/_modules/madmom/features/onsets.html), [madmom evaluation.onsets](https://madmom.readthedocs.io/en/v0.16/_modules/madmom/evaluation/onsets.html)). essentia's `OnsetDetection` ships `hfc` (default, "accurately detects percussive events"), `complex`, `complex_phase` (which "tends to over-detect percussive events"), `flux`, `melflux` and `rms`, with an `Onsets` picker whose adaptive threshold is a fraction `alpha=0.1` of the local mean ([essentia OnsetDetection](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_OnsetDetection.html), [essentia Onsets](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_Onsets.html), [essentia SuperFluxExtractor](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_SuperFluxExtractor.html)). aubio's `aubioonset` defaults to HFC at threshold 0.3 with a 20 ms minimum inter-onset interval and exposes adaptive whitening ([aubio CLI manual](https://aubio.org/manual/latest/cli.html), [aubio onset.h](https://aubio.org/doc/latest/onset_8h.html)).

The reported numbers put these in order. An independent 2024 comparison at ±50 ms gives, on GuitarSet, **SuperFlux F1 0.916, madmom CNN 0.930**, and on IDMT electric-guitar licks with bends and vibrato SuperFlux 0.884 versus CNN 0.874, with the CNN costing about 1.1 s per track against 9 ms for SuperFlux; the same paper shows vanilla complex-domain ODF collapsing to F1 0.46 on ornamented licks and plain spectral flux to 0.77 on GuitarSet because of "pseudo-onsets" from the plectrum ([Joysingh et al., arXiv 2408.13734](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13734)). Böck's 2012 benchmark over 25,966 onsets at ±25 ms ranks log-filtered spectral flux at F 80.3 (precision 88.3) against 74.5 for plain flux and 86.6 for the offline RNN ([Böck 2012](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2012/paper/000049.pdf)). On strummed guitar specifically, the ISMIR 2025 KIT system scores spectral flux at **79.5 % F1 and SuperFlux at 74.4 % on clean pickup audio, against 97.6 % for its CRNN**, and shows the classic ODFs detecting down-strums far better than up-strums (spectral flux 85.4 % down, 68.6 % up) ([arXiv 2508.07973](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973v1)). Yousician's MERT-based strum head reaches **96.9 F1 on the four-stem "other" stem, 95.4 on the six-stem guitar stem, against 83.0 for a librosa onset-peak baseline**, with separation artefacts described as "quite detrimental" and bleed from other guitar parts blamed for the guitar stem scoring lower ([arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756v1)). Basic Pitch is the only learned onset head that ships: a 16,782-parameter CNN whose `predict()` uses `onset_threshold=0.5`, `frame_threshold=0.3` and a 127.7 ms minimum note, trained on GuitarSet among others ([ar5iv 2203.09893](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.09893), [basic-pitch inference.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/inference.py), [spotify/basic-pitch README](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch)). The strum-level systems converge on the same post-rule: probability above 0.5 and one event per 40–150 ms window; KIT's earlier work pruned doubles at 150 ms and noted that "conventional note-based transcription methods cannot distinguish the direction" ([KIT 1000183784](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000183784/163349643), [KIT 1000184108](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000184108)).

### What the measurement theory says

The Böck peak-picker that librosa ports is three conditions: local maximum within `w1..w2`, above the moving mean over `w3..w4` plus δ, and at least `w5` frames since the last onset; Böck's grid search found offline optima of `w2=3, w4=1, w5=0` at 100 fps and explicitly used different averaging windows for different ODFs because "some of the ODFs have fewer peaks and hence need less averaging" ([Böck 2012](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2012/paper/000049.pdf)). The theory of the threshold is that it must be dynamic: "mean, median or combinations are commonly used to filter the ODF", and whole-signal normalisation "cannot be used in an online scenario". The volume-robustness experiment is the key result for the pipeline: attenuating inputs by up to −15 dB, log-filtered flux was best at every level because "computing the difference of two adjacent frames of the logarithmic spectrum has the effect of dividing the magnitude at frame n by that at frame n−1, resulting in the relative magnitude change rather than the absolute difference", with adaptive whitening (`P(n,k)=max(|X|, r, m·P(n−1,k))`, Böck's m=10, r=0.005) third ([Böck 2012](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2012/paper/000049.pdf)); aubio's whitening uses a 20–500 s relaxation time, and QMUL describes it as evening out "temporal and frequency variation ... in audio with big variations in dynamics" ([aubio awhitening.h](https://aubio.org/doc/0.4.7/awhitening_8h.html), [soundsoftware publication 8](https://code.soundsoftware.ac.uk/publications/8)). Neural ODFs need only "a simple thresholding" because their peaks are sharp probabilities; a signal-level ODF does not ([madmom source](https://madmom.readthedocs.io/en/v0.16/_modules/madmom/features/onsets.html)). Multi-band ODFs are the documented route to envelopes that one loud band cannot mask: `onset_strength_multi(..., channels=[...])` aggregates per band group ([librosa onset.py](https://github.com/librosa/librosa/blob/main/librosa/onset.py)), and SuperFlux's 24-band log filterbank is what Böck credits for its 88.3 % precision ([CPJKU/SuperFlux](https://github.com/CPJKU/SuperFlux/blob/master/SuperFlux.py)). The chirp-group-delay paper adds a valley-to-peak picker (`T = μ × max valley-peak distance`, μ 0.75–1) and reports that smoothing the ODF helps legacy detectors too ([arXiv 2408.13734](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13734)); the ISMIR 2022 separation-enhancement authors thresholded `onset_strength()` directly because `onset_detect()` "was hard to correctly tune" **[search-summary only; the passage is not on the abstract page]** ([arXiv 2208.12387](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.12387)).

The grid-level question (eighths versus sixteenths, code_map §1 `choose_slots_per_bar` with `ODD_SIXTEENTH_SHARE` 0.25 and `SIXTEENTH_MIN_MS` 105) has a settled theoretical answer: the tatum is a whole-passage statistic, "the time division that most highly coincides with all note onsets", chosen as "an equilibrium between (1) how well a regular grid explains the onsets, and (2) how well the onsets explain this grid" ([Gouyon 2002 posting](https://auditory.org/postings/2002/138.html)). The IOI-histogram method takes "virtually all pairs of onsets in a temporal frame", smooths the histogram, generates candidates as integer fractions of the dominant IOI and picks the one that "predicts the harmonic structure of the IOI best according to the 2-way mismatch error function" ([US patent 7,273,978](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7273978)); autocorrelating an accent signal is "comparable to building an IOI histogram, without needing to discretize the signal into discrete onsets" ([US patent 7,612,275](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7612275)). Audacity's MIR module classifies the tatum rate first and then comb-filters the ODF autocorrelation for bar divisions consistent with it ([Audacity MIR docs](https://doxy.audacityteam.org/namespace_m_i_r.html)); Klapuri, Eronen and Astola estimate tatum, tactus and measure jointly ([TUNI record](https://researchportal.tuni.fi/en/publications/automatic-estimation-of-the-meter-of-acoustic-musical-signals/), [Nokia patent](https://patents.justia.com/patent/9418643)). Cemgil names the pipeline's failure directly: "the usual approach ... [is] to quantize each onset to the nearest grid point implied by the tempo and a suitable pre-specified minimum note duration", which ignores relations between neighbouring onsets ([Cemgil et al. 2003](https://www.mcg.uva.nl/mmm-2003/papers/cdk-2/cdk-2.pdf)). Yousician avoids per-onset grid choice entirely: strum positions are continuous 0–1 within the bar, scored against 1–2 bar patterns by a two-way mismatch, and the grid level is a property of the decoded pattern ([arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756v1)).

For drum bleed, no reachable work solves it on a guitar stem. The documented strategies are choosing the stem where the separator is strong (Yousician's "other"), harmonic/percussive separation so the two onset kinds are detected separately (FitzGerald's median filters; an ISMIR 2013 rhythm study tracked both parts and the original), gating upper frequencies before onset detection (DARC, no parameters), and onset-count sanity filters on stems ([FitzGerald 2010](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.com/resources/aps-w23/papers/2010_FitzGerald_HarmonicPercussiveSep_DAFx.pdf), [ISMIR 2013 paper 165](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2013/paper/000165.pdf), [arXiv 2601.02357](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02357), [arXiv 2106.08703](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08703)). Schlüter and Böck's CNN gains were credited partly to "separate detectors for percussive and harmonic onsets" ([JKU record](https://research.jku.at/en/publications/improved-musical-onset-detection-with-convolutional-neural-networ/)), and a percussiveness detector independent of energy catches hits energy misses ([Maynooth ELE-Bob9](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/699/1/ELE-Bob9.pdf)); Holzapfel shows onset accuracy "depends on the type of instruments as well as on the style of performance" ([Holzapfel IEEE onset paper](https://www.csd.uoc.gr/~hannover/MMILab-Andre_files/HolzapfelIEEEOnset.pdf)).

### What transfers

Four changes need no new dependency and attack the reported symptoms directly. First, remove file-level normalisation: run `onset_strength` with `max_size=3` (SuperFlux) and `onset_detect(normalize=False)` with `delta` expressed in log-flux units against the moving mean, or normalise the envelope per rolling window; the notes stress that a rolling-percentile normaliser is an inference, but the moving-average threshold and whitening are cited practice. This is the literature's answer to "raising sensitivity re-normalises and breaks other sections": the pinned per-file max is what makes δ=0.07 mean "7 % of the loudest frame in the stem". Second, widen `wait` to 50–60 ms (2–3 frames at this hop, or use a finer hop), matching madmom's 30 ms combine and SuperFlux's 50 ms `post_max`, since a strum spreads over tens of milliseconds; re-tune `pre_avg/post_avg` whenever the ODF changes, as Böck did. Third, compute band-grouped flux with `onset_strength_multi` and require positive flux in k of N groups (or a product across groups) at a candidate: a strum re-excites all bands, a held distorted tone with vibrato moves energy within one; this is also a snare filter, since snare flux sits in a broad noisy band with no pitched flux. Fourth, run `librosa.decompose.hpss` on the stem and require harmonic-component flux at a candidate, implementing the "harmonic onset" half of the CNN split; and compare the same ODF on the drum stem the separator already produces, rejecting a guitar-stem onset whose drum-stem flux dominates by a fixed ratio. The notes mark the cross-stem rule as an inference from DARC and HPSS practice, not a published method, so it should be measured before it is trusted. For grid level, replace `choose_slots_per_bar`'s odd-sixteenth share with a per-section IOI histogram over all onset pairs or an autocorrelation of the unnormalised envelope (`librosa.autocorrelate` or `tempogram`), scoring eighth and sixteenth candidates by two-way mismatch so that empty sixteenth slots are an explicit penalty, and add a switch margin so a section's grid changes only when the evidence margin clears a band, mirroring Yousician's transition cost. The autocorrelation route is immune to the threshold choice because it needs no discrete onsets. Basic Pitch's onset head can serve as a second opinion with no licence friction, provided its note-level onsets are merged with the same 40–150 ms combine window; the memory notes record that it needs a Python 3.11 interpreter because TensorFlow lacks a 3.12 wheel (project memory, not a web source).

### What does not transfer, and why

madmom's CNN is the best-scoring reachable detector but its PyPI release is a **2018 source-only sdist with Python 2.7–3.7 classifiers**, Cython must be installed before `setup.py`, `np.float` aliases break import on NumPy ≥1.20, a Windows user needed Visual Studio Build Tools, and the model weights are CC BY-NC-SA ([PyPI madmom JSON](https://pypi.org/pypi/madmom/json), [CPJKU/madmom README](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom), [issue 463](https://git.tdem.in/CPJKU/madmom/issues/463), [issue 469](https://git.tdem.in/CPJKU/madmom/issues/469), [issue 485](https://git.tdem.in/CPJKU/madmom/issues/485), [issue 527](https://git.tdem.in/CPJKU/madmom/issues/527)). essentia publishes wheels only for Linux x86-64 and macOS 15+, under AGPLv3 for non-commercial use ([PyPI essentia](https://pypi.org/project/essentia/), [essentia licensing](https://essentia.upf.edu/licensing_information.html)); aubio 0.4.9 is a 2019 sdist with Windows binaries only via conda-forge ([PyPI aubio](https://pypi.org/project/aubio/), [aubio installing](https://aubio.readthedocs.io/en/latest/installing.html)). Neither 2025 strum detector released code or weights, and the Yousician guitar-stem result already shows the learned detector losing accuracy to bleed. The ISMIR 2015 technique paper warns only that onset/offset detectors "may not be robust" to distortion; no source evaluates onsets on distorted electric guitar as a condition ([ISMIR 2015 paper 119](https://ismir2015.uma.es/articles/119_Paper.pdf)), and MIREX leaderboards were unreadable (JS-rendered, TLS mismatch). The Stowell and Plumbley whitening paper itself was unreachable; the formula is Böck's restatement.

### Evaluation protocol

Use `mir_eval.onset.f_measure` (50 ms window, best-case one-to-one matching, F/P/R, returns zeros on empty input) on ear-marked onsets per section ([mir_eval onset.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mir-evaluation/mir_eval/main/mir_eval/onset.py)), merge ground-truth onsets closer than 30 ms as madmom and Böck do, count strictly (a second detection in one window is one true and one false positive), and report MIREX's extra diagnostics: false-positive rate, the share of false positives that are doubled onsets and of false negatives that are merged onsets; MIREX's dataset is 85 files including 9 plucked-string solos and 15 complex mixes, and submitters trace a P-R curve over ten values of one parameter ([MIREX 2018 Audio Onset Detection](https://music-ir.org/mirex/wiki/2018:Audio_Onset_Detection), [Böck 2012](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2012/paper/000049.pdf)). The three 2024–2025 guitar papers all use 50 ms via mir_eval; Yousician uses 70 ms for bar lines; DARC used 70/30/100 ms for onset/kick/snare ([arXiv 2408.13734](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13734), [arXiv 2508.07973](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973v1), [arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756v1), [arXiv 2601.02357](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02357)). The notes add two inferred layers that match the sheet's unit: a grid-cell score (hit/no-hit per slot per section) and a density ratio (predicted strikes per bar over annotated), because onset F-measure hides double-density errors when extras land near real strikes. The doubled and merged rates come free from `mir_eval.util.match_events`. To choose one δ and `wait` generically, sweep them across every ear-marked section of every song pooled and pick the point on the flat part of the pooled curve.

Further sources read for this area: the 2025 transfer-learning paper that re-measured madmom's shipped RNN and CNN at F1 0.849 and 0.913 while warning of possible split leakage ([arXiv 2507.04858](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04858)); the Foster and Dixon abstract on whether separated-audio onsets match stem onsets ([FosterDixon DualOnset](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/pub/2024/FosterDixon-DualOnset-Abstract.pdf)); Klangio's product positioning and the MusicRadar review noting it "performs better if you tell it what to listen for" ([Klangio at MuseHub](https://www.musehub.com/partner/klangio), [MusicRadar review](https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/klang-io-promises-to-turn-audio-into-notated-transcriptions-lead-sheets-and-guitar-tabs-but-does-it-actually-work-klang-io-transcription-studio-review)); Chordify's published pipeline, which documents no strum algorithm ([ISMIR 2015 LBD 42](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/LBD/LBD42.pdf)).

## Area 2: Pattern votes should cluster, canonicalise syncopation and penalise switching; direction stays unmeasured

### What others do

No consumer product derives a section strumming pattern from audio. Chordify's support article says "we're not able to accurately detect strumming patterns on Chordify - this is near-impossible to detect accurately with algorithms" (read through a headless browser in one note, obtained as a search snippet in another; the page returns 403 to plain fetchers) ([Chordify Support](https://support.chordify.net/hc/en-us/articles/360019489618-Does-Chordify-show-the-strumming-patterns-)); its Song Lessons patterns are curated ([Chordify: Song Lessons](https://support.chordify.net/hc/en-us/articles/33951891429917-What-are-Song-Lessons)). Moises shows chords on bars and beats with no strumming feature ([Moises blog](https://moises.ai/blog/latest/advanced-chord-detection/), [Moises features](https://moises.ai/blog/moises-news/moises-features/)); Ultimate Guitar's official tabs are made by its team and its strumming row uses arrows, small X for muted strum, big X for palm mute, ">" for accent and empty space for rest ([UG Help: Tabs](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741306-tabs), [UG Help: Strumming patterns](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6744662-strumming-patterns-how-to-read-play)); Soundslice's strum arrows are a per-note manual toggle ([Soundslice: strum directions](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/creating/notations/337/strum-directions/)); Songsterr's AI "can't see – yet? — exactly how to finger or strum strings" and often ignores palm muting ([heise](https://heise.de/-10356152)), though it hosts a human-tabbed strumming collection ([Songsterr](https://www.songsterr.com/a/wsa/strumming-patterns-tabs-a25846)). Yousician's app scores whether chords land on the beat and credits master-recording access ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/yousician-metallica-guitar-instruction-course-140027954.html)); Klangio's Guitar2Tabs has a user-selected strumming mode and its researcher authored the ISMIR 2025 direction paper ([MuseHub Guitar2Tabs](https://musehub.com/app/guitar2tabs), [KIT staff page](https://www.iiit.kit.edu/murgul.php)).

The one production-grade system is Yousician's: 931 recordings of 410 songs transcribed by experts into 924 distinct patterns, "most" one or two bars, finest subdivision a sixteenth; the observation likelihood is a two-way mismatch (each observed strum to its nearest pattern strum and each pattern strum to its nearest observed strum, both as squared distances in a Gaussian), positions are continuous 0–1 within the bar, two-measure patterns compare bars m and m+1, empty patterns cover silent bars, staying on a pattern costs 0, switching costs c1, switching time signature c1+c2 (values unpublished), and a flat prior over patterns (favouring common ones "did not help"). Results: reconstructed-strum F1 96.8 with ground-truth bar lines, 95.3 with BeatThis bars, 94.7 with post-processed bars; bar-line F1 87.2 at 70 ms; pattern discontinuity 27.8 % to 15.4 % with transition costs at no accuracy loss, lowest (11.9 %) on simplified arrangements. It does not predict direction, muted strokes or syncopation types; the companion repo holds a `downbeat.py` post-processor and eight 10 s excerpts with no licence ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05756v1), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756v1), [YousicianGit repo](https://github.com/YousicianGit/rhythmic-pattern-transcription)).

For summarising many bars into one pattern, Dixon, Gouyon and Widmer resample each bar's RMS envelope to 72 samples, run k-means with k=4 "to remove outliers", take the largest cluster and average it, and warn that Euclidean distance "treats all time points independently, so that ... peaks which almost match are penalised as heavily as peaks which are far from being aligned"; the averaged envelopes show swing (about 2:1) and syncopation directly ([ISMIR 2004 paper 165](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2004/paper/000165.pdf)). Toussaint's measures for binary rhythm strings (Hamming, swap, directed swap, chronotonic) do not require equal onset counts, and chronotonic was "the best measure" in his phylogenetic study ([ISMIR 2004 paper 134](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2004/paper/000134.pdf)); listening studies disagree on which matches perception, directed swap in one UPF study and chronotonic in a flamenco study ([MTG](https://mtg.upf.edu/node/1564), [Canadian Acoustics](https://jcaa.caa-aca.ca/index.php/jcaa/article/download/1893/1640/2030)). Ellis's eigenrhythms work stresses that bar patterns must be tempo- and downbeat-aligned before modelling ([Ellis 2005 slides](https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~dpwe/talks/musicie-2005-10.pdf)); pattern length can be read from the first peak of a bar-level self-similarity curve or from diagonals on a beat-synchronous SSM ([US patent 7842874](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7842874), [US patent 9547715](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9547715), [Nieto & Farbood MIREX 2013](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/NietoFarbood-PatDiscoMIREX2013.pdf)).

For direction, Barbancho et al. show that "all the information about the movement is contained at the very beginning of the chord waveform": they subtract a decayed copy of the previous log-spectrum frame to expose new partials, read stroke speed from the spacing of those arrivals, and classify the spectral-centroid trajectory (rising for low-to-high) with LDA or SVM, reaching **93.3 % on synthetic guitar chords with known inter-string delays (11–55 ms), 91.7 % with estimated delays (up 87.8 %, down 95.6 %), 77.5 % on real autoharp**; crucially they define UP as "from the lowest to the highest pitch note", which is the physical guitar down-stroke, so labels must be flipped ([ISMIR 2014 paper 130](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000130.pdf)). The physical ordering is uncontroversial ([MuseScore forum](https://musescore.org/en/node/301189), [US patent 8614389](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8614389)). KIT's multimodal work used audio for events and a wrist IMU for direction (F1 85 % up / 92 % down) and reports audio-only CNN and LSTM direction classifiers at only **about 72.5 % and 70 %** ([KIT 1000183784](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000183784), [KIT PDF](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000183784/163349643)); its Apache-2.0 dataset is ten examples, about five minutes, four chords, with a motion-labelled baseline of down F1 91.9 / up 84.7 and no audio-only baseline ([Klangio/KLANGIO-GST-MM-T](https://github.com/Klangio/KLANGIO-GST-MM-T)). The ISMIR 2025 CRNN (log-mel at 16 kHz, biGRU, two regression targets) reaches **F1 any/down/up of 97.6/87.9/84.9 on pickup audio and 92.8/85.5/79.0 on phone-microphone audio**, with ±6 semitone pitch-shift augmentation helping most, "down-strum F1 ... higher than up-strum F1 for all methods", no code or model released, and muted strokes left as future work ([arXiv 2508.07973](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973v1), [KIT 1000184108](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000184108)). Hexaphonic pickups make string order observable but are unavailable from a mix ([NIME 2012](https://nime.org/proc/reboursiere2012)); player-reported timbre differences between strokes are unmeasured ([Troy Grady forum](https://forum.troygrady.com/t/trapped-vs-escaped-pick-stroke-tone/10867)).

### What the measurement theory says

Three results bear on `choose_pattern`, `best_pair` and the shuffle test (code_map §2). First, the standard recipe is cluster-then-prototype, not slot-wise majority: a majority over all bars flattens syncopation precisely when the pushed bars are a large minority, producing a slot vector no bar actually played, while a medoid by mean Jaccard is k-medoids with k=1 and can be dragged to a noisy bar by one flipped slot in several bars; medoids are "less influenced by outliers" than means, which is why k-medoids is preferred in noisy settings ([arXiv 1509.00692](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.00692), [Wikipedia k-medians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-medians_clustering)). Jaccard and Hamming count a one-slot push as two errors; swap and chronotonic count it as one. Second, syncopation has a formal definition that preserves strike count: Longuet-Higgins and Lee define it as "a note onset in a position with a low weight that is not followed by an onset in the following pulse with a higher weight", and Sioros and Guedes reformulate it on the silent strong slot and define de-syncopation as shifting the preceding onset forward, with the shift type given by the difference of metrical levels and the rule that an onset cannot be shifted past another; their metrical template keeps only subdivisions of at least about 100 ms, so **the sixteenth level exists only below roughly 150 BPM** ([Sioros & Guedes 2014](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/4/23414/files/2022/01/SiorosGuedes2014.pdf)); SynPy implements seven syncopation models per bar ([SoundSoftware syncopation project](https://code.soundsoftware.ac.uk/projects/syncopation-dataset), [publication 155](https://code.soundsoftware.ac.uk/publications/155)). Swing should be estimated from autocorrelation, not snapped onsets, because "small errors in the onset detection propagate considerably into the swing ratio estimates" ([ISMIR 2015 paper 143](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/143_Paper.pdf), [DAFx 2015](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/dAxTOO3pHjYwTaMRKMs8PQ), [MPI DS swing](https://www.ds.mpg.de/swing)); chord recognisers also show systematic lateness at changes ([madmom issue 403](https://git.tdem.in/CPJKU/madmom/issues/403)). Third, no published pattern system reports a per-pattern significance test; the proxies are Yousician's discontinuity rates, Holzapfel's mutual agreement between estimators as a confidence signal ([INESC TEC listing](https://www.inesctec.pt/pt/publicacoes?page=3357), [MIREX 2012 multi-feature tracker](https://investigacion.upb.edu.co/en/publications/infograf%C3%ADa-beat-tracking-task-mirex-2012-multi-feature-beat-track/)) and Dixon's cluster size. The surrogate-data literature warns that "simple shuffle bootstraps ... can severely underestimate the distributional tails" when they destroy autocorrelation ([arXiv 1806.09963](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09963), [spynal randstats](https://spynal.readthedocs.io/en/stable/modules/randstats.html)). The within-bar shuffle null keeps strike count but destroys metrical position, so it tests "are onsets position-dependent at all", which almost any strummed section passes; it does not test "is this pattern right among close alternatives", which is where the 1.6 and 1.7 errors were.

### What transfers

The slot alphabet (struck / muted / rest) is the right vocabulary, since it matches UG's notation. Beyond that, the vote can adopt Dixon's step of discarding minority clusters before averaging (cluster bars with k≈4 on a swap or chronotonic distance, prototype the largest cluster), which keeps a noisy bar from becoming the prototype unless noise dominates; score candidate patterns against every bar with a soft mismatch and sum, letting a two-bar candidate compete on equal footing, since Yousician's 924-pattern vocabulary shows two-bar patterns are normal rather than an edge case; canonicalise with Sioros' de-syncopation rule so a pushed onset plus a silent strong slot votes as one strike on the root pattern, then vote separately on which strong slots are anticipated, which is how to decide push versus straight without inflating strike count; apply a switch penalty between adjacent sections (do not change pattern unless the margin clears a band), the remedy Yousician found more effective than a larger vote margin; and replace or supplement the shuffle test with a null that preserves structure (circular shift of each bar by one slot, or bootstrap over bars and report how often the winner changes) plus two cheap confidence signals, the score gap between the top two candidates and a minimum bar support. For fast tempi, suppress the sixteenth grid above about 150 BPM, where the 100 ms floor makes it below the perceptual subdivision limit and timing jitter prints as syncopation; and estimate a global swing ratio from autocorrelation and warp the grid before snapping, so a 2:1 off-beat eighth at 0.67 of the beat does not snap to a sixteenth slot. Muted strokes should vote with struck strokes on rhythm and separately on type, so a mute/strike disagreement does not split the vote. Barbancho's centroid-slope over the first 3–5 frames after an onset is cheap enough to compute as a confidence flag rather than a printed label.

### What does not transfer, and why

The parity rule (`direction_for_slot`, code_map §2) is the implicit baseline every paper beats; the ISMIR 2025 classic-ODF baselines that presumably assign direction by alternation score roughly 85 % down and 68 % up, which is the error profile of parity. A measured direction row would need the CRNN plus training data, and neither exists for download. Worse for this pipeline, the 11–55 ms string-arrival window that carries direction is the part most smeared by separation and masked by drums, the ISMIR 2025 drop from pickup to phone mic on clean solo recordings suggests mixed-stem accuracy would be lower still, and a four-string re-entrant ukulele breaks the "low-to-high pitch" cue because the first string struck on a down stroke is not the lowest. No paper reports direction accuracy on ukulele or on stems, and the ISMIR 2025 paper is internally inconsistent about its IMU sign convention. Yousician's transition constants and Gaussian variance are unpublished, so they must be fitted on the project's own sections; its pattern vocabulary and model are proprietary. beat_this installs on Windows with pip after torch ([beat_this README mirror](https://gitblind.noratr.app/CPJKU/beat_this)), but madmom's install story is the same as in Area 1 ([madmom install docs](https://madmom.readthedocs.io/en/latest/installation.html), [madmom issue 478](https://git.tdem.in/CPJKU/madmom/issues/478)). Mute detection is supervised on labelled solo-guitar data everywhere (IDMT-SMT-Guitar's "muted" plucking style, CC BY-NC-ND; Stefani's GPL-3.0 feature-vector classifier with Palm Mute among eight classes; TART's per-note classifier; Stefani and Turchet's embedded recogniser, which finds "individual player styles noticeably affect recognition performance") and none is validated on separated stems ([Zenodo 7544110](https://zenodo.org/records/7544110), [Fraunhofer IDMT](https://www.idmt.fraunhofer.de/en/publications/datasets/guitar.html), [ExpressiveGuitar-TechniqueClassifier](https://github.com/domenicostefani/ExpressiveGuitar-TechniqueClassifier), [EURASIP JASMP 2025](https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13636-025-00413-6), [arXiv 2510.02597](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02597)); no dataset labels ukulele chucks.

### Evaluation protocol

Score strums with mir_eval at 50 ms and bar lines at 70 ms as Yousician and KIT do ([mir_eval onset docs](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/latest/api/onset.html), [arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05756v1), [arXiv 2508.07973](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973v1)); report pattern F1 against the ear-truth bars and, as readability metrics, pattern discontinuity (changes per bar), time-signature discontinuity and measure-length discontinuity (bars differing more than 35 % from the previous), which are the published analogue of "printed two wrong patterns as certain". Report direction accuracy separately for down and up, since every method is asymmetric. Dittmar's swing tolerance (an onset triple accepted only if its span is within a factor α of the beat period) shows tolerances are expressed relative to the beat, and the notes record that no published snapping tolerance for strumming exists, only evaluation tolerances.

Further sources read for this area: the syncopation-tree framing of binary patterns ([Sioros & Guedes 2014](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/4/23414/files/2022/01/SiorosGuedes2014.pdf)); Stefani's and Reboursière's technique classes ([GitHub](https://github.com/domenicostefani/ExpressiveGuitar-TechniqueClassifier), [NIME 2012](https://nime.org/proc/reboursiere2012)).

## Area 3: Cut-versus-ring stroke length is an offset problem the whole field has stopped scoring

### What others do

Commercial tools print duration as "until the next onset, quantised to the grid". AnthemScore documents the only rule: "By default, each note ends where the next note starts", with per-note auto-stops that can be removed; its 5.0.0 notes list "Options to prevent/allow note stop times being auto-set" ([AnthemScore documentation](https://lunaverus.com/documentation), [AnthemScore version history](https://www.lunaverus.com/versionHistory)). Soundslice's "let ring" is a manual tab marking drawn as a dashed line that "doesn't affect synthetic playback", and its audio features only align existing notation ([Soundslice: let ring](https://soundslice.com/help/en/creating/tablature/121/let-ring), [Soundslice blog 290](https://www.soundslice.com/blog/290/new-auto-align-audio-with-notation/)). Moises shows chords and tabs in-app; a competitor's claim that it exports audio only is commercially interested and unconfirmed **[secondary]** ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/gear/moises-new-ai-tools/), [Songscription vs Moises](https://www.songscription.ai/blog/songscription-vs-moises)). Klangio lets users set key, tempo and time signature; a reviewer found rhythm detection "fails to identify swing, expressive timing and irregular rhythms" and no duration rule is documented ([MusicRadar review](https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/klang-io-promises-to-turn-audio-into-notated-transcriptions-lead-sheets-and-guitar-tabs-but-does-it-actually-work-klang-io-transcription-studio-review), [Guitar2Tabs App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1589660528)). Melody Scanner supports solo instruments only and says nothing about durations ([melodyscanner.com](https://melodyscanner.com/)). Melodyne, the commercial reference baseline, scored onset+offset F1 0.13 on GuitarSet and 18.40 note-with-offset F1 on MAPS ([Gardner et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03017), [Hawthorne et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11153)).

Every transcription model ends a note when a frame activation falls below a threshold. Onsets and Frames reports MAPS note F1 82.29 but **note-with-offset F1 50.22**, and motivates offsets perceptually ("a piece played with only onset information would either have to be entirely staccato or use some kind of heuristic") ([Hawthorne et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11153)); its code is archived at TensorFlow 1.15 ([Magenta O&F README](https://github.com/magenta/magenta/blob/main/magenta/models/onsets_frames_transcription/README.md)). Kong's regression model detects an offset when its offset head peaks above 0.3 or the frame output drops below 0.3, reaching MAESTRO onset+offset F1 82.47 against onset F1 96.72, and remarks only that "the waveform ... can be blurred" ([Kong et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01815)); its pip package says "Windows has not been tested" ([PyPI piano-transcription-inference](https://pypi.org/project/piano-transcription-inference/0.0.5/)). Basic Pitch tracks a note forward "until the likelihood falls below a threshold τn for longer than ... 11 frames", drops notes shorter than about 120 ms, and uses the no-offset F-measure as its headline "since the definition of offsets is less objective than onsets"; on GuitarSet it scores **Fno 0.79 but F-with-offsets 0.56** ([Bittner et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893), [spotify/basic-pitch README](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch)). MT3 scores GuitarSet onset F1 0.90 and onset+offset 0.78, collapsing to 0.32 onset F1 zero-shot, and uses a tolerance sweep to diagnose label timing ([Gardner et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03017), [magenta/mt3](https://github.com/magenta/mt3)). Omnizart reports no onset+offset score and scores 59.0 onset F1 zero-shot on GuitarSet in Riley's table ([Wu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00497), [omnizart README](https://github.com/Music-and-Culture-Technology-Lab/omnizart), [Riley et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15258)). Riley's guitar model is "evaluated on onsets only" on mostly clean guitar, and TART's zero-shot F50 of 81.35 % again omits offsets ([Riley et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15258), [Gupta et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11904)). Kehling's IDMT system defines an isolated note's offset as "the first frame after the envelope peak with less than 5 % of the peak magnitude" (−26 dB) and uses a 200 ms offset tolerance "due to smoothly decreasing note envelopes", reporting 0.98 offset F on clean isolated notes ([Kehling et al. 2014](https://www.dafx14.fau.de/papers/dafx14_christian_kehling_automatic_tablature_trans.pdf)). GuitarSet's offsets are not human labels: they come from an offset strength function combining negative spectral flux and log-RMS drop, with the first candidate after 30 ms chosen, and muted notes are excluded from the annotation ([Xi et al. 2018, GuitarSet](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/188_Paper.pdf)).

### What the measurement theory says

Three threads. The piano "offset penalty" is 14–32 F1 points and the GuitarSet one looks smaller (12 for MT3, 23 for Basic Pitch) only because GuitarSet offsets are a decay heuristic the networks can mimic. Palm or right-hand muting is a distinct, detectable timbre class with "a 'noisier' attack and a faster decay": Su, Yu and Yang's seven-tone dataset (line-in rendered through tones "which involve different levels of distortion, reverb, delay and chorus") gives muting F 68.7 % with **35 % of mutes called "normal"**, 56.1 % with a plain spectrogram ([Su, Yu, Yang 2014](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000213.pdf)); Kehling gets 93 % plucking-style accuracy from spectral-shape and envelope statistics "computed for the attack and the decay part of each note" ([Kehling et al. 2014](https://www.dafx14.fau.de/papers/dafx14_christian_kehling_automatic_tablature_trans.pdf)); Abeßer's bass work reaches 93–96 % ([Abeßer et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICASSP.2010.5495945)); TART's 160k-parameter CNN-BiLSTM reaches palm-mute F1 97.9 % on pooled datasets after an earlier version showed "distribution shift and overfitting" across datasets ([Gupta et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11904), [Gupta et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02597)); Guitar-TECHS supplies 138 palm-muted notes per player with miked amps at flat EQ ([Guitar-TECHS](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03720v1)). Staccato, by contrast, is defined by timing, not timbre: a micropause of "about 60 % of the inter-onset-interval" in piano performance research ([Bresin, KTH](https://www.speech.kth.se/music/publications/thesisrb/)), and the key-overlap ratio KOR = (next onset − this offset) / IOI turns articulation into one number per note ([arXiv 2406.08454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.08454)). The violin offset study found spectral-flux detectors "take the beginning of decay as the offset position" and fire early, while a pitch-confidence detector beat energy (F 0.654 vs 0.508) because "the decaying phase exhibits similar signal characteristics as soft onsets when 'looking reversely'"; energy was competitive only for sharp-envelope techniques ([Liang et al. 2015](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/118_Paper.pdf)).

On distortion, the literature confirms the 1.7 finding indirectly. EGDB-PG shows onset F1 falling 84.0 to 81.6 to 78.9 from low-gain to crunch to high-gain and MT3 struggling "particularly in the later stages of the ADSR envelope" ([arXiv 2504.07406](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07406)); Chen et al. warn that onset/offset methods "may not" work "in the presence of sound effects such as distortion or delay" ([Chen et al. 2015](https://ismir2015.uma.es/articles/119_Paper.pdf)). Effect-recognition work classifies effect chains at 99.2 % from 541 spectral, cepstral and harmonic features of the sustain segment ([DAFx 2010 Stein](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/snbvFS1CWb6-HVRq1z6msA), [IDMT audio effects dataset](https://www.idmt.fraunhofer.de/en/publications/datasets/audio_effects.html), [Schmitt & Schuller 2017](https://dl.gi.de/items/7bb35456-dee4-42f2-ad3c-f11ce33a0a07/full)), which is evidence that distortion rewrites the decay portion, not that any decay feature survives it. Physical sustain is defined as decay to a 30 dB drop ([Fleischer, ASA](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/137th/fleischer.html)); Herbst's study of distorted rhythm guitar is psychoacoustic, pointing to "frequency-related compression" ([Herbst 2017](https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/33249/)). The notes' inference is that clipping acts as a compressor, holding amplitude near the ceiling for most of the ring, so a dB-per-slot decay (code_map §3 `stroke_decay_db`, `RING_SECTION_DB` 5) measures the clipper's knee, not the string; the separator's time-varying noise floor further inflates any "fall to −X dB" measure on quiet tails.

### What transfers

Keeping the renderer's "until the next onset" default is itself the state of practice. If a per-stroke measurement is tried again, the literature points away from amplitude slope toward whether a pitched partial structure is still present in the slot: pyin's voiced probability or a harmonic-to-noise ratio over the slot, and spectral flatness, crest, inharmonicity or odd/even ratio trajectories over the decay segment, which change when a string is damped even while clipped amplitude is flat. This is an inference from Stein, Kehling, Su and Liang, not a tested result. The KOR framing gives one number per stroke (sounding fraction of the IOI) and lets the ear label be defined reproducibly (for example, stops before 50 % of the gap). Palm mute as a per-stroke timbre class, with a small supervised classifier on attack/decay-segmented features, is supported by the literature as feasible in torch; "cut after ringing" as a release event is not. Guitar-TECHS (CC BY 4.0) and EGFxSet (CC BY 4.0, 690 clean notes through 12 hardware effects) are usable datasets for a spike ([Guitar-TECHS](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03720v1), [Pedroza et al. ISMIR 2022 LBD](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~iran/papers/Pedroza_et_al_ISMIR_2022.pdf)).

### What does not transfer, and why

No source reports with-offset F for any model on distorted or amp-rendered electric guitar, none evaluates offsets on separated stems, and no study measures decay rate as a function of gain. GuitarSet cannot serve as cut/ring ground truth because its offsets are algorithmic. The palm-mute classifiers are trained on isolated, segmented notes with known boundaries, mostly clean; the one distortion-inclusive result shows muting leaking 35–44 % into "normal", matching the overlap the 1.7 spike found. MT3 (T5X/JAX, Colab-only, "we do not (easily) support training") and Magenta O&F (TF 1.15) do not fit the environment; Omnizart is TensorFlow with ARM caveats. Model sizes for MT3, O&F and Omnizart were not stated in fetched pages.

### Evaluation protocol

The annotation protocol is Liang's: normalise, transcribe pitches, place the offset at "the first perceived disappearance" of the pitch, use the next onset as the offset for continuous notes, replay slowed when unsure, at a cost of 1–2 minutes per note for a professional ([Liang et al. 2015](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/118_Paper.pdf)). Score with mir_eval's offset window, max(20 % of reference duration, 50 ms), or IDMT's 200 ms, with MIREX differing by greedy matching and no 50 ms minimum ([mir_eval transcription docs](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/stable/api/transcription.html)), and clip it by Liang's onset-aware rule (post-tolerance = min(Δt to next onset + 50 ms, tol)) because the conventional window overlapped the next onset's window for more than 80 % of notes at 100 ms. Inter-annotator agreement is low even on clean solo vocals: vocadito's two annotators agree at **F 0.64 with offsets and 0.74 without** ([vocadito](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05580)); a 2026 refinement paper treats 100 ms as typical human annotation error for string datasets ([Snapping Matters](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11903)). The notes therefore propose (as their own protocol, not a cited one) a three-way per-stroke label from two independent listeners on slowed stem playback with the mix for context (rings into the next stroke / stops before it / undecidable), reporting agreement first; if agreement on distorted stems is near chance, no detector can be validated, which is itself a legitimate finding. Per-section flags should be derived statistics (fraction of strokes labelled "stops"), since the 1.6 medians sitting on the threshold suggest sections are mixed.

## Area 4: Riff-versus-strum needs harmonic-domain texture cues and repetition-first discovery

### What others do

No product makes the decision automatically. Klangio's Guitar2Tabs makes the user choose "strumming mode" or "picking mode" up front, exports PDF, MIDI, Guitar Pro and MusicXML, and a user review says it "even turns the accidental background noises into tabs" ([MuseHub Guitar2Tabs](https://musehub.com/app/guitar2tabs), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1589660528)); Soundslice's transcription is human and its only automation is stems ([Soundslice transcribing](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/creating/transcribing/), [Soundslice stems](https://www.soundslice.com/blog/298/automatic-multitrack-stem-generation/)); Songsterr emits full tabs with technique errors and "does not always make the correct distinction" between guitar tracks ([heise.de](https://heise.de/-10356152)); Moises and Chordify output chords ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/gear/moises-new-ai-tools/), [Moises App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/1515796612), [GuitarChalk](https://www.guitarchalk.com/chordify-review/), [Hooktheory](https://www.hooktheory.com/blog/chordify-alternatives/), [MusicRadar on Chordify](https://www.musicradar.com/news/chordify-lyrics-live-chord-detection)), with Chordify's co-founder estimating "about 70 % to 95 %" chord accuracy in 2013 ([TNW 2013](https://thenextweb.com/apps/2013/05/02/chordify-taps-soundcloud-and-youtube-to-show-you-the-chords-to-your-favorite-songs/)); Songscription lets users edit the output ([Songscription](https://songscription.ai/guitar-tab-generator)). None gates output on confidence as the pipeline does.

Research systems that come closest to lead-versus-rhythm use the pitch tracker's own reliability. Lachambre et al. separate monophony from polyphony with the short-term mean and variance of the YIN confidence indicator ([IRIT SAMOVA](https://irit.fr/SAMOVA/site/?p=454)); Foulon, Roy and Pachet report it needs about two minutes of training and a 6.3 % global error, and their own three-mode classifier (bass, melody, chords) on isolated guitar keeps YIN pitch, YIN inharmonicity, harmonic-to-noise ratio, spectral spread, centroid and kurtosis after selection, rejects transient and silent frames, and goes from F 0.87 per frame to **0.96 after aggregating about one bar (1.5 s)**, with 0.949–0.971 leave-one-out across guitar types and a timbre-sensitivity caveat ([Foulon et al. 2013](https://www.francoispachet.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/foulon2013classification.pdf)). Pati and Lerch detect electric-guitar solos in full rock mixes with 17 timbre features, Melodia pitch and confidence, and two structural features (segment repetition count and normalised duration from msaf), reaching **macro-accuracy 78.6 %, precision 63.3 %, recall 71.8 %** after erosion/dilation and a 4 s minimum, against a majority baseline of 74.7 %; they separate solos (foreground, non-repeating) from riffs, which "rely on the use of power chords" and repeat, and note that "solos can be very similar to ... riffs or licks" ([Pati & Lerch 2017](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf), [GT Music Informatics](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/?p=320)). On symbolic tab, Régnier, Martin and Bigo reach F1 0.95 with 31 bar features and **F1 0.87 from "chords/single notes presence" alone**, define a chord as "at least two notes that are plucked simultaneously", find chords in 80 % of rhythm bars and single notes in 92 % of non-rhythm bars, and note that lead "can be alternately associated with solo parts, riffs and licks" ([Régnier et al. ISMIR 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000006.pdf)). Smaller results: a Cypriot folk mono/poly classifier at precision 0.88 / recall 0.78 **[secondary listing]** ([SCITEPRESS 2014 via Unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org/10.5220%2F0005049101440151)); a DLSU study of chroma evenness reaching about 85 % on 22 files **[snippet-only; PDF 404]** ([DLSU 2016 TPHS-10](https://www.dlsu.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/pdf/conferences/research-congress-proceedings/2016/TPHS/TPHS-10.pdf)); a kernel generative model over whole spectra ([Pattern Recognition 2019 listing](https://datalearner.com/academic/journal-papers/0031-3203/volumes-and-issues/471/paper-detail/24399)); Chen, Su and Yang's technique detector at F 74 % ([NTU record](https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/handle/123456789/636366)).

TabCNN reproduces at multi-pitch F1 0.826 and **tablature F1 0.748** on GuitarSet, falling to 0.447 on real electric tone with the augmented model recovering to 0.585 **[secondary review, unverified]** ([arXiv 2405.14679](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14679v3), [themoonlight.io review](https://themoonlight.io/review/leveraging-real-electric-guitar-tones-and-effects-to-improve-robustness-in-guitar-tablature-transcription-modeling)); its repository has no licence and targets Python 2.7 ([andywiggins/tab-cnn](https://github.com/andywiggins/tab-cnn)); Guitar-TECHS retrains it with modest gains ([arXiv 2501.03720](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03720)). FretNet estimates continuous contours grouped by string ([arXiv 2212.03023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03023v2), [amt-tools](https://clickpy.clickhouse.com/dashboard/amt-tools)); Riley's model and MT3 reach about 90 F50 ([arXiv 2402.15258](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.15258), [MT3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03017)); TART averages 81.35 % audio-to-MIDI F50, 71.8 % string-fret Tab F1 and 54.08 % end-to-end Tab F1 zero-shot ([arXiv 2609.11904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11904), [arXiv 2510.02597](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02597)). No surveyed method publishes a rule for choosing the canonical occurrence; the proxies are first occurrence or highest-salience n-gram.

### What the measurement theory says

Chroma cannot separate a power chord from a single note. A single note already deposits chroma on its fifth (third harmonic) and octave; a power chord is root plus fifth at about 3:2, and distortion adds intermodulation partials at sums and differences ([Wikipedia: Power chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_chord)); chroma folds energy into twelve classes by design and partials distort it, which is why transcription-like front ends exist ([Müller, Kurth, Clausen 2005](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.com/content/05_fau/professor/00_mueller/03_publications/2005_MuellerKurthClausen_ChromaAudioFeatures_WASPAA.pdf), [chordabra README](https://github.com/High5Apps/chordabra/blob/master/README.md)). So a chroma-entropy ceiling (code_map §4, `RIFF_ENTROPY_MAX` 0.82) cannot separate a palm-muted power-chord hook from a single-note riff (both low-entropy), while a clean single-note riff with vibrato or bends on a distorted stem can spread chroma above it; that is the observed false positive and the observed miss. Pitch-class entropy is used in symbolic work as tonal focus and in composer identification, never for mono-versus-poly ([Jazz Transformer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.01307), [Kaliakatsos-Papakostas 2011](https://www.epitropakis.co.uk/sites/default/files/pubs/KaliakatsosEV2011_MCM.pdf)). The literature discriminates in the harmonic domain: YIN/pyin confidence and inharmonicity, odd/even partial energy, polyphony-count heads inside multi-pitch nets ([arXiv 2202.09198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.09198)), comb-filter polyphony estimation at 6 % mean error ([SCITEPRESS 2005](https://scitepress.org/Papers/2005/11750/pdf/index.html)). Pati's and Régnier's definitions both put power-chord riffs in the rhythm class. One-pitch riffs are a repetition phenomenon, not a pitch-change one; no surveyed method uses an interval-change ratio as a texture cue, so the pitch-change share rule (`PITCH_CHANGE_MIN` 0.26) has no published counterpart, and Régnier's data say non-rhythm bars are characterised by single notes, not pitch variety. Pattern-discovery methods treat repetition as the primary evidence of a riff; the pipeline treats it only as a post-hoc tab gate.

### What transfers

The project already runs pyin per onset, and its voiced probability is the Lachambre/Foulon feature, currently unused as a texture signal; aggregating it over about one bar with a minimum-duration rule follows Foulon and Pati, and computing the single-note share only on steady-state frames after onsets follows Foulon's transient rejection. A dyad-versus-note test should look at pyin voiced probability per onset (low for dyads), inharmonicity, or the odd/even partial-energy ratio of the candidate fundamental, all computable on CPU from the existing pass. Basic Pitch is the drop-in polyphonic replacement that fits the constraints and exposes min/max frequency limits for a guitar stem. Reversing the order of the riff test (find bar-length repeats on beat-synchronous chroma or on the onset/pitch sequence first, then ask whether the repeated figure is single-note) would catch one-pitch riffs, whose repetition is strong even when pitch-change share is near zero, and reject non-repeating strums regardless of entropy; a chroma-level, transposition-free cosine agreement between repeats is the measure the audio pattern literature uses, and is a softer test than "repeats agree > 0.70" against pyin-named notes. If power-chord riffs are to be printed as tab, they need a separate "dyad riff" class rather than being squeezed through the single-note rule.

### What does not transfer, and why

The gate thresholds have no published counterpart, and the "named share > 0.6" gate demands from pyin on a stem what the best GuitarSet transcribers barely achieve on clean studio audio (about 10 % of notes wrong at onset level; tab accuracy 0.45–0.6 on real electric tone), which is consistent with one riff in eleven songs passing. No paper evaluates a lead-versus-rhythm classifier on separated stems; all audio work is isolated guitar or full mixes, so stem-bleed effects are unquantified. TabCNN has no licence, TART has no code, FretNet's code was not located, Melodia requires essentia, and CREPE requires TensorFlow. Lachambre's own accuracy table and Basic Pitch's current-model GuitarSet numbers were not retrievable.

### Evaluation protocol

Three layers, diagnosed separately. Section flags: precision, recall, macro-accuracy with the majority-class baseline stated and a minimum segment length, as Pati does; F1 and leave-one-piece-out because "bars can highly repeat", as Régnier does. Notes: mir_eval transcription with `offset_ratio=None`, onset 50 ms, pitch 50 cents, as every guitar paper does ([mir_eval transcription API](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/stable/api/transcription.html)). Tab: string/fret F1 or TDR on the ukulele mapping, only after the note layer passes. GuitarSet's comp/solo halves give a free labelled mono-versus-poly test for the texture rules before touching the project's songs, and Pati's GSD dataset gives public solo annotations ([GuitarSoloDetection dataset](https://github.com/ashispati/GuitarSoloDetection/tree/master/Dataset)); mir_eval's pattern module gives establishment, occurrence and three-layer scores if the riff is treated as a repeated pattern ([mir_eval pattern API](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/stable/api/pattern.html)). No public dataset labels riff sections in audio.

## Area 5: Two parts in one stem are assigned after separation, not re-separated; bass bleed is a least-squares fit

### What others do

Commercially, Moises Pro claims lead/rhythm separation with no metrics ([Moises guitar page](https://moises.ai/made-for/guitar-backing-tracks/)); LALAL.AI merged acoustic and electric into one "Guitars" stem because a model "changing its mind midway" split one performance across two stems, claiming roughly 20 % fewer failures **[vendor claim, unverified]** ([LALAL.AI Andromeda](https://www.lalal.ai/blog/andromeda-supports-guitars-updates-piano/), [LALAL.AI Perseus](https://www.lalal.ai/blog/lalalai-perseus-supports-piano-guitars/amp/), [Mixdown](https://mixdownmag.com.au/news/lalal-ai-brings-six-stem-separation-offline-and-inside-your-daw-with-major-vst-plugin-update/)); AudioShake sells one electric-guitar stem ([AudioShake](https://www.audioshake.ai/audioshake-models/electric-guitar-separation)). String-level separation exists ([Bastas et al. ### What the measurement theory says

Voice separation rests on four principles (monophonic voices, all voices sound together at least once, pitch proximity, no crossing), and Chew and Wu's contig mapping segments where the simultaneous-note count changes, orders fragments by pitch and links them by minimum pitch distance ([Guiomard-Kagan et al. ISMIR 2015](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/180_Paper.pdf), [Chew & Wu VoSA](https://infolab.usc.edu/imsc/research/project/vosa/vosa.pdf)). On a pop MIDI corpus the trivial baseline, one reference pitch per voice estimated from the fullest segments with each note going to the nearest, scores **89.5 % note precision, beating contig mapping (84.6 %)**; contig creation alone is 99.4 % correct; "none of the algorithms successfully handles the voice crossing"; and a file where two voices share notes drops to about 50 %. Huron's pitch-proximity principle explicitly excepts octave leaps in the bass ([SAGE encyclopedia](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/music-in-the-social-and-behavioral-sciences/chpt/voice-leading-rules)); other families allow chords within streams or learn from data ([Kilian & Hoos 2002](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~hoos/Publ/KilHoo02.pdf), [Gray & Bunescu](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.03028), [VISA](https://www.academia.edu/92253245/Visa_The_Voice_Integration_Segregation_Algorithm), [Jordanous paper](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/42367/), [Jordanous code](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/42883), [de Valk & Weyde 2018](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/304_Paper.pdf), [Cluster and Separate](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.21030)). The only maintained Python implementation is partitura's `estimate_voices` (contig mapping, with known rough edges including a `Contig.duration` that computes `offset − offset`) ([partitura voice_separation](https://partitura.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/partitura/musicanalysis/voice_separation.html), [partitura paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13144)). The spike's MIDI 60 split (code_map §5) is literally this baseline with the per-song reference-pitch estimate removed.

Register is the weakest cue on record: Peterschmitt's pitch-mismatch solo detector "did not give reliable results for instruments such as piano and guitar"; predominant pitch alone scored below timbre features for solo detection (69.5 % vs 74.3 % macro) because it "contain[s] no information about which instrument is actually contributing" ([Pati & Lerch 2017](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2017/06/Pati_Lerch_2017_A-Dataset-and-Method-for-Electric-Guitar-Solo-Detection-in-Rock-Music.pdf)). NMF with pitch- or instrument-indexed bases is the principled version, and even there "overlapping harmonics make separation hard" ([Bay et al. 2012](https://www.mit.edu/~paris/pubs/bay-ismir2012.pdf), [Bryan et al. 2013](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2013/paper/000188.pdf), [Grindlay & Ellis 2009](https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~grindlay/pubs/WASPAA_2009.pdf)); a Japanese NMF lead-guitar paper was not read ([IPSJ record](https://ipsj.ixsq.nii.ac.jp/record/98456/files/IPSJ-MUS14102016.pdf)).

For bleed, the multitrack crosstalk literature gives a recipe: Seipel and Lerch model one track's magnitude spectrogram as the clean part plus a weighted sum of the other tracks, fit the weights λ by gradient descent on about 85 ms frames, clip negatives to zero and subtract; the estimated λ matrix correlates **0.957 with truth at −12 dB crosstalk and 0.937 at −18 dB but only 0.678 at −6 dB**, with overlapping-range instruments and tutti sections harder and sparse percussion yielding harsh subtraction ([Seipel & Lerch 2018](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/wp-content_nondefault/uploads/2018/06/Seipel-and-Lerch-2018-Multi-Track-Crosstalk-Reduction.pdf)); related interference-reduction work estimates interference matrices ([KAMIR](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.com/content/05_fau/professor/00_mueller/03_publications/2015_PraetzlichBittnerLiutkusMueller_KAMIR_ICASSP.pdf), [Di Carlo et al.](https://hal-univ-tlse3.archives-ouvertes.fr/INRIA/hal-01713889v1), [IRMR](https://github.com/its-rajesh/IRMR), [Tampere thesis](https://trepo.tuni.fi/handle/10024/234175)). Instrument activity detection at 1 s is established ([Gururani et al. ISMIR 2018](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf), [anthology record](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/2018.ismir_conference-2018.75)); OpenMIC ships VGGish features and a random-forest baseline, with PaSST and DyMN-L at 0.84–0.86 mAP ([cosmir/openmic-2018](https://github.com/cosmir/openmic-2018), [Zenodo 1432913](https://zenodo.org/records/1432913), [benchmark listing](https://vercel.hyper.ai/en/sota/tasks/instrument-recognition/benchmark/instrument-recognition-on-openmic-2018)); activity-gated scoring takes medians only over active frames ([Spheres dataset](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21247)). MoisesDB records per-track `has_bleed` flags by hand ([moises-db track.py](https://github.com/moises-ai/moises-db/blob/main/moisesdb/track.py), [moises-ai/moises-db](https://github.com/moises-ai/moises-db)).

### What transfers

Three generic moves. Replace the fitted MIDI 60 constant with per-song reference pitches estimated from the maximal-polyphony segments, the ISMIR 2015 baseline's own rule, and use contig creation (segmenting by simultaneous-note count, the robust 99.4 % part) to find two-part sections, linking by pitch proximity only within them; this assigns detected notes to parts rather than re-separating audio, which GuitarDuets shows is where the gains are. Fit λ_bass by non-negative least squares of the guitar-stem magnitude spectrogram on the bass-stem spectrogram at about 85 ms frames, possibly restricted below about 250 Hz, and treat the fraction of guitar-stem energy explained by λ_bass × bass as a per-section bleed score: the separator's outputs are exactly the time-aligned, delay-free multitrack case Seipel and Lerch assume. Their −6 dB warning cuts both ways: when the stem *is* the bass melody (the Badge case) the weights are unreliable, but a very high explained-energy fraction is itself the signal that the stem has no independent guitar content. Add repetition structure as the cue on top of register (riffs and strums repeat across bars; solos do not), and combine activity ("no guitar in this second") with bleed ("λ_bass explains most of the stem") into a "stem is bass, skip riff test" rule that depends on no pitch threshold. The notes mark the application of cross-stem correlation to separator outputs as an inference; it has been published only for microphone multitracks.

### What does not transfer, and why

Re-separating two guitars from one stem is not an option without training: the task-trained model leaves the weak part near 1 dB, and no open model is trained to put lead and rhythm on different outputs, since every open "guitar" target is the MoisesDB stem (acoustic plus clean plus distorted summed). The stronger RoFormer family needs the ZFTurbo or audio-separator inference stack and checkpoints of about 640 MB. Essentia's TensorFlow models are Linux and macOS only ([Essentia machine learning](https://essentia.upf.edu/machine_learning.html)); OpenMIC is not a ready classifier, so a PaSST-style tagger fine-tuned on OpenMIC is the realistic Windows route if activity detection is wanted. MoisesDB is CC BY-NC-SA. Contig mapping proper needs polyphony counts and durations that audio multi-pitch gives unreliably, which is why GuitarDuets saw little gain from estimated notes. No source quantifies bass bleed into the Demucs guitar stem; the MVSep numbers cannot be mapped to MoisesDB; no 2024 challenge with a guitar stem exists.

### Evaluation protocol

Three cheap, standard measurements: per-part note-level onset F with mir_eval (50 ms, 50 cents, `offset_ratio=None`) ([mir_eval transcription API](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/latest/api/transcription.html)); voice-assignment metrics from the symbolic literature, note precision (needs a voice correspondence, "deeply penalize[s]" one mid-piece split) and transition precision/recall (no label matching needed), plus entropy-based over/under-segmentation; and for any audio split, BSSEval via museval ([sigsep-mus-eval](https://github.com/sigsep/sigsep-mus-eval)) on synthetic mixtures of GuitarSet comp plus solo from the same player and lead sheet (CC BY 4.0, 8.2 GB) ([GuitarSet site](https://guitarset.weebly.com/), [Zenodo 3371780](https://zenodo.org/records/3371780)), with the GuitarDuets caveat about same-timbre SDR. Test bleed the SDX way: inject the bass stem at −7 to −12 dB after a random low- or band-pass, and at 0 dB with the guitar removed for the "stem is the bass" case, and check that the bleed score and the riff test respond. For the project's actual question (do the printed strokes follow the strum, not the lead?), the transition metric is the right one: ear-truth strum onsets as the reference voice, detected onsets assigned to strum as the estimate, onset precision/recall at 50 ms per section plus the share wrongly pulled from the lead. Report macro-accuracy per song with song-level folds, as Pati and Lerch do, whose GSD annotations are public ([GSD dataset repo](https://github.com/ashispati/GuitarSoloDetection)). Related scores: supervised GuitarSet F50 of 89.7 and TART's 0.70–0.84 are comparable only within the same split ([arXiv 2402.15258](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.15258), [arXiv 2510.02597](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02597), [FretNet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.03023)).

## Area 6: Rests and activity want a song-relative reference, hysteresis and max-pooling, not a mix-ratio floor

### What others do

Published instrument activity detection works at 1 s or coarser and derives its own ground truth from stem energy through a logistic. Gururani, Summers and Lerch normalise audio by RMS, chunk to 1 s, aggregate MedleyDB activation confidence by **maximum** over the window "so that instruments [have] a large activation value in the snippet even if they were active for a small period of time, as opposed to a value close to 0 if the mean was chosen", binarise at 0.5, exclude multitracks with bleed, and reach mean AUC of 77.5 (CNN/CRNN at 1 s); a fixed 0.5 threshold gave zero detections for some classes, so per-class thresholds (0.02–0.55) were tuned and found to correlate 0.9 with class frequency; clean electric guitar was among the weakest classes ([Gururani et al. 2018](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf)). Hung and Yang tune per-instrument thresholds on a 0.01–0.99 grid ([Hung & Yang 2018](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1806.09587)); Hung and Lerch gate separation with frame-level activity and find that gaps "of a few milliseconds" are audible and that a median filter "generally increases performance across all instruments", noting that "Piano, Electrical Guitar, and Bass tend to have lower detection rates" ([Hung & Lerch 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00616)). OpenMIC's authors concede no dataset gives "at each instant ... full information about all active instruments" ([Humphrey, Durand, McFee 2018](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2018/paper/000248.pdf)); a 2025 minority-instrument study scores individual instruments at micro-F1 0.41–0.45 ([arXiv 2506.21167](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21167v1)); joint activity and separation "remains unexplored" ([ISCA archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2018/kumar18c_interspeech.html)); the Cocktail Fork detector median-filters (size 5 on about 93 ms frames) and thresholds at 0.5, scoring segment-based F 0.97 for music at 1 s but only 0.77–0.82 event-based ([Petermann et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07327)).

Separation toolchains ship no "empty stem" gate. museval raises on silent references, sets silent frames to NaN and uses 2 s windows with 1.5 s hop ([museval metrics.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sigsep/sigsep-mus-eval/master/museval/metrics.py), [sigsep-mus-eval README](https://github.com/sigsep/sigsep-mus-eval)); Wave-U-Net notes SDR "is undefined" in silence ([Stoller et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.03185)); others filter zero stems by design ([arXiv 2412.06965](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.06965)) or zero near-silent 1 s windows below 0.01 absolute amplitude ([arXiv 2503.07352](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07352)); a real-time DSP paper reports silent frames distorting aggregate SDR **[per search summary; verify in PDF]** ([arXiv 2609.12201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12201)). MVSEP's Bleedless and Fullness metrics penalise positive and negative mel/dB spectrogram differences ([MVSEP FAQ](https://mvsep.com/en/faq)); leakage between real stems is detected by amplitude or spectral similarity between stems ([arXiv 2609.13881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13881)); Parry and Essa argue source inactivity patterns are themselves a separation cue ([DAFx archive](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/APiD_NX49sgBq-Ql6Z9Dsg)); a third-party Demucs guide repeats the "okay guitar, bleeding piano" description **[third-party blog]** ([aireiter.com](https://aireiter.com/blog/demucs-stem-separation-models-flags-cost)). Among transcription tools, only Basic Pitch documents silence logic (onset 0.5, frame 0.3, 127.7 ms minimum note) ([basic_pitch inference.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/inference.py), [spotify/basic-pitch README](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch)); chord tools use an explicit "N" class on the beat grid ([madmom chords docs](https://madmom.readthedocs.io/en/v0.16/modules/features/chords.html), [autochord on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/autochord/0.1.2)); Chordify fills uncertain beats by harmony rules ([Chordify LBD](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/LBD/LBD42.pdf)); Soundslice rests are manual objects ([Soundslice rests](https://soundslice.com/help/en/creating/notations/318/rests), [Soundslice transcribing](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/creating/transcribing/)); Finale offers "Minimize Number of Rests" so a slightly short note does not spawn a stray rest ([Finale quantization](https://usermanuals.finalemusic.com/FinaleWin/Content/Finale/QUANTMORESETTINGS.htm)).

### What the measurement theory says

Every mature energy detector normalises to the signal itself and uses hysteresis or hold time. MedleyDB's reference implementation uses a Hann 4096 window at hop 2048, an envelope follower, normalisation of each stem by the **summed-stem** peak envelope scaled by the stem count, an absolute floor of 0.01 (−40 dBFS) below which energies are zeroed, a zero-phase Butterworth low-pass at normalised 0.075 (about 0.8 Hz), and a logistic with **θ = 0.15 and λ = 20** ([medleydb activation_conf.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/marl/medleydb/master/medleydb/annotate/activation_conf.py)). librosa's `effects.split` compares frame RMS in dB to the signal's own peak (`top_db=60`, `ref=np.max`) ([librosa effects.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/effects.py)); essentia's silence thresholds are absolute (−60 dB) ([StartStopSilence](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_StartStopSilence.html), [SilenceRate](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_SilenceRate.html)). pyannote's VAD exposes separate onset and offset thresholds and minimum durations, tuned to 0.684/0.577 with min_on 0.181 s and min_off 0.037 s on AMI and 0.767/0.377 on DIHARD3 ([pyannote/segmentation](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/segmentation)); sound-event detection's double threshold (φ_hi 0.75, φ_low 0.2) extends confident detections to neighbours while probability stays above the low value, proposed because a median filter "raised the overall score mainly by favoring long clips at the expense of short ones" ([Dinkel & Yu 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.03841), [arXiv 2101.07687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.07687), [arXiv 2208.09201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.09201)). Speech VAD hangover bridges 40–300 ms of inactivity and shortens adaptively when the following period is "clearly inactive", comparing against a running background estimate rather than a fixed threshold ([US 7,180,892](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7180892)); energy-only VAD "works reasonably well ... where the SNR is good but not for low SNR" ([US 10,607,633](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10607633)); bioacoustic detectors apply a minimum duration after hold-time merging ([ohun energy_detector](https://docs.ropensci.org/ohun/reference/energy_detector.html)). For tail versus new attack, the standard answer is an ODF relative to its own local average: madmom's docstring says to set `pre_avg` and `post_avg` to zero only for activations "that don't depend on signal level, such as neural network outputs" ([madmom onsets.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CPJKU/madmom/main/madmom/features/onsets.py)); essentia's SuperFluxPeaks thresholds as a ratio to the novelty average ([SuperFluxPeaks](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_SuperFluxPeaks.html)); aubionotes suggests 0.5 "in case of over-detections" with a 30 ms minimum interval ([aubionotes man page](https://www.mankier.com/1/aubionotes)); phase-based ODFs are "more robust to steady-state signals" ([yup onsets docs](https://yup.readthedocs.io/en/latest/dsp/onsets.html)); plucked-string onsets are "characterized by high energy across the frequency range" ([arXiv 2408.13734](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13734v1)); on pickup audio the CD-ODF reaches recall 98.15 % at precision 68.50 %, the profile of a detector that fires on tails ([arXiv 2508.07973](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07973)); and `librosa.decompose.hpss` (kernel 31, power 2) splits sustaining tails from attacks ([librosa decompose.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/librosa/librosa/main/librosa/decompose.py)).

Mapped to code_map §6 (`REST_RATIO_MIN` 0.05 against mix RMS, `REST_LOW_SHARE_MIN` 0.005, `SECTION_MIN_RATIO` 0.10): the 5 % mix-RMS floor is an uncalibrated constant relative to the literature's practice, and its reference moves with the drums and vocals in that bar, so the same quiet guitar part scores differently in a loud chorus and a sparse verse. The three failure modes in the notes map cleanly: a bar-mean RMS under-weights a one-beat pickup (0.024 is consistent with one quiet beat in four) where the literature max-pools; bars at 0.050 and 0.062 flicker because there is one threshold where the literature uses two separated by a factor of 1.2–3.75; and carried-over tails have onsets but no new strokes because a global ODF threshold is the wrong reference, where the literature uses `pre_avg` relative to the immediately preceding context.

### What transfers

Express the rest decision in dB relative to the stem's own song-level statistic (for example its 90th-percentile bar RMS, as `top_db` does against peak) rather than the per-bar mix; add a hysteresis pair (enter rest below about 0.035, leave only above about 0.07, if the current ratio scale is kept) so borderline bars inherit their neighbours' state; set `min_duration_off` (a lone resting bar inside a played phrase is probably a tail or a quiet bar) and `min_duration_on` (a lone playing bar inside a rest needs onset evidence) as independent knobs in bars, as pyannote's tuned values show they are; max-pool activity within the bar rather than averaging; and decide each bar from two features, per-bar percussive energy or flux peaks above the local average and per-bar harmonic energy, so that tail-only bars (harmonic energy, no peaks) print a tie or let-ring rather than strokes or a rest, quiet pickup bars (a flux peak relative to preceding silence even at 0.024) print strokes, and bleed-only bars (neither) rest. Estimate the stem's bleed floor from bars the onset detector marks attack-free and express the threshold as dB above that floor, the VAD background-estimate pattern. A spectral-similarity check between the guitar stem's bar spectrum and the "other" stem's (high similarity at low level is bleed, low similarity is a real but quiet part) is the generic form of MVSEP's and the leakage paper's measure. Look-behind and look-ahead of about a beat across the bar line catch strokes that land on the boundary.

### What does not transfer, and why

The DNN activity detectors are 1 s or coarser, trained on hundreds of multitracks, still weakest on guitar, and the pretrained ecosystem is awkward on Windows (Essentia TF models Linux/macOS only; OpenMIC is features plus labels). No published RMS-to-mix ratio exists for "stem is bleed only"; all found thresholds are absolute or relative to the signal's own peak or sum. The literature's activity truth is itself energy-derived, so ear truth is the stricter standard and no paper evaluates per-bar rests for a separated stem against listening. No source quantifies how long a strummed chord's tail stays above typical bleed levels, nor gives hysteresis ratios for music stems; the ratios above are speech and SED values.

### Evaluation protocol

The v1.7 record (51 resting bars, 7 disputed stretches, one wrong) is a segment-based evaluation with one-bar resolution; report it as per-bar P/R/F with sed_eval's error-rate decomposition (deletions = missed playing bars, insertions = false rests), using `time_resolution` set to a bar and the 0.2 s collar for event-based variants ([sed_eval docs](https://tut-arg.github.io/sed_eval/sound_event.html)), pooled across songs as Forman and Scholz recommend rather than averaged per fold. Choose the ratio and hysteresis pair by sweeping (for example 0.02–0.10) on all songs pooled, plotting per-bar P/R or AUC, picking the point on the flat part, and confirming leave-one-song-out that no constant helps one song only; Gururani's 0.9 correlation between tuned thresholds and class priors is the formal version of the overfit risk, so report the operating point with the pooled curve and check that the 0.050 and 0.062 bars sit on its flat part. Weight misses and false rests explicitly rather than optimising F alone, since a mis-rested pickup bar is a deletion that sed_eval penalises like an insertion. DCASE practice tunes threshold and median window per class on a validation grid **[secondary]** ([scrapbox note](https://scrapbox.io/main-custard/音響イベント検出におけるメディアンフィルタ)); OpenMIC reports accuracy relative to the class bias point ([Humphrey et al. 2018](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2018/paper/000248.pdf)).

## Area 7: Key errors are fifths and relatives by design; metadata needs an evidence order with uploader last

### What others do

Scores use mir_eval's weighted key metric (correct 1.0, fifth 0.5, relative 0.3, parallel 0.2) ([mir_eval key docs](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/latest/api/key.html)). Essentia's `Key` offers nine profiles (diatonic, krumhansl "works generally well for pop", temperley, shaath, noland, edma, edmm, braw and the default bgate), a `strength` output and a `MajMin` third class for ambiguous tracks ([Essentia Key reference](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/streaming_Key.html)); but its Python bindings "are not supported yet" on Windows ([Essentia installing](https://essentia.upf.edu/installing.html)). On GiantSteps (604 EDM previews) the EDM profiles score 65.6–70.1 weighted and the Temperley-style EDMT 44.6; on Billboard EDMA scores 78.7 (70.8 correct, 11.8 fifth) while EDMM collapses to 28.9 with 42.4 % parallel errors ([Korzeniowski & Widmer 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1706.02921), [GiantSteps repo](https://github.com/GiantSteps/giantsteps-key-dataset), [JKU page](https://www.cp.jku.at/datasets/giantsteps/)); bgate on the KeyFinder pop set scores 72.4 (65.0 correct, 8.6 fifth, 6.5 relative) ([Korzeniowski & Widmer 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.05340)); a plain Krumhansl matcher scores 53.4 % MIREX on FMAKv2 ([S-KEY 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12907v2)). Temperley's 1999 revision adds segment-wise binary presence and a penalty for changing key ([Temperley 1999](https://music.informatics.indiana.edu/courses/I546/pdf/temperley.pdf)); Albrecht and Shanahan found most profiles "more accurate on major-mode works than on minor-mode works" ([University of Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-use-of-large-corpora-to/9984446432402771)); a signature-of-fifths method beat correlational profiles by 9.27 points on Bach, Chopin and Beatles ([DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/1b116c5c9ce14567b8416f3ea4df7910)).

madmom's CNN ensemble returns a 24-way probability vector ([madmom key docs](https://madmom.readthedocs.io/en/v0.16.1/modules/features/key.html)) and its paper reports **Billboard 85.1 weighted (79.9 correct, 5.6 fifth, 4.2 relative, 6.2 parallel), Isophonics 82.5, Rock 74.3**, with misclassified Rock excerpts having a median length of 51 s against 131 s for correct ones ([Korzeniowski & Widmer 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.05340)); the 2017 predecessor trained on Billboard drops to 57.3 on GiantSteps with wrong-mode errors dominant ([Korzeniowski & Widmer 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1706.02921)). An independent 2025 re-measurement puts the shipped model at 73.1 % on FMAKv2, 71.0 % on GiantSteps and 79.3 % mode accuracy, with S-KEY's self-supervised model matching it and both swinging from above 90 % on country to below 50 % on blues ([S-KEY 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12907v2)). Other deep models: Schreiber and Müller's directional CNNs at 58.5 % plain accuracy on GiantSteps ([Schreiber & Müller 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.10839)); InceptionKeyNet at 75.68 % with extra data, MIT code with no weights ([paperswithcode](https://astro.paperswithcode.com/paper/deeper-convolutional-neural-networks-and), [ISMIR 2021 PDF](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000004.pdf), [inceptionkeynet repo](https://github.com/stefan-baumann/inceptionkeynet)); STONE and KeyMyna ([STONE](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07408v1), [arXiv 2604.10021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10021)); foundation-model transfer ([arXiv 2411.19371](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.19371)). madmom's install is the same 2018 sdist story ([piwheels madmom](https://www.piwheels.org/project/madmom), [madmom issue #463](https://git.tdem.in/CPJKU/madmom/issues/463), [madmom issue #478](https://git.tdem.in/CPJKU/madmom/issues/478)). No paper reports a calibrated abstain threshold.

For metadata, yt-dlp fills `track`, `artists`, `album` and `release_year` from YouTube's "Music in this video" rows or an auto-generated description, and its extractor sets `multiple_songs = True` and **skips Album, Artist and Song entirely** when more than one song is credited; the embedding map falls through artist → artists → creator → creators → uploader → uploader_id, and `--parse-metadata "title:%(artist)s - %(title)s"` is the documented title-splitting path ([yt-dlp README](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/README.md), [yt_dlp youtube/_video.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/_video.py), [youtube-dl commit](https://gitdab.com/w/youtube-dl/commit/92bc97d398cb66e4968070f9d73f02a367193c2b), [yt-dlp #8917 patch](https://git.myzel394.app/Myzel394/yt-dlp/commit/1732eccc0a40256e076bf0435a29f0f1d8419280.patch)); ytmusicapi exposes "Oasis - Topic" style authors and `musicVideoType` ([ytmusicapi reference](https://ytmusicapi.readthedocs.io/en/0.19.0/reference.html)). AcoustID is free for non-commercial use at 3 requests per second, needs a client key, duration and fingerprint, and returns scored results ([AcoustID web service](https://acoustid.org/webservice)); pyacoustid 1.3.1 (April 2026, MIT, Python ≥3.10) rate-limits itself, caps decoding at 120 s and needs `fpcalc.exe` or the Chromaprint DLL ([pyacoustid on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pyacoustid/), [beets chroma plugin](https://docs.beets.io/en/stable/plugins/chroma.html)); MusicBrainz core data is CC0 and requires a descriptive User-Agent at about 1 request per second ([MusicBrainz rate limiting](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_API/Rate_Limiting), [MusicBrainz data licence](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/About/Data_License)). shazamio is a reverse-engineered client (MIT, Python ≥3.10) whose signature "uses the first 12 seconds of audio" ([ShazamIO repo](https://github.com/shazamio/ShazamIO), [shazamio on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/shazamio/), [apis.io Shazam limits](https://apis.io/rate-limits/shazam/shazam-rate-limits/)). Title parsing: `youtube-title-parse` (last pushed 2022) and npm's `get-artist-title` with a `defaultArtist` option because the channel name "might be relevant" ([youtube-title-parse on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/youtube-title-parse/), [ecosyste.ms record](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Flttkgp%2Fyoutube_title_parse), [get-artist-title on npm](https://npmjs.com/package/get-artist-title)).

### What the measurement theory says

The Badge result (D major printed; references say G major or E minor, code_map §7) is a textbook fifth error, and fifth errors run 5.6–13.6 % on pop/rock even for the best CNN (Bernardes' adaptive mode bias scores 13.6 % fifths on Isophonics) ([Korzeniowski & Widmer 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.05340)). The dominant shares six of seven pitch classes with the tonic, so any chroma-profile vote is inherently weak here; the discriminating evidence is where phrases cadence. Music theory says why chord labels alone cannot separate relative keys: the defining degrees for major are the fourth and seventh, held in the dominant and leading-tone chords, and "overlapping, common chords in closely related keys can create ambiguity" ([RWU Music Theory](https://rwu.pressbooks.pub/musictheory/?p=269)). Lee and Slaney's joint model, one HMM per key "similar to an isolated-word recognizer", estimates the key and "gives higher chord recognition accuracy than a universal, key-independent model", which is the formal version of coupling chords and key ([ISMIR 2007 paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2007/paper/000245.pdf), [Kyogu Lee, CCRMA](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~kglee/)); a 2026 piano pipeline runs key first and maps chords to scale degrees after ([arXiv 2605.06685](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06685)). Stems are not a documented accuracy win: no study measures key on Demucs-style stems versus mixes; HPSS is treated as generic preprocessing with asserted benefit ([Park & Lee ISMIR 2015](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/219_Paper.pdf)); detector confidence "hover[s] around 0.5" in low-energy stem passages ([arXiv 2609.26956](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26956)); and the best pop/rock numbers come from the full mix. Mode is the weakest component (about 79 % for both supervised and self-supervised models) and the 24-key taxonomy "fits blues, jazz and hip-hop poorly" ([S-KEY 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12907v2)). Relative-key disagreement between published tabs for the same song is evidence that a hedge of the form "G major (or E minor)" is a legitimate output class, with Essentia's `MajMin` as the shipping precedent.

### What transfers

Estimate on the whole song, or at least more than two minutes of it. Make the hedge (code_map §7 `KEY_HEDGE_MARGIN` 0.05) category-aware: classify the top-two pair with mir_eval's categories and hedge only when the runner-up is fifth-, relative- or parallel-related and the margin is small; print a hard answer when the runner-up is unrelated, because then the leader is usually right. Add a tonic-cadence veto against the dominant specifically: when the leader is a fifth above the runner-up and the song's last chord or the bass stem's final note is the runner-up's tonic, prefer the runner-up; this builds on the existing `FINAL_CHORD_BONUS` and `SECTION_END_WEIGHT` logic but targets the one error class the project hit. Weight voters by their measured per-category error rather than equally: a profile voter is strong at rejecting "other" keys and weak on fifth/relative; a chord/cadence voter is the reverse; the stems-based voter is an engineering experiment to validate on the blind songs, because the bass stem carries chord roots and the cadence bass line (a practical reason, not a measured one). For metadata, read `artists` and `track` first, treat `uploader` as the artist only when the channel matches the parsed artist or ends in " - Topic", distinguish "absent" (the `multiple_songs` blanking) from "disagrees", and order the evidence: credited fields, then AcoustID with a high score (whose absence of a match is itself a signal that the video is not the canonical recording), then shazamio (best on covers and live uploads), then title parsing with the channel as default artist, then raw uploader; when two ranked sources disagree, print the higher with the other in parentheses. The "Day Tripper by Natan Santos" line is precisely yt-dlp's own uploader fallback (code_map §7 `clean_artist_from`).

### What does not transfer, and why

Essentia is unavailable on Windows pip, so its profile zoo and `MajMin` class would have to be reimplemented; madmom's CNN would be the cheapest accuracy gain but its build on Python 3.12/3.13 and NumPy 2.x on Windows is unconfirmed; the deep models ship code without weights or weights without numbers. No published calibration of "margin below which to hedge" exists; it must be fitted on the project's labelled set. AcoustID's score semantics are undefined in the docs, shazamio's service terms are unaddressed and its raw result structure unconfirmed, and no corpus-level precision for yt-dlp's music fields exists; the YouTube Data API was not researched. GiantSteps' commercial benchmark table and Lee and Slaney's numbers were not retrievable, and no ISMIR 2023–2025 key paper surfaced.

### Evaluation protocol

Score with mir_eval's weighted key metric and report the per-category breakdown (correct, fifth, relative, parallel, other), which is what every cited paper does and what makes a fifth error visible rather than hidden in a weighted score; use the blind songs plus published-tab keys as truth, accepting relative-key pairs as semi-correct per the 0.3 weighting. For metadata, the only defensible evaluation is per-source precision over a labelled corpus of uploads of several kinds (official, Topic channel, fan re-upload, cover, compilation), since none exists in the literature.

Further sources read for this area: datasets and context ([GTZAN/LMD key, Schreiber & Müller](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.10839)).

## Area 8: Products hide uncertainty behind tiers and editors; only Soundslice asks the user

### What others do

The product landscape splits into four camps. ### What transfers, and what the conventions imply

The industry's uncertainty conventions are: warn in prose and hand over an editor; offer a simplified tier; show blank where nothing was detected (Capo's "none at all", UG's empty space, Yousician's empty patterns); Soundslice's scanner turning an uncertain detection into a question on a review screen ([Soundslice blog 226](https://soundslice.com/blog/226/pdf-and-photo-scanning-beta), [Soundslice blog 256](https://soundslice.com/blog/256/improvements-to-pdf-image-importer-sept-21), [Soundslice PDF import](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/creating/pdf-import/), [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Jun/20/music-scanning-by-soundslice)); and autochord's two-file overlay. Greyed strokes have no commercial precedent; the nearest vocabulary is editorial notation, where dashed means conjectural and brackets mean editorially supplied ([Dorico help](https://steinberg.help/dorico/v1/en/dorico/topics/notation_reference/notation_reference_slurs_styles_r.html), [DIAMM symbols](https://www.diamm.ac.uk/documents/692/Payne-F-Symbols_and_Abbreviations_hHJEaQg.pdf), [CMO wiki](https://projects.academiccloud.de/projects/corpus-musicae-ottomanicae-cmo-public-wiki/wiki/7-notes-on-transcription)), with a speech-to-text confidence map as a non-music analogue ([Medium](https://medium.com/@nautilustechlabs/speak-notes-your-ultimate-audio-transcript-generator-882e4e1f00bd)). A reviewer could reasonably ask whether a grey stroke row reads as "quiet" rather than "unsure", since UG readers are trained that blank is rest and X is mute, with no symbol for doubt; dashed or bracketed strokes would borrow an established meaning. "Riff heard, not transcribed" resembles Capo's blank and Transcribe!'s "guess" labelling more than anything in the chord apps. The sheet should state the assumed ukulele tuning (high-G or low-G) as the Gumroad arrangements do. The industry pattern for two guitars is to let the user ask for rhythm and lead as separate parts (Songsterr) or separate a stem first; every independent reviewer's failure list (multi-guitar mixes, palm mutes, swing) matches the pipeline's, so the review can treat Area 5 as an unsolved industry problem rather than a pipeline defect. The 73 % / 54 % annotator agreement gives a defensible ceiling argument for any chord-accuracy claim in the project's record.

### Evaluation protocol

None exists at product level: every commercial number is self-reported or anecdotal (Chordify 70–95 %, Melody Scanner "up to 86 %", Chord it "95 %+") and no independent benchmark of any product on a public set was found. The rigorous numbers are Yousician's and KIT's, on proprietary or small sets: the product survey's own reading of the two abstract pages confirms neither releases code ([arXiv 2508.07973 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07973), [TART abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02597)), and Chordify's support answer on strumming is the same under either URL form the notes recorded ([Chordify support](https://support.chordify.net/hc/en-us/articles/360019489618-Does-Chordify-show-the-strumming-patterns)).

## Five rules recur across all eight areas

Reading the eight areas together, the same five design rules appear in every literature the notes reached, and each one maps to a constant in the code map. First, make the signal scale-invariant before thresholding: log-flux differencing and whitening for onsets, summed-stem or peak normalisation for activity, pitch-tracker confidence rather than absolute chroma for texture. The fixed constants `delta` 0.07 on a min-max envelope, `REST_RATIO_MIN` 0.05 against the mix, and `RIFF_ENTROPY_MAX` 0.82 each sit on a quantity that moves with loudness, arrangement or timbre rather than with the musical fact being measured. Second, decide against a local reference with hysteresis and a minimum duration: Böck's moving mean and `w5`, pyannote's onset/offset pair, Basic Pitch's 0.5/0.3 split, Yousician's switch cost. The pipeline's single thresholds and per-bar or per-onset decisions are where flicker, double density and grid flips come from. Third, decode against a structured prior at the unit the sheet prints: whole-bar patterns from a vocabulary rather than slots, a tatum from an IOI histogram rather than per-onset snapping, a de-syncopated root pattern rather than a raw majority. Fourth, use repetition as evidence: it is Pati's riff-versus-solo feature, Yousician's transition prior, the pattern-discovery field's definition of a riff, and the one cue the voice-separation literature says register lacks. Fifth, choose constants by pooled sweeps across all ear-marked sections with leave-one-song-out checks, reporting the operating point with the curve; Gururani's 0.9 correlation between tuned thresholds and class priors is the formal statement of the owner's generality rule.

Two areas resist all five. Cut-versus-ring has no feature that survives clipping and no annotation protocol with better than 0.64 agreement even on clean vocals, so the literature's position matches 1.7's: print until the next onset and treat palm mute, a timbre class, as the only measurable articulation. Direction has one published audio-only accuracy on real strumming, about 70 %, and a physical cue that ukulele's re-entrant tuning weakens; the parity rule is the baseline every system beats, and nothing downloadable beats it.

## Conclusion

The pipeline's weak areas are not weak because the code is wrong but because they measure the right musical quantities in the wrong coordinate system: absolute level where the literature uses relative change, a single bar or onset where it uses a passage, a point threshold where it uses a band with memory, and texture where it uses repetition. That reframing is more useful than any single fix, because it predicts which future failures will appear (every new song with a different loudness profile, arrangement density or tempo will move a fixed constant off its band) and which will not (a scale-invariant, hysteretic decision against a passage-level prior degrades gracefully). The notes also show how little new machinery is needed: librosa already contains SuperFlux, multi-band flux, HPSS and autocorrelation; pyin already emits the voiced probability the texture literature uses; the separator already produces the drum and bass stems a cross-stem bleed estimate needs; and Basic Pitch and mir_eval are the only pip additions with clean licences and Windows support.

What changes for the reviewers is the standard of evidence. Each area now has a published protocol that turns the existing ear-truth clips into pooled curves (onset and grid-cell F at 50 ms with doubled and merged rates; pattern F1 and discontinuity; per-bar sed_eval error rate; riff-flag macro-accuracy with the majority baseline; per-part transition precision; weighted key with category breakdown), so a proposed constant can be shown to sit on a flat region across all songs rather than argued from one. The two areas with no good answer in the literature, stroke length and direction, are better served by an explicit "not measured" on the sheet, with the vocabulary of editorial notation rather than grey, than by a feature that cannot be validated against listeners who disagree with each other.
